# Brand + Digital Growth Sprint: agenda and exercises

CLIENT: [CLIENT]
PROJECT: [PROJECT]
FORMAT: 1-day or 2-day (choose in §2)
SOURCE SCOPE: Everything produced in this workshop is PROJECT-LEVEL and unverified until confirmed by a source (§9).

## 0. What this workshop is, and is not

**The question it answers:** does [CLIENT]'s current brand and digital experience still represent the business it has become, and if not, where are the biggest gaps worth closing first?

**What it produces:** a Brand & Digital Opportunity Blueprint (§8), including a Digital Experience Roadmap. The roadmap may or may not include a website rebuild. That is decided by the evidence and the Decider, not by the agenda.

**What it is not:**
- Not a Design Sprint. Nothing is prototyped or tested with customers inside it. It is a structured discovery and alignment workshop. Where it finds a big, risky page or flow decision, the right next step is a Design Sprint on that one page or flow (`design-sprint.md` §1, §5), or a cheap Lean test (`discovery-and-lean-loop.md` §5).
- Not research. It organizes evidence gathered in pre-work and the team's beliefs, and labels which is which.
- Not a brand strategy. It produces shared language and priorities that go to `brief-machine` as a raw source.

**Integrity rule.** The facilitator does not steer toward any outcome, including a website. The roadmap template has a slot for "the website is not the priority" and for "change nothing yet". If the pre-work already shows the answer is obviously non-digital (for example a pricing, product or sales problem), say so before the workshop and reconsider the format.

## 2. Choose 1 day or 2 days

| | 1-day | 2-day |
|---|---|---|
| Use when | Offer and audiences are broadly understood; the team mostly agrees who the brand is; pre-work is strong | New products or audiences since the site was built; leadership disagrees on who the brand is; several product lines compete for prominence |
| Brand lens | Compressed Brand Sprint (values, audiences, sliders, landscape) | Full Three-Hour Brand Sprint |
| Business lens | Offer-and-audience canvas (six boxes) | Lean Canvas, full, per priority offering if needed |
| Customer lens | Friction map of one priority journey | Friction maps of two or three journeys |
| Prioritization | Impact × Effort, single pass | Impact × Effort, then evidence check and a second pass on day 2 |
| Risk | Rushing prioritization. Protect the last session | Day-1 fatigue. Hard stop; do the evidence review overnight |

## 3. Pre-work (two to three weeks before)

TRAFFIK prepares; client supplies access. All pre-work findings carry source and date and are labeled OBSERVED (seen in data or heard from a customer), DERIVED (our reading) or ASSUMED.

| Pre-work | Owner | Method | Output on the board |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stakeholder interviews** (5–8, 30–45 min each) | Facilitator | Ask the Experts structure (`design-sprint.md` §4a): what changed in [N YEARS]; what success looks like; biggest risk; what customers misunderstand; what the site says that is no longer true. Silent HMW notes during listening | "What changed" timeline; quotes (role, not name); early HMWs |
| **Customer or prospect voice** (5 conversations, or equivalent recorded material) | Facilitator or strategist | Problem-interview structure (`discovery-and-lean-loop.md` §5a): context, how they chose, what they tried to do, where they got stuck, how they would describe [CLIENT] to a friend | Customer quotes wall (OBSERVED), sample stated |
| **Analytics and site audit** | UX / analytics | Heuristic Review (LUMA) against the top tasks; entry pages, drop-off, search terms, device split, form completion, content age; what products or capabilities have no page or a buried one | Digital evidence wall: 10–15 findings max, each with source |
| **Content and product-hierarchy inventory** | Strategist | List offerings now vs. what the site presents and in what order | "Business today vs. site today" side-by-side |
| **Competitor and category scan** (4–6 competitors, including one non-obvious) | Strategist / design | Public sites and messaging only. What each claims, whom it addresses, how it looks. No invented data | Competitor cards for the landscape 2x2 |

Pre-read to participants (one page, sent three days before): the question (§0), the agenda, and "bring one example of something the business now does that a customer would not learn from the site today."

Synthesis rule: TRAFFIK presents pre-work as evidence with labels, not as a recommendation. Do not open with a proposed answer.

## 5. Board setup (Miro or FigJam)

One frame per exercise, left to right in agenda order. Two note colors throughout: **yellow = belief or opinion from the room; blue = evidence from pre-work** (with source tag). Green notes for decisions. Red for OPEN.

| Frame | Contents before the session | Filled in the room |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Question and rules | The sprint question; the integrity rule; the note color key; parking lot | Parking lot |
| 1. What changed | [N YEARS] timeline from stakeholder interviews (blue) | Additions (yellow) |
| 2. Evidence wall | Customer quotes, analytics findings, audit findings, product inventory side-by-side (all blue) | Reactions: Rose / Thorn / Bud tags |
| 3. Brand | Brand Sprint templates: values, audiences, four sliders, blank 2x2 with competitor cards ready to place | Votes, positions, Decider marks |
| 4. Three-layer reality check | Three rows (Brand, Business, Digital) × four columns (Strength, Weakness, Opportunity, Threat) + a "source" field on every note | Notes; evidence tag per note |
| 5. Offer and audience canvas | Lean Canvas boxes (6 or 9) with an evidence-label field in each | Notes; labels |
| 6. Journey friction | Swimlane per journey: stage → customer goal → touchpoint → what the site shows → friction → evidence | Friction notes; HMWs |
| 7. Opportunity list | Empty; fed by HMW clustering | Opportunity cards (one per card: "HMW..." + the lens it came from) |
| 8. Impact × Effort | 2x2 with an evidence-strength marker per card | Placement, dots, Decider supervote |
| 9. Roadmap draft | Now / Next / Later / Not now / Test first columns | Cards moved from frame 8 |

Remote: 50-minute blocks, 10-minute breaks, cameras on for votes, visible timer, private-mode or hidden-until-reveal for voting.

## 6. Exercises, and where they depart from the strategy notes

| Notes proposed | What this template uses | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Brand Archetype | **Brand Sprint exercises** (Top 3 Values, Top 3 Audiences, Personality Sliders, Competitive Landscape), with archetypes optional and later | Archetype systems (Jungian, Mark & Pearson) are not in CreativeEngine's reviewed frameworks, and in a room they tend to produce a borrowed label ("we're a Sage") that sounds agreed but gives design and copy little to act on. Sliders and values are sourced method (`design-sprint.md` §3), produce specific positions, and show disagreement as a spread. If the client asks for an archetype, `design-champ` and `word-smither` can use one later as a shorthand, derived from the sprint output, not as the workshop's anchor |
| Three-layer SWOT (Brand / Business / Digital) | **Kept**, as a "three-layer reality check", with two rules: every note carries a source tag (blue evidence vs yellow belief), and Opportunities / Threats are seeded from the competitor scan, not generated from memory | The three-layer split is a good idea: it stops "digital" from absorbing everything. Unsourced SWOT in a room produces confident lists of opinions. The tag makes that visible |
| Lean Canvas | **Kept, scaled**: six boxes (Customer Segments, Problem, UVP, Solution, Channels, Key Metrics) in the 1-day; full nine in the 2-day, with Revenue, Cost and Unfair Advantage supplied by the client only | `discovery-and-lean-loop.md` §5a. A canvas is most useful when the offer or audience has changed, which is the premise here. Fill in order of certainty, not left to right |
| Customer / Journey Friction | **Kept**, as Experience Diagramming (LUMA) seeded with pre-work evidence | Journeys drawn only from staff memory are ASSUMED. The pre-work is what makes this exercise honest |
| Experience Prioritization, Impact × Effort | **Kept**, as LUMA Importance/Difficulty Matrix, plus an evidence-strength marker and non-digital options on the list | Adds the evidence dimension (`discovery-and-lean-loop.md` §3) and removes the built-in bias toward digital fixes |
| "Stakeholders identify themselves that 'our experience no longer reflects our business'" | **Removed as a goal.** The room may reach that conclusion, or may not | Engineering a scripted realization is steering. It is also fragile: a client who later feels led will distrust the roadmap. See §0 integrity rule |
| (not in notes) | **Added: customer voice in pre-work; a Decider precondition; HMW notes; a "Not now" and "Test first" column** | The four lenses include Customer, but the notes' workshop has no customers in it. The rest are standard sprint safeguards |

## 7. Run sheets

Rule for every exercise: **silent solo writing → share → cluster → vote → Decider decides.** Unresolved disagreement goes on the board as OPEN, in red.

### 7a. 1-day variant (about 7 hours of working time)

| Time | Session | Purpose | Steps | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## 8. Output: Brand & Digital Opportunity Blueprint

Delivered five to ten working days after the workshop. Each section states its evidence label. Written by TRAFFIK from the raw board; the raw board is kept unedited in `raw/`.

```
BRAND & DIGITAL OPPORTUNITY BLUEPRINT
CLIENT / PROJECT / DATE / PARTICIPANTS (roles)
STATUS: PROJECT-LEVEL. Workshop output. Unverified until confirmed.

1. The question and the short answer (half a page)
   What we set out to answer; what we found; what we did not find.

2. What changed in [N YEARS]                              [OBSERVED from interviews]
3. Brand summary                                           [team belief]
   Values (#1 marked), ranked audiences, slider positions, competitive space,
   OPEN disagreements. (Archetype only if requested; derived from these.)
4. Audience and customer priorities                        [labels per item]
   Who matters most, what they are trying to do, what they don't know about [CLIENT].
5. Offer and product hierarchy                             [canvas; labels per box]
   What the business leads with now vs. what the experience leads with.
6. Three-layer reality check (Brand / Business / Digital)  [evidence vs. belief marked]
7. Experience friction                                     [journey maps; evidence per point]
8. Content gaps                                            [audit + inventory]
9. Competitive whitespace                                  [our read of public material]
10. Prioritized opportunities                               [matrix + votes + evidence marker]
11. Digital Experience Roadmap
      NOW / NEXT / LATER / NOT NOW / TEST FIRST
      Each item: opportunity, lens, evidence strength, owner, first step.
      May include: messaging and positioning work; content fixes on the current site;
      a Design Sprint on one page or flow; IA / UX work; a light brand evolution;
      a rebuild; non-digital actions; or "no change yet".
12. What this workshop does NOT show
13. OPEN items, risks, and who decides them
14. Recommended next step (one), and the alternative
```

Handoff: the Blueprint and raw board go to `brief-machine` as PROJECT-LEVEL raw sources. Brand summary goes to `design-champ` and `word-smither` as inputs, not conclusions. Any follow-on creative work is judged by `creative-engine-director`; a workshop priority is not an ADVANCE.

## 9. Evidence rules

- Workshop statements (values, audiences, claims, competitor readings, priorities) are **PROJECT-LEVEL and unverified**. They do not become client knowledge without deliberate review and the user's confirmation.
- Customer-lens statements from staff are ASSUMED unless backed by pre-work customer evidence.
- Pre-work findings state sample and source. Five conversations show patterns, not percentages.
- Competitor readings are TRAFFIK's read of public material, cited as our analysis.
- Impact votes are stated preference, not measured impact.
- No invented data, quotes, benchmarks or projections in the Blueprint. If a gap matters, it becomes a Test first item.
- Build note: if the roadmap leads to page production at TRAFFIK, the route is native Instapage from an approved Figma specification (`instapage-production` skill). A workshop sketch or sprint prototype is never the production source.

## 10. Facilitation notes

- **Neutrality is the product.** The facilitator never says what the answer is. If a TRAFFIK observer wants to argue for a direction, it waits until after the session.
- **Watch the website gravity.** Participants will reach for "the website" as the answer to everything because it is visible. When a card says "new website", ask: which job, for whom, on what evidence? Rewrite it as the job.
- **Belief vs. evidence out loud.** When a debate stalls, ask the evidence lead: "what do we actually know?" If nothing, the item is a test, not a decision.
- **Protect the last session.** Prioritization is where the value is. Cut earlier exercises before cutting it.
- **Disagreement is data.** An OPEN brand position in red is more useful than a fake consensus.
- **Rotate who shares first.** Customer-facing staff go first in the friction session. In the brand session, do not let leadership go first by default.
- **Silence first, always.** Solo writing before discussion so the most senior voice does not set the answer.
