# Writers' room

> WordSmither knowledge. Four lenses for channelling how great writers and agencies attack a brief. Method, not surface style. Added 2026-09-23 at Andres's request.

## What this is

The copycraft framework (`shared/frameworks/hey-whipple-copycraft.md`) is strong on tests: what to kill and why. This file is its generative partner: four distinct ways of *finding* the idea and the voice, each drawn from a writer or agency whose work proves the approach.

Each lens is a question to put to the brief, a set of moves, a voice, a list of what it kills, and a critic's question. Running two or three lenses on one brief produces genuinely different mechanisms, not rewordings of one line ("Write wide, show few" in `WordSmither/AGENT.md`).

| Lens | Believes | Asks the brief |
|---|---|---|
| **Sullivan** | The truth, said simply and specifically, beats clever | What's the one true thing, and what picture makes it land? |
| **Droga** | Do something, don't just say something | What could the brand *do* that people would talk about? |
| **Wieden+Kennedy** | The brand is a person with a point of view | What would this brand say if it had the nerve? |
| **Goodby Silverstein** | Human truth first, then comedy | What do people notice only when it's missing? |

## House rules

- **Channel the thinking, not the costume.** A lens borrows a way of attacking a problem. Borrowing a reference campaign's structure, look or device directly is allowed (Andres's taste profile). Its claims never become client facts.
- **No invented quotes.** Never attribute a sentence to Sullivan, Droga, Wieden, Kennedy, Goodby or Silverstein that isn't well documented. Principles here are CreativeEngine's summaries of the work, not quotations. The only quoted lines are famous campaign lines.
- **Reference shelf is titles, not text.** Cite campaigns by name, brand, year and mechanism. Do not reproduce their scripts, manifestos or long copy in client work.
- **The client still outranks the room.** Brief, claim ledger, mandatories and the client's voice beat any lens. A lens that needs a claim, offer or behavior the client hasn't approved is flagged, not written around.
- **Taste is Andres's.** `shared/knowledge/ANDRES_CREATIVE_OS.md` decides fit. The lenses widen what gets written; they don't decide what gets shown.

## 1. Sullivan

**Who.** Luke Sullivan, copywriter (Fallon McElligott, The Martin Agency, GSD&M), author of *Hey Whipple, Squeeze This*. CreativeEngine's copycraft framework already operationalizes his approach; this lens is the short version for the room. Do not duplicate the framework: open it for the full toolbox.

**Believes.** Find the one simple, true, specific thing. Say it like a person. Let the picture carry half. Write a hundred to find one.

**Asks the brief.** What's the one true thing, and what picture makes it land without the line explaining it?

**Moves.**
- The specific over the general: a named object, a real hour, a real place (framework §9 compression, §6 truth + conflict).
- 1 + 1 = 3: the line and the picture each say something the other can't (§2).
- Honest self-awareness: admit the obvious flaw, then turn it (§8 enemy, §13 smile in the mind).
- Volume: a long, ugly list first; the good line is usually around number sixty (§4).

**Voice.** Plain words, short sentences, wry, warm. Sounds like a smart friend, never like a brochure.

**Kills.** Adjectives from the brief, the category's clichés (§10), lines that caption the picture, cleverness with no idea behind it.

**Critic asks.** Is it true, is it simple, and could only this brand say it?

**Where it misfires.** Alone, it can produce tidy, correct work that nobody remembers. Pair it with a bolder lens when the brief needs fame.

## 2. Droga

**Who.** David Droga: Saatchi & Saatchi and Publicis creative leader; founded Droga5 in New York in 2006; after Accenture acquired Droga5 in 2019, he went on to lead Accenture Song.

**Believes.** The strongest advertising is often not an ad. It's an act: something the brand does, makes or changes that is useful, surprising or newsworthy enough that people pass it on. Purpose only counts when it has teeth. Every idea has to deserve to exist.

**Asks the brief.** What could the brand *do*, make or change that people would talk about, and that only this brand could credibly do?

**Moves.**
- **Turn the message into a behavior.** If the brand says it cares about X, what action would prove it without a word of copy?
- **Borrow a system.** Hijack something that already exists (a menu, a map, a book launch, a restaurant bill) and give it a new job.
- **Make it useful.** A tool, a service, a moment of help. Utility is the most defensible kind of attention.
- **Make it news.** Would a journalist write about it? If not, it's still an ad.
- **Say the uncomfortable truth.** Name the thing the category avoids, then act on it.

**Voice.** Confident, economical, often serious with a flash of wit. Lets the act speak; the copy is the caption to a real event, not the event.

**Kills.** Purpose statements with no action behind them, "awareness" as an idea, stunts that don't connect to the brand's truth, anything that only exists as media.

**Critic asks.** Why does this deserve to exist, and would anyone share it if we didn't pay to show it to them?

**Reference shelf.**
| Work | Brand | Year | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tap Project | UNICEF | 2007 | Borrowed the restaurant bill: pay for tap water, fund clean water |
| The Great Schlep | Jewish Council for Education & Research | 2008 | A comic call to act, aimed at one specific behavior (visit your grandparents in Florida) |
| Decode | Jay-Z / Bing | 2010 | A book launch turned into a citywide hunt: the product becomes the medium |
| I Will What I Want | Under Armour | 2014 | The rejection letter read over proof of the opposite |
| If We Made It | Newcastle | 2015 | The Super Bowl ad they couldn't afford, described instead of made |
| The Truth Is Hard | The New York Times | 2017 | A brand saying the uncomfortable thing at the moment it cost something |

**Where it misfires.** Acts need client operations, budget, legal sign-off and time. Never present an act as approved or feasible; flag what the client would have to do. Fake acts (a stunt staged only for the case-study film) fail CreativeDirector's case-study deception test.

## 3. Wieden+Kennedy

**Who.** Founded in Portland, Oregon, in 1982 by Dan Wieden and David Kennedy. Independent. Offices worldwide, including London and Amsterdam.

**Believes.** A brand is a person with a point of view, and people respond to conviction. Talk to the audience as adults. Be brave in the culture, not just in the category. Risk failure to make something alive; the agency's own motto is "Fail harder."

**Asks the brief.** What would this brand say if it had the nerve? What does it believe that someone would disagree with?

**Moves.**
- **Find the belief, then say it plainly.** Three words can carry a whole brand if the belief is real ("Just do it.").
- **Write it spoken.** Rhythm, breath, the way a person actually talks. Read it aloud; if it doesn't sound like a person, rewrite it.
- **Tension with heart.** Provoke, then earn it with warmth or truth. Swagger with no heart is just noise.
- **Commit to the absurd.** When it's funny, go all the way, and play it with a straight face (Old Spice).
- **Hand the mic to the culture.** Let the athlete, the city, the fan say it in their own words.

**Voice.** Direct, rhythmic, sometimes defiant, often funny, never corporate. Short lines that feel inevitable once you've heard them.

**Kills.** Hedging, committee language, "solutions," brand-speak, any line a lawyer would be relaxed about because it says nothing.

**Critic asks.** Does this have a point of view someone could disagree with, and does it sound like a person said it?

**Reference shelf.**
| Work | Brand | Year | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Just Do It | Nike | 1988 | A belief compressed into an imperative that fits every athlete |
| Cog | Honda (W+K London) | 2003 | A product truth (engineering) turned into two minutes nobody skips |
| Grrr | Honda (W+K London) | 2004 | An engineer's hatred of noise, sung as a cheerful anthem |
| The Man Your Man Could Smell Like | Old Spice | 2010 | Absurd confidence, played straight, then turned into real-time replies |
| Write the Future | Nike (W+K Amsterdam) | 2010 | The stakes of one moment, played out as consequences |
| Imported from Detroit | Chrysler | 2011 | A city's pride and grit handed to a brand |
| Dream Crazy | Nike | 2018 | A belief that cost something, said anyway |

**Where it misfires.** Bravado on regulated claims (education, finance, health) invents promises the brand can't keep. Keep the attitude in the voice, never in the claim.

## 4. Goodby Silverstein

**Who.** Goodby Silverstein & Partners, founded in San Francisco in 1983 by Jeff Goodby and Rich Silverstein. Known for putting account planning (Jon Steel's *Truth, Lies and Advertising*) at the center of the creative process.

**Believes.** Start with a human truth that research or observation uncovers, then make it entertaining. Comedy works when it's built on something true. The strategy should be as surprising as the execution.

**Asks the brief.** What do people notice only when it's missing? What true, slightly embarrassing thing do people do that nobody says out loud?

**Moves.**
- **Deprivation.** Show life without the product or the behavior, and let the absence make the case ("Got milk?": people only think about milk when they run out).
- **The observed truth.** Find the small, specific, universal behavior and make it the whole story.
- **Comedy of consequences.** Take one decision and follow it to its ridiculous conclusion.
- **The planner's twist.** Reframe what the category thinks it's selling (not milk, the moment you don't have it).
- **Characters who carry a campaign.** A recurring, lovable device that can run for years (the Budweiser lizards).

**Voice.** Warm, clever, story-led, generous to the audience. Humor that makes people feel smart rather than sold to.

**Kills.** Product-benefit ads with no human in them, jokes with no truth under them, strategy that says what everyone already says.

**Critic asks.** What's the human truth here, and would anyone recognize themselves in it?

**Reference shelf.**
| Work | Brand | Year | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Got milk? | California Milk Processor Board | 1993 | Deprivation: the product is noticed only in its absence |
| Budweiser Lizards | Budweiser | 1990s | Characters hijack the brand's own famous campaign |
| The Computer Is Personal Again | HP | 2006 | Famous people told through their stuff, never their faces |
| Dalí Lives | The Dalí Museum | 2019 | Technology used to bring the subject back to talk to visitors |

**Where it misfires.** Comedy can punch down at an audience with real constraints. The truth must be affectionate, never at the audience's expense.

## How WordSmither runs the room

### EXPLORE
- Pick **two or three lenses** whose question fits the object and the brief. Not all four by default: a proof-point caption needs no Droga act.
- Write each lens's answer as its own territory, labelled with the lens. The goal is different mechanisms: a line, an act, a voice, a truth.
- A lens that produces nothing different from another is dropped, not padded.

### DEVELOP
- **Voice pass.** Take the lens the chosen idea came from and ask its voice question. W+K: does it sound spoken? Sullivan: can a word go? Goodby: is the truth still visible under the joke? Droga: is it still an act, or has it become an ad about an act?
- **Read-aloud check.** Read every headline, script and body copy aloud. Mark any line where you take a breath in the wrong place, repeat a sound by accident, or sound like a brochure. Vary sentence length on purpose.

### JUDGE
- **Critic panel.** Put the finished work to the critic questions of the lenses that apply. Count each weakness once, under its most precise diagnosis (`WordSmither/AGENT.md`, senior self-critique).
- Lens verdicts are recommendations to CreativeDirector, never verdicts.

### In the handoff
Add one line to the handoff block: `LENS: [which lens produced it]`. It tells CreativeDirector what kind of idea it is looking at and which critic question to ask.

## Adding a lens

More lenses can be added later (for example Bernbach and DDB, David Abbott, Fallon). Each needs: who (documented facts only), believes, asks the brief, moves, voice, kills, critic asks, reference shelf and where it misfires. A new lens must produce a mechanism the existing four don't; otherwise it isn't added.
