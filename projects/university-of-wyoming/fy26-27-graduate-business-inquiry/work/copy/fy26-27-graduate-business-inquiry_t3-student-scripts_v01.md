## How these scripts stay honest

These are scripted lines, delivered by real students, **drawn from that student's own experience**. That works on one condition: **every line is true for the person saying it.**

- **Each script is a casting spec first.** The MUST BE TRUE column is what UW looks for when it supplies students. A script is only shot with a student for whom that column is true.
- **Lines never move between people.** If a line doesn't fit the student UW supplies, we rewrite it from their intake conversation. We don't find someone to say it.
- **The week items are placeholders.** Each script's opening week is illustrative. The real week comes from the student.
- **The student confirms, then UW approves.** Before the shoot, the student confirms each line is true for them. S1 p3 makes testimonial content a client-approval item.
- **No claims in anyone's mouth.** No student says anything about salary, promotion, job offers, pass rates, rankings, cost or program facts. Program facts appear only as supers, word for word from the claim ledger, marked VERBATIM.
- **Identify them honestly.** An end super gives first name (with permission) and CURRENT STUDENT or ALUM.

Not legal advice. Endorsement rules for scripted testimonials, including the FTC's in the US, turn on the same point: scripted wording is fine; scripted experience isn't. UW's compliance or legal reviewer should see this approach before casting starts.

## The idea

**T2 asks the question. T3 is someone who already answered it.**

T2 ends on "Too stubborn to stop? You sound like a Cowboy." T3 shows the person that line describes: a real UW student or alum saying their own week, and the one thing they thought would rule them out.

Every script uses the same spine, so the two territories read as one system:

```
0–3s   THE WEEK      Spoken to camera, in T2's grammar: three collisions, then "Same week."
                     Super: the week, set as T2 sets it. UW logo + program label in frame (S1 p6).
3–10s  THE TURN      What they thought would rule them out, and what they found instead.
                     Program fact super, VERBATIM, about 6–10s (S1 p6: benefit and proof by 10s).
10–15s LAND          Last line lands on the student. End frame: logo, program label, name and status.
                     Apply Now is the Meta button, never drawn.
```

**Narrative-craft calls:**
- **Obstacle, not a sequence.** The week is the obstacle, and the belief is the second obstacle. The turn dismantles the belief. That's the strategy ("prospects rule themselves out") told by someone who didn't.
- **One turn.** No T2 answer line ("You sound like a Cowboy.") at the end of T3. It would be a second resolution after the student's own, and the student's line is stronger. Cowboys language also still waits on Q1.
- **Brand entry: participant.** The student *is* UW. The program label from frame 1 keeps it from reading as a tag-only story.
- **Budget: 24–35 spoken words.** That's roughly two to three words per second, leaving room for a held beat before the last line. Read every script aloud against a stopwatch at intake. The spoken lines are written as speech, not as copy.

## Intake conversation (about 20 minutes per student, before the shoot)

1. Describe one real week in the program: three things that collided.
2. Before you applied, what did you think would rule you out?
3. Who questioned it, and what did you tell them?
4. What almost made you stop, and why didn't you?
5. What would you tell someone at your old job who thinks this isn't for them?
6. Read the draft script aloud. What would you never say? Say it your way.

Then the student confirms, in writing, that the final lines are true for them. That confirmation goes with the release (who holds releases: confirm with UW).
