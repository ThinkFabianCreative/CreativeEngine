The campaign in one line

We take the thing our audience gets teased for, being stubborn, and tell them it's the qualification. In Wyoming, stubborn has a name: Cowboy.

Manifesto

Somebody closed the restaurant, finished the pitch deck after close, and still gave the toast on Saturday.
Somebody covered for two people who didn't show, and made the recital anyway.
Somebody ran month-end close, counted the stockroom, and got to the rec-league final in time for warm-ups.

None of that goes on a résumé. People just call you stubborn.

Keep it.

A master's degree doesn't wait for a quiet week. It gets done in the weeks you already have, next to everything else. The coursework is hard. Not stopping is harder, and you've had years of practice.

In Wyoming there's a word for somebody who won't quit when the week says they should.

Too stubborn to stop?
You sound like a Cowboy.

Rationale

What the brief asks

The ad may be a prospect's first exposure to UW. In a few seconds it has to name UW and the program, give a credible reason to care, and move someone to apply. The measure is application starts and completed applications, not clicks.

What stands in the way

Most of the people we're reaching aren't comparing UW with another school. They're comparing a master's degree with the week they already have: a shift to cover, a team to hold together, kids, a side project. Somewhere in that week they decided grad school is for people with more time. They rule themselves out before they ever rule UW in.

Who we're talking to

They're not the boss yet. They're the one holding it together. Twenty-one to thirty-five, in Wyoming (plus two Colorado counties for the Online MBA), working in restaurants and retail, on plant floors, in bank branches and at 9-to-5 desks. Some are still in college. They want the promotion, or to be their own boss, and a master's is how they picture getting there. What they doubt is whether it fits.

The insight

The people with the least time are already doing the hardest part.

They finish things in weeks that should have stopped them. The people around them have a word for it, and it isn't usually a compliment: stubborn.

The move

We take that word and turn it around. Then we give it a name only UW can use. The Cowboy is UW's mascot, and in Wyoming being called a Cowboy is a badge of honor. UW's own campaign, The World Needs More Cowboys, takes it beyond the stadium: Cowboy is a way of living, not a place you were born. Forging your own path against the odds is leadership, it's entrepreneurial, and it's the Code of the West. So when someone recognizes their own week and admits they're too stubborn to stop, UW answers: you sound like a Cowboy.

Why it works in feed

  • It starts with them, not with us. The first thing anyone sees is their own week. Recognition stops the scroll faster than a campus photo.
  • The question makes them answer. "Too stubborn to stop?" gets a yes in their head before they've decided to pay attention.
  • The answer turns a flaw into belonging. The brand arrives at the turn, so UW is the one who says the flattering thing.
  • The offer still lands on time. Program, one proof point and Apply Now all arrive inside 15 seconds, with the UW logo and program label on screen from the first frame.

Why it's UW's

Any university can tell people they can do it. Only the home of the Cowboys can call them one for it. The line hands the school's own name to someone who has earned it.

Bringing it to life

Every program runs as one post in one of two forms, a five-card carousel or a 15-second animated video, with the same five beats. Here is the Online MBA, card by card:

Post text: You've never needed a quiet week. The University of Wyoming Online MBA is 100% online and asynchronous: 30 credits, full- or part-time, with no requirement to visit campus. Apply Now.

The week is written as a list, and each item gets crossed off in the person's own pen. Everything is done. That's the point: they finish everything, and "too stubborn to stop?" arrives as recognition, not a dare.

4. Then we meet the people who answered it

The Cowboy ads ask the question. The student films show people who already answered it: real UW students and alumni, shot in Laramie, telling their own week and the one thing they thought would rule them out. Draft example for the On-Campus MBA, to be rewritten around the real student:

Last double. Two weeks' notice. New lease. Same week.
I thought an MBA was for people who already managed people.
I'd been running a kitchen crew for five years.
(beat) Nobody called it management.

Every line has to be true for the person saying it. Students confirm their lines; UW approves them. No student speaks about salary, jobs, rankings or cost.
