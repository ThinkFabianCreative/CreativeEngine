## Part 1 · Strategic assets

**Brand Promise**
College of Business: **"Wyoming-Built. Globally Prepared. Entrepreneurially Driven."** *(client document; role in this campaign OPEN)*
University (Brand Book 2020): UW "develops creative, outside thinkers who thrive by building grit, integrity, independence and optimism." *(currency OPEN)*
*Which one leads this campaign still has to be confirmed with UW.*

**Core Creative Idea**
**ASSUMPTION:** ***Stories where UW graduate business students do the real work before they graduate.***
Why this one: it turns V1's rule, "Show the Opportunity. Don't Simply Claim It.", into a kind of story. It can carry every program: the Jackson Hole summit, a CPA-prep milestone, a family-office visit, a promotion earned mid-program. It also fits the College narrative ("More Than a Graduate Degree") and the Cowboy spirit without needing either one. *Whether "The World Needs More Cowboys" is mandatory is still OPEN with UW.*

**Universal Human Truth**
**ASSUMPTION:** *"I want my career to move, but I can't stake time and real money on a degree that only adds letters after my name."*
The tension is ambition against the fear of a wasted investment. It sits behind every question in V1: "Is this program worth the investment?", "What will this degree help me achieve?"

Leadership Development · Entrepreneurial Mindset · Global Perspective · Experiential Learning · The Code of the West · The Cowboy State Advantage

## Part 2 · The problem

**Business challenge**
UW's four graduate business programs each serve a different audience with a different value proposition. The budget reaches only prospects who have already shown interest or intent, so the job isn't awareness. It's moving them to apply and enroll, and giving them a clear reason to choose UW over other programs. MS FP&WM is newer and less established, so it also needs some introductory messaging inside retargeting.

**Message architecture, all programs** *(from V1)*
Prospect Need → Program Benefit → Proof → UW Differentiation → Next Action

| Stage | Prospect's question | Message job | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inquiry | Should I apply? | A program-specific reason to apply | Apply Now |
| Application started | Is it worth finishing? | Less explaining, more urgency: real deadlines and start dates where they apply | Complete Your Application |
| Applied / not enrolled | Why should I choose UW? | Reinforce the value and move them toward enrollment | Visit Campus (On-Campus MBA) · Take the Next Step / Enroll Now (online programs) |

### JTBD 1 · On-Campus MBA
**PERSUADE** prospects who have inquired **TO** apply, and then to choose UW in person.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Target audience** | Recent business graduates · early-career professionals seeking applied leadership development · UW degree completers · regional candidates considering experiential MBAs · entrepreneurship-oriented students |
| **Behavior to change** | **ASSUMPTION:** they treat MBAs as interchangeable, so they default to a bigger-name or closer program, or put it off "one more year" |
| **Insight** | **ASSUMPTION:** *"Every MBA brochure says 'hands-on'. Not one has shown me what I'd actually be doing on a Tuesday."* |
| **New belief** | UW's MBA happens in the real world, beyond a traditional classroom, at an affordable price |
| **New behavior** | Apply, then visit campus |

**Core positioning:** an affordable, immersive MBA built around applied experiences, executive access and close faculty relationships. It is the clearest expression of the UW brand.
**Creative question:** *What will I actually experience as a UW MBA student?*
**Barrier type:** ☒ **Aspirational**: they need to see themselves in the experience.

### JTBD 2 · Online MBA
**PERSUADE** working professionals who have inquired **TO** apply, without stepping away from their careers.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Target audience** | Working professionals preparing for management · individual contributors moving into people leadership · professionals who can't leave full-time work · UW alumni and regional professionals · value-conscious professionals comparing accredited online MBAs |
| **Behavior to change** | **ASSUMPTION:** they've inquired at three or four online MBAs, keep comparison tabs open, and stall at "not this term" |
| **Insight** | **ASSUMPTION:** *"Every time I'm ready to start, work gets busy. If I wait for a quiet year, I'll never do it."* |
| **New belief** | A UW MBA fits around my career, and the value makes advancement within reach |
| **New behavior** | Apply, and complete the application |

**Core positioning:** an AACSB-accredited MBA that fits around a full-time career, with the flexibility and value to make advancement attainable.
**Lead territory (V1):** *Advance Your Career Without Stepping Away From It.*
**Barrier type:** ☒ **Credential**: is it accredited, flexible and good value? Lead with proof.

### JTBD 3 · MS Accounting
**PERSUADE** accounting prospects who have inquired **TO** apply, as their route to CPA readiness and advancement.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Target audience** | Accounting graduates pursuing CPA eligibility · students needing credits toward CPA requirements · early-career accountants seeking advancement · candidates comparing online and on-campus · employer-referred talent |
| **Behavior to change** | **ASSUMPTION:** they treat the master's as credit-collecting to reach 150 hours, and pick the cheapest or most convenient credits |
| **Insight** | **ASSUMPTION:** *"I need the credits anyway. The real question is whether they get me through the exam or just make me eligible to sit it."* |
| **New belief** | UW's MS Accounting is designed for CPA readiness and career advancement, with real support |
| **New behavior** | Apply, and enroll |

**Core positioning:** a STEM-designated, AACSB-accredited master's built to strengthen CPA readiness and career advancement, with online and on-campus options.
**Territory (V1):** CPA Readiness + Career Advancement + Value.
**Barrier type:** ☒ **Credential**.

### JTBD 4 · MS Financial Planning & Wealth Management
**PERSUADE** finance prospects **TO** understand what makes the program unique, and then to apply.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Target audience** | Finance and accounting graduates entering wealth management · early-career financial-services professionals moving into advisory roles · bank, trust and family-office employees · private banking, estate and investment advisory professionals · advisors seeking deeper credentials |
| **Behavior to change** | They don't know the program yet. **ASSUMPTION:** they plan to earn credentials on the job and don't see a master's as the faster way in |
| **Insight** | **ASSUMPTION:** *"Clients trust a network as much as a credential, and I don't have the network yet."* |
| **New belief** | This degree gives me specialized expertise *and* access to an industry ecosystem |
| **New behavior** | Apply |

**Core positioning:** a specialized graduate degree connected to Wyoming's Jackson Hole wealth ecosystem and the careers around it.
**The story:** not "earn a master's in financial planning", but *gain specialized expertise and access to an industry ecosystem.*
**Sequence:** Introduce the value → Establish credibility → Create interest → Drive action.
**Barrier type:** ☒ **Aspirational**: they need to see the access and the network.

**Who they are** *(ASSUMPTION: composite people, built to brief against; not research)*
- **On-Campus MBA · 24, two years out of a business degree,** working a first job in Laramie, Casper or the Front Range. Feels capable but under-used. Afraid that waiting means falling behind, and equally afraid of spending a year on theory. Judges a program by what it would *let them do*.
- **Online MBA · 34, operations or team lead,** in Cheyenne, Gillette or northern Colorado, with kids or caring duties. There's a promotion conversation coming and an MBA would settle it. Judges a program by whether it fits a real week, and by the price.
- **MS Accounting · 22, accounting senior or new associate,** with an offer that depends on CPA progress. Practical, numbers-first, allergic to fluff. Judges a program by exam readiness and cost per credit.
- **MS FP&WM · 27, in a bank, trust or brokerage role,** watching advisors with books of business. Knows the field runs on relationships. Judges a program by who it puts them in a room with.

**Cultural thought-starter** *(ASSUMPTION)*
Grad school has a credibility problem: people increasingly ask whether degrees are worth it, and employers increasingly say they hire for skills. The opening for UW: stop arguing for the degree and show the skills and the access being used. Proof beats promise.

## Part 4 · Creative ambition

**Target rung on the Creative Ladder**

| # | Rung | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | Cultural Movement | Sets a new socio-cultural trend *(an outcome; target only)* |
| 9 | Contagious | Earned outpaces paid; enters pop culture *(an outcome; target only)* |
| 8 | Game Changer | Breaks the category's codes and creates new ones |
| 7 | Showtime | People enjoy it for its originality, craft and entertainment |
| 6 | Fresh | A new spin on known solutions, distinctively done |
| 5 | Ownable | Within category codes, but clearly ours. **The floor** |
| 4 | Deja Vu | Stereotype work any brand could run ✗ |
| 3 | Puzzling | Confusing; the message gets lost ✗ |
| 2 | Disconnected | Borrowed interest with no brand meaning ✗ |
| 1 | Calamity | Reckless; damages the brand ✗ |

**Our target:** **ASSUMPTION: 6 · Fresh as the floor for every cell, 7 · Showtime for the hero cells** (On-Campus MBA and MS FP&WM at the Inquiry stage) · **Why:** retargeting audiences have already seen the generic grad-school ad and ignored it. Only work that is unmistakably UW and specific earns the click. The two programs with the most distinctive proof (real experiences, Jackson Hole access) can carry more ambition. *To be agreed with UW.*

**Three benchmarks at that rung** *(ASSUMPTION: proposed by Traffik, all from outside the category)*
1. **The Economist, red posters:** one smart line, one color, modular across hundreds of executions. A model for a static system that stays fresh.
2. **Apple, "Shot on iPhone":** the proof *is* the ad. Show the outcome, never claim it.
3. **Spotify, data billboards ("Thanks 2016, it's been weird"):** real facts turned into wit. A model for using UW's proof points with personality.

**Fame**
☒ **ASSUMPTION:** small-scale fame. Not press, but *would a current student or alum repost it?* Work that UW's own community shares gets a second life in exactly the network prospects trust.

**Category stereotypes to avoid**
Generic higher-ed lines (V1's banned list) · graduation caps and gowns · laptop-in-a-coffee-shop "flexibility" · handshakes and skylines · diverse students laughing at a table · stereotypical cowboy imagery (the UW Campaign Guide asks for "the opposite of a stereotypical image cowboy") · unsupported "#1", "premier" and "best value" claims
