## The principle

Take a phrase normally used *against* the audience and let it read two ways. First read: an insult or a flaw. Second read: the exact quality that makes them extraordinary. The flip happens in the viewer's head, which is where "see something in themselves" happens.

Channel 4 earned it because the attitude belonged to the athletes. Our phrase must be one this audience actually hears or tells itself. S6 (user-reported, unverified) describes a possible "chip on their shoulder"; that is a hypothesis to test, not proof.

## What gets said about this audience

- "You're doing too much."
- "Why would you do that to yourself?"
- "Must be nice to have the time."
- "You're so stubborn."
- "You don't know when to quit."

## Lines that read two ways

| Line | First read (flaw) | Second read (grit) | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Some people don't know when to quit.** | A complaint about you | Grit, said in a Western voice | Lowest. Lead candidate |
| **Nobody asked you to do this.** | Dismissive | Self-driven: nobody forced you | Can sound cold without the list above it |
| **Too stubborn to stop.** | Character flaw | Perseverance | Close to "never quit" absolutism |
| **You're doing too much.** | Concern | Yes, and you're doing it | Needs a visual answer; flat as type alone |
| **Some people never learn.** | Stupid | Keep coming back to learn | Pun for a school; could read as a joke on the viewer |

## Lead sequence for comps

| Card | Copy | Viewer's move |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | MONTH-END CLOSE. RECITAL. SICK KID. SAME WEEK. | "That's my week." |
| 2 | SOME PEOPLE DON'T KNOW WHEN TO QUIT. | Heard as an insult, read as a credential |
| 3 | UW lockup · program label | Apply Now (Meta CTA) |

Static: card 1 + card 3, lockup. "Not for everyone." becomes optional: the new line already carries the challenge, and two verdicts compete.

**Relationship to "Real cowboys never quit".** Same instinct, with the Channel 4 twist: it describes instead of commands, gatekeeps no one and doesn't shame people who once stopped. It sits naturally beside UW Cowboys language if that route is used (Q1).

## Guards

- No copying of the reference's sentence structure ("To be an X, there's got to be…").
- No mental-health words as the flaw ("crazy", "insane", "obsessed").
- No glorifying exhaustion or suffering (brief §11.4 rev 5 exclusions still in force).
- No program claims: difficulty, selectivity, attrition or schedule fit.
