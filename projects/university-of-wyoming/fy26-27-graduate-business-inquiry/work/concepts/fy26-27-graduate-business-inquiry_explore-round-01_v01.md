## CP1 · On-Campus MBA (P1)

### 1A · YOU HAD TO BE THERE

**Idea.** What matters in this MBA happens in a room, so the ad can only caption it.

**Observation / tension.** Hypothesis: early-career people want what happens in the room, and a credential can't hold that. Basis: S1 direction and AP2. No audience evidence.

**What they experience.** Bracketed sound-description captions, the kind used for things you can't hear, laid over real classroom footage. The captions admit the feed can't carry the room.

**Static (rough).**

- Approved photo of applied case work.
- Caption bar across it: "[applied case work in progress]".
- "You had to be there."
- "One-year, in-person MBA in Laramie."
- Lockup · Apply Now.

**15s.**

- **0–3s:** room footage; "[you can't hear this part]"; UW On-Campus MBA.
- **3–10s:** "[marker on whiteboard]" "[real-world problem solving]"; "One year. In person. Laramie."
- **10–15s:** "You had to be there."; lockup; Apply Now.

**Proof.** C10 (verbatim), C12 (the caption terms stay within C12). C01 optional.

**Why this program.** Being in person is the product. The one-year format and Laramie are its stated facts.

**Weakness / dependency.**

- "You had to be there" is a familiar idiom.
- The joke must stay on the ad's own limits, never on screens. UW also sells an Online MBA.
- Captions must name objects and C12 terms only, never invented discussion formats.
- Canto needs in-room footage.

### 1B · GET IT WRONG HERE FIRST

**Idea.** Applied case work turns your first big business calls into practice.

**Observation / tension.** Hypothesis: early-career fear of the first consequential decision. Only C12 is sourced.

**What they experience.** Revision made visible: a decision written, struck through and rewritten, with the crossed-out drafts left legible.

**Static (rough).**

- Approved photo of a whiteboard: "Call: A" struck through, "Call: B" beneath. Neutral content; no case is invented.
- "Get it wrong here first."
- "Applied case work and real-world problem solving."
- "One-year, in-person MBA in Laramie."
- Lockup · Apply Now.

**15s.**

- **0–3s:** marker writes a call; "Get it wrong here first."; UW On-Campus MBA.
- **3–10s:** struck through and redrawn twice, never marked "correct"; "Applied case work. Real-world problem solving." "One year, in person, in Laramie."
- **10–15s:** lockup; Apply Now.

**Proof.** C12 (verbatim), C10 (verbatim).

**Why this program.** It rests on the program's stated applied case work and the one-year in-person format, not on a generic "learning" promise.

**Weakness / dependency.**

- "Safe to fail" is an education trope.
- "First" hints you'll get it right at work later (outcome, C57). Fallback line: "Get it wrong here."
- Depends on whiteboard footage.

**Also explored for CP1.**

- The Runtime Is the Year: 15 seconds standing for twelve months.
- Pause, Opened: a pause glyph becomes a doorway.
- Case 01: You: the application as the first case.
- Solid Line / Dotted Line: confirmed vs conditional proof as a visual grammar.
- Home Base: Laramie.

**Spain is not load-bearing in either territory** (see calibration questions).

## CP2 · Online MBA (P2)

### 2A · NO ASTERISK

**Idea.** The online MBA carries no qualifier: it is held to the same standards, and its transcript doesn't say how it was earned.

**Observation / tension.** Hypothesis: prospects suspect an online degree reads as lesser. Evidence: S1 offers C02 specifically as "online program proof", so the source anticipates the doubt. Audience evidence: none.

**What they experience.** A degree line with nothing attached to it. The designed absence is the argument.

**Static (rough).**

- "Master of Business Administration" set large. The space where a qualifier or asterisk would sit is visibly empty.
- "No asterisk."
- "Same learning expectations and accreditation standards as in-person programs."
- "Transcripts do not identify the delivery format."
- Lockup "UW · Online MBA" · Apply Now.
- Approved work-setting photo as a side panel.

**15s.**

- **0–3s:** "No asterisk."; the degree line appears; UW Online MBA.
- **3–10s:** a loupe slides along the line and finds nothing. The standards clause, then the transcript clause (C02), then "AACSB-accredited College of Business".
- **10–15s:** lockup; Apply Now. No typing motion, to stay distinct from 2B.

**Proof.** C02, both clauses, standards clause first. C01.

**Why this program.** C02 is a stated UW practice for its online routes, the most specific proof the Online MBA has. It is not established as unique to UW.

**Weakness / dependency.**

- **Concealment risk.** Led by the transcript clause alone, it reads as "no one will know".
- **Self-contradiction risk.** Any legal footnote required on the ad breaks "No asterisk".
- **Imitation risk.** Must not imitate an official transcript.
- **Implied claim.** Must not imply how employers judge the degree (C30).
- **Unconfirmed proof.** C02 must be confirmed for this route.

### 2B · THE UNSENT OUT-OF-OFFICE

**Idea.** The MBA moment people picture is announcing they'll be away from work, and this announcement never gets sent.

**Observation / tension.** Hypothesis: an MBA is imagined as stepping out of work. Basis: S1 "without pausing their career" is direction, not evidence; "advance" stays restricted (C58).

**What they experience.** A behavior: someone begins the familiar leaving-work message and stops.

**Static (rough).**

- An unbranded, custom-drawn out-of-office panel over an approved office photo.
- Panel text: "I'll be away from work for the next—", with the rest selected for deletion.
- "100% online and asynchronous."
- Lockup · Apply Now.
- No Send or Discard button.

**15s.**

- **0–3s:** the out-of-office types itself; UW Online MBA.
- **3–10s:** it pauses and backspaces; "100% online and asynchronous. Full- or part-time."
- **10–15s:** the panel closes unsent; lockup; Apply Now.

**Proof.** C25 (verbatim). No proof inside the deleted text, or the deletion erases it.

**Why this program.** The out-of-office is only unnecessary because the program is 100% online and asynchronous.

**Weakness / dependency.**

- Familiar email-UI trope.
- It must not suggest "effortless" or a workload the source doesn't state.
- It assumes an office job. S1 says working professionals; targeting also includes "In college".
- Check Meta policy on ads that look like system interface.

**Also explored for CP2.**

- You Found Time to Read This: the scroll proves the gaps exist.
- Set in the Gaps: copy set only in a calendar's white space.
- Pace Dial.
- 9 of 30: elective credits, with Energy Business as an option.
- Still in the Chart.

## CP3 · M.S. in Accounting: one shared concept (P3 on-campus · P4 online)

Both territories rest only on what both formats share: C01 and C36, "prepares students for the CPA exam", which is preparation only. Route facts sit in a single route line.

- **P3 route line:** "On campus. Full- or part-time." (C35). STEM appears only as a conditional internal placeholder (C34, Q3), never in the shared line and never in P4. No Laramie: a location is not sourced for this route.
- **P4 route line:** "100% online and asynchronous. GRE/GMAT not required." (C35). C02 optional. No on-campus experiences.
- **Not paired:** full-time with one year, or part-time with two. The source doesn't pair them.

### 3A · ONLY ACCOUNTANTS WILL GET THIS

**Idea.** The ad speaks fluent accounting, so the people it's for recognize themselves before they read the program name.

**Observation / tension.** Hypothesis: accountants are rarely addressed in their own professional language. No evidence.

**What they experience.** An in-group line that non-accountants scroll past and accountants stop on. Recognition is the participation.

**Static (rough).**

- A single piece of accounting shorthand set large. Placeholder only: "Yes, it ties."
- "If that made sense: M.S. in Accounting."
- "Prepares students for the CPA exam."
- Route line · lockup · Apply Now.
- Campus photo (P3) or work/life photo (P4).

**15s.**

- **0–3s:** the shorthand line; UW M.S. in Accounting (On Campus or Online).
- **3–10s:** "If that made sense: the program prepares students for the CPA exam."; route line.
- **10–15s:** lockup; Apply Now.

**Both formats.** The joke and C36 are identical. Only the route line and photography change.

**Proof.** C36 (verbatim), C01. Route: P3 C35 (C34 placeholder only); P4 C35, C02.

**Why this program.** The in-group language fits an accounting audience, but UW's role is weak; any accounting program could run it.

**Weakness / dependency.**

- Everything rides on a line good enough to be true insider humor. The placeholder isn't.
- Jargon must be accurate and must not imply a competence claim.

### 3B · PREPARED FOR: YOU

**Idea.** An accountant's document header, addressed to you, whose only note is exactly what the degree does.

**Observation / tension.** Hypothesis: this audience trusts documented statements over promises. Basis: the S1 caveats; audience evidence: none.

**What they experience.** A working-paper header in which UW is literally the preparer and the viewer is the recipient.

**Static (rough).**

```
PREPARED FOR:  You
PREPARED BY:   University of Wyoming · M.S. in Accounting
NOTE 1:        The program prepares students for the CPA exam.
NOTE 2:        CPA education and licensing requirements vary by state.   (optional)
BASIS:         [route line]
```

Lockup · Apply Now. The "Prepared for" line is the focal point.

**15s.**

- **0–3s:** the header assembles; "PREPARED FOR: You"; UW M.S. in Accounting (per route).
- **3–10s:** NOTE 1, then BASIS (route line), then NOTE 2 as a legible caption.
- **10–15s:** the signature line becomes Apply Now; lockup.

**Both formats.** The header and NOTE 1 are shared. BASIS carries the route. The photography changes.

**Proof.** C36 (verbatim), the S1 state-variation caveat (C39 row), C01. Route: P3 C35 (C34 placeholder only); P4 C35.

**Why this program.** UW sits inside the device as the "preparer", and the one note is the program's exact stated purpose.

**Weakness / dependency.**

- Can read dry or bureaucratic.
- Notes fight minimal copy and mobile legibility.
- Wording discipline: "Prepared for: the CPA exam" would overstate C36 as readiness (C42), so the recipient must stay "You".

**Also explored for CP3.**

- Tick and Tie: swapped out for duplicating 3B and breaking minimal copy.
- Note 1: the disclosure as headline.
- Before: the program's place ahead of the exam; hints at licensure.
- Different Entries, Same Total: withdrawn for pairing pace with duration.
- Show Your Work: generic.
- Credits, Both Kinds: implies eligibility.

## CP4 · Online M.S. in Finance, FP&WM / CFP pathway (P5)

### 4A · A LIFETIME, NOT A QUARTER

**Idea.** This M.S. in Finance is organized around a person's life, not a company's reporting calendar, and its topics are moments in that life.

**Observation / tension.** Source-indicated: S1 insists "not corporate finance", which suggests a real risk of confusion. That prospects are confused is a hypothesis.

**What they experience.** Scale contrast: a quarter is a short segment, and a life is a line that runs off the frame.

**Static (rough).**

- A hairline across the full frame, aligned to an edge in an approved work/life photo.
- A short grey segment labelled "a quarter".
- The five topics spread along the whole line, not in course order.
- "A lifetime, not a quarter."
- "Topics include investment management, tax planning, estate planning, insurance and retirement planning."
- "30 credits. 100% online."
- Lockup · Apply Now.

**15s.**

- **0–3s:** "Q1 Q2 Q3 Q4" bars collapse into one short segment; "Not a quarter."; UW M.S. in Finance.
- **3–10s:** pull back as the line extends; "A lifetime."; all five topics appear at once; "100% online. One- or two-year completion."
- **10–15s:** lockup; Apply Now as the line runs on.

**Proof.** C45 (verbatim; keep "include"), C44, C01.

**Why this program.** The contrast exists only because the degree is named "M.S. in Finance" while its focus is personal planning.

**Weakness / dependency.**

- "Lifetime" is the wealth industry's default word; the leap lives in "not a quarter".
- Could read as disparaging corporate finance.
- Rests on the working name (Q7).
- Must not imply course sequence or career longevity (C51).

### 4B · CERTIFICATE / CERTIFICATION

**Idea.** A few letters separate the certificate from CFP certification, and the ad is exact about which one this is.

**Observation / tension.** Hypothesis: credential confusion is common in this field, and precision earns trust. Basis: C47's own caveat; audience evidence: none.

**What they experience.** Typography as the argument: one shared stem forks into two endings, one solid and one outlined.

**Static (rough).**

- "certificat" set once, forking into "E" (solid) and "ION" (outlined).
- "The CFP graduate certificate fulfills the education coursework requirement for CFP certification."
- "It is not CFP certification." (carried by the fork)
- "Registered with CFP Board."
- Lockup "UW · M.S. in Finance" · half-frame work/life photo · Apply Now.

**15s.**

- **0–3s:** the stem appears; UW M.S. in Finance.
- **3–10s:** the endings branch apart (no deletion or cursor); C47 in full; "Registered with CFP Board."
- **10–15s:** lockup; Apply Now.

**Proof.** C47 (verbatim; "The CFP graduate certificate" stays the subject, or the M.S. would appear to meet the requirement). C46 (verify; it covers both the program and the certificate).

**Why this program.** The CFP Board-registered certificate is the stated core of this pathway.

**Weakness / dependency.**

- **Possibly unproducible until Q7.** The proof concerns the certificate while the lockup sells the M.S., and their relationship is unstated.
- **Tone.** It leads with a limit.
- **Legibility.** Nuance at scroll speed.
- **Mark rules.** CFP mark-usage rules likely apply (general knowledge, unverified).

**Also explored for CP4.**

- Definition 2: the dictionary's second sense of "finance"; merged into 4A.
- Not That Kind of Finance: merged into 4A.
- The Shoebox: household paperwork sorted into the syllabus; needs a new shoot.
- Questions, in Order: risks invented client voices.
- This Is Not Financial Advice: repeats a disclosure device.
- Take Your Own Advice: implies return on investment.
- Not a Portfolio: implies credit weights.

## Cross-round observations (for calibration, not decisions)

- **Precision as a repeated voice.** Saying exactly what's true appears in 2A (removing a mark), 3B (borrowing a document convention) and 4B (a typographic cut). It could be a deliberate family voice for UW graduate business, or one trick in three costumes.
- **Editing gestures.** 1B (strike-through) and 2B (backspace) both use text revision. No other territory uses typing or deletion.
- **UW-specific identity is thin.** Most territories are program-specific, not UW-specific. Place (Laramie) carries only CP1. Wyoming, Brown & Gold, Steamboat and "The World Needs More Cowboys" were available as provisional references but aren't load-bearing anywhere.
- **Shared dependencies.**
  - Canto footage for 1A and 1B.
  - A confirmed C02 and no required footnote for 2A.
  - Q7 for 4A and 4B.
  - Q15, video intro/exit, for all videos.
- **Photography is secondary almost everywhere.** Most territories are type- or behavior-led, and photography supports them. That fits minimal on-image copy but may test "people, action and real experiences" (S1 p6).
