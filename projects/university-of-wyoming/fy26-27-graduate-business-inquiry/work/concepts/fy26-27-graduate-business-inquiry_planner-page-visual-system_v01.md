## Concept sentence
The week someone is already carrying, written in their own hand, with one line still unticked.

## The mechanism
The week line stops being typography over a photograph and becomes **an object inside the story**: a torn daily-planner page, jotted in ballpoint, lying on the evidence of that week.

- Items already done are **struck through** in the person's own pen.
- The **last line is untouched**: APPLICATION. That is the ad's whole argument, made without a sentence: they finish everything except the thing that is for them.
- Stage swaps the unticked line: APPLICATION. → HIT SUBMIT. → DAY ONE.
- Program swaps the items: MONTH-END CLOSE / AUDIT INTERNSHIP / COVERING THE BRANCH.

This keeps the strike-through in the character's hand. It is never a checkbox, progress bar or interface, and it never implies the ad knows what the viewer has done.

## Two registers, one frame
| Register | Holds | Share of frame |
|---|---|---|
| **Upper: the page** | Torn planner page, handwritten week, one line unticked | ~40% (1:1 and 4:5), 14–43% band in 9:16 |
| **Lower: the evidence** | Still life of that week, shot dark and warm | ~60% |
| **Foot** | UW lockup + program label | bottom band, clear of Reels UI |

The page solves the old problem of an empty dark area for type. The top of the frame is now doing work.

## Camera angles (the story, told four ways)
1. **Overhead, 90°** (reference 2). The whole week laid out: planner, textbook, work badge, recital flyer, keys. Hard top light, deep shadow at the edges. Best for card 1 and the hero static: it reads as inventory, and it is unmistakably a phone-scale composition.
2. **Raking low, 10–15°** across the table, lens near the surface. The page is sharp; the room falls off into dark. Reads as late, and as one person's point of view.
3. **Macro, pen on paper.** The moment of the strike-through. Best as carousel card 1 (tight crop) and as the first frame of the video.
4. **Framed through an object** — past a laptop edge, a mug, a locker door — so the page sits inside a natural border. Best for 9:16, where the object fills the unsafe top.

Rule: one angle per card, and the sequence moves closer, never further away.

## The page itself
- **Real paper, photographed.** Torn from a spiral daily planner: ragged edge, spiral fringe, one curled corner, a soft shadow under it. Slight rotation, 3–6°, never straight.
- **Real handwriting,** ballpoint, fast, not calligraphic. Lowercase or mixed case. The date line at the top is blank or struck out.
- **Strike-throughs in the same pen.** UW Gold appears once, as a second pen or a highlighter stripe under the unticked line — the only gold on the frame.
- **Digital fallback:** photograph the blank page and set the handwriting as a layer, so the list can change per program and stage without a reshoot. Keep the paper's shadow and texture in camera.
- **Legibility:** handwriting must pass at 375 px. Minimum stroke weight and size to be proved on a phone before the shoot; if it fails, set the list in Gotham (Montserrat stand-in) on the paper, which loses some intimacy but survives the feed.

## What it rules out
No sticky notes as a graphic device (generic), no phone screenshots, no calendar app UI, no checkbox graphics, no drop shadows faked in Figma, no torn-paper clip art, no handwriting fonts pretending to be handwriting.

## Production
One tabletop session covers every program: same table, same light, the props and the page changing per program. No talent, no releases, no location fees. Canto photography can supply the underlying desk or campus surface where UW prefers it (S8: Canto is the primary image source).
