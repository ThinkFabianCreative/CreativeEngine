## Creative-center test

**Working hypothesis (Andres):** "A Cowboy is recognized by what they do when life already asks a lot of them."

**What the test found:**

- **Rule holds.** "Recognize the character already present" survives in all three systems below.
- **Universal-circumstance risk.** "When life already asks a lot" reads as universal. It leans on C66 (jobs, spouses, children), which S6 doesn't establish as typical. The P1 audience also includes recent graduates.
- **Load risk.** It can tip toward glorifying load.
- **Alternative to test, not adopted:** WordSmither proposes "A Cowboy is recognized by what they do when something real depends on it."
- **Middle position.** DesignChamp adds that an act only reads as character when some of the day around it is visible: context, not burden. The systems below use that.

## Language controls applied to all systems

- **Cowboy is identity.** "Cowboy" is used only as UW's institutional identity, with no gendered pronouns.
  - Casting follows S2 p4: "a modern cowboy can be anyone".
  - Whether "Cowgirl" (S2 p7) appears in graduate ads is Q16.
- **No shortcuts on screen.** "To cowboy" means to act recklessly. Every act shown is careful, never a shortcut; this matters most for Accounting and Finance.
- **No pressure devices.** No children, spouses or parental guilt as pressure. No hours, exhaustion or sacrifice-as-proof. "Slave away" never appears.

## System A · COWBOY, OBSERVED

**1. Umbrella idea.** Each ad catches one small, careful business act in the middle of a real day, and only then calls it Cowboy.

**2. Human tension.**
- *User-reported (S6, C69):* being a Cowboy is about behavior and character, not appearance.
- *Hypothesis:* people with direction don't count their everyday diligence as character. It's just what they do.

**3. Repeatable rule.**
- Act first, name last. One act per ad.
- The act is business-real and careful, with a glimpse of the day around it: context, not suffering.
- The recognition word arrives only after the act is seen.

**4. Why UW can credibly say it.**
- "A Cowboy isn't what you are but who you are" (S2 p4, S4 p5).
- UW's integrity line, "do the right thing — even when no one is looking" (S4 p4).
- "Say more with less" (S4 p4).
- Behavior-as-character themes (S6, user-reported).

**5. Graduate-business relevance.** The acts are business judgments: correcting a figure, keeping a delivery date, testing your own process, owning a sign-off.

**6. Constant across all five.**
- Documentary light, one act, one visible glimpse of context.
- The recognition word ("Cowboy.") arrives last.
- UW lockup with a restrained program label.

**7. Changes by program.** The discipline the act comes from, a setting consistent with the delivery format, the label, and the post text.

**8. Behavioral thumbnails.**
- An eraser corrects one digit on a printout. A grocery list and a gym bag sit soft at the edge of the desk. *(ancestor ADLOB 02 #26)*
- Coat already on after closing, someone shows a coworker how the day's register tape reconciles.
- A community clean-up sign-up sheet with their name written in, taped to a fridge beside a work shift schedule.

**9. Rough static.**
- **Image:** documentary photo. The act is sharp; the context object is soft at the frame edge.
- **Caption:** one plain line naming the act, ≤6 words, lower third.
- **"Cowboy."** set small, clearly separate from the Steamboat mark.
- **Layout:** UW lockup and program label bottom-left · Apply Now.

**10. Carousel** *(swipe order is placement-dependent)*

| Card | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | Tight crop of the act, deliberately ambiguous |
| 2 | Wider: the coat on, the closed sign, the coworker |
| 3 | Type only: "That's a Cowboy." |
| 4 | UW lockup · program label · Apply Now |

The swipe turns an ordinary moment into character.

**11. 15-second structure.**

| Beats | Picture | Caption |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | The act, close; UW lockup in the corner | names the act |
| 3–10s | Pull back to the day around it | "Nobody asked." |
| 10–15s | End frame: UW lockup, program label, Apply Now | "Cowboy." |

**12. Adaptation.**

| Variant | Identifier | In-asset act | Post text |
|---|---|---|---|

| P2 | Online MBA | The back of a business card listing what they handled on Tuesday (#8) | PT-P2 |

| P5 | M.S. in Finance · Online | A hand signs the only sharp line of a planning document (#30) | PT-P5 |

The act-then-name structure is the umbrella; only the act's discipline, the label and the post text change.

**13. With "The World Needs More Cowboys"** *(if authorized)*
- **Placement:** the line replaces "Cowboy." as the end frame, set in the S2 campaign typography (S2 p11); the act is its evidence.
- **Needs:** Q1 and Q16. Any new line such as "That's a Cowboy." needs Institutional Marketing headline approval (S2 p8).

**14. Without that campaign language.**
- **With UW's Cowboy identity word but without the S2 line:** survives. "Cowboy." as recognition is S4 brand vocabulary (currency Q17).
- **Without any Cowboy language:** weak. It becomes generic business-virtue vignettes, and UW ownership falls to tone.

**15. Largest risks.**
- **Strategic:** the acts assume a current job and a full life (C66 is not universal), which can lose recent graduates.
- **Creative:** an "everyday heroes" or "good employee" montage that preaches. "Cowboy" becomes a sticker on any decent act. The acts must not all be office acts, or the idea excludes people.
- **Claim:** implying the program creates these behaviors, or that alumni perform them (C60, C62).

## System B · EARNED, NOT ISSUED

**1. Umbrella idea.** The character UW calls Cowboy can't be handed out, so each ad shows the wear that only a person's own commitment leaves on the tools of their work.

**2. Human tension.**
- *User-reported (S6, C67, C69):* graduate study can require hard work and nights, and being a Cowboy can operate as a badge of honor.
- *Hypothesis:* committed people distrust programs sold as easy, and are equally put off by programs sold as trials.

**3. Repeatable rule.**
- Show the mark that only use makes (worn, polished, rounded) on an everyday work object.
- Never a badge object: no star, patch or pin. That's costume.
- Never exhaustion.
- One flat line.
- Commitment facts sit in the post text, stated plainly.

**4. Why UW can credibly say it.**
- "Grit isn't given. It's earned." (S4 p4)
- "A Cowboy isn't what you are but who you are" (S2 p4, S4 p5)
- Badge-of-honor and pride themes (S6, user-reported).

**5. Graduate-business relevance.** The worn objects are business tools (a calculator, a marker, a pen), and the commitment reads as professional practice.

**6. Constant across all five.**
- A wear-mark macro, single light source, a thin environment band matching the delivery format.
- "Earned, not issued." in a fixed position.
- UW lockup and program label.

**7. Changes by program.** The object, the environment band, the label and the post text.

**8. Behavioral thumbnails.**
- A calculator whose "=" key is worn blank.
- A pen clip polished bright where it rides in a pocket.
- A notebook corner rounded soft from being carried every day.

**9. Rough static.**
- **Image:** macro still life where the worn mark is the only highlight, with a thin photographic band of setting.
- **Line:** "Can't be issued."
- **Layout:** UW lockup and program label at the bottom · Apply Now.

**10. Carousel** *(placement-dependent)*

|---|---|
| 1 | A pristine pen: "Issued." |
| 2 | The same model, worn: "Earned." |
| 3 | A second worn object |

The comparison is the argument. The worn object must read as the viewer's, never as a verdict on newcomers.

| 10–15s | End frame: UW lockup, program label, Apply Now | "Earned, not issued." |

**13. With "The World Needs More Cowboys"** *(if authorized)*
- **Placement:** end frame after the wear sequence, with "Earned, not issued." as a subhead.
- **Needs:** S2 p8 headline approval, plus Q1 and Q16.

**14. Without that campaign language.**
- **With UW's Cowboy identity word (what is earned is "Cowboy"):** survives.
- **Without any Cowboy language:** collapses. "Earned" points at the degree and becomes generic completion language, drifting toward outcome implication.

**15. Largest risks.**
- **Strategic:** it depends most on authorization of the Cowboy language, and it drifts toward "UW confers the identity".
- **Creative:** it glorifies the grind (wear read as exhaustion), tips pride into self-congratulation, or shames people just starting.
- **Claim:** sacrifice implied as a path to success (C67), or completion pride implied as an outcome (C69).

## System C · "YOU DON'T HAVE TO GO FAR TO GET FURTHER": tested, does not hold, replaced

- **On-campus variants.** "Not far" is true only for viewers near campus. P1 requires Laramie (C10), and P3 has no sourced location, so the thought depends on where the viewer lives.
- **Online variants.** "Not far" can only be shown as studying from where you are, which is online flexibility (C25, C35), a variant-only claim. The umbrella can't depend on it.
- **"Further".** It reads as advancement and restates C63 ("connections that can take someone further") almost exactly. That is an unsupported outcome.
- **No idea underneath.** Without place or format, the only thought is "near is enough". It is a paradox slogan.

**Replaced by C′,** built from the supplied material: S6 compassion and giving-back themes (user-reported), and ADLOB 02 #12, #17 and #27.

## System C′ · SOMEONE'S COUNTING ON IT

**1. Umbrella idea.** Every careful business judgment lands in someone else's day, so each ad shows the act and then the ordinary moment where it lands.

**2. Human tension.**
- *User-reported (S6):* compassion and giving back to the community were named as values.
- *Hypothesis:* careful work is invisible, because when it's done well nothing goes wrong for anyone else.

**3. Repeatable rule.**
- Two beats: the act, then the uneventful other side.
- The receiver is a colleague, customer, team or community member. Never children or family as pressure.
- No rescue, no drama.

**4. Why UW can credibly say it.**
- The integrity line (S4 p4).
- Responsibility and accountability among S3's Code of the West values (provisional; authorship unconfirmed).
- "Based in reality" (S4 p4).
- "Who you are" (S2 p4, S4 p5).

**5. Graduate-business relevance.** Clients, teams and customers are where leadership, accounting and planning decisions land.

**6. Constant across all five.**
- An object handed across a cut; the actor's face unseen; an understated receiver.
- "Someone's counting on it." (the accounting double meaning is a bonus, not the idea)
- UW lockup and program label.

**7. Changes by program.** Whose day it lands in, a setting consistent with the delivery format, the label, and the post text.

**8. Behavioral thumbnails.**
- A ticked "Friday" line, then a client opening that envelope on Friday. *(ADLOB 02 #27)*
- Someone tests the returns line from the customer side, then the next customer gets through quickly. *(#12)*
- A redrawn process arrow, then a new hire's first-day checklist that finally makes sense. *(#17)*

**9. Rough static.**
- **Image:** one frame. The act (hand and object) in the left third; the receiver soft but present in the right two-thirds; a thin rule linking them.
- **Line:** "Someone's counting on it."
- **Layout:** UW lockup and program label bottom-right · Apply Now.

**10. Carousel** *(placement-dependent)*

| Card | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | The act |
| 2 | "On the other side:" the receiver |
| 3 | A second act and receiver pair |
| 4 | UW lockup · program label · Apply Now |

**11. 15-second structure.**

| Beats | Picture | Caption |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | The act, close; UW lockup in the corner | names the act |
| 3–10s | Match cut: the object leaves one frame and arrives in the receiver's hands | "Landed in someone's Friday." |
| 10–15s | End frame: UW lockup, program label, Apply Now | "Someone's counting on it." |

**12. Adaptation.**

|---|---|---|---|

| P2 | Online MBA | A training plan finished, then a new hire's prepared first morning | PT-P2 |

| P4 | M.S. in Accounting · Online | A Friday delivery, then a colleague's quiet "received" | PT-P4 |

**CreativeDirector adjustments to the specialists' rows:**
- P3 and P4 receivers changed from a shop owner's filing and a client to a manager and a colleague, so the scenes don't imply licensed practice (C39).
- P5 keeps a household receiver. It must not imply the viewer holds professional authority or CFP certification (C47).

**13. With "The World Needs More Cowboys"** *(if authorized)*
- **Why it fits:** the most natural fit of the three. "Needs" becomes literal, because someone is counting on it.
- **Placement:** end frame after the landing beat.
- **Needs:** Q1, Q16 and S2 p8 approval.

**14. Without that campaign language.** Survives intact, with or without the Cowboy word. Wyoming ownership thins to tone plus the integrity line.

**15. Largest risks.**
- **Strategic:** the thinnest UW-specific ownership of the three.
- **Creative:** "counting on it" can land as pressure, or as a helper-hero cliché. Tone can become dutiful.
- **Claim:** implying graduates serve clients or teams better (an outcome), or receivers that read as testimonials. Accounting and Finance scenes must not imply professional authority the programs don't grant.

**Observations for Andres** (not selection):

- **A and C′ are two halves of one thought:** A names the character, C′ shows why it matters. They could be tested merged or kept apart. Keeping both costs some distinctness; merging risks a system that does two jobs.
- **B has the sharpest single image** and the most direct link to "Grit isn't given. It's earned." It is also the most exposed to the tensions you listed (proud but not self-congratulatory; hard-earned but not punishing), and it depends most on Cowboy authorization.
- **Wyoming ownership still depends largely on Q1.** A and B need the Cowboy language; C′ doesn't, but owns less without it.

## Testimonial-led work: what would be needed

None of the three is testimonial-led. If a testimonial route is pursued later, it needs:

- the original session notes or recordings (R19)
- participant identities, and signed releases or permissions for use in advertising
- exact approved verbatim quotations, never paraphrases or composites
- substantiation for any outcome in a quote: advancement, applicability of classes, connections, class sizes, CEO or VP roles (C60–C65)
- UW approval of testimonial use (Q16), plus a check that no quote implies typical results
