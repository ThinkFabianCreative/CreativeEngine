## DIRECTION 1 · YOU DON'T HAVE TO GO FAR TO GET FURTHER

**1. Umbrella idea.** Each ad puts the life someone is close to and the thing they want next in the same frame, so wanting more stops looking like having to leave.

**2. Human tension** (user-provided hypothesis).
- People picture graduate study as a departure: from a town, a job, their people, a way of doing things.
- What they actually want (a business, more responsibility, a wider skill set) sits inside the life they already have.

**3. Creative rule.**
- **Near and far in one place.** One close, familiar thing and one further aim, both clearly the viewer's own, share one frame.
- **The aim is already there** from the first frame. The crop, pull-back or swipe only reveals how much the near place holds.
- **UW is a place that's near,** never the engine that moves anyone.
- **"Further" is shown as intent only** (a sketch, a plan, a question), never as a result (a title, a nameplate, pay).
- **No time-lapse growth after UW appears.** It would imply causation.

**4. What the line means / does not mean.**
- **Means:** you can pursue what you want without leaving what this variant defines as close.
- **Does not mean:**
  - UW will get you further.
  - Study is easy or convenient.
  - UW is near everyone.
  - Staying is better than leaving.
  - Viewers can't go far.

**5. Why UW can credibly express it.**
- **The name:** it is a state identity. S4 p2 describes UW as "a land-grant, flagship institution".
- **Targeting:** Wyoming counties for every variant, plus two Colorado counties for P2 (S5).
- **Formats:** P1 is in person in Laramie (C10); P2, P4 and P5 are online (C25, C35, C44).
- **Tone:** "based in reality" (S4 p4) supports the plainness.
- **Internal only:** S6 (user-reported) describes students balancing existing lives (C66) and UW feeling approachable (C68).

**6. Why it matters to graduate business** (hypothesis).
- Business lives are built on the clients, relationships and communities people already have.
- The decision to study is weighed against what someone would leave behind.

**7. Constant across all five.**
- The line.
- The near-and-far-in-one-place composition, with type set in depth: "You don't have to go far" beside the near object, "to get further." across the deeper plane.
- One continuous pull-back, in daylight.
- UW lockup and Apply Now.

**8. Changes by program.** What "far" means, the aspiration artifact, the setting (consistent with the delivery format), the label and the primary text.

**9. Thumbnails.**
- **Shop counter:** a till, a mug, and a hand-painted sign for a second location drying face-up beside them.
- **Work desk:** the usual in-tray, and a pinned team schedule with more names on it than the desk has chairs.
- **Kitchen table:** a coffee ring and car keys, beside a thick binder tabbed with a plan's sections.

**10. Rough static.**
- **Composition:** deep focus. The anchor object (mug or keys) is sharp in the foreground; the aspiration artifact is sharp further back on the same surface.
- **Type:** "You don't have to go far" set small beside the near object; "to get further." across the deeper plane. The type itself maps near and far.
- **Branding:** UW lockup bottom-left, with the restrained program label beneath.

- **Placement:** the D1 line stays the headline. "The World Needs More Cowboys" becomes a small end-frame sign-off in video only (S2 p11 lowercase campaign type). Casting follows S2 p4, "a modern cowboy can be anyone".
- **"Bucking the System Since 1886" doesn't fit.** Using it as "here since 1886" would claim what 1886 marks, which the sources don't state.
- **College themes:** "Wyoming-Built" is the S3 theme closest in spirit. Its companion "Globally Prepared" implies global experience that is scoped to P1 and conditional (C13). Treat it as provocation only.
- **Approvals:** Q1, Q16, S2 p8.

**16. Route without that exact language.** Survives fully. The line carries itself, and UW ownership comes from the name and each variant's near meaning.

**17. Strongest creative opportunity.** Type set in depth plus the pull-back: one ordinary close frame turns out to already hold the ambition. For many Wyoming viewers, staying stops reading as a compromise and becomes a deliberate choice (hypothesis).

**18. Where it breaks.**
- **Generic:**
  - kitchen-table-ambition stock imagery
  - a "shop local" or stay-close sentiment
  - a nine-word slogan with no image idea under it ("Not far. Further." is the compression to test)
- **Confusing:**
  - P1 and P3 require being on campus, so for viewers who would relocate "not far" is untrue and the abstract "familiar ground" meaning must carry it
  - "same state" fails for P2's Colorado viewers
  - it can read as assuming viewers already know UW
- **Condescending:** it can suggest viewers can't go far.
- **Unsupported outcome claim:**
  - "further" pictured as a title, nameplate, promotion or pay (C62, C63)
  - any before/after growth sequence implying UW caused it
- **Convenience claim:** home-centered online executions or primary text that stresses ease.

## DIRECTION 2 · NOT FOR EVERYONE

**1. Umbrella idea.** Each ad states the standard first, then shows one ordinary act that meets it, and lets the viewer recognize themselves before the program appears.

**2. Human tension** (user-provided hypotheses).
- Some prospects carry a chip on the shoulder and want to be associated with modern Cowboy traits: curiosity, perseverance, integrity, hard work, ethics, compassion, independence, responsibility, giving back, challenging expectations.
- Being challenged moves them more than being welcomed; being gatekept repels them.
- The intended internal response is "Yes. Not for everyone. But for me."
- S6 (user-reported, internal only) describes Cowboy as a badge of honor (C69).

**3. Creative rule.**
1. "Not for everyone." set quietly and firmly: sentence case, never shouting caps.
2. One observable, unheroic act, performed by visibly different people across the campaign. Never a virtue word; never exhaustion or night-grind.
3. The viewer recognizes themselves. It is never written.
4. Program label and a place to act.
5. Apply Now as their answer.

- **Never shown:** who is left out, people leaving, seats or scarcity.
- **Banned words:** "few", "selective", "exclusive", "limited", "only".

**4. What the line means / does not mean.**
- **Means:** not everyone chooses this commitment or these behaviors. It is about willingness and character. Anyone can; not everyone does.
- **Does not mean:**
  - selective or artificially exclusive admission
  - a particular gender, identity or background
  - professional status as worth
  - physical toughness
  - suffering as a path to success
  - that people who don't attend graduate school lack these qualities
- **How beat 2 prevents the admission misreading.** The filter is visibly an act anyone could do, shown in the same frame or the first second. No gate, applicant pool, percentage or seat count appears.

**Provocations tested.**

| Provocation | Finding | Proposed handling |
|---|---|---|
| "Do you have what it takes?" | As an opener it puts UW in the judge's seat and reads as an entrance exam, bringing back the admission misreading. It is also a generic recruiting line. | DesignChamp: may work only after the act is shown, when "it" clearly means that act. WordSmither: keep internal. |
| "Unlock the Cowboy in you" | "Unlock" implies the quality is locked or missing and UW holds the key, which is rescue. It echoes S1's banned "unlock your potential". | Replace with language where the character already exists: "Put it to work." (any route), or "The Cowboy in you, called on." (Cowboy route only). |

**5. Why UW can credibly express it.**
- "A Cowboy isn't what you are but who you are" (S2 p4, S4 p5).
- "A modern cowboy can be anyone" (S2 p4). With the line, it reads as "anyone can; not everyone does".
- The brand promise: grit, integrity, independence, optimism (S4 p3).
- "Bucking the System" (S2, provisional).
- Casting rule: "Trailblazers come in all shapes and sizes" (user direction).

**6. Why it matters to graduate business** (hypothesis). Graduate business study is a chosen commitment, and the standards business runs on (accuracy, accountability, owning a decision, the honest number) are visible behaviors, not credentials.

**7. Constant across all five.**
- "Not for everyone." as the opener.
- One ordinary act, with varied casting across the set.
- The recognition left unsaid.
- An end line: "Put it to work." (WordSmither), or the empty, lit place with "Your call." (DesignChamp). Both are kept for review.
- UW lockup, program label, Apply Now.

**8. Changes by program.** The act and its discipline, the closing place (consistent with the delivery format), the label, the primary text, and the casting.

**9. Thumbnails.**
- **Corrected report:** someone reopens a sent report, circles one cell nobody flagged, and resends it with "corrected" in the subject line.
- **Honest note:** a handwritten note to a colleague reads "Honestly, the numbers don't work yet."
- **Community chairs:** after a community meeting, one person stacks the last chairs. The lights are on; it isn't late-night drama.

**10. Rough static.**
- **Composition:** mid-shot of the corrected-report moment: the circled cell on screen or paper, a hand on the resend. Generous negative space.
- **Type:** "Not for everyone." set small in the empty space. The act says it; the type only names it.
- **Casting:** any age or body.
- **Branding:** UW lockup bottom-left, program label beneath.
- **CreativeDirector note:** DesignChamp's static used the stacked chairs. The static uses the report act for business relevance, and the chairs move to the carousel.

**15. Route with the authorized Cowboys system** *(conditional; not approved)*
- **Placement:**
  - "Not for everyone." stays as beat 1.
  - "The World Needs More Cowboys" becomes the 10–15s end frame or static sign-off (S2 p11 lowercase campaign type).
  - "The Cowboy in you, called on." is available as the closing line.
  - "Bucking the System Since 1886" is an alternative end frame where the act questions a default (for example P1's process test). No claim about what 1886 marks.
  - The modern Cowboy identity governs casting (S2 p4).
- **Effect:** the Cowboy language reduces risk. Together the lines read as "anyone can; not everyone does", which undoes the admission misreading.
- **College themes:** "The Code of the West in Action" (S3) is the closest in spirit, because the acts are character in action. Its exact wording can't be the answer, and its authorship is unconfirmed. "Character. Courage. Innovation. Leadership." conflicts with the behavior-not-virtue-words rule.
- **Approvals:** "Not for everyone" and "Put it to work" are new headlines needing S2 p8 Institutional Marketing approval. Also Q1 and Q16.

**16. Route without that exact language.** Survives, but is riskier. "Not for everyone" plus the act still works. Without a named identity, the line leans toward a premium-brand or recruiting cliché, and UW ownership falls to the name and the behaviors.

**17. Strongest creative opportunity.** The gap between beats 1 and 2. A line that sounds like gatekeeping is answered by a humble act, so the standard turns out to be behavior, not pedigree. Varied casting makes it character, not identity, and Apply Now reads as the viewer's own answer.

**18. Where it breaks.**
- **Exclusionary:**
  - beat 1 seen alone reads as selective admission, for example a cropped thumbnail, a headline-only placement or truncated text (Q38)
  - an empty, polished space reads as membership scarcity
  - showing others leaving, or pushing "some people", implies others lack the traits
  - narrow casting (all office, all one type) breaks "all shapes and sizes"
- **Arrogant at first exposure:** an unfamiliar brand saying "not for everyone" strains approachability (C68, user-reported).
- **Generic:** a "prove them wrong" montage, macho casting, or the known empty-chair device without a specific, variant-true setting.
- **Glorified exhaustion:** late nights, dim light or last-one-standing framing (C67).
- **Unsupported outcome claim:** any hint that the program sorts winners or produces results. P3–P5 scenes must not imply licensed or certified professional authority (C39, C47).

## Qualitative comparison (no scores, no verdict, no winner)

| Criterion | Direction 1 · Go far / further | Direction 2 · Not for everyone |
|---|---|---|
| Conceptual compression | The thought is compact. The line is long (nine words); "Not far. Further." is the compressed test | Very compressed: three words plus one act |
| Layered meaning | Rich by design: "far" shifts per variant, and that is its engine. It also carries confusion risk | Mainly one layer (willingness), and deliberately so; the double reading (gate vs character) is the tension |
| Desire to respond | Quiet permission: a sense of "this could fit my life" | Active provocation: a sense of "that's me" |
| Audience self-recognition | Through their own place and plans | Through their own behavior |
| Challenge without empty aggression | Low challenge, low aggression risk | High challenge. Aggression risk managed by beat 2 and casting |
| Aspiration without promised outcomes | The core risk: "further" must stay intent, never result or causation | Lower outcome risk; the risk moves to exclusion |
| UW / Wyoming ownership | Strongest for Wyoming-based viewers through state and name. Weaker for P2's Colorado audience. Survives without Cowboy language | Strongest with Cowboy language ("anyone can; not everyone does"); thinner without it |
| Graduate-business relevance | Through aspirations (business, responsibility) placed in real work settings | Through business behaviors (accuracy, the honest number, keeping a delivery) |
| Span across five variants | Works through deliberately different meanings of "far". P1/P3 lean on the "familiar ground" meaning; P2 avoids "state" | Works through discipline-specific acts. P5 needs care on professional authority |
| Visual strength | Type in depth and the pull-back: one image holding near and far | The act answering the line; the empty lit place as invitation (a known device) |
| Useful Meta behavior | The swipe and pull-back complete the sentence and change what "far" meant | Continuing to swipe is itself the self-selection |
| Campaign system vs slogan | A system if the near-and-far composition rule holds; without it, a slogan | A system if the line-then-act sequence holds; without the act, a slogan |

**Open dependencies shared by both.**
- Q1 and Q16: Cowboy language and new-headline approval (S2 p8).
- Q14: targeting geography for D1's P1/P3 meanings.
- Q38: whether beat 1 or the primary-text opener survives truncation in each placement.
- Q39: S6 materials, if any route later leans on student experience.
