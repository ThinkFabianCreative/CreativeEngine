## D1-4 · MARGIN NOTES  *(replaces D1-3 in the Direction 1 set)*

**The idea in one sentence.** The place someone already works is where their bigger questions come from, and those questions were already theirs before UW appeared.

**Why it is stronger than D1-3.**

- **Frame 1 carries it.** The questions are on screen in the first frame, so no repetition or sequence is needed.
- **The person acts.** Noticing a system and questioning it is a business behavior, not a mood. It gives Direction 1 the graduate-business relevance that D1-1 and D1-2 lack.
- **Recognize them, don't rescue them.** The notes are in the person's hand, not UW's. UW gets only the line and the lockup. It never answers the questions.
- **It adapts honestly.** The place changes per variant to match delivery format, which D1-3's shared front step could not do.

### Static, 4:5 / 9:16

- **Scene:** a cast adult mid-task at their current workplace, in daylight. Master example: behind the front counter of a busy local business, with a handwritten order board on the wall and a short line of customers, all soft.
- **The notes:** two handwritten annotations sit directly on the photograph, drawn in a quick marker hand as if on a printout:
  - a loose circle around the order board: *"why by hand?"*
  - an arrow toward the line: *"Saturdays: 2nd register?"*
- **Two voices in the type.** The notes are handwriting, the person's own voice. "You don't have to go far to get further." is set in UW's typeface (Q17), UW's voice. The contrast carries the idea: their questions, UW's line.
- **Focal point and reading order:** the person → the nearest note → the second note → the line → UW lockup and program label.
- **Layout:** in 4:5, the notes sit on the scene and the line runs across the clear upper band. In 9:16, the person sits in the middle band, both notes stay inside the safe zone, and the line sits above the bottom 35% with the lockup and program label just above the safe line.
- **Absent:** answers, charts going up, new job titles, anything showing the question solved. No roads, maps, windows onto scenery or distance-staring. The person is looking at the task, not the camera.
- **Copy count:** two notes of at most four words each, plus the line, the lockup and the label. See "where it fails" on S1 p6 minimal on-image copy.

### 15 seconds

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–1s | Frame 1 is the full static: person, both notes, line, UW lockup visible. |
| 1–3s | The person glances at the order board. A third short note draws on in one quick stroke: *"who decides this?"* |
| 3–10s | Locked shot. They carry on with the task. At about 7s, one note gets underlined twice. Nothing is solved. |
| 10–15s | End card: UW lockup · program label · Apply Now. |

**Sound-off.** The notes and line are on-screen text, so the ad reads without sound. Captions are still required (S1 p6); the notes should also appear in the caption file so screen-reader and caption users get them.

### Rough Meta primary text

One opener across all five, followed by the standard program facts (PT-P1 to PT-P5 from restored-directions §PT, same claim IDs).

| Variant | Opener | Facts |
|---|---|---|
| All five | The questions start where you already are. | + PT for the variant |

The opener names where the questions come from, not what the program does with them. It does not assume the viewer is already considering graduate study (S1 p5).

### Why it works

- The idea is a picture of thinking, not a picture of a result. "Further" is visible as a question.
- It turns an ordinary workplace, the least aspirational image available, into the most ambitious one in the frame. That is the "Oh, of course" leap Direction 1 needs.
- It is a generative premise: each variant is a different place with different questions, not the same trick with a new label.

### Where it fails

- **Familiar device.** Handwritten annotations over footage are a common social-video treatment. No comparison was checked, but it feels familiar. It needs the specificity of the questions to lift it.
- **Too much copy.** Two notes plus the line strains S1 p6's "keep on-image copy minimal". If the static crowds at phone size, cut to one note in the static and keep the second for video.
- **Complaint, not curiosity.** Notes such as "why by hand?" can read as criticizing an employer. Write every note as a question the person could ask their boss out loud.
- **Rescue by implication.** If the end card seems to answer the notes, the ad says "you can't solve this without us." The notes must stay unanswered, including in the primary text.
- **No Wyoming.** Like the rest of Direction 1, ownership rests on the UW name and each variant's meaning of "near", not on the image.
- **Legibility.** Handwritten type must pass contrast and size checks at actual phone size (S4 accessibility; Q17).

### Claim flags

- **Asset:** none if the notes stay questions. P1 uses C12 as scene context only.
- **Primary text:** C10, C12 (P1) · C25, C02 (P2) · C36, C35 (P3, P4) · C44, C45 (P5). P3/P4 carry "CPA education and licensing requirements vary by state."

### Cowboys route

Neutral. The questions fit "a Cowboy isn't what you are but who you are" (S2 p4), but a sign-off would add a third voice to a frame that already has two. Video end card only, if at all (Q1, Q16).

### Production

- **Locations:** two, not five. One workplace for P2, P4 and P5, redressed per variant, and one campus room for P1 and P3.
- **Talent:** one cast adult per location, with releases. This is a new shoot, not UW/Canto library photography, so the S1 p6 "approved UW/Canto photography" route needs confirming (Q17, Q27). Video shot outside UW Video Services may also need Institutional Marketing review (Q15).
- **Camera:** locked off; no moves.
- **Notes:** 2D handwriting animation, drawn from a real hand, not a script font.
- **Tradeoff against D1-3 in v02:** heavier (two locations against one), but every variant matches its delivery format.

## Alternate held: TURNED AROUND  *(thumbnail depth only)*

- **Static:** a breakroom table with the people the person already works with. They have just turned a napkin around so the others can read it: a rough sketch of an idea, with a question mark. One coworker is leaning in. "You don't have to go far to get further."
- **Motion:** 1–2s, the napkin turns around. 2–3s, one coworker leans in. Hold.
- **Why hold it:** it is warmer and keeps people close in the most literal way, since the person brings the idea to the people rather than leaving them.
- **Why not first:** the napkin sketch is a well-worn "big idea" shorthand. The business content stays vaguer than in MARGIN NOTES. It needs a group of cast people in every variant.
