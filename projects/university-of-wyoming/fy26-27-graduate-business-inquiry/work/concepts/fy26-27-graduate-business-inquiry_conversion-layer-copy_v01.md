## The idea in one sentence

People stubborn enough to run the weeks in the prospecting ads can handle the next form, so each conversion message shrinks the next task to its real size, in the same voice, and never says where the viewer stopped.

**How it hangs off T2.** The prospecting hook says "TOO STUBBORN TO STOP? / YOU SOUND LIKE A COWBOY." and proves it with a week line ending "SAME WEEK." The conversion layer keeps that verbal system and points it at one small task: start, submit, send the documents, enroll. Grit gets applied to paperwork, with a little humor. It never turns into a verdict on the person.

**Three rules I held throughout.**

1. **No status implication on Meta.** Every Meta line is true and inoffensive for anyone who sees it, including people not on the matched list. No "you started", "your application", "we saw", "still", "now", "finally", "left". Articles, not possessives ("the application", "an application"). This also matches Andres's no-possessive direction for week lines (brief §11.4, 2026-09-14).
2. **No guilt.** Lines frame the task as small next to the person's week, never the person as someone who failed to finish. Dares ("Too stubborn to stop at…?") are offered as options with a tone flag, not recommended where the viewer is likely stuck on something outside their control.
3. **No invented urgency.** Deadlines, start terms, requirements, advisor contacts and destinations are visible placeholders (Q10). If the client supplies no deadline, the deadline line is deleted, not softened into "soon" or "today".

## Stage 2 · Application started, not submitted

**Message job.** The unfinished application becomes a task that fits the same week, with no penalty for doing it late at night or later than planned.

**Platform connection.** "SAME WEEK." is the campaign's verbal signature; here it absorbs one more item. "Done at 11 p.m. still counts as done" is anti-guilt: it lets the viewer off the hook for timing instead of scolding them for delay.

**Meta headline options**

| # | Headline | Chars | Note |

| B | Hit submit. You've had harder weeks. | 36 | Warmer; overlaps Stage 1's "harder things" mechanism, so the same person would see the trick twice |
| C | Done at 11 p.m. still counts as done. | 37 | Used as primary-text opener and email subject |

> Done at 11 p.m. still counts as done. The University of Wyoming Online MBA is 100% online and asynchronous. Application deadline: [DEADLINE: client to supply]. Apply Now.

Proof ends at character 107. Claim C25, SOURCE-STATED. Delete the deadline sentence if no deadline is supplied (Q10). "11 p.m." refers to finishing an application, not to coursework schedules (C67 guard: no workload or late-night study claim).

**CTA.** Apply Now. Destination should be the application portal sign-in, not the program page: [DESTINATION: client to supply].

**Platform connection.** Stubbornness applied to the least glamorous part. The week-line device lists requirements instead of life events. I avoided calling the paperwork "quick" or "short": some documents may depend on other people (transcripts, references), and requirements are unknown.
