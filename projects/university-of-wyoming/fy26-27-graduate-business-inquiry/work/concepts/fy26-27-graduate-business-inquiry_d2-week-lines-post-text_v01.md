## Task 1 · Week lines

**Rules applied to every line.** Three collision items plus SAME WEEK. Nine words or fewer. Each describes the prospect's life before enrolling, with no classes or midterms. No program claims, parental guilt, illness, exhaustion-as-virtue, military words, "real cowboys" or mental-health words. Life stages vary across the set: only P2 involves a child, and that item is a recital, not a crisis.

**Why the stubbornness has to show.** A busy week alone is only recognition. The strongest items show the viewer *choosing* to do more (a pitch deck, a volunteer tax night, an acting role), so "Too stubborn to stop?" reads as a description of them, not of their schedule.

### P1 · On-Campus MBA

Audience: recent graduates and early-career professionals seeking an immersive, applied path into leadership (S1 p3). Age 22–30 and "entrepreneurship-oriented" per S5. Not parents by default.

| # | Option | Words | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | CLOSING SHIFT. PITCH DECK. BEST FRIEND'S WEDDING. SAME WEEK. | 9 | A job they're outgrowing, something they're building, a young social life |
| B | NEW JOB. SIDE BUSINESS. LEASE ENDS. SAME WEEK. | 8 | Safer "professional" read. Flatter; "lease ends" hints at moving |
| C | FIRST REVIEW. MARKET BOOTH. REC LEAGUE FINAL. SAME WEEK. | 9 | Charming and local. "Market booth" is less clear at a glance |

**Recommended: A.** "Pitch deck" puts the stubbornness in building something, which carries into an immersive year better than juggling does.

Stress test with the question and answer:
- **Stopping power:** PITCH DECK is the snag; nobody expects it next to a closing shift.
- **Specificity:** reads 20s and early-career, not a parent.
- **Tension:** "Too stubborn to stop?" asks whether they'll keep building. The risk is that "stop" is read as keeping the job while in class, which an in-person year may not allow. This week keeps the grit in ambition, not in schedule.
- **Compression:** "BEST FRIEND'S" could go, but it carries the life stage. Keep it.
- **Rhythm:** two short beats, then a longer one, then the stop.
- **Visual:** DesignChamp needs to settle a conflict. S1 wants campus imagery for on-campus variants, but this week happens before campus.
- **Reward and memory:** carried by the constant answer.

Flag: "closing shift" may read as below "early-career professional" (S5). Option B is the fallback if UW objects.

## Task 2 · Meta primary text

**Structure.** An opener that continues the idea without repeating the headline, then one proof point, then any required caveat, then "Apply Now." (S1 p1 CTA wording).

**Reading order.** In Feed, primary text usually sits above the creative, so each opener has to work read first or second. None uses "stubborn" or "Cowboy", which keeps the reveal for the asset.

**Truncation figure and source.** The knowledge base publishes recommended primary-text lengths, not a truncation point:
- `production.md`: "Visibility and truncation depend on placement and configuration."
- 125 characters: Instagram Feed image (META-002) and Instagram Feed carousel (META-036, SOURCE_ISSUE)
- 50–150: Facebook Feed image (META-001)
- 80: Facebook Feed carousel (META-035)

I used 125 as the working limit: every proof point ends at or before character 125. None fits within 80. The P1 proof ends at 92, the closest. Required caveats fall after character 125 in P3, P4 and P5 (see flags).

**Never used:** STEM on any MBA; on-campus experiences in online variants; flexibility claims on on-campus variants; rankings, cost, ROI, salary, outcomes, pass rates, deadlines, dates or urgency; C37, C38 or C42 in any wording.
