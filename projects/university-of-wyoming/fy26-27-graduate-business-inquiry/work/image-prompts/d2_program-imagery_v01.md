## The image's job

1 + 1 = 3 with the week line and question. The week line lists the collisions. The picture proves they are real by showing **a person in the seam between two parts of their week**, with one or two of the named collisions physically present. It is not a pose of stubbornness, not a metaphor, and not someone "studying late".

Three rules follow. They fix what went wrong in the v01 comps.

1. **One collision object, large and unmistakable.** Every scene is built around one non-school object taken from its week line: a server's apron, a softball bat bag, a bouquet, a puppy, a spread of old family photographs. Each is big enough to render and to read at phone size. A second item may support it; there is no shopping list of small props.
2. **A person mid-action.** Hands moving, body turned to a task. Never looking at the lens, never still, never exhausted.
3. **The light source is never in the frame, and never beside the headline.** The brightest thing in the picture is the lit task, in the lower half.

**The image shows one or two items from the week line, never all three.** The viewer's reading of the line completes the week; the picture is the evidence, not an illustration of every word.

**On-campus variants (P1, P3).**
- **Two sources pull different ways.** S1 asks for "immersive campus experience". The week lines describe the prospect's life before enrolling.
- **Design resolution:** the setting is campus, and the collision objects are the life they bring with them.
- **Risk:** this can read as "keep your job and add an in-person program" (word-smither flag 5). See Flag 2.

## Set consistency: one campaign, Brown dominant

Apply these rules to every lead, alternate, placeholder and final photo. Brief the photographer or the Canto search against them.

**Moment.** Every scene is the seam of a week: the minutes between two commitments. Something is on the way in or on the way out: the apron still tied, the bat bag packed, the laptop closing as the recital begins.

**Time of day.** Edge of the day only: dusk, evening or pre-dawn. The outside world never appears as a view; windows are blinds or dark glass. Keep the set season-neutral (no snow, holiday decor or summer clothes), because flight dates are not established.

**Light.**
- One dominant warm practical source: a tungsten lamp, spill from a doorway, stage footlights. Place it out of frame, below or to the side of the subject.
- It lights the hands and the collision object. The fall-off leaves the upper frame in shadow.
- Nothing bright in the type zone: no pendant, lamp, bulb, window or screen glow. No flat overhead fluorescent look.
- Key-to-shadow about 8:1.
- Laptop screens are side-on or steeply angled: a soft glow, never readable content. No device-mockup screens.

**Lens and camera.**
- 35mm equivalent for environment scenes; 50mm for close hand scenes.
- Standing or seated eye height, level or slightly down. No top-down flat lay, drone, Dutch tilt or wide-angle distortion.
- f/2–2.8: the collision object and hands are sharp; the setting stays soft but still legible as a place.
- Observed, not staged. Subjects never look at the lens.

**Grade: "Brown hour".**
- Shadows sit in the UW Brown family (about #2A1C15 to #492F24), never neutral black or blue-black.
- Highlights are warm cream (about #F3E6CF), never pure white, so the white headline stays the brightest element in the ad.
- Gold enters only through warm practical highlights or one ochre or rust object.
- Overall saturation about −25%. Blues and greens pull toward desaturated slate and olive, reds toward rust. Flowers and a puppy's coat are muted too.
- Fine 35mm grain. No bloom, glow, lens flare, heavy vignette or gradient fade.
- The darkness must be in the photograph, not in an overlay: pack file 10 forbids gradients and black fades. If a type zone is too bright, recrop or reshoot. The only acceptable fallback is a flat solid Brown panel.

**People.**
- Casting follows "Trailblazers come in all shapes and sizes": varied ages, genders, ethnicities and body types.
- Ordinary clothes. No UW apparel in placeholders; in final on-campus photos, only as UW supplies or approves.
- Placeholders show no faces: backs, hands, over-the-shoulder, out-of-focus figures. Final photos may show released faces in profile or three-quarter view, eyes on the task.
- Never show yawning, eye-rubbing, heads on desks, piles of energy drinks, clocks at late hours, tears or guilt.

**Set-wide exclusions.**
- Stock "studying late" setups.
- Mountains, trails, roads or horizons.
- Graduation caps or gowns.
- Cowboy hats, boots as costume, horses or western decor.
- Stock meetings, heroic poses, glorified exhaustion, parental guilt, device-mockup screens.
- Readable text, numbers, logos or brand names on anything.

## Composition: shared zones

The Figma layout is fixed across variants. Week line and headline sit flush left in the dark upper zone, the lockup sits bottom-left, and the subject is weighted **center-right**. Every scene is composed so the bottom-left corner stays dark for the lockup.

## Carousel: one image cropped progressively, or different images per card

**Recommendation:** one photograph per variant, cropped progressively.
- **Card 1:** tight on the collision object.
- **Card 2:** the wide scene revealed.
- **Card 3:** the answer and lockup over the dark zone.

**Reasons.**
- **The idea is one week in one life.** Different photos per card become a montage of moments. That reads as a stock "busy life" sequence and loses the 1 + 1 = 3.
- **Card 1 must work alone.** Next to the week line, a tight crop on the apron pocket, glove, bouquet, sleeping puppy or old photograph is a small puzzle: whose week is this? Swiping to the wide frame answers it as "TOO STUBBORN TO STOP?" lands, which gives the swipe a visual reason.
- **Tight to wide, not wide to tight.** The photo comps review suggested wide → laptop → mug. Tight to wide keeps the reveal on the question card, where the idea turns.
- **Fewer images to produce.** One released image per variant instead of three means fewer releases, fewer Canto searches and one grade.
- **Resolution cost.** A 1080 px square tight crop taken from about a quarter of the frame needs a source around 4000+ px on the short side. AI placeholders will look soft at that crop; finals need full-resolution UW or shoot files. If the source is too small, use **two frames of the same moment from the same shoot** (detail and wide), never unrelated images.
- **Video.** The same sequence carries into the 15s video: detail, then reveal, then the dark zone for answer and lockup. Statics, carousel and video stay one system.
- **S1 condition.** This is still a narrative carousel, not "several distinct proof points" (compliance check #6). The imagery choice does not change that.
