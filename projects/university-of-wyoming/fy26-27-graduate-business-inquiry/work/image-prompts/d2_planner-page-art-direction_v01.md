## 0. The idea, before any layout

**Barry §1**

```
TRUTH:          The week is already full, and the one line that is for them is the one that never
                gets crossed off.
CONCEPT:        A torn planner page carries the week in the person's own pen; everything is struck
                through except the line that is for them.
AUDIENCE SEES:  A ripped spiral-planner page lying on the evidence of that week, four items, three
                crossed out in ballpoint, the last one untouched with a gold highlighter stripe under it.
AUDIENCE GETS:  They finish everything except the thing that is for them. Nobody had to say it.
CARRIED BY:     Visual-verbal, with the verbal inside the image (Barry §3). The words are handwriting
                in the photograph, not typography over it.
```

**Visual-craft §12**

```
FIRST:       The untouched line. Everything else in the frame is struck, dark or soft.
FEEL:        Unfinished.
COLOR JOBS:  #492F24 Brown dominant (the grade, not a fill) · warm cream #F3E6CF highlights,
             secondary · #FFC425 Gold accent, exactly one event per frame, as the highlighter stripe.
IMAGE:       One warm practical source out of frame, below or beside; key-to-shadow ~8:1; brightest
             point on the page, below the middle of the frame. Face-free. Documentary, not styled.
CODES:       Adopted — the documentary tabletop. Subverted — the to-do list, which advertising
             normally draws as an interface. Avoided — checkbox, progress bar, calendar app, sticky
             note, torn-paper clip art, handwriting fonts.
SYSTEM:      CONSTANT = the page, the pen, the one untouched line, the single gold event.
             VARIABLE = the items (program), the unticked line (stage), the angle (card position).
REAL SIZE:   375 px feed width; 9:16 safe area y 270–1248, x 65–1015.
RISK:        The handwriting dies at thumbnail and the whole idea becomes an illegible texture.
             §4 is the protocol that has to clear it before a shoot is booked.
```

### Angle notes that matter

**A1 · Overhead.** The one angle where a phone-scale composition is automatic: it is the shape of a phone looking down at a desk. Use it wherever the list must be read whole. Hands may enter from the bottom edge (face-free rule, imagery v02); a head never enters. Do not centre the page — it sits left of centre, the evidence weighted right, so the reading runs diagonally into the frame.

**A2 · Raking.** At 10–15° a page lying flat is nearly edge-on and unreadable, so the page is **propped, not flat**. That is a real gesture (you lean the list where you can see it), it keeps the list legible at the specified angle, and it lets the set line own the dark top. This is the only frame where the page is not on the table.

**A3 · Macro.** This is where the gold stripe lands (see the gold rule below). Do not shoot the pen crossing out an item here — shoot it stopped, nib resting at the left of the untouched line, ink not yet on it. The struck items sit above, out of focus. The withholding is the point (Pricken: omission — the audience completes the line).

**A4 · Framed through.** Approved objects for the Online MBA: an open locker door edge, the corner of a flattened moving box, a dry-cleaning bag's plastic edge, a truck door frame, a doorway jamb. Not approved: a laptop lid (reads as device mockup, pack 10), a phone, a window (imagery v02 excludes windows and horizons), a hat brim (cowboy costume, pack 10).

### Sequence rule (revises the carousel order in imagery v02)

The still-life carousel in `d2_program-imagery_v02.md` goes **tight → wide → dark**. The planner carousel goes **wide → closer → closest**, because card 1 now has to be *read*, not just recognised. A macro on card 1 hides the list, which is the week line.

| Card | Angle | Set type | Gold |
|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | A2 raking | TOO STUBBORN TO STOP? | STUBBORN |
| 3 | A3 macro | YOU SOUND LIKE A COWBOY. | **the highlighter stripe** — COWBOY is White |

| 5 | flat UW Brown (on-campus: Canto campus band) | one proof line + lockup | gold rule under the proof line |

**The gold rule for this system.** One gold event per frame, and on a frame where the page appears the event is the highlighter stripe or the set word, never both. Therefore:

- **Card 1 / hero static:** the crop stops above the unticked line's stripe is *not* used — the stripe is present, and no set word is gold.
- **Card 2:** the crop lands above the last line. The untouched item and its stripe are below the frame edge. STUBBORN is gold as usual.
- **Card 3:** the stripe arrives as the reveal. COWBOY is White (pack 12 emphasis Version C, Blackhawk Italic lowercase, real font only, is the recommended substitute).

This diverges from pack 12's "one Gold word per card" on card 3 and from pack 11's "Gold marks the task word only". Flagged in §8; needs CD sign-off before the pack is updated.

### Where "SAME WEEK." goes

The page cannot carry a set week line, so the campaign's verbal signature needs a home.

- **Recommended:** the planner's **date slot at the top of the page** is filled, in the same hand, with `same week.` A person's own shorthand where a date should be. It keeps card 1 free of set type and keeps the signature in character.
- **Fallback:** "SAME WEEK." set in Montserrat Bold caps 48 (1:1 / 9:16) / 64 (4:5), White, flush left on the margin, in the dark band immediately below the page. Costs the purity of card 1 but guarantees the signature survives if the handwriting is regraded or cropped.

Either way the words are word-smither's, not design's. Flagged in §8.

## 3. Page craft rules

The page is a built object, photographed once per program, then versioned digitally. Specify it like a prop, not a graphic.

**Stock.** 70–80 gsm uncoated wood-free planner paper, warm off-white (not bright white, which blows out against the Brown-hour grade and reads as a Figma rectangle). Ruled at 7 mm with pale blue-grey lines, not black. No margin rule, no printed dates, no page numbers, no branding, no printed day names — the page must carry no institutional or pre-printed text at all.

**Tear.** Torn *across* the sheet, not down. Long axis horizontal. Pull the page against the spiral so the tear runs from left to right at a slight downward drift and leaves 2–4 mm of white fibre feathering; never a straight or scissored edge, never a perforated edge (a perforated tear reads as designed).

**Spiral fringe.** Along the **top** edge. 8–14 torn ring holes, at least three of them ripped through into open half-moons, the rest intact. This is the one detail that makes the object a planner page and not a receipt or a notepad sheet. Do not crop it out in any format.

**Rotation.** −3° to −6° from horizontal, never 0°. Left side slightly higher. Same direction across a program's set so the family reads; the *amount* may vary by frame.

**Shadow.** In camera, never in Figma (pack 10 forbids drawn shadows). One curled corner lifted 3–6 mm off the surface so the light throws a soft shadow 8–20 mm long under the torn edge. That shadow is what proves the page is an object. If it is not there, the frame is unusable.

**Pen.** Blue-black ballpoint, 0.7 mm, on its way out — the ink slightly starved, with one or two skips. Not gel, not fineliner, not felt tip, not fountain. Ink reads as #1F2A33 to #2B3640 in the graded frame, never pure black.

**Hand.** Fast, lowercase, unjoined or half-joined. Not calligraphic, not "designer handwriting", not the same letterforms twice. Each program's page written by the **same hand** (one writer for the whole campaign) so the set is one person. Cap height 4.5–6 mm on the physical page.

**Stroke weight.** Ink stroke must survive to at least **1.5 px** in the final 375 px-wide render. On a 936 px-wide page that means a minimum 3.5 px stroke in the master file. Test, do not assume (§4).

**The strike-through.** Drawn, not typeset. Rules:
- One stroke per item, left to right, in the same pen.
- Overshoots the word by 8–16 px at each end (a real strike starts before and ends after).
- Sits at 40–48% of the cap height from the top, i.e. through the middle of the lowercase, not on the baseline.
- Tilts 1–3° against the writing, so it does not look like an underline or a typographic strikethrough.
- Never a double line, never a scribble-out, never a tick, check, box or cross beside it. Pack 11 forbids checkmarks and strike-throughs as an interface device; this survives that rule only because it is ink in the character's hand, on their own list. If it starts to look like a UI treatment, it has failed.

**The gold stripe.** UW Gold #FFC425, applied as a **highlighter pass**, not a rule:
- One stroke, left to right, under the untouched line only.
- 5–7 mm tall on the physical page; 24–36 px in the master files.
- Starts 8–12 px before the first letter and ends 12–20 px after the last: a highlighter overruns.
- Slightly uneven — heavier at the start, one thin miss near the end, edges not parallel.
- Tilted −2° to −3°, independent of the page rotation.
- Set to Multiply at 85–95% so the paper texture comes through. A flat opaque gold bar reads as a drawn rectangle and kills the object.
- **Under**, never over the words. Gold over type fails contrast (pack 10) and would look like a redaction.
- Appears on **one frame per unit** (see §1 gold rule). It is an event, not a texture.

**Digital-layer method (versioning without a reshoot).**

1. Shoot the **blank page** on set, in position, in the real light: correct paper, correct tear, correct spiral fringe, correct curl and shadow. Shoot it once per angle per program setup.
2. Shoot a **separate ink plate**: the same writer, the same pen, every line for every program and stage, written on an identical blank sheet, photographed flat under even light. Also shoot the strike strokes and 6–8 highlighter strokes as loose marks.
3. Cut the ink and the strokes from the plate, drop them on the page image as Multiply layers, and warp to the page's perspective and curl using the ruled lines as the guide.
4. This gives every combination of 5 programs × 3 stage lines from one shoot day, and lets a line be re-written without re-lighting anything.
5. The paper's shadow, texture, tear and fringe stay **in camera**. Nothing about the object is generated or drawn.
6. Comp stage only: until the ink plate exists, the list is set in Montserrat Medium lowercase on the placed blank-page image, labelled as a stand-in. See §4 for what that costs.

## 4. Legibility test protocol

Run before a shoot is booked. It costs an afternoon and one phone.

**Setup**
1. Write the **longest** line in the set by hand on the real paper stock with the real pen: Online MBA's `two people short.` is not the longest — use `covering the branch.` (P5) and `volunteer tax night.` (P3) as the worst cases, plus the untouched line `application.`
2. Write it at three cap heights: **4.5 mm, 5.5 mm and 6.5 mm.**
3. Strike two of the items with the real pen; leave one untouched; lay one highlighter stroke under it.
4. Tear the page from a real spiral planner. Lift one corner.

**Photograph**
5. Shoot the page in the A1 overhead setup, at the specified light and grade, at each of the three cap heights.
6. Place each frame into the 1080 × 1080 layout at the §2 coordinates. Apply the Brown-hour grade.

**View**
7. Export at 1080 px. Downsample to **375 px wide**. View on an actual phone in a feed, not on a monitor at 35%.
8. Also view the 375 px version with the screen at 40% brightness (a feed at night) and after one round of JPEG compression at quality 70.

**Pass / fail**

| # | Criterion | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Struck items | Each one readable in under 2 seconds |
| 2 | Untouched line | Readable in under 1 second, and read **first** |
| 3 | Strike-throughs | Visibly cross the words; not mistakable for underlines |
| 4 | Gold stripe | Reads as gold, not as brown, tan or a shadow, at 40% brightness |
| 5 | Object | Still reads as a torn planner page: fringe, tear and shadow all survive |
| 6 | Stroke | Ink measures ≥ 1.5 px wide at 375 px; does not break up after compression |
| 7 | Squint test | With eyes narrowed, the untouched line is the only line still legible |

**Fail rules.** Criteria 2, 4 and 7 are hard fails — the idea does not work without them. If 1 or 3 fail, go up one cap height and re-run; do not compensate by adding contrast in the grade, which will break the Brown-hour palette. If 6 fails at 6.5 mm cap height, the page is too small in the layout: increase the page's share of the frame before increasing the ink.

**Fallback if handwriting fails at every size.** Set the list on the photographed paper in Gotham (Montserrat stand-in), Medium, lowercase, dark blue-black at 85% opacity, warped to the page's perspective.

**What that costs the idea, honestly:**
- The list stops being *this person's* and becomes the ad's. The strongest thing about the mechanism — that nobody wrote this for an ad — is the first thing lost.
- A set list with a strike-through starts to look like a **checklist graphic**, which is exactly the interface the whole system (pack 11, conversion layer §5.6) forbids. The fallback survives only if the strike-throughs and the highlighter stripe stay **scanned real strokes** over the set type. A typographic strikethrough style is not an option at any point.
- The page becomes a container for typography rather than an object in a photograph, which weakens the reason for shooting an angle at all: at that point the two registers are a photo and a caption again.
- Recommendation if it comes to this: keep the handwriting for the **untouched line only** (one short line, largest, easiest to make legible) and set the struck items. The one line that matters stays in the person's hand.

**The honest position.** The planner-page system is a **tabletop still-life system**, and tabletop still life is the cheapest thing in photography: one table, one light, no talent, no releases, no location fee (planner system v01, Production). If the client holds Canto as a hard mandatory for all imagery, the mechanism does not survive it, and that is a strategy conversation, not an art-direction one.

## 8. Flags

**Gold.** On card 3 the highlighter stripe is the frame's only gold, so COWBOY is set in White. This diverges from pack 12 ("one Gold word per card") and pack 11 ("Gold marks the task word only"). Recommended substitute emphasis: pack 12 Version C, COWBOY in Blackhawk Italic lowercase glyphs, real font file only. Needs CD sign-off before packs 11 and 12 are updated.

**"SAME WEEK."** The page cannot carry set type, so the signature moves into the handwriting as the page's date-slot line (`same week.`) or, as fallback, into a small set line below the page. This is word-smither's call, not design's.

**Carousel order.** The planner carousel runs wide → closer → closest. `d2_program-imagery_v02.md` runs tight → wide → dark for the still-life system. The two cannot both be the T2 rule; if the planner page is selected, the imagery v02 card order is superseded for planner units only.

**Strike-through vs. the interface ban.** Pack 11 and the conversion-layer design both forbid strike-throughs on "done" items as an interface device. This system survives that only because the strike is ink in the character's hand on their own list. It is a narrow line. If a comp starts reading as a checklist graphic, the rule has been broken, not bent. The §4 fallback is the highest-risk moment for this.

**Canto.** None of the four planner angles can come from UW's library (§6). The raw brief names Canto as the primary imagery source. This is the largest single risk to the mechanism and needs a client answer, not a workaround.

**"Hero static."** Pack 11 records Andres, 2026-09-17: each post is a carousel or an animated video, no single static. The §7 build is a hero frame for presentation and for carousel card 1. If a true single static is now wanted, pack 11 needs updating.

**Untested.** The legibility protocol (§4) has not been run. Every cap height, stroke weight and type size in §2 and §7 is a calculated target, not a measured result. Nothing should be shot until §4 passes.

**Open, inherited.** Whether UW accepts handwriting as a "specialty font used on a limited basis for emphasis" (S4 p15). Whether library photography may be regraded or composited (imagery v02 flag 10; Q17, Q27). Claims: nothing on the page states a program fact, and nothing here changes the claim ledger.

## Tone reference (added 2026-09-23, CD)

Andres supplied a shot breakdown as the tone target: an overhead insert of a student at a library desk covered in open books, Paul Thomas Anderson, shot by Robert Elswit, 35mm film, 2.39 anamorphic, soft light, centred composition, cool desaturated palette, 1990s. Published work is creative reference only; it is not client evidence, and we borrow the craft logic, never the film's look or content.

**What we take**
- **Soft light, not raking.** A large diffused source high and slightly behind camera. This supersedes A1's "one large warm practical raking at 35°": raking light throws ballpoint shadows across the handwriting and costs legibility, which is the whole gate on this idea. Keep grazing light only for A3 macro, where paper tooth is the subject.
- **Centred insert.** The overhead is centred and near-symmetrical, the page square to the frame within a few degrees. Stronger at phone scale than an off-centre flat lay, and it reads as evidence rather than styling.
- **Honest clutter.** Objects overlap and sit where a person left them. Nothing is spaced for the camera.
- **Film character.** Fine grain, gentle contrast, soft highlight roll-off, slight halation on the brightest paper. No digital clarity or micro-contrast.
- **The person, anonymous.** The reference's back-of-head framing lets a person be present without a face: the viewer looks over their shoulder at their own week. This gives us S1 p6's "people, action" without casting a face in comps.

**What we do not take**
- The cool blue palette. UW Brown and Gold stay dominant. Where depth is needed, a cool neutral ambient may sit behind a warm practical, with shadows staying warm brown.
- Anamorphic 2.39. Our canvases are 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16.
- The film's setting, period or subject.

**New angle A1b · overhead with the back of a head.** A1's composition, with the top of a head and one shoulder entering the bottom edge of the frame, out of focus. Person present, unidentifiable. Use where an ad needs a human without a casting approval; in production, released talent shot from behind.
