# Creative platforms: EXPLORE round 2 (v02, 2026-10-08)

CLIENT: Grand Junction Airport / GJRASA · PROJECT: ski-aspen-itinerary-ads · AUDIENCE: Coloradans (Front Range)
MODE: EXPLORE, pushed past first thoughts (Barry *1-in-10 / write first, edit last*). v01 platforms were the obvious layer; this round digs for Colorado-specific truths only a local would recognize, and forces a different mechanism per idea (Pricken §7).
Lines are placeholders. Guardrails as v01: no speed / time-saved claims, no "skip I-70" (the GJT route uses ~85 mi of I-70), no road promises, resorts as destinations only.

## New truth bank (what v01 missed)
- **Coloradans speak in exit numbers.** "Stuck at 244", "Floyd Hill", "past the tunnel": mile markers are local vocabulary.
- **Sunday flow.** Sunday ski traffic pours *east* into Denver. Driving Aspen → GJT on Sunday heads *west*, away from it. [DERIVED, UNVERIFIED: confirm with CDOT traffic patterns before any claim.]
- **Palisade = peaches.** Every Coloradan knows Palisade from summer peach stands. Nobody thinks of it in winter.
- **The Brachiosaurus.** GJT is installing a Brach replica (site, Aug 2026); the original was dug up on Riggs Hill. A free, ownable, already-happening brand asset.
- **The Front Range barely registers the Western Slope.** Half the state is a blank on locals' mental map.
- **Locals already pay to avoid the drive** (Winter Park Express, $19).
- **I-70 trauma is a running joke**: 5 a.m. alarms, 511, the tunnel queue.

## A · THE EXIT STRATEGY
**Idea:** Borrow the enemy's language. The itinerary is written as green interstate exit signs: EXIT 37: LAND · EXIT 44: WINE COUNTRY · EXIT 116: HOT SPRINGS · ASPEN: 60 MI. Coloradans know exits by heart; we give them new ones to remember.
**Mechanism:** Context shift (§2.6): highway signage repurposed as holiday itinerary. Barry *symbol with a twist*.
**Three executions:** (1) carousel: one exit sign per card, final card "NEXT EXIT: ASPEN" · (2) Stories: sign flips past like driving, sound of a turn signal · (3) girls' trip: a hand-painted "EXIT HERE FOR WINE" sign.
**Why it's stronger:** a full visual system, instantly local, generative across all four audiences.
**Check:** exit numbers must be exact; MUTCD-style signs read as official, so stylize them.

## B · I-70 SUPPORT GROUP
**Idea:** Treat I-70 fatigue as a recognized condition. Deadpan testimonials: "It's been 3 weekends since I sat at the tunnel." "Ask your doctor if a hot spring is right for you." Pharma-ad parody: "Side effects may include Palisade wine, steam, and arriving in Aspen in a good mood."
**Mechanism:** Exaggeration (§2.5), deadpan, with Whipple §8 enemy.
**Three executions:** (1) 15s video: support-circle chairs, a skier shares, cut to Glenwood pool · (2) static: "Hi, I'm Karen. Floyd Hill took my Saturdays." · (3) boomer: "30 years of I-70. Today I'm free." (in a vapor cave).
**Why it's stronger:** a funny, shareable format with built-in recognition (Whipple §13). Locals would tag friends.
**Check:** Barry *exaggerate, don't contrive*: the pain must be real (it is). Keep the tone affectionate, not whiny. Medical parody may trigger platform policy; test the copy.

## C · THE WRONG DIRECTION
**Idea:** On Sunday every Coloradan crawls east. You're the one heading west, against it. A whole campaign about being smugly on the other side of the median.
**Mechanism:** Reversal (§2.2) / Barry *Opposite Tool*.
**Three executions:** (1) aerial: a jammed eastbound I-70, one car on the empty westbound side · (2) "Go west, skier." (Western-film type) · (3) the family: kids asleep, empty road, red rock.
**Why it's stronger:** the sharpest single insight, and genuinely different from every ski ad.
**Check:** **UNVERIFIED and the riskiest claim here.** Only usable if CDOT data supports the Sunday direction pattern, and even then it's framed as an observation, not a traffic promise.

## D · PEACH SEASON NEVER ENDED
**Idea:** Coloradans think of Palisade in August. Show them winter Palisade: snowy vines, warm tasting rooms. The Western Slope you know, in a season you've never seen it.
**Mechanism:** Context shift (§2.6): a familiar place in the wrong season.
**Three executions:** (1) a peach-crate label redrawn as a ski trip ("PALISADE · WINTER HARVEST · SKI ASPEN") · (2) girls' trip: rosé in puffer jackets · (3) boomers: "You've bought the peaches. Now taste the wine."
**Why it's stronger:** a cultural truth only Coloradans hold; gives the girls' trip and boomer couple a native hook.
**Check:** narrower. It's an audience/stop idea and may be a sub-campaign rather than the platform.

## E · BRACH WOULDN'T DRIVE IT
**Idea:** Grand Junction's Brachiosaurus becomes the campaign host. The longest neck in Colorado has seen the I-70 traffic from up there, and it's taking the scenic route. Ties the ski campaign to the airport's own new landmark.
**Mechanism:** Analogy (§2.4) + an owned character, Barry *symbol with a twist*.
**Three executions:** (1) Brach in a beanie at baggage claim, "Welcome to the scenic side" · (2) Brach neck peering over a snowy Rim Rock Drive · (3) family: kids measure themselves against the Brach, then the gondola.
**Why it's stronger:** an asset no other airport or resort has, already in the client's news; builds a character for years, not one season.
**Check:** needs client buy-in on using the Brach; licensed or illustrated imagery (no real photo yet). It could feel kid-skewed, so test it with the couples.

## F · THE OTHER HALF
**Idea:** Coloradans have explored half of Colorado. The Western Slope is the half they keep driving past. Ski trip as discovery of your own state.
**Mechanism:** Omission (§2.1): the map with the west side blank, filled in stop by stop.
**Three executions:** (1) a Colorado map, east half worn and full of pins, west half blank; the carousel fills it in · (2) "You've never been to half of Colorado." · (3) a passport-stamp series per stop.
**Why it's stronger:** a pride angle (local identity), not a pain angle; a good counterweight to B and C.
**Check:** relies on the map device landing; avoid patronizing the Western Slope.

## G · THE SKI PLANE
**Idea:** Colorado already has a ski train for Winter Park. Aspen has a ski plane. Frame the DEN → GJT flight as the Front Range's own ski commute: practical, local, obvious once named.
**Mechanism:** Analogy (§2.4) to the Winter Park Express.
**Three executions:** (1) retro rail-poster style "THE ASPEN EXPRESS*" (*a plane and a scenic drive) · (2) the boarding pass as a ski ticket · (3) "Packed: skis. Skipped: Floyd Hill."
**Why it's stronger:** names a behavior locals already accept, so it lowers the strangeness of flying in-state.
**Check:** can't imply it's quicker or cheaper; "Express" sounds like speed, so use a different word.
