## A · THE EXIT STRATEGY

**Concept sentence (Barry §1):** The ski trip written as highway exit signs, so the road Coloradans dread becomes the itinerary they'd actually want.
**Truth + conflict (Whipple §6):** Coloradans navigate by exit numbers *and* hate the road those numbers belong to.
**Mechanism:** Context shift (Pricken §2.6), Barry *symbol with a twist*: the interstate's own visual language repurposed.

**Platform rule (Barry §8)**
- CONSTANT: green-sign graphic language (stylized, not MUTCD-exact) · exit number + destination · rust GJT tab on every sign · final sign = Aspen
- VARIABLE: which exits, what the "destination" says, who's in the photo, tone of the sign text

**Executions by audience**
| Audience | Carousel (3–5 cards) | Sign wit (candidates) |
|---|---|---|
| Girls' trip | EXIT 31 LAND → EXIT 42 PALISADE "WINE, OBVIOUSLY" → EXIT 116 "HOT SPRINGS / GROUP PHOTO" → ASPEN "APRÈS, DAY 1" | "NEXT EXIT: ROSÉ" |
| Family | EXIT 31 LAND → EXIT 116 "GONDOLA / CAVES / POOL" → SNOWMASS "S'MORES 1/2 MI" | "REST AREA (ACTUALLY RESTFUL)" |
| Millennial couple | EXIT 31 LAND → EXIT 19 / MONUMENT "RED ROCK, FRESH SNOW" → PALISADE "NIGHTCAP" → ASPEN | "SCENIC OVERLOOK. FOR ONCE, STOP." |
| Boomer couple | EXIT 31 LAND → PALISADE "TASTING ROOM, NO CROWDS" → EXIT 116 "VAPOR CAVES" → ASPEN "DINNER AT 7" | "YOU'VE EARNED THIS EXIT" |

**Video (15s):** signs pass overhead in sequence, turn-signal tick as the beat, each sign cuts to the stop; end sign: ASPEN + "Fly GJT. Start at Exit 31." + Book Now.
**End card (stands alone in Stories):** single green sign: "ASPEN · VIA GJT" + Book Now.
**Line candidates:** Start at Exit 31. · Take the scenic exits. · Same interstate. Better exits. · Your ski trip, exit by exit. · Exit 31. Then everything else.
**DEVELOP flags:**
- Exit numbers must be exact on every sign (Palisade 42 and Glenwood 116 to confirm on a map).
- Stylize the signs (rounded, rust tab, brand type) so they don't read as official CDOT signage.
- Generativity holds: four audiences give four different sign sets, plus seasonal and event signs ("EXIT: X GAMES WEEKEND").

## B · I-70 SUPPORT GROUP

**Concept sentence:** Coloradans treat I-70 as a shared trauma; we run the support group, and the recovery is a trip that starts at GJT.
**Truth + conflict:** identity vs action. Locals complain about I-70 every weekend and keep driving it (research: 5 a.m. alarms, Floyd Hill, the tunnel queue).
**Mechanism:** Exaggeration (Pricken §2.5), deadpan; enemy = the Front Range I-70 ritual (Whipple §8). Barry *exaggerate, don't contrive*: every complaint is real.

**Platform rule**
- CONSTANT: support-group format (folding chairs, name tags, coffee urn, sharing voice) · real I-70 pain named specifically · the "recovery" is a stop on the GJT route
- VARIABLE: who's sharing, which pain, which recovery

**Executions by audience**
| Audience | Share (testimonial) | Recovery beat |
|---|---|---|
| Girls' trip | "We used to spend our girls' weekend in a Subaru outside Idaho Springs." | Glenwood hot springs, cocktails up |
| Family | "Three kids. One bathroom stop. Floyd Hill. Never again." | Glenwood Caverns gondola, everyone smiling |
| Millennial couple | "We once broke up at the Eisenhower Tunnel. We got back together at Exit 116." | Couple in steam at Iron Mountain |
| Boomer couple | "Thirty years of I-70 on Saturdays. Today I'm free." | Vapor caves, robes, calm |

**Video (15s):** circle of chairs → one share (0–3s, GJT name tag) → "the twelve steps" are the itinerary (land, sip, soak, ski) → end: "Recovery starts at GJT." + Book Now.
**Static device:** a "HELLO, MY NAME IS" sticker as the headline holder ("Hello, my name is Karen. Floyd Hill took my Saturdays.").
**Line candidates:** Recovery starts at GJT. · You're not alone. You're just on I-70. · One day at a time. Starting on Day One. · Admitting it is the first step. Landing is the second.
**DEVELOP flags:**
- Keep it affectionate: we laugh *with* locals, never at mountain towns or CDOT.
- Avoid medical or pharma imitation in paid social (policy risk). The support-group frame is safer than the "ask your doctor" parody; drop the pharma bits.
- "Never again" must not imply never driving: the GJT route still drives 2 h. Keep it about the Front Range stretch.
- Humor wears out, so rotate shares and plan 8–10 testimonials.

## F · THE OTHER HALF

**Concept sentence:** Coloradans have explored half their own state; the ski trip through GJT fills in the other half.
**Truth + conflict:** expectation vs experience. Proud locals who "know Colorado" have mostly only driven past the Western Slope.
**Mechanism:** Omission (Pricken §2.1): the blank west half of the map, completed card by card.

**Platform rule**
- CONSTANT: Colorado's rectangle as the frame (instantly recognizable) · east half worn, pinned, familiar · west half blank until filled · rust route line from Exit 31 to Aspen
- VARIABLE: which pins get added, who's adding them, what the "half" means per audience

**Executions by audience**
| Audience | Carousel story |
|---|---|
| Girls' trip | A pin board of every brewery and trailhead on the Front Range; the west half fills with Palisade, Peach Street, Glenwood |
| Family | Kids' sticker map: "We've been to Colorado 400 times. Never this half." Brach sticker optional |
| Millennial couple | A worn map with a coffee ring over Denver; the new pins are Rim Rock and Lunch Loops |
| Boomer couple | "Fifty years in Colorado. Turns out there's more." Vintage atlas style, the wine and caves pinned |

**Video (15s):** map with west half blank → pins drop in rhythm as the stops cut in → Aspen pin → full state lit → "See the other half. Fly GJT."
**Line candidates:** You've only seen half of Colorado. · The other half has hot springs. · Colorado, the full version. · Ski the half you know. Start in the half you don't.
**DEVELOP flags:**
- Don't patronize the Western Slope or GJ locals (the client's community): frame it as Front Range people's blind spot, not "empty" land.
- The map must be accurate (GJ, Palisade, Glenwood, Aspen positions).
- A pride angle balances B's pain angle.
