## 1. Problem framing: Statement Starters (Understanding)
- How might we make a night in Grand Junction feel like **the first chapter** of an Aspen ski trip, not a layover?
- How might we give Front Range skiers a reason to **stop** in a town they've only ever driven past?
- How might we remove the practical barriers to a GJ night (car, lodging, what's open in winter)?
- How might local organizations **co-own** the GJ day so it's real on arrival, not just in the ad?

## 4. Rose, Thorn, Bud (Understanding): the GJ-day idea
- **Rose (what's working):** it gives GJT something no other Aspen gateway has; "rested" becomes literal; R1 already lists downtown GJ.
- **Thorn (what's hard):** adds a night (cost, time off); the car barrier; winter hours; partnerships can't appear in the 10/14 ads without approval.
- **Bud (potential):** a GJRASA-brokered "GJ Night" bundle (flight + car + hotel) for a later phase; a Brach arrival moment; a CMU student "locals' guide".

## 6. Looking: cheap research to replace assumptions (before or alongside DEVELOP)
- **Interviewing:** 5–8 Front Range skiers per audience: "Would you add a night in GJ? What would stop you?"
- **Walk-a-Mile Immersion:** one team member flies DEN→GJT on a winter weekend, does the GJ night and the drive, and documents it (also produces honest content).
- **What's on Your Radar?:** ask partners (Downtown GJ, Visit GJ, wineries) what's genuinely open and good Dec–Mar.
Findings stay PROJECT-LEVEL (framework §8).

Ski Aspen tomorrow."**: one page showing the 3-day journey (GJ evening → desert morning → soak → Aspen), the four audience versions, the collaborator ring, and the two decisions GJRASA owns: (1) which partners to approach, (2) whether a bundle is in scope for a later phase.
