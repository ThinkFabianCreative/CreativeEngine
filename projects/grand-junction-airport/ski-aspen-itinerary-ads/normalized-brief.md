## 4. Insights [DERIVED] (strategy improvement layer)

**4.1 The drive isn't the enemy. *The Denver drive* is.**
To skiers, "I-70 out of Denver" means Friday gridlock, chain laws, Vail Pass and the tunnel queue. It's the punchline of every Colorado ski trip, and Front Range skiers joke about it themselves. The GJT drive runs the other way, up the Colorado River through Glenwood Canyon, with no pass and no tunnel. *Same state, different drive.*

**4.2 People don't mind travel time. They mind dead time.**
Two hours of fighting traffic feels like lost holiday. Two hours with a hot-springs soak in the middle feels like holiday. The brief's own logic shows this: we can't win on minutes, so we win on what the minutes are made of. **The stops turn transit time into trip time.**

**4.3 Day one of a ski trip is the day nobody skis.**
Every Aspen itinerary writes off arrival day. GJT can give it back, as an extra day that isn't on the lift ticket: red rock, wine, a soak. "Ski Aspen rested" is the payoff, and "a day you didn't know you had" is the hook.

**4.4 Snow on red rock is visual property nobody else in ski country has.**
Every ski ad shows white peaks. Colorado National Monument under snow (red sandstone, white dusting) makes the trip look like it starts somewhere else entirely. It's an ownable first frame, and it's on the way.

**4.5 The real audience is already changing planes.** (Section 10, Q1)
Denver locals won't fly a 1-hour hop and then drive two more hours. The persuadable traveller is the **out-of-state skier** who connects through DEN anyway, or who has a direct route into GJT. For them it's a different last leg rather than an extra one, and that's a much easier sell. Targeting and the hook depend on it.

**4.5b Catchment context** [client/sources S4, Traffik RFP]: the program goal is GJT as airport of choice in a 200-mile catchment and reduced leakage. That makes this ski campaign an *inbound* add-on; origin-market targeting (Q1) still decides the hook.

**4.6 Next year makes this campaign strategic.** [RESEARCH] (internal only, never in ads)
Aspen/Pitkin County Airport is scheduled to close from about April to November 2027 for a roughly $575M rebuild (GJ Sentinel, May and July 2026). A skier who learns the GJT route this winter is already in the habit when Aspen's own airport goes dark. This campaign is the trial run.

## 5. USPs [DERIVED from SOURCE facts]

1. **The scenic way in.** Glenwood Canyon, the Colorado River and Mount Sopris, not the I-70 Front Range grind. *No pass, no tunnel.*
2. **The only ski gateway with a desert attached.** Red rock and Palisade wine country 20 minutes from the gate, and Aspen powder the same day.
3. **Built-in hot springs.** Glenwood Springs sits right on the route. The soak happens on the way, not as a separate day trip.
4. **Holiday from wheels-down.** Downtown Grand Junction dinner ~10 min from GJT, the Monument ~15–20 min, wine ~20 min. [SOURCE distances]
5. **An easy regional airport.** [NEEDS CLIENT PROOF] Small-airport ease (short walk, quick car pickup, simple parking) is an obvious benefit but isn't in R1. Use it only once the client confirms it.

## 7. Audience packets

Each packet: **one person, one truth, one itinerary, one feeling.**

### P1 — Millennial Girls' Trip
- **Truth:** The trip is the content. They want the bits between the skiing as much as the skiing: the toast, the photo, the story.
- **Itinerary:** Land GJT → Palisade tasting room / Peach Street Distillers → Glenwood hot-springs soak at dusk → Aspen après.
- **Hook idea:** *Our group chat planned stops.* / *Wine country → hot springs → Aspen. One day.*
- **Feeling:** The trip that starts with a toast, not a traffic jam.
- **Visual:** Steam, laughter, snow-dusted vines. Avoid lone-skier hero shots.

### P2 — Multigenerational Family
- **Truth:** Everyone runs out of patience in the car. Grandparents' knees, kids' moods. Long drives are where family trips go wrong.
- **Itinerary:** Land GJT → Riverfront Trail stretch or Glenwood Caverns gondola → Glenwood Hot Springs pool → Snowmass Base Village ice rink and s'mores.
- **Hook idea:** *Less "are we there yet." More "can we stop here again?"*
- **Feeling:** The drive becomes part of the holiday everyone remembers.
- **Visual:** Three generations in one steaming pool, with snow on the rim.

### P3 — Millennial Couple
- **Truth:** They want an adventure with a bit of earned luxury. A morning hike makes the evening dinner better.
- **Itinerary:** Land GJT → snowy Rim Rock Drive / Lunch Loops hike → Peach Street nightcap or downtown GJ dinner → Aspen Mountain via Silver Queen Gondola / Maroon Bells snowshoe.
- **Hook idea:** *Red rock at breakfast. Powder by lunch.* (Timing must be honest, so frame it as a two-day story if needed.)
- **Feeling:** Two landscapes, one trip, nobody else's itinerary.
- **Visual:** Snow on red sandstone, the hero frame (4.4).

### P4 — Boomer Couple
- **Truth:** They've done the I-70 grind for years and earned the right to skip it. Comfort is no longer a compromise; it's the plan.
- **Itinerary:** Land GJT → Palisade winter tasting → Yampah Vapor Caves → Carbondale coffee with Sopris views → a downtown Aspen dinner a short walk from the gondola.
- **Hook idea:** *You've earned the scenic route.* / *Arrive in Aspen like you've already been on holiday.*
- **Feeling:** Unhurried, warm, a little indulgent.
- **Visual:** Quiet tasting room, a steam cave, an evening glow on the peaks. Not "old people" clichés.
