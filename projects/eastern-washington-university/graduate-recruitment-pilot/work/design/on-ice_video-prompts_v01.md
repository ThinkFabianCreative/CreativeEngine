# On Ice: slow-motion video prompts (v01)

CLIENT: Eastern Washington University · PROJECT: graduate-recruitment-pilot · 2026-11-08
ANIMATIC / PITCH ONLY. The film is shot practically (real ice, high-speed camera such as a Phantom at 1,000 fps). AI clips show the idea on Nov 13.
Built from the 16:9 hero prompt + DesignChamp punch-ups (ice is the block itself below the thigh, cloudy and bubbly; show the break, not the windup).

## Shared look (paste at the end of every shot)
> Same woman in every shot: Mexican American, about 36, average build, dark hair tied back, navy cardigan over a work polo, lanyard with a blank badge. Black studio, one hard spotlight from high above-left, thin cool rim light from behind, low rolling cold mist, haze in the beam, visible breath. Ice is real block ice: cloudy white at the core, dense with trapped air bubbles and frost, clear only at fresh cracks; below mid-thigh she is encased in a tall narrow column whose base spreads into a frozen mound across the floor, no plinth. Sledgehammer with a deep crimson handle (#B7142E), plain steel head, the only color in the frame. Near-monochrome cool greys and blacks, true warm skin with pores and sweat, fine film grain. Shot on a high-speed cinema camera, true slow motion, 24 fps playback, cinematic, beautifully lit. No text, no logos, no music video glow, no blue fantasy, no sparkles.

## Edit v02: the misdirect (Andres, 2026-11-08)
We think we're watching an ice sculptor at work. Every shot hides that the block is around her. The wide Self Made Man frame is the climax: she is the sculpture, carving herself out. Then she breaks free and walks away.

| # | Time | Shot | What the audience thinks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–3s | Light up on ice | A sculpture studio |
| 2 | 3–6s | Hands, chisel, sledgehammer | An artist at work |
| 3 | 6–9s | Macro impacts | Craft, precision |
| 4 | 9–12s | Her face, grunts | Effort, focus |
| 5 | 12–15s | Climax: the wide Self Made Man reveal | She's carving herself free |
| 6 | 15–18s | The break | |
| 7 | 18–24s | She walks away | |

Framing rule for shots 1–4: never show her below the chest, never show where the ice meets her body, never show her feet. Keep the camera on the block, the tools and her face.

**Shot 1, light up (3s)**
> Total darkness. A hard spotlight slowly fades up from high above-left onto the rough, cloudy, bubbly surface of a large block of ice in a black studio; mist rolls across a frozen floor. Very slow macro push along the ice surface: trapped bubbles, frost, old chisel marks. It looks like a sculptor's block. No person visible. Sound: low room tone, ice creaking, a slow breath off-screen.

**Shot 2, the artist's tools (3s)**
> Close-up, slow motion: a woman's hand sets a steel chisel against the rough ice with care, like a sculptor; the edge of a navy cardigan sleeve in frame. Cut to the sledgehammer, crimson handle (#B7142E), lifting out of frame above. We see only hands, tools and ice, never her body or legs. Sound: the scrape of steel on ice, a measured inhale.

**Shot 3, macro impacts (3s)**
> Extreme macro, side-on, super slow motion: the steel sledgehammer head strikes the chisel; cracks race out through the trapped bubbles like lightning, a bloom of ice dust and shards hangs glowing in the spotlight, droplets spin through the air. Shallow depth of field. Repeat as a second, closer impact. Sound: a deep, echoing crack, glassy splintering.

**Shot 4, her face (3s)**
> Tight close-up on her face and shoulder in slow motion, framed from the chest up, as she swings again: a short effortful grunt, sweat and ice crystals flicking off her skin, hair loosening, eyes locked down on her work. Nothing below the chest. Sound: grunt, crack, her breath.

**Shot 5, CLIMAX: the reveal (3s)**
> The camera pulls back and drops low in one slow move to a wide, three-quarter front-left angle: composed exactly like Bobbie Carlyle's sculpture "Self Made Man". The block is a tall, narrow column of cloudy, bubbly ice, and she is part of it: free from mid-thigh up, her legs below are still solid ice, the chisel driven into the ice at her own thigh, the sledgehammer raised high overhead, crimson handle at the top of the swing, head bowed to the strike. She has been carving herself out of the ice all along. Extreme slow motion, ice dust hanging in the beam. Sound: everything drops to near silence, one heartbeat-slow breath.

**Shot 6, the break (3s)**
> Wide, low angle, extreme slow motion: the final strike lands; the column splits and large slabs peel away from her legs, falling and shattering on the frozen floor, mist and ice dust bursting outward through the spotlight. She pulls one leg free, then the other, and steps down off the broken mound. Sound: a huge crack, slabs hitting the floor, stretched and heavy.

**Shot 7, she walks away (6s)**
> Slow motion, camera low at floor level among the broken ice: she walks away from the shattered column toward the right of frame and out of the spotlight, sledgehammer hanging loose in one hand, crimson handle catching the last light, mist swirling around her legs, ice chips falling from her clothes. She does not look back. The broken column steams behind her. Sound: footsteps crunching on ice, one long exhale, silence.

**Shot 8, end card (24–27s, made in edit, not generated)**
Black. Line: *Stop waiting to be chosen.* EWU wordmark. Program CTA. The crack sound returns once under the line.

## Sound and VO v03: every excuse gets a slam (Andres, 2026-11-08)

Device: each excuse or rejection is heard, then answered by a sledgehammer strike. The voices are off-screen and unidentified: some are other people's, some are hers. No face, no boss, no office; it's the culture of waiting, not a villain. The misdirect holds: we hear the excuses but still think she's sculpting. At the reveal we understand that every strike was against the waiting.

Rhythm: voice → half-beat of silence → SLAM (crack, ice burst) → breath. The gaps shrink and the strikes get harder as the excuses stack. At the reveal: silence, no voice. On the break: her voice, once. End card: the line.
