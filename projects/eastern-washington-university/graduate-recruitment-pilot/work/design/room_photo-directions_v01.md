**The idea these directions serve (unchanged):** we never show the student; we show the people watching her work, and the prospect stands where she stands. Every direction below keeps the reverse shot, the 2:1 peer-to-faculty ratio, warm attention (never verdict), and the watchers' gaze landing just past the lens.

## 0. Rules common to all three directions

- **The reverse-shot camera height.** The camera sits where the student's eyes are: standing eye height, about 160–165 cm, for MS and MOT. For MFA the camera is on the stage, so it sits about 1 m above the front-row eyeline and looks gently down into the house. Nobody looks down at the viewer; seated watchers look slightly up.
- **Gaze.** Eyes just past the lens, to camera left or right, at the student's face. Into the lens reads as a portrait and a sales pitch; far off-axis loses the "me".
- **One light source, the student's side.** Faces are lit from the presenter's position (slide glow, stage spill, clinical overhead). That light is the second strongest cue after the gaze: it tells the viewer where the attention is coming from.
- **The red floor band is never generated.** GPT-Image renders brand reds off-hue and soft-edged. Every prompt asks for a real floor or stage edge running horizontally across the bottom 8–10% of the frame, empty of people and props. The band (#B7142E) is laid on that edge in Figma, cut to the line, so it reads as the floor you stand on and not a lower-third. If the edge in the render is tilted, crop or rotate before the band goes on; never float the band above a visible floor.
- **Room descriptions are text only.** Catalyst sim lab, Get Lit! and the MS room are described in words. No EWU photo is uploaded as a reference without EWU's permission. No EWU logo, signage or regalia in any prompt.
- **Casting (standing rules).** Specific descriptors, real ages, average and plus-size bodies, everyday clothes. Mexican, Afro-Latino, Black, white, East Asian, South Asian, Indigenous named specifically, never "diverse". MS: working adults 26–50. MFA: an audience that looks like Spokane's literary public, 20s–70s. MOT: cohort mostly 22–35, instructor older.
- **Program limits.** MS: panel + cohort only; no workplace guests until EWU confirms who attends. MFA: no location cues (Cheney vs Spokane OPEN). MOT: sim lab only, no patients; a manikin or a classmate playing the client, seen from behind or shoulder only; instructor at the side, never centre.
- **Anti-gloss block.** Every prompt carries: film stock or grade, visible grain, a named lens and aperture, imperfect skin (pores, under-eye, uneven tone), unretouched, no beauty lighting, no smiling at camera. Remove any adjective GPT adds in a rewrite ("vibrant", "stunning", "cinematic lighting").
- **Format.** 4:5 portrait, 1080×1350 for feed. Generate at 4:5 if offered, otherwise 2:3 and crop to 4:5 keeping the floor edge.
- **Labels.** Every comp is labelled "pitch comp, generated, stand-ins; to be shot in EWU's rooms". Referenced photographers and DPs are credited in the deck.

## Direction 1. FRONT ROW (documentary-candid)

**Principle.** We were there, at a real session, and someone was mid-sentence. The image is caught, not composed: the moment after a good point lands, when the room leans in. Truth over control.

**References**
- Larry Fink, *Social Graces* (1984): attention at events, faces close and unguarded, bodies tilted toward a point off-frame. We take the absorption, not his flash.
- Frederick Wiseman, *At Berkeley* (2013): observational coverage of rooms full of people listening; ordinary light, no performance for the camera.
- Bruce Davidson, *East 100th Street* (1970): dignity, closeness, real people in real interiors without decoration.
- Mark Steinmetz, *Paris in My Time* / Southern trilogy: soft available light, slightly imperfect framing, warmth without sentiment.

**Light.** Available only: the projector, the stage spill, the lab's ceiling panels. Underexposed backgrounds, faces at the edge of what the light reaches.
**Lens.** 35mm at f/2, handheld; occasionally 28mm when the room is small (MOT).
**Camera height.** Reverse-shot rule (§0), handheld, a degree or two off level is allowed.
**Stock / grade.** Kodak Portra 800 pushed one stop: warm mids, lifted blacks, visible grain, slightly muddy greens. No added contrast.
**Casting.** The least "cast" of the three: uneven attention (one person writing, one with chin on hand, one mid-sip), real clothes with creases, a backpack on a chair.
**Red band.** Lives on whatever floor the frame catches: carpet edge, the lab's vinyl, the stage lip. Because framing is loose, the floor line will vary card to card; that inconsistency is honest but weakens the band as a system.
**What it risks.** Thumb-size reading: loose framing scatters gaze, so "they are watching me" can blur into "a room of people". Generated "documentary" is the hardest look for GPT-Image to fake; it tends to come out too tidy, which is the exact tell the style forbids. Best direction for the real shoot; weakest for comps.

## Direction 2. THE REVERSE ANGLE (staged-cinematic, motivated light)

**Principle.** A single film frame: the reverse shot from the scene where she speaks. Everything real (the room, the people, the clothes), but the light, the gaze and the floor line are directed so that one idea reads in half a second. Staged to look unstaged.

**References**
- Bradford Young (DP), *Selma* (2014) and *Pariah* (2011): faces carved out of darkness by a single motivated source; rich, warm, underexposed skin tones on dark skin and light skin alike.
- Shabier Kirchner (DP), Steve McQueen's *Small Axe: Education* and *Lovers Rock* (2020): rooms of people seen from inside, 16mm texture, attention as the subject.
- Harris Savides (DP), *Elephant* (2003): institutional rooms and fluorescent light made beautiful without lying about them. Key reference for MOT.
- Philip-Lorca diCorcia, *Heads* (2001): ordinary faces caught in a placed light, absorbed and unaware of themselves.

**Light.** One key from the presenter's position, motivated by the room's real source (slide glow cool, stage spill warm, overhead clinical flat), plus a little bounce. Background falls a stop or two.
**Lens.** 40mm at f/2.8 (slightly tighter than 35mm, so the front three faces each hold about 10% of the frame width at thumb size; the 35mm in the visual v01 stays as the shoot's wide). Shallow depth: front row sharp, back rows soft.
**Camera height.** Reverse-shot rule (§0), locked off, level, as a dolly would hold it.
**Stock / grade.** Kodak Vision3 500T in look: tungsten-balanced, cool shadows, warm skin, fine-to-medium grain, soft highlight roll-off. MOT shifts toward Savides' greenish fluorescent neutrality. No teal-and-orange.
**Casting.** Faces chosen for warmth: two or three in the front row leaning in, one with a hand at the mouth, one about to ask. Everyone's gaze converges on one point just past camera.
**Red band.** Most controllable here: the floor or stage edge is a deliberate horizontal across the bottom 8–10%, empty, so the band reads as the place you stand in every card. This is the direction that can prove (or disprove) "the band is a floor" in the stranger test.
**What it risks.** Prettiness. Too much grade and it becomes a movie poster or a bank ad, and the campaign loses "this is real". Over-converged gaze can look eerie. GPT-Image may default to glossy faces; the anti-gloss block and the stock name are load-bearing.

## Direction 3. AUDIENCE (editorial-portrait tableau)

**Principle.** The watchers as a group portrait. Large-format stillness: everyone is sharp, everyone is present, the room is a portrait of a community paying attention. The dignity of the audience is the message.

**References**
- Thomas Struth, *Audiences* (2004) and *Museum Photographs* (1989–): people photographed while they look at something just beyond the camera. The closest precedent to The Room's grammar anywhere in photography.
- Dawoud Bey, *Class Pictures* (2002–2006): direct, respectful portraits of students; real bodies, real attention.
- Rineke Dijkstra, *Beach Portraits* and her school series: plain light, no flattery, a held moment.
- Alec Soth, *Sleeping by the Mississippi* (2004): large-format colour, ordinary American interiors with quiet weight.

**Light.** Soft, even, a large source from the presenter's side; little falloff, so every face reads.
**Lens.** 50mm-equivalent on 4×5 / medium format at f/8: deep focus, everyone sharp, no bokeh.
**Camera height.** Reverse-shot rule (§0); tripod, perfectly level, frontal.
**Stock / grade.** Kodak Ektar 100 / Portra 160 on large format: fine grain, accurate colour, high detail, gentle contrast.
**Casting.** Fewer, more distinct people (5–7), each a portrait in their own right; still, attentive, not performing.
**Red band.** The floor is very visible in frontal large-format compositions, so the band can sit cleanly; but the stillness makes it read as a graphic plinth under a picture.
**What it risks.** Stillness reads as a jury: rows of sharp, frontal, still faces is precisely the "panel judging" failure in the review. Warmth is hardest here. Also the most "art" and least "about to perform": the nerve of "Somebody's going to see this" goes quiet.

## 3. Recommendation: THE REVERSE ANGLE (Direction 2)

**Why.** The comps exist to pass one test: a stranger at thumb size reads "people are watching me" in under two seconds, and reads the red band as the floor. Direction 2 is the only one that controls all three cues that carry that reading: converging gaze, a single light from the viewer's side, and a clean empty floor edge for the band. Front Row is truer but scatters the gaze and gives GPT-Image its worst look to fake. Audience is beautiful but walks straight into the jury failure the review named. Direction 2's risk, prettiness, is fixable in the grade; the other two directions' risks are structural.

**For the real shoot**, carry Direction 2's rules (one motivated source, gaze point, floor line, 40mm) into a Direction 1 method: real sessions, minimal staging, a small lighting assist from the presenter's position. The comps then promise something the shoot can actually deliver.

**Stranger-test card set it implies (8–10 people, thumb size, two seconds, "what's happening and where are you?", then "what is the red stripe?")**
1. 2-MS, 2-MFA, 2-MOT with the red band on the floor line, no copy. The test.
2. The same three with the band removed. Tells us whether the band helps, hurts or is ignored.
3. One control: 1-MOT or 3-MS (whichever renders best). Checks whether the cinematic look is doing the work or the idea is.
4. Optional: one people-free fallback (empty room, chairs turned toward you, band, caption) once EWU permits real room plates. Not generated.

Pass: most say "people are watching me / I'm about to present". Band pass: some say "floor", "stage", "where I stand". If nobody reads floor, keep the band as branding and stop calling it the idea (review §4).
