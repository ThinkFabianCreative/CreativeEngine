## 1. Character bible: Waiting

Waiting is the nicest person she has ever dated, which is the problem. Waiting never raises its voice and never says no; it says "not yet", "next year", "next cycle", "you're fine where you are". It remembers her birthday and forgets her plans. It wants one thing: for nothing to change, because nothing changing feels like safety to Waiting, and it honestly believes it is protecting her. It talks in soft time words (soon, eventually, when things calm down, once you have a bit more experience), it agrees with every ambition and then schedules it for later, and it never argues, it just reassures. Its tells: it shows up at deadlines, it sounds like her own voice at 11 p.m., and it is most persuasive right after a good day. Waiting is not her boss, her employer, her family or any person. It is a habit with good manners. She doesn't hate it. She's just done.

**Voice rules for Waiting:** warm, lowercase when texting, patient to a fault, never cruel, never desperate, never sexual. It loses gracefully. It is never the punchline of a joke about her; the joke is always on the habit.
**Voice rules for her:** short, kind, final. She thanks Waiting. She never explains herself twice. She ends on a date.

Note: "you're doing fine" was in the brief; "doing" is on the banned list, so Waiting's catchphrase is "you're fine where you are". Andres to confirm.

## 2. The letters (final draft)

Format for all three: dated top-left, addressed "To: Waiting" (or its alias), signed by hand, closing line set as the sign-off: **Stop waiting to be chosen.** EWU lockup and program name below the signature.

### MS in Management (she keeps her job) · 118 words

[Date, e.g. October 26, 2026]

To: Waiting

I know how this sounds, so I'll be quick.

You've been good to me. You were there every review, every "next cycle", every night I priced a program and closed the tab. You kept me calm. You kept me ready. Ready for what, neither of us ever said.

I'm not leaving my job. I'm leaving you.

On [term start, EWU to confirm] I start the MS in Management at Eastern Washington University. By [defense window, EWU to confirm] I'll stand in a room and defend a portfolio of management work that is mine, out loud, in my own words.

Please don't call at the deadline. You always call at the deadline.

Stop waiting to be chosen.

Dana

### MFA in Creative Writing · 112 words

[Date]

To: Maybe Next Year

We've been together a long time. Since the first notebook, really.

You were sweet about it. "When you have more time." "When it's ready." "When you feel like a real writer." I believed you every January.

Here's the thing. I edit other people's pages all week and leave mine at the bottom of the pile. You liked it there. It was safe there.

On [term start, EWU to confirm] I start the MFA in Creative Writing at Eastern Washington University. Two years with my own draft on top.

You can keep the notebooks from before. I'm taking the one I'm on.

Stop waiting to be chosen.

Mara

### Master of Occupational Therapy (leaving her role, warmly) · 121 words

[Date]

To: Waiting

First, thank you. I mean it.

You and I had good years on that floor. I learned the rhythm of a shift, the names, how a hard morning turns around. Everyone said give it a few more years, and you agreed with everyone. You always do.

I'm leaving my role this summer. Not because it was wrong. Because I already know where I'm going next, and you were going to keep me here admiring it.

On [MOT cohort start, EWU to confirm] I start the Master of Occupational Therapy at Eastern Washington University. Fieldwork begins [first fieldwork date, EWU to confirm].

I'll miss the floor. I won't miss you.

Stop waiting to be chosen.

## 3. Waiting talks back (social)

Format: text-message screenshot in Waiting's grey bubble, her reply in the brand colour, end card "Stop waiting to be chosen." + program + date. Waiting is never blocked or insulted; she just answers.

1. **Waiting:** u up? we could talk about grad school next cycle
   **Her:** I start [date].
2. **Waiting:** saw the application deadline. want me to come over and make it feel far away?
   **Her:** Already submitted. Thanks though.
3. **Waiting:** no pressure. it'll still be there next year
   **Her:** So will you. That's the problem.
4. **Waiting:** remember how calm we were
   **Her:** I remember how calm. Not much else.
5. **Waiting:** you're fine where you are 🙂
   **Her:** I was fine for three years. Class is [day, date].
6. **Waiting:** maybe wait until things calm down at work?
   **Her:** Work's staying. You're not.
7. **Waiting:** i just don't want you to rush
   **Her:** Two years of notebooks. Nobody's rushing.
8. **Waiting:** ok. i'll be here if you change your mind
   **Her:** I know. That's sort of your whole thing.

(Line 7 is MFA-only; line 6 is MS-only. One emoji on Waiting's side maximum, never on hers.)

## 4. Print series

**Structure:** each headline is one sentence she says to Waiting. Layout is the letter itself, full-bleed paper on a plain field: date top-left as the first thing read, headline set as the letter's opening line, body is the rest of the letter (sections 2 above, trimmed to 70–90 words for print), sign-off "Stop waiting to be chosen." as the closing line in her hand, EWU lockup and program below. No photo of a second person, ever. The page is the breakup.

1. **MS:** "I'm not leaving my job. I'm leaving you."
2. **MFA:** "Dear Maybe Next Year, this is the year."
3. **MOT:** "Thank you for the good years. I start on [date]."

Body copy rule: opens warm (thanks Waiting), names one thing Waiting always said, states the dated EWU act with the program fact, closes with one dry line and the sign-off. Price and program detail sit in a small footer, not in her voice.

## 5. Email nurture: Waiting tries to win her back

Device: the sender stays EWU Graduate Studies (a sender named "Waiting" would read as spam). Each email quotes a message from Waiting at the top in grey, then her reply, then the practical content. The date never moves.

**Email 1 · after inquiry**
Subject: Waiting left you a message
Preview: "No rush. Really."
Body: Waiting's note ("Grad school sounds great. Maybe after the holidays?"). Her reply: "Applications close [date]. I'm putting it in the calendar." Then: program page, cost, application checklist. CTA: Put the date in your calendar.

**Email 2 · two weeks before deadline**
Subject: Waiting says next cycle is basically the same
Preview: It isn't. [Date] is.
Body: Waiting's note ("next cycle has more time, and you'd be more ready"). Her reply: "I've been more ready for two years." Then: what the application needs, how long it takes (EWU to confirm), who to ask. CTA: Start the application.

**Email 3 · deadline week**
Subject: One last message from Waiting
Preview: You can let this one go to voicemail.
Body: Waiting's note, gracious ("ok. I'll be here."). Her reply: "I know. I'll be in class [term start]." Then: deadline, start date, contact. CTA: Finish your application by [date].

Rule: Waiting always loses politely. No guilt, no "don't miss out" urgency language; the deadline itself is the pressure.

## 6. Risks

- **Breakup clichés.** "It's not you, it's me", "we need to talk", "I deserve better", "my glow-up", "on to bigger things". Banned. "We need to talk" and "it's not you, it's me" are the first things an AI or a junior will reach for; the letters avoid both. Watch "I'm moving on" too.
- **Toxic ex drift.** Waiting must never be controlling, manipulative, jealous or frightening. No "he wouldn't let me", no red flags, no "gaslighting". Once Waiting becomes abusive, the ad is about a bad relationship and reads as a domestic-abuse metaphor. Keep it kind; the joke is that it is too kind.
- **Reading as a person.** Waiting must not look like a man, a manager or a spouse. Never cast Waiting as an actor in a romantic frame. If film needs a figure, use an empty chair, an object or voice only (DesignChamp to decide). The MS letter's "I'm not leaving my job" protects the employer; keep it.
- **Bitterness.** Every letter opens with thanks. If a line could be read as revenge, cut it.
- **EWU as the new partner.** EWU is never the rebound or the one who "chooses" her. She ends on a date, not on being picked.
- **Banned words checked:** count(s) in, are you in, show your work, doing, hands-on, real world, new you. None appear in copy. (See note in section 1 on "doing fine".)
- **Claims checked:** no promotion, job, publication or licensure; MOT no patients.
- **Open:** live-ad check on "stop waiting" and breakup-letter formats in higher ed (strategy notes QUT "Wait No More", 2023, intake timing).

## 7. Add-on (Andres, 2026-10-26): Take a number / Now serving

The deli ticket machine is where Waiting lives: the paper ticket, the red digits, the number creeping up, waiting to be called. **Rule:** the payoff is never her number being called, because that is being chosen. She stops waiting for a number. Three payoffs are allowed: she leaves the ticket behind, the "Now serving" display shows her own start date, or Waiting talks like the machine and she doesn't answer it.

### Lines (4–6)

1. **Waiting (machine voice):** Please take a number. Your turn is coming. Next cycle.
2. **Waiting:** Now serving: someone else. Please continue to hold your ticket.
3. **Her:** I'm not waiting for my number. I picked a date.
4. **Her:** Kept the ticket for three years. Left it on the counter.
5. **Display (payoff):** NOW SERVING · [TERM START DATE]
6. **Waiting:** Your patience is appreciated. **Her:** It was. Past tense.

(Line 5 works as a headline: the display shows a date, never a number. A number would bring back the queue.)

### Print: the ticket clipped to the letter

Each letter in section 2 gets a torn paper ticket paper-clipped to its top corner, like a receipt attached to a resignation. The ticket shows a high number (e.g. #47) and the year she first took it ([year], placeholder), so the time she spent waiting shows up as an object, with no sentence needed. The tear runs through the number. Reading order: ticket (the wait), date (her act), headline, letter, sign-off. Optional MS variant: the ticket is clipped facing down, number unread. Never show a ticket printed "Now serving #47" with her name. She is not called, she has left. Production note for DesignChamp: a real thermal-paper ticket, photographed, not an illustration.

### Social sequence (6 frames, story or carousel)

1. Red display: NOW SERVING #44. Her hand holding ticket #47.
2. NOW SERVING #45. (Waiting VO, machine-polite: "Your turn is coming.")
3. NOW SERVING #46. The ticket is creased now.
4. NOW SERVING #47… Empty counter, ticket lying on it.
5. #47? … #47? Nobody comes forward.
6. The display changes: NOW SERVING [TERM START DATE]. Card: Stop waiting to be chosen. EWU + program.

Frame 5 is the joke and frame 6 is the turn: by the time the number comes up, she has already left. Copy never says where she went. The date says it.

### Risks for this device

- **The number gets called.** This is the trap Andres named. Any edit where she steps forward at #47 is dead. Check every cutdown: in a 6s version, frame 5 must not be cut so that #47 reads as her moment.
- **The deli queue as an admissions queue.** It must not suggest EWU has a waitlist or that EWU is the one calling numbers. The machine belongs to Waiting, not to EWU, and no EWU branding appears on the machine or the ticket.
- **Mocking the people in line.** Others in the queue, if shown at all, are out of focus and treated with dignity. The habit is the target, not the people waiting.
- **Cliché check:** "take a number" is a common idiom; it earns its place here because the payoff (a date instead of a number) reverses it. The live-ad check should add "now serving" to the search terms.
- **Humanizer (diagnostic):** the lines are short and in character. No device flagged. Banned words: none.
