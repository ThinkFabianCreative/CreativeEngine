CLIENT: Eastern Washington University · PROJECT: graduate-recruitment-pilot · BRAND: EWU graduate programs (MS in Management, MFA in Creative Writing, MOT) · SOURCE SCOPE: PROJECT-LEVEL, TRAFFIK proposal, not approved by EWU · Author: word-smither · 2026-10-26 · Mode: DEVELOP · Lead campaign (Andres, CD, 2026-10-26; clearances file). Supersedes nothing; "Breaking up with Waiting" (breakup-with-waiting_copy_v01.md) is now secondary.

Rules carried from the decision: the figure in the ice is her present self (no costume, no "new you"); she breaks herself out (nobody frees her, EWU included); shown mid-break; ice = waiting = cool grey/blue, never red; red = her move (glove, tool, date) and EWU. ## 1. The ice language

| Phrase | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **put on ice / on ice** | USE. The spine. | It is literally what happened to her ambition: postponed, kept, not killed. Business idiom, so it lands with working adults without a pun wink. Pair with a year ("on ice since 2022") to make the duration the sting. |
| **frozen** | USE, sparingly | Good for objects (her draft, her application, her plan). On a person it slides into "frozen with fear", which blames her. Freeze the thing, not her. |
| **on hold** | USE as the bridge | Plain-English cousin of "on ice"; works in email and search where the metaphor needs no decoding. Hold music is a nice secondary texture. |
| **freeze** (hiring freeze, budget freeze) | USE WITH CARE | Instantly true for MS audiences, but it points at an employer. Use only as weather she's been living in, never as a villain. One headline at most. |
| **thaw** | AVOID AS HER ACT | Thawing is passive; it is waiting for spring. Useful only as the contrast: "Thawing takes years. Breaking takes a date." |
| **melt** | STUNT ONLY | Same problem as thaw (time does it, not her). It is exactly why the OOH stunt works: the ice melts on its own while the copy asks how long hers has sat there. Melting is the wait; the break is her. |
| **break the ice** | CLICHÉ. BANNED in lines. | Means small talk at a party. Every brainstorm reaches it first; it turns her breakout into an icebreaker game. Allowed only in internal craft talk ("she breaks out of the ice", never "break the ice"). |
| **cold feet** | CLICHÉ, and wrong | Blames her nerves; the idea says she was postponed, not scared. Banned. |
| **in cold storage** | OPTIONAL | Fresh variant of "on ice" for the MFA (manuscript in cold storage). Use once, not as a system. |
| **chip away / crack** | USE as verbs of her act | Active, physical, hers. "Crack" carries the carousel. |
| **ice-cold, chill, cool it, frosty, Ice Ice Baby, Frozen/Let it go** | BANNED | Puns and pop-culture traps. "Let it go" especially. |

Rule of thumb: ice words describe the wait; her verbs (break, crack, start, sign, pick a date) describe the move. Never let an ice word do her move.

## 4. Print body copy (70–100 words)

### MS in Management · headline: Your career's been on ice since 2022. · 84 words
Nobody said no. That's the trick of it. They said next cycle, and you were reasonable about it, and the plan went in the freezer in good condition. It's still there. Your judgment, your ideas about how the place should run, your hunch that you'd be good at the bigger version of this job. Kept fresh, going nowhere. The MS in Management lets you keep your job and build a portfolio of management work you defend out loud. You start [term start].
**Stop waiting to be chosen.**

### MFA in Creative Writing · headline: Your novel's been on ice since 2019. · 83 words
You didn't abandon it. You put it somewhere cold and sensible while you fixed other people's sentences, which you are very good at. Forty pages, a title you still like, a chapter three that's been waiting for you longer than any editor ever made you wait. Writers aren't picked first and then allowed to write. They write. The MFA in Creative Writing at EWU gives you two years with your own draft on top of the pile. First workshop: [date].
**Stop waiting to be chosen.**

### MOT · headline: "A few more years." You've heard it for a few years. · 82 words
People mean well. Get more experience, they say, and you did, and they said it again. You already know what comes after the part you're allowed to do. You've watched it, you've anticipated it, you've kept it to yourself. That's been on ice long enough. EWU's Master of Occupational Therapy is built around 24 weeks of supervised fieldwork, in Spokane, with a cohort that starts on a date you can put in a calendar: [cohort start].
**Stop waiting to be chosen.**

Claims check: no promotion, job, publication, licensure or patients. MS = keeps job, defended portfolio (MNGT 591). MFA = draft, workshop; no publication, no award. MOT = supervised fieldwork as program fact; no practice outcome. Note: "you did" in MOT body is the verb "did", not the banned "doing".

## 7. OOH: real melting ice stunt

Setup: a clear block of ice in a public spot (Spokane, downtown or campus edge), a red EWU date sign frozen inside, visible but unreadable through the ice. It melts in public over the day. Copy on the plinth; the date is legible only once the ice is mostly gone. Crucial: melting is the wait. So the last frame is not the ice melting fully on its own. A person (student, consenting) walks up mid-afternoon and breaks the remaining block with a red-handled tool and takes the date out. The film is: hours of waiting, seconds of choosing.

Lines:
1. **This ice will be gone by 4 p.m. How long has yours been there?** ← BEST
2. Melting takes all day. Breaking takes a second.
3. Watch it wait.
4. On ice since 9 a.m. Some careers since 2019.
5. Plinth sign-off: Or skip the wait. [Term start]. Stop waiting to be chosen. · EWU

Production flags for DesignChamp/account: the date inside must be red; the block grey-blue lit, never warm. Weather and permit dependent; time the "gone by" hour from a test melt. If filmed, show a clock in frame.

## 8. Risks

- **"Break the ice."** The obvious headline and the wrong idiom (small talk). Banned from lines, OOH, social and subject lines; checked: none appear. Reviewers will propose it; hold the line.
- **"New you."** The figure is her present self in her own clothes; copy never says reborn, transformed, unleashed, emerge, the person you were meant to be. "Out of the freezer in good condition" is the counter: she was fine, only postponed.
- **Self Made (Bobbie Carlyle) cliché.** Ice + idiom + her present self are the difference. Avoid "carve", "sculpt yourself", "self-made" in copy.
- **Blaming a boss.** "Next cycle" and "a few more years" are phrases she's been told, with no speaker. "Hiring freeze" appears nowhere; keep it out of MS paid.
- **Blaming her.** Avoid "frozen" on her body, "cold feet", "fear". She was postponed, not scared.
- **Outcome claims.** No promotion, job, publication, licensure, funding. MOT: no patients in copy or frame; fieldwork is supervised and dated. MFA: draft, never book deal; teaching awards never framed as being chosen.
- **EWU as chooser / rescuer.** EWU never breaks the ice for her and never says "we chose you". The hub CTA lets her pick the date.
- **Thaw/melt as her act.** Passive. Kept to contrast and stunt only.
- **Year headlines.** "Since 2022" must not read as a statistic. Rotate years in testing.
- **Live-ad check (open):** "on ice" + graduate/career, "stop waiting", melting-ice stunts in higher ed and recruitment.
- **Banned words checked:** count(s) in, are you in, show your work, doing, hands-on, real world, new you. None in copy.
