## B. THE ROOM (replaces A)

Idea: we show the people watching her work, and the prospect stands where she stands. Rooms hold peers and the public, not only faculty. The student is mostly out of frame; the faces carry it.

Who is in the room, per program:
- **MS:** the defense panel plus her cohort in the seats behind it. (Public or employer guests at defenses: unconfirmed; do not show until EWU says yes.)
- **MFA:** the editors' table (peers) for the submission meeting; the public audience at a Get Lit! reading for the turn. Her work in the room is always her editorial work on another writer's piece, or her reading another writer's work she championed; never her own publication.
- **MOT:** the cohort of 32 and the supervising instructor at the edge of a Catalyst sim lab. No patients; classmate scenarios only.

### B1. Campaign line candidates (ranked)

| # | Line | Why | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Somebody's going to see this.** | Both a nerve and a promise; it is the room speaking. Works over every reverse shot. | Could read as a threat out of context; the faces must be leaning in, not frowning. |
| 2 | **Pick the room you want to impress.** | Hands the prospect agency: choosing a program is choosing an audience. Strong for the request-information job. | "Impress" is soft; test against #1. |
| 3 | **Do it in front of people.** | Plainest form of the core idea. | Flat on its own; strongest as an end-card line. |
| 4 | **Stand where she stands.** | The camera-position idea as a line; best on the hub. | Needs the image; weak in search or email. |
| 5 | The room is watching. Good. | Turns the fear into the want. | The period-break device; earned here, but it is a device. |
| 6 | Bring it to the room. | Invitation, short, works with any proof. | Generic out of context. |
| 7 | Better with witnesses. | Wry, ownable in tone. | Sounds legal/courtroom (the register killed with V2). |

Killed: "All eyes on you" (stage cliché), "Your audience awaits" (sales register), "Be seen" (any brand).

### B2. Headlines per program

**MS in Management**
1. A panel, your cohort, and the decision you make every week.
2. You already run the room. Now present to one.
3. They'll ask the hard question. You've answered it on the floor for two years.
4. Pick the room you want to impress: a defense panel.
5. No management experience required. Just the nerve to stand up in front of a panel.

**MFA in Creative Writing**
1. Five editors, one stack of submissions, and your case for the story at the bottom.
2. You already make other people's writing better. Do it at a table that argues back.
3. The room that hears the story you fought for.
4. Somebody's going to read this. You picked it.
5. No GRE. The first room that reads you is the admissions committee.

**Master of Occupational Therapy**
1. Thirty-one classmates are watching. So is your instructor.
2. You know what the patient needs next. Say it to a room that will check.
3. Before any clinic, a sim lab full of people who know the steps.
4. Somebody's going to see this transfer. Get it right in front of them.
5. Two and a half years, full-time, in rooms that watch you work.

### B7. Sign-off: "Before you graduate, you'll have…"

Fits The Room well: it names the rooms. Use on end cards and email footers, not as a headline.
- **MS:** Before you graduate, you'll have presented your work to a panel.
- **MFA:** Before you graduate, you'll have argued for other writers' work in a room of readers.
- **MOT:** Before you graduate, you'll have worked in front of your cohort, and in 24 weeks of supervised fieldwork.

Flag: these describe what the program contains, not what follows it. MFA wording avoids the selective Willow Springs roles; if "a room of readers" overstates workshop, fall back to "in workshop".

### C2. On the hub form

The badge becomes the request-information form. The red blank is the name field.

- Visual: badge with the old title struck through in grey; the red blank below it.
- Field label (visible, WCAG): **Your name**
- Placeholder inside the red blank: *first and last name*
- Fixed line under the field, set by the program hub: **MS in Management candidate** / **Notes by you** / **OT student, under supervision**. Copy beside it: "This line is what you'd be working toward. Nothing here applies yet." *(plain-language guard; trim if Graduate Studies prefers)*
- Button: **Request information**
- After submit: "Thanks, [first name]. The program will be in touch about what you'd do here and who you'd do it in front of."

### C3. Relation to The Room

C is the hub and badge layer for B: the room watches the work; the badge says what she is while she does it. The hub form's fixed credit line sits under the B hero. Final call is the director's.
