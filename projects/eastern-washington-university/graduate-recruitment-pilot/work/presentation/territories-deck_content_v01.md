## 2. The business problem (brief §2)
Good candidates put it off. They wait for a promotion, for their employer's backing or for "next year". Then they shortlist on cost and pick the cheapest, fastest online option, or nothing at all.
The job is to get working adults who are considering a master's to request information from an EWU program.

## 3. Who she is (brief §3, an invented portrait)
She's 33 and lives in Spokane Valley. For two years she's run the shift: she trains the new hires, fixes the schedule when it breaks, and she's the one the floor manager calls. Her title still says "lead". At her last review she heard "next cycle" again. She's priced master's programs twice and closed the tab both times, half waiting for someone at work to tap her.
- MFA: she already edits other people's work and keeps waiting to feel "real" enough to apply. On her desk: other people's manuscripts, with her own draft underneath.
- MOT: she's a rehab aide who knows what the patient needs next and keeps hearing she should get a few more years in first. EWU asks for 40 hours of experience, not years.

Her tension: waiting to be chosen, or moving herself.

## 4. The insight (brief §2)
**Capable working adults treat a master's as something they have to be chosen for**, whether by a boss, an employer's budget or the feeling of being ready. Doubt about cost becomes one more way of waiting.

What people say (public forums, not EWU prospects):
> "I'm having a hard time justifying the total investment."
> "i don't have enough leadership experience to be in the MBA program"
> "even considering a fully funded one feels kind of ridiculous to me lol."

The main barrier is doubt that the degree is worth the cost. The second is being told to wait, or not feeling ready. Agency reframes this at the brand stage, and price and real dates prove it at inquiry.

## 5. The strategic idea
**Stop waiting to be chosen.**
EWU gives the means: the program, the work she builds, a public price. She makes the move.
Her move, per program (brief §2):
- MS in Management: she stops waiting to be put in charge and builds a defended portfolio of management work that's hers. No management experience is required.
- MFA: she stops waiting to be picked as a writer and spends two years writing.
- MOT: she starts the path toward practicing, with 24 weeks of supervised fieldwork in Spokane.

Why only EWU can say it: we found no university using "stop waiting to be chosen". The closest is WGU's "They Said", which ends on study pace. Ours ends on a step she takes on a date she picks.

## 6. Creative rationale (how the four territories fit together)
All four territories dramatize the same move from waiting to starting. They differ in tone and in where the emotion comes from:

| | Territory | What it shows | Emotional lever | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Don't put your career on ice | The cost of waiting | Recognition, then release | Cinematic, physical, tense |
| 02 | Everyone has a story | Your life as the next chapter | Authorship, belonging | Whimsical, artistic, storybook |
| 03 | Don't undream your dreams | The future self you could become | Hope, possibility | Whimsical, dreamlike |
| 04 | Aspirational quotes | Words to move by | Encouragement | Typographic, shareable (TBD) |

These are territories, not finished executions. Each one shows how a single idea carries a prospect from the first ad to the inquiry.

## 7. The prospect journey (one system per territory)
Each territory runs through the same three stages. The look, the people and the images stay the same at every step, so the click lands on what the ad showed.

| Stage | Job | Where | What it carries |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Awareness | Stop the scroll; get EWU and the program noticed | Meta Reels, Stories, feed; Performance Max | The territory's idea, with the program named |
| 2. Education | Help her see the program, and herself in it | Immersive program landing page | Program-specific insights and facts, real student testimonials, what her first term looks like |
| 3. Conversion | Turn interest into a step with a date | Landing page, guide download, retargeting, email | Request info / download the program guide; a real start date; retargeting that continues the story |

## 8. Territory 01: Don't put your career on ice
The idea: We think we're watching an ice sculptor. Then she raises a red hammer and we see the truth: she's carving herself out of the block. Every "so sorry, maybe next cycle" she's heard turns to frost. The red tool is EWU, and the move is hers.

Insight: *The politeness is the ice.* Nobody is cruel. "So sorry," "you're so close," "just a few more years." Kind words freeze a career as well as unkind ones do. With no villain, there's no one to fight, so she has to break out herself.
From the brief: This is "next cycle" made visible, the exact phrase she heard at her last review.

The journey
- Awareness: a :30 film for each program (MS, MFA, MOT), with 6s and 15s cutdowns for Reels and Stories, and an ice-block campus stunt to earn attention. "For many reasons, you've put your career on ice for way too long." / "Not anymore."
- Education: the landing page opens on the moment she breaks free, then thaws into the program: what she'll build, how it works, and students who stopped waiting, in their own words.
- Conversion: "Stop waiting to be chosen." Request info or download the guide, next to a real start date. Retargeting picks up the red tool.

## 9. Territory 02: Everyone has a story
The idea: A storybook told in silhouettes and hand-drawn textures: a writer at her desk, a therapist walking with a patient, a manager with her own plan. Each program is a chapter, and the reader turns the page into her own.

Insight: *People wait for permission to start the next chapter as if someone else were writing the book.* The story is already theirs; they've just stopped turning the page.
From the brief: The MFA writer is the purest case: she edits everyone else's story, with her own draft underneath. Each program gets its own chapter object: her draft (MFA), her day-one fieldwork schedule (MOT), her portfolio (MS).

The journey (built on the Story page in Figma)
- Awareness: Meta Reels and Stories as book covers, one per program: "Every writer has a next chapter," "Turn compassion into your career," "Build your own business path." The CTA is "Turn the page."
- Education: an immersive, illustrated landing page that reads like a book, with parallax and page-turn transitions. Each chapter is one program truth. For the MFA: running the press, the workshops (10–15 writers), the book-length thesis, the visiting writers. Testimonials appear as student stories in the same illustrated style.
- Conversion: the last chapter is hers. Request info or download the guide to "start chapter one", next to a real start date.

## 10. Territory 03: Don't undream your dreams
The idea: an aspirational campaign that urges prospects to keep pursuing the career they've dreamed of. Real-world professionals with graduate degrees, shown in the self-made work life they wanted, rendered with dreamlike, ethereal aesthetics: the writer floating among clouds and open books, the therapist walking beside an older patient, the manager suspended over a city at night. Each frame takes her back to her childhood ambition to reach the top of her field.

Insight: *Nobody gives up a dream in one go. We undream it a little at a time.* Every "maybe later" shrinks it, until we've talked ourselves out of the person we meant to become. A master's is how you hold on to it.
From the brief: This is the cost barrier, felt from the inside. "Even considering a fully funded one feels kind of ridiculous to me." That's undreaming, in her own words.

The journey
- Awareness (Dream page in Figma): Reels, one per program, each under the headline "Don't undream your dream": "Get your MFA in Creative Writing in under two years," "Get your Masters in Occupational Therapy in under three years," "Get your Master of Science in Management degree completely online."
- Education: the landing page walks from today to the future self, one program step at a time: what she learns, what she builds, where she trains. Graduates speak as the "future self."
- Conversion: "Don't undream it." Request info or download the guide, next to a real start date.
