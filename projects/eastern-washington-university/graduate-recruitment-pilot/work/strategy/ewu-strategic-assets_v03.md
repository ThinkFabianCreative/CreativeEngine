# EWU strategic assets, graduate recruitment (v03)

CLIENT: Eastern Washington University · PROJECT: graduate-recruitment-pilot · BRAND: EWU graduate programs (MS in Management, MFA in Creative Writing, MOT) · SOURCE SCOPE: PROJECT-LEVEL, proposed by TRAFFIK, not approved by EWU · Author: creative-strategist · 2026-10-26 · Strategy loop, Round 1 (draft + red team). Supersedes v02 (kept).

Labels: "(from …)" is a sourced fact; "(our synthesis)" is our reading of evidence; "(assumption)" is a labeled proposal to confirm. Nothing here is client knowledge until EWU approves it. These are strategic assets, not creative: no lines, names or layouts.

**What changed from v02 and why.** New research shows CWU and WWU both claim "hands-on" learning, and WWU's MFA offers "teaching and editorial experience" (from competitor map, 2026-10-26). v02's handover idea is therefore closer to the pack than it looked, especially on the MFA. v03 keeps it as a candidate, adds three others, and recommends one whose tension the others do not claim.

## 1. Existing assets to respect

- **"The Region's Polytechnic"**: EWU's live positioning and campaign since September 2024 (idfive; anthem :30 on TV; Merit, 2026 Educational Advertising Awards). Proof device: "hands-on". (from our brand scan)
- **"ALL IN"** creed: Grit, Grace, Gratitude, Greatness. Red #B7142E and white, Swoop, "Eags", #Eagle4Life. (from our brand scan)
- **Mission and vision**: affordable academic excellence, applied learning in high-demand fields, upward social mobility; first-generation and low-income students named. (from EWU's RFP)
- **Known tension**: faculty call the polytechnic label "in name only" and fear for the humanities. (from our brand scan, RANGE Media)

**Fit.** Everything below sits under The Region's Polytechnic as its graduate proof; it never replaces the line. Because the MFA is a humanities program, a graduate idea that holds for writers as well as therapists helps answer the faculty tension. (assumption: EWU MarCom accepts a graduate story line under the master campaign)

## 2. Brand promise (3 candidates)

| | Promise | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 (v02) | EWU exists to hand people the real work of a profession, within reach of those who thought it wasn't for them. | Concrete; carries access. | "Real work" is now CWU and WWU territory. |
| P2 | EWU exists to turn the ability people already have into the standing to use it, at a public price within reach. | Starts from the person's capability, not a gap; links to mobility in EWU's vision. | "Standing" is abstract; needs proof to land. |
| P3 | EWU exists so the region's work is done by people from the region. | Strong fit with "Region's Polytechnic". | Institutional, not personal; weak for the national MFA. |

**Recommend P2.** It is the only one that respects the audience as already capable (no deficit), and it holds for all three programs: the manager without the title, the writer without the publication, the aide without the license. (assumption, built from EWU's vision and voice-of-customer)

**Recommend C2.** It passes all seven tests and holds against six of seven competitors; the seventh, WGU, is answered by the DNA: EWU's proof is real work judged by people in the field (a defense, a magazine, supervised patients), not a course exam. It keeps v02's proof objects as evidence while moving the subject from the institution to the person's tension, which CWU and WWU do not claim. Doubt is never the subject; capability is. C1 is the fallback if EWU prefers an institution-led idea, but it needs the named EWU objects in every piece to survive CWU and WWU. (our synthesis)

**Rule the idea carries:** every story rests on a real, checkable EWU proof (the defense, *Willow Springs*, supervised fieldwork) and implies no licensure, funding or job guarantee. MOT students work under supervision; graduating does not confer licensure.

## 5. Creative target

*Invented portrait, drawn from the broad target, to make the tension human. Not a real person and not a persona for media.* (assumption)

She is 33 and lives in Spokane Valley. For two years she has run the shift: she trains the new hires, fixes the schedule when it breaks, and is the one the floor manager calls. Her title still says "lead". She writes on Sunday mornings and has never shown anyone. She is not short of ability; she is short of anything on paper that says so. She has priced master's programs twice and closed the tab both times, not because she doubts herself but because she will not spend two years and real money on letters after her name that change nothing on Monday. Proof she could point to would change that.

**Why this portrait.** The tension (capable now, unrecognized now) explains the behavior in the JTBD (delay, or the cheapest online box-tick), and it describes her by what she has, not what she lacks. The same tension holds for the aide who wants to treat patients and the teacher who writes. (our synthesis)

## 6. The one cross-program Job to Be Done

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Job** | PERSUADE working adults considering a master's TO request information from an EWU program, then start the application this cycle. |
| **Target** | The broad target (§3); the pilot's paid reach is its active slice. (assumption) |
| **Behavior to change** | They put it off, or pick the cheapest, fastest online option as a box to tick. (our synthesis, from category voices) |
| **Insight (our synthesis, not a quote)** | I can already do more than my title says. I'll only pay for a degree that makes that count. |
| **Voices behind it (category voices, public forums, not EWU prospects; small sample)** | "I'm having a hard time justifying the total investment." (r/ESCP) · "has the masters made getting promoted/a new job that much easier?" (r/managers) · "Only apply to funded programs." (GradCafe) · "I would never advise someone to go to OT school for more than $50k debt." (r/OccupationalTherapy) |
| **New belief** | At EWU I'd prove what I can do on real work, judged by people in my field, at a price I can defend. (fits P2) |
| **New behavior** | Request information this cycle; start the application rather than wait a year. |
| **Barrier type** | **Primary: credential**, as value: doubt the degree will change their standing for the cost. Answered by functional benefit and DNA proof. **Secondary: aspirational** ("not for someone like me"), carried inside stories, never the subject. **HYPOTHESIS** until EWU prospect voice exists. |

**Per-program barrier lines**
- **MS in Management:** will an MS count against an MBA, and against cheaper, faster online options such as WGU? Answer: the oral defense of an applied project.
- **MFA in Creative Writing:** funding comes first, and WWU offers editorial and teaching experience too. Answer: *Willow Springs* work and the chance to compete for one of 7–10 funded TA spots. **Selective, not universal.**
- **MOT:** debt, distance and master's versus doctorate. Answer: supervised Level II fieldwork, Spokane classes, public tuition. No licensure claim.

## 8. Go-to-market outline

Across EWU's four journey stages (from field guide): **Brand** rides inside program paid social and video, since the proposal excludes institutional brand-awareness spend; its job is to make the tension and the proof recognizable to people not yet shopping. **Inquiry**: paid search and social send people to the three program hubs, where the six guides are gated to capture the inquiry. **Application**: email nurture moves inquirers to Application Started, where TRAFFIK's nurture ends (from contract 3.F.4). **Yield**: paid retargeting only, toward admitted and in-progress applicants. Every channel carries the same idea, with its proof shifting from emotional (Brand) to practical (cost, deadlines, requirements at Application). Creative is produced incrementally, starting with what Inquiry needs. (our synthesis, from contract and field guide)

## 9. Measurement priorities

1. Enrollments and return on ad spend (from RFP and proposal hierarchy).
2. Applications started and submitted, per program (Slate; OTCAS for MOT, in-progress activity untracked: from field guide).
3. Cost per qualified inquiry and hub-to-inquiry conversion.
4. Guide-to-application rate: does the proof content move people?
5. Test-and-learn: proof-led versus cost-led messages on the hubs; the tension in paid social against category-standard ads. (assumption: test design set with EWU)
