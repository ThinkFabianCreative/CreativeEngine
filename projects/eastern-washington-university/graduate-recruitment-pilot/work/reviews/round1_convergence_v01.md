## 3. Selected for DEVELOP

### 1. The Red Mark (ADVANCE, lead)
**Idea:** every field has a mark that means "this holds up", and at EWU it lands on your work, in red, in front of the people who make it.
**Why it wins:** it owns Eagle Red as an act rather than a colour, which is the one asset no competitor in the swap can borrow; it gives all three programs one system with three different marks; it runs today on the Ownable fallback, and gets better with every real artifact.
**Weakest point:** it can collapse into a beautiful type-and-paper system with no person and no room, which loses "in front of others" and slides toward the type-only manifesto cliché (4/29).
**DEVELOP must solve:** (1) put the room into every execution, at least one carousel card and the hub; (2) find who makes the mark per program, credited by role and field, without the institution becoming the judge; (3) prove the mark reads without explanation on card one at thumb size; (4) the MFA mark must sit on another writer's work, never her own; (5) write the line family (WordSmither), avoiding "show your work" as the headline.

### 2. The Room (ADVANCE)
**Idea:** we never show the student; we show the people watching her work, and the prospect stands where she stands.
**Why it wins:** it is the most direct expression of the core idea, and it solves the brief's named weakness: MOT gets the strongest room (the cohort of 32 and the instructor in the Catalyst sim lab). The reverse shot breaks the smiling-portrait cliché by construction.
**Weakest point:** ownability depends entirely on real EWU rooms; on generated pitch imagery it looks like any school's panel, and the watchers risk reading as judges.
**DEVELOP must solve:** (1) peers and public in the room, not only faculty; (2) a specific EWU room per program that no swap school can claim; (3) the Clock (duration) and Second Reader (you watch, then you're watched) tested as devices; (4) a credible pitch comp before access, which means locking the sim-lab consent path early.

### 3. Before the Title (ADVANCE)
**Idea:** the work is already finished and real; only the title is missing, so we print the work and leave the title as a red blank.
**Why it wins:** it dramatizes the gap exactly as the strategy states it, it is the creative target's own life (Shift Lead, Uncredited, Rehab Aide), it needs nothing from EWU to pitch, and the hub turns the device into the conversion (the red blank is the request-information name field).
**Weakest point:** nobody watches; and if the blank fills with a job title it becomes the outcome-proof cliché and a licensure risk for MOT.
**DEVELOP must solve:** (1) the blank fills with a program credit (editor, consultant on the applied project, OT student), never a job; (2) bring the room in by the carousel's third card; (3) test whether it is a sibling of the Red Mark (same red act) or a separate campaign; it may end as the hub and guide system inside A.
