## Verdict: ADVANCE

Not BET THE PITCH yet. The idea is right and the craft thinking is strong, but the bet depends on three things that do not exist yet: a stranger reading card one correctly without copy, comps on real EWU rooms, and confirmed access (MS room and attendees above all). The convergence set the same gate; nothing has passed it. Ready for the bet once the to-do list below clears.

## 1. Core idea and the one job, across MS / MFA / MOT

- **Core idea** ("show what they can do, in front of others, before their field counts them in"): The Room is the most literal carrier in the department. The reverse shot makes "in front of others" the image itself, and casting the viewer as the watched person is a real idea, not a style. The concept sentence holds: we show the watchers; the prospect stands where she stands.
- **"Before their field counts them in"** is carried weakly by the image alone. It comes through the role-credit captions and Before the Title's badge layer. That is the right division of labour, and it is why C belongs inside B.
- **One job (request information):** solved structurally. The red band where you stand holds the form on the hub; card 5 is the red end card; C's red blank becomes the name field. The hub is the best conversion idea on the table.
- **By program:** MOT strongest (the cohort in the Catalyst sim lab; the brief's named weakness becomes the lead). MFA strong and specific (Get Lit!, Willow Springs), carried by the Second Reader turn. **MS is the weak leg**: a presentation room is generic, and the only thing that makes it EWU's (who attends) is unconfirmed. See §2 and §6.

## 3. Ownability (swap test)

| Competitor | Could they run it unchanged? |
|---|---|
| Sullivan "School of Doing" | The generic reverse shot, yes; it is a doing-in-front-of-people idea next door. With named EWU rooms, role captions and the red floor band, no. Avoid any "doing" verb in lines. |
| CWU, WWU | They have rooms too. Ownability rests entirely on the named rooms and the captions. MS is swappable today. |
| WGU "They Said" | No: an online school has no room. This is the sharpest differentiation in the set; keep it implicit, never a dig. |
| Fayetteville State "Are You In?" | No, as long as "count(s) in" and "are you in" stay off the ads (kept). |

Against the Bar: **Ownable is reachable** with real rooms; on generated or generic imagery it fails. **Fresh** comes from the reverse shot, which the category reference does not show (the scan is portraits, campuses, manifestos). Conclusion: the idea is ownable in its rooms and captions, not in its line. Do not let the pitch rely on the line for ownership.

## 4. The red floor band

Keep it, with one condition. As drafted it risks being the most common layout move in advertising: a brand-colour bar along the bottom carrying copy. Read cold, it is a lower-third, not a floor. It becomes the device only if it is **motivated by the photograph**: the band must sit on the real floor or stage edge in each frame (the stage lip at Get Lit!, the sim-lab floor line, the front of the MS room), so it reads as the place where you stand. On the hub, where the form literally sits "on the spot where you would stand", it works. Also: one red device only. C's red blank must live inside the band (hub field, guide cover), never as a second red element competing in the same frame. Test the band at thumb size in the stranger test: if nobody reads "floor/stage", it is still useful branding, but stop calling it the idea.

## 5. The top line: **"Somebody's going to see this."**

Chosen. Why:
- It is the room speaking, so it works over every reverse shot, in every program, without changing a word. That is the campaign-line job.
- It holds the exact tension of the core idea: a nerve and a promise at once. The prospect feels the watchers before she reads the program.
- It does not name a judge. "Pick the room you want to impress" makes the audience something to win approval from (the grading register that killed the Red Mark), and "impress" is soft. It does have agency and suits the request-information moment: keep it as an MFA hub or email line, not the platform.
- "Do it in front of people" is the plainest statement of the idea and flat as a headline. Use it as an end-card or guide line if needed.

Conditions: the faces must be warm, absorbed, leaning in, never frowning, or the line reads as a threat. And the line is generic out of context, so it never runs without the room (search and text-only placements need program-specific lines instead).

## 6. Per-program headlines

**MS**
- Keep #2 "You already run the room. Now present to one." Best MS line: it carries the creative target and the room. Check that "run the room" does not imply a management title; it shouldn't.
- Cut #3 "…answered it on the floor for two years": it invents an experience requirement against "no management experience required".
- Rework #1, #4, #5: all put "a panel" at the centre. Show cohort and (once confirmed) guests; the panel is one presence among them.
- Defense wording: never "only" or "in person" (respected). Do not describe who attends beyond the panel until EWU confirms.

**MFA**
- Too long for card one: #1 (13 words) and #2. Cards need 6 words or fewer over the image. Move #1 to carousel card 2 or body.
- Keep #3 "The room that hears the story you fought for." and #4 "Somebody's going to read this. You picked it." #4 is the best MFA headline: the platform line turned for her editorial role, on another writer's work.
- Cut #5 framing (admissions committee as the first room). Keep "No GRE" as a fact on card 5.
- "Five editors", "Monday's submission meeting": placeholders only until we see the real meeting. "Student editor, Willow Springs" applies only to students who hold the role.

**MOT**
- **"Thirty-one classmates are watching. So is your instructor."** Do not run as written, and do not use as the hub hero. Risks: (a) it assumes the cohort of 32 is a fixed current number and that she is one of them; the brief sources "a cohort of 32" from the program page, so it is citable as cohort size, not as a headcount in a room; (b) it implies the whole cohort watches every sim session, which is unverified and probably untrue (sim sessions are likely small groups); (c) DesignChamp's caption says "14 classmates", so the work already contradicts itself. Fix: "Your cohort is watching. So is your instructor." or use the real number from the photographed session in the caption only. Carousel card 2 "Thirty-one classmates. One instructor." and email "Thirty-one classmates, one sim lab" take the same fix. "Joining the 32" (O1 guide) is acceptable as cohort size if EWU signs off the wording.
- #2 "You know what the patient needs next": no patients in sim. Change to "client" or "the person in the bed"; keep it a classmate scenario.
- Keep #3 and #4. #5 fine.
- Keep supervision and "OT student" everywhere; licensure figures stay copy-only, in EWU wording (96/95 vs 82 divergence OPEN).

## 7. Claim risks (summary)

1. MOT "thirty-one classmates" (above). Highest-frequency risk; it appears in five places.
2. MS defense attendees: colleagues and public unconfirmed; DesignChamp's MS room and caption ("two colleagues from work") must not appear in comps shown as fact. Label stand-in comps as such.
3. MS "applied business project presentation" room vs "portfolio with oral defense": confirm these are the same event before captions name it.
4. MFA: funding stays "selective teaching assistantships"; no own-publication; no location cues (OPEN divergence).
5. Durations (Clock): placeholders only.
6. MOT access: DesignChamp says clinical partner permission is not needed for the sim lab; the brief lists the clinical partner as the top MOT ask (for fieldwork). Both can be true; confirm with the program director, and we do not shoot fieldwork sites.
7. C's hub form: "This line is what you'd be working toward. Nothing here applies yet." keep the guard; let Graduate Studies trim.

## 8. Before the Title inside The Room

Agree with DesignChamp's call: hub and guide system, not a sibling campaign. It adds the "before the title" half of the core idea and the conversion. Rules: fills with a program credit only; MOT always "OT student, under supervision"; MFA "Notes by [Name]" is the best credit in the set. Badge static allowed as a performance test variant under B's look. Guide titles: use "Who's at the table", "Getting to the room", "Who watches you learn", "Joining the 32" (with sign-off); replace "What the panel will ask" and "The first room that reads you".

## 9. Before client presentation (required for BET THE PITCH)

1. **Comps:** three card-one comps (MS, MFA, MOT) on real EWU plates with our stand-ins, labeled "pitch comp, stand-ins", red band aligned to the real floor line. Plus the people-free fallback for each.
2. **Stranger test:** 8–10 people outside the project, thumb size, two seconds, "what's happening and where are you?". Pass: "people are watching me / I'm about to present". Also ask what the red band is.
3. **Access asks** to EWU with owner and date at kickoff: MS defense room, date and who may attend; Catalyst sim-lab session and consent path; Get Lit! 2027 rights or archive; Willow Springs submission meeting format; MOT cohort wording and outcomes wording.
4. **Copy rework** (WordSmither): fixes in §6; align carousel to DesignChamp's card order; platform line "Somebody's going to see this."
5. **Hub prototype** (DesignChamp + WordSmither): band-as-floor with the red-blank name field, one program (MOT first).
6. **Defense build** (selling-the-work, later): why the line stays generic on purpose and the rooms carry ownership; what we fall back to if MS access fails.
