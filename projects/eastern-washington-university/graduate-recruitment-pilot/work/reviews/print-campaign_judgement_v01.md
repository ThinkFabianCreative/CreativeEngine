## The print test

Andres's frame: ADLOB. Visual + headline + logo + body. A series of three (MS, MFA, MOT) that each stand alone on a page or poster. No carousel, no motion, no card two to explain card one.

That changes the field. Most of the chair work was built as carousels: the idea arrives on card 3. In print the whole idea must be in one frame, and the headline must add the meaning the picture can't, not repeat it (Whipple: don't say what you show; Barry: 1+1=3, and if it needs explaining, it's not done). Calibration: "Lemon" (one word turns a perfect car into a confession), "Think Small" (the white space is the argument), The Economist (the headline is the whole ad, the reader completes it), Harvey Nichols (the visual is the joke, the line lands it). Each one leaves the reader a gap to close. That's the test below.

Constant across all: real EWU rooms and consenting students; red floor band (Eagle Red #B7142E) as the one red device; no regalia, campus exterior or smiling-at-lens portrait.

## Why Your Title Can Watch is the one

- **The reversal is the brief.** The category sells the title at the end ("become a manager", "become a therapist"), which v19 forbids anyway. This puts the title in the audience. She's up front without it. That is the brand promise in one picture (the proof comes before the field counts you), and it can't drift into a promotion claim, because the title doesn't move.
- **The reader is in the ad.** The camera is where she stands. The line speaks to the viewer. Like The Economist, the reader closes the gap: "that's my chair, and I'm not in it."
- **It keeps the current job dignified.** The title isn't crossed out, mocked or left behind. It just watches. Prospects keep their jobs while they study; this says so without saying so.
- **SLIP IT:** Simple (one wrong chair), Logical (tape label is how offices mark property), Inevitable once you see it, Personal (her title, in marker). Passes.
- **Series discipline:** one shot, one line, one variable (chair + tape). Three pages side by side read like "Lemon" next to "Think Small": same voice, new fact.
- **Risks, stated once:** empty chair as grief code (fix: the chair is visibly the wrong type for the row, a stool among seminar chairs, and someone in the next seat is smiling toward the front); tape titles must be real titles consenting students hold, written in their hand.

Andres's "Take your seat" earns a place as the last line of body copy: she stood, now she takes a seat among peers. It's honest there and keeps the warmth he liked.

## Claim checks (both campaigns)

- No promotion, job, title change, publication, licensure or funding implied. The title stays put in campaign 1, which is the point.
- MFA: "selective teaching assistantships", never "funded"; she reads another writer's work.
- MOT: "OT student, under supervision"; classmate in the client role; no "patient", no licensure; fieldwork location unstated.
- MS: audience is cohort and faculty until EWU confirms defense attendees. Body says "a room of people from every kind of job" only if the cohort mix is real; otherwise "your cohort".
- No "only", no cost totals or comparisons. Banned words scanned: no "count(s) in", "are you in", "show your work", "doing", "hands-on", "real world".
- Every taped title must belong to the consenting student in the shot.
- "Then you take your seat with them" is earned (after the work, among peers), not a reserved place. Keep it out of any placement with deadlines or cohort size.
