## The problem
Good candidates for EWU's graduate programs rule themselves out before EWU ever hears from them. They shortlist on cost, funding or whether the degree counts, see EWU as the regional default, and inquire elsewhere or wait a year.

EWU wants leads that arrive on their own, are worth the staff's time, and that staff can handle without us.

## Who we're talking to
People who could do the work but have decided not to apply: early-career, career-changing or finishing a degree, mostly in the Inland Northwest. The MFA also draws writers nationally.

## The human truth
People rule themselves out of the good option before anyone else does. Too junior, not good enough, can't afford it.

## Where this sits in EWU's brand
EWU calls itself the Region's Polytechnic: a place where students turn classroom knowledge into real-world impact, through hands-on learning rooted in the liberal arts. Our core idea expresses that position; the phrase itself stays out of campaign copy.

## The core idea
Stories where the real work is handed to people who didn't think it was meant for them.

**Brand promise (proposed):** EWU exists to put affordable academic excellence and applied work in high-demand fields within reach of people who'd ruled themselves out.

EWU talks about upward mobility; we show it through the work. Every piece shows a real job a student does at EWU and one fact about access. If it doesn't, it doesn't run.

The visual lead is the proof object: something a student makes or holds before graduating. People are shown doing the job, never being chosen for it.

## The programs

### MS in Management
**Who and their doubt.** Early-career professionals, career changers and new managers. They feel too junior for an MBA, suspect anything else is second-best, and wait.

**The line:** "Earn the master's you can defend, before you're a manager."

**Why only EWU.** Students defend a real business solution to faculty in a final oral defense, before anyone has called them a manager. *Needs EWU sign-off.*

**Proof.** No management experience required. An applied project built on your own work. Fully online, 37 quarter credits (proposed).

### MFA in Creative Writing (fiction, poetry)
**Who and their doubt.** Writers and poets, including strong ones who think they won't get in. They apply only to funded programs, yet want a community that improves their work.

**The line:** "Edit a literary magazine on the strength of your pages, not your test scores."

**Why only EWU.** Four paid second-year roles exist only here: Managing Editor of Willow Springs, Managing Editor of Willow Springs Books, Program Assistant for the Get Lit! festival, and Student Director of Writers in the Community.

**Proof.** Admission rests on your writing sample, with no GRE. Almost all classes are at EWU Spokane. Workshops of 10 to 15 and a book-length thesis. Seven to ten teaching assistantships a year: full tuition waiver, a stipend of about $9,000 to $10,000, one composition course a term. *Needs EWU sign-off.* A possible alumna voice: Lauren Hohle, class of 2017, now Managing Editor of The Gettysburg Review, only if she agrees.

### Master of Occupational Therapy
**Who and their doubt.** Inland Northwest pre-OT graduates and career changers who meet the prerequisites. They assume they need a doctorate, see programs they can't afford, and stall or move away from family.

**The line:** "24+ weeks treating patients under supervision, with classes in Spokane."

**Why only EWU.** The public master's route to OT in Spokane. *Needs EWU sign-off.*

**Proof.** Two and a half years, full-time and in person, with classes in Spokane's Catalyst Building. A cohort of 32. At least 24 weeks treating patients under supervision, with a focus on placement in rural communities. 96% graduation and 95% national board pass rate. We never imply fieldwork is in Spokane.

## By stage
- **Inquiry:** "Is this for me, can I afford it, why EWU?" Guide, then request for information.
- **Application:** "Can I get in?" Requirements and application.
- **Yield:** "Is it worth it?" Through to deposit.

## How we'll know it worked
Enrollments first, then applications, then cost per inquiry. Targets to agree with EWU.

## Tone
Warm, plain-spoken, concrete and credible, with a little Inland Northwest in it. EWU's own social voice is warm and proud: "Eags," and posts celebrating named students. Ours sits comfortably beside it, but more specific. No guarantees or clichés. If Gonzaga, Whitworth, UW or a national online program could run it unchanged, it fails.
