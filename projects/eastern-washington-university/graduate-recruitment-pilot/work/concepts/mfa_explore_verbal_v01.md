# MFA in Creative Writing: verbal territories, EXPLORE v01

CLIENT: Eastern Washington University · PROJECT: graduate-recruitment-pilot · JOB: MFA in Creative Writing · MODE: EXPLORE (no verdicts, no scores) · AUTHOR: word-smither · 2026-10-25
Sources: work/brief/creative-brief_v04.md (MFA job), work/research/mfa-alumni-and-faculty.md, work/research/voice-of-customer.md, work/reviews/2026-10-25_clearances.md.

Guardrails carried into every territory:
- Proposition: you don't only write the book; you learn to edit, publish and put it on a stage (Willow Springs magazine, Willow Springs Books, Get Lit!, Writers in the Community).
- Funding: teaching awards exist and are selective (7–10 a year). Never "funded."
- Alumni: Shann Ray, Leyna Krow, Kate Peterson (cleared names; personal consent is a production step). Jess Walter is EWU BA '87 / 2026 honorary doctorate, never an MFA grad. Laura Read is faculty.
- Deadline: early March 2027, unpublished. Lines say "apply by March" only once confirmed.

---

## 1. The Masterclass, Inverted (Andres's territory)
**Idea:** EWU writers teach one real lesson on camera, the way MasterClass does, except each lesson is something you only learn by running a magazine, a press or a festival.
**Why it's true:** The format turns alumni into proof of craft, and the lesson topics are the practica themselves, so the USP is the curriculum, not a voiceover.
**Rough lines / scene:**
- Leyna Krow, "Lesson 3: What the slush pile taught me about my own first page." Ends: *This class is two years long. It's in Cheney.*
- Kate Peterson, "How to put a poet on a stage in front of 500 people. (I run the festival.)"
- Shann Ray, "Seventeen books. Lesson one: learn to be edited."
- Laura Read (faculty) in a workshop-of-12 cutaway: *MasterClass is a video. This is a room.*

## 2. The Other Side of the Desk
**Idea:** Every MFA applicant has been rejected by a magazine; here you become the person reading the submissions.
**Why it's true:** Willow Springs is an editing practicum; it reframes the "am I good enough" barrier as power and insight.
**Rough lines:**
- *You've gotten the form rejection. Come write it.*
- *Learn why the slush pile says no. Then stop getting it.*
- Scene: a nervous applicant's submission email, cut to a grad student at Willow Springs opening it.

## 3. Acknowledgments Page
**Idea:** Mock up the acknowledgments page of a future debut, thanking the magazine, the press, the festival and the workshop that made it.
**Why it's true:** The book you will write already owes its existence to EWU's institutions; Pricken: context shift (ad as book matter).
**Rough lines:**
- *"Thanks to the editors at Willow Springs, who taught me to cut, and to the Get Lit! crowd who heard chapter four first."*
- Headline: *Write the book. We'll be in the acknowledgments.*

## 4. Red Pen, Microphone, Spine
**Idea:** Three objects, three verbs: edit, publish, perform. The proposition as a still life.
**Why it's true:** It is the "Say this" line compressed into props; rough visual-verbal fusion for DesignChamp.
**Rough lines:**
- *Edit. Publish. Read it out loud.*
- *Most MFAs give you a workshop. This one gives you three more rooms.*

## 5. The Honest Funding Ad
**Idea:** Lead with the thing the forums say first, and answer it plainly.
**Why it's true:** Brief says meet funding head-on; uses VoC language without promising more than "selective."
**Rough lines:**
- *"Only apply to funded programs." Fair. Here's exactly what we have: 7–10 teaching awards a year, competitive. And a magazine, a press and a festival for everyone.*
- *We won't tell you it's free. We'll tell you what you get.*
- Note: "7–10" needs EWU sign-off before use.

## 6. Apply Anyway
**Idea:** Speak directly to strong writers who've talked themselves out of applying.
**Why it's true:** Brief names "strong writers who think they won't get in"; VoC: "considering a fully funded one feels kind of ridiculous."
**Rough lines:**
- *The writers we want are the ones drafting the email to say they're not ready.*
- *Your doubt is not a writing sample.*
- *Shann Ray has seventeen books. He started with one application.*

## 7. Spokane Is a Setting
**Idea:** Place as material: *Fire Season* is set in Spokane; the region is a literary map, not a flyover.
**Why it's true:** Krow's novel proves the place produces books; differentiates from campus-anonymous low-residency ads.
**Rough lines:**
- *Leyna Krow wrote Spokane into a Viking novel. Your turn.*
- *Some programs give you a mentor. This one gives you a setting.*

## 8. Galley Proofs
**Idea:** Ads designed as marked-up proofs, editor's marks visibly correcting the copy into the final line.
**Why it's true:** Show, don't tell: the editing practicum is demonstrated in the ad itself (Barry: show don't tell; Pricken: literal language).
**Rough lines:**
- *~~You'll write a book.~~ You'll write a book, edit a magazine and publish someone else's.* (strikethrough as editor's mark)
- *~~Just a workshop.~~ stet: a press.*

## 9. Open Mic to Main Stage
**Idea:** Follow one poem from a notebook to a Get Lit! stage in a single unbroken shot.
**Why it's true:** Narrative version of "put it on a stage"; Get Lit! founded 1998 gives depth.
**Scene:** Notebook page in workshop (10–15 chairs), red-penned at Willow Springs, typeset, then read aloud to a full room. Super: *Two years. One poem. Every room it needs.*

## 10. The Byline Ledger
**Idea:** A counter of how many bylines a student touches in two years, their own and everyone they edited, published and introduced.
**Why it's true:** Exaggeration grounded in real practica; reframes value beyond the student's own book (counters the "you won't pay it off with writing" fear: you leave with literary-life skills).
**Rough lines:**
- *Two years. One thesis. Hundreds of bylines that passed through your hands.*
- Placeholder number; needs real Willow Springs volume from EWU.

## 11. Jess Walter Week, Every Week (adjacent, flag)
**Idea:** EWU already hosts writers like Jess Walter (BA '87, honorary doctorate); MFA students are the ones who book, host and introduce them.
**Why it's true:** Uses EWU's most famous writer correctly (as a visitor/alum of EWU, not of the MFA) to dramatize the stage practicum.
**Rough lines:**
- *Somebody has to introduce Jess Walter. At EWU, it's a grad student.*
- Risk: must never read as Walter being an MFA grad; needs Walter's own consent.
