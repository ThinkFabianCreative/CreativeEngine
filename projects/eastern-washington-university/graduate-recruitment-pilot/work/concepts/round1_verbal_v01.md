# Round 1 verbal: campaign ideas (EXPLORE, diverge)

CLIENT: Eastern Washington University · PROJECT: graduate-recruitment-pilot · SOURCE SCOPE: project-level · MODE: EXPLORE round one · AUTHOR: word-smither · 2026-10-26
Base: brief/creative-brief_v19.md; steal-sheet_v01.md; T1_core-idea-lead-territory_v01.md; mfa_explore_verbal_v01.md; reviews/2026-10-25_clearances.md.

No verdicts, no scores. All lines are rough and unapproved. The brief's do-not-lift sentences (promise, CCI, proposition, new belief) are not used as copy anywhere below.

**Claim guardrails applied throughout:** MFA funding = "selective" assistantships, never "funded"; MFA proof = her editorial work on other people's writing, never her own publication; no licensure (graduation, NBCOT and licensure kept apart, and MOT numbers carried as fact lines, never as the scene); no cost comparisons or totals; no "only"; fieldwork never placed in Spokane; MS defense never "only" or "in person". Avoided: "doing", "hands-on", "real world", "are you in". Watch item: "count/counted in" sits near Fayetteville State's "Are You In?". Ideas 3 and 9 lean on that word family and are flagged.

**The MOT problem, stated once.** Of the three, MOT has the weakest moment in front of others. Candidate moments used below: a Level II fieldwork session observed by a supervising OT (consent from the clinical partner needed); a Catalyst simulation-lab run with the cohort watching; a home-visit or rural-placement plan presented to a clinical team; the case presentation to faculty. Each idea picks one.

---

## 1. The Real Work, Handed Over (T1, carried)

**Idea:** the real work of the field is handed to people who didn't think it was meant for them.
**Steal:** MasterClass lesson format (Andres's vessel) + Battles Won's character-in-action.
- **MS:** the applied business project handed over by a real organization, then the portfolio defended.
- **MFA:** a Willow Springs submission pile handed to a second-year editor.
- **MOT:** the supervising OT steps back and hands over the session plan at Level II fieldwork.
**Rough lines:** "Before you graduate, you'll have defended it." · "Before you graduate, you'll have read 400 submissions for someone else's magazine."
**Paid social:** static = hands passing the artifact, line + fact. Carousel = handed / worked / shown / fact. **Hub hero:** "Here's the work. It's yours." **Guide cover:** the artifact photographed flat, title over it. **Email subject:** "This is what we'll hand you"
**Push past it:** T1's weakness is that "handed" makes EWU the giver. Ideas below move the agency to her.

## 2. Exhibit A

**Idea:** every master's is a case you build; the campaign shows the evidence, labeled like courtroom exhibits, then the moment it's presented.
**Steal:** legal exhibit tags and evidence boards; the "Serial" case-building cadence; the steal sheet's proof-object carousel.
- **MS:** Exhibit A, the applied project; Exhibit B, the portfolio; the defense is the hearing.
- **MFA:** Exhibit A, an edited manuscript with her margin notes (another writer's work); Exhibit B, the issue she helped put out.
- **MOT:** Exhibit A, a treatment plan; the hearing is the case presentation to a supervising OT.
**Rough lines:** "Exhibit A: the thing you'll point to." · "You already know you can do it. This is the part where you can prove it." · "Bring evidence."
**Paid social:** static = one evidence-tagged object, red tag (Eagle Red does the work). Carousel = Exhibit A, B, C, then "the hearing" (the room), then fact + request info. **Hub hero:** "Exhibit A: [program artifact]." **Guide cover:** a manila evidence folder with the program name on the tab. **Email subject:** "Exhibit A is missing"

## 3. The Lead Without the Title

**Idea:** start with the title they actually hold ("lead", "aide", "reader") and show the work that outgrew it, in front of the people who rely on them.
**Steal:** Barry's "opposite"; Apple "Shot on iPhone" credit-line format, rewritten as a job-title credit; NHS unvarnished register.
- **MS:** name badge: "Shift Lead". Scene: she's running the room. Line resolves to the MS.
- **MFA:** credit line: "Uncredited". Scene: her edits on another writer's page.
- **MOT:** badge: "Rehab Aide". Scene: she sees what the patient needs next; the OT asks her opinion.
**Rough lines:** "Title: Lead. Job: everything else." · "Uncredited. For now." · "You already know what comes next. Learn to be the one who decides it."
**Paid social:** static = tight crop on a name badge, line underneath. Carousel = badge / the work / the room / the program. **Hub hero:** her badge, the program name stamped over it. **Guide cover:** a badge clipped to the guide. **Email subject:** "Your title is behind"
**Flag:** some lines drift toward "count" language; keep clear of "are you in".

## 4. Show Your Work

**Idea:** the instruction every math teacher gave becomes the campaign: the answer was never enough, they want to see how you got there, and so will your field.
**Steal:** the school margin note "show your work" in red pen; Moleskine/process-documentary aesthetics; the chalkboard story shape from the steal sheet.
- **MS:** the work behind the decision she makes every week, shown and defended at the portfolio.
- **MFA:** the editor's red pen on another writer's draft. Red pen = Eagle Red.
- **MOT:** the clinical reasoning written out and walked through with a supervisor in the sim lab.
**Rough lines:** "You've had the answer for years. Now show your work." · "Full marks need working." · "Show your work. To people who check."
**Paid social:** static = red-pen annotation over a real artifact. Carousel = answer / working / reviewer / fact. **Hub hero:** "Show your work." over a marked-up page. **Guide cover:** the guide itself annotated in red. **Email subject:** "Show your work"
**Note:** generic phrase; ownability rides on the red pen + real EWU artifacts. Competitor swap risk is high, so it lives or dies on execution.

## 5. The Room

**Idea:** the campaign is about the audience, not the student: who is in the room when she shows it. We shoot the watchers.
**Steal:** reaction-shot editing (the audience-first cut in talent shows, 12 Angry Men's faces); Barry's "imply, don't reveal": we never see the work, only the faces seeing it.
- **MS:** the defense panel leaning in.
- **MFA:** an author reading the editor's notes on her own story, nodding.
- **MOT:** the cohort of 32 watching a classmate run a sim-lab scenario; the supervising OT's face at fieldwork.
**Rough lines:** "Pick the room you want to impress." · "Somebody's going to see this." · "Thirty-two people are about to watch you get it right."
**Paid social:** static = a face, mid-reaction, line over it. Carousel = faces, faces, faces, then the student, then the fact (a reveal held back). **Hub hero:** the panel/cohort shot, student out of frame. **Guide cover:** empty chairs, set for an audience. **Email subject:** "Who's watching"
**Strength for MOT:** gives the thinnest program the strongest room (cohort + supervisor).

## 6. Margin Notes

**Idea:** the field leaves notes on your work; the campaign is built from the real notes faculty, editors and supervisors write on student work.
**Steal:** Hemingway's annotated manuscripts; the steal sheet's proof-object close-up; Spotify Wrapped-style "data as personal story", rewritten as feedback-as-story.
- **MS:** faculty note on the applied project: "Defend this."
- **MFA:** her notes on someone else's story, and the author's note back.
- **MOT:** supervisor's sign-off note on a fieldwork goal (no patient data).
**Rough lines:** "The best feedback you'll ever get is written in the margin." · "Write back."
**Paid social:** static = one handwritten note, huge. Carousel = note, page, person, fact. **Hub hero:** a margin note as headline. **Guide cover:** handwriting across the cover. **Email subject:** "Someone left a note on your work"

## 7. Before Monday

**Idea:** the creative target's test ("letters that change nothing on Monday") inverted: every piece is set on a specific weekday and shows what's different by then.
**Steal:** the calendar/countdown device; "The Office" cold-open structure; diary formats.
- **MS:** Monday, 9 a.m.: she walks into the ops meeting with the analysis she built for her project.
- **MFA:** Thursday: the submission meeting at Willow Springs, her pick on the table.
- **MOT:** Tuesday, 7:40 a.m.: first session she leads with the supervisor in the corner.
**Rough lines:** "What changes by Monday?" · "Tuesday, 7:40. Your session." · "Not someday. A Thursday."
**Paid social:** static = day + time stamp, scene. Carousel = a week, one card per day. **Hub hero:** "Your Monday, rebuilt." **Guide cover:** a weekly planner page. **Email subject:** "What's on your Monday?"
**Risk:** implies job change; keep the scenes inside the program (project, editorial meeting, fieldwork), never at a promoted job.

## 8. The Second Reader

**Idea:** in every field there's a second reader, the one who checks the work. The campaign shows students becoming the second reader for others, then being read themselves.
**Steal:** academic "second reader" convention; peer review; Get Lit! / Writers in the Community teaching structure.
- **MS:** she reviews a teammate's plan, then faces her own defense.
- **MFA:** she reads unsolicited manuscripts as an editor, the clearest second reader there is.
- **MOT:** she observes a classmate in the sim lab and gives feedback; then it's her turn.
**Rough lines:** "First you read theirs. Then they read yours." · "Learn to judge the work. Then hand yours over."
**Paid social:** static = two hands, one page. Carousel = she reads / she's read. **Hub hero:** split frame, reader and read. **Guide cover:** two names on a routing slip. **Email subject:** "Your turn to be read"

## 9. Proof of Concept

**Idea:** borrowing the startup/engineering phrase, the master's is her proof of concept, a small working demonstration shown to the people who decide.
**Steal:** tech demo days and pitch-day staging (Y Combinator demo day); prototype labels.
- **MS:** the applied business project as a pitched proof of concept to a real panel.
- **MFA:** a Willow Springs Books title she worked on, shown at a reading she helped run.
- **MOT:** a home-adaptation plan shown to the clinical team at a rural placement.
**Rough lines:** "You're the concept. This is the proof." · "Demo day, every term."
**Paid social:** static = "PROOF OF CONCEPT" stencil on the artifact. Carousel = concept / build / demo / fact. **Hub hero:** "Proof of concept: you." **Guide cover:** spec sheet. **Email subject:** "Your proof of concept"
**Note:** strongest for MS; tonally strange for MFA, which may be the interesting part.

## 10. Rough Draft

**Idea:** the strange one: the campaign itself runs as visible drafts. Ads ship with tracked changes, crossings-out, faculty comments. The degree is the edit.
**Steal:** Google Docs suggesting mode; The Economist's crossed-out headlines; Wieden's "the work is the ad" (steal sheet media idea).
- **MS:** a draft business plan, live-edited across a carousel until it's defensible.
- **MFA:** an editor's tracked changes on another writer's paragraph (never her own).
- **MOT:** a treatment plan revised with supervisor comments until signed off.
**Rough lines:** "~~I'm not ready~~ I'm ready to be checked." · "Draft 1: you. Final: defended." · "Accept all changes?"
**Paid social:** static = one ad with a visible comment bubble from a real faculty member. Carousel = draft 1 → 4, the last one approved. **Hub hero:** the page loads with tracked changes resolving. **Guide cover:** "DRAFT" watermark, struck through. **Email subject:** "3 suggested edits to your plan"

## One look, briefly
Every idea above can carry Eagle Red as the mark of judgment (the red tag, the red pen, the comment, the stamp). That's a shared visual hinge worth handing to DesignChamp regardless of which idea advances.
