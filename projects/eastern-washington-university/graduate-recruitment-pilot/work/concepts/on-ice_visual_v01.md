CLIENT: Eastern Washington University · PROJECT: graduate-recruitment-pilot · BRAND: EWU graduate programs (MS in Management, MFA in Creative Writing, MOT) · SOURCE SCOPE: PROJECT-LEVEL, TRAFFIK proposal, not approved by EWU · Author: design-champ · Mode: DEVELOP · 2026-10-26

Anchor (Andres, clearances 2026-10-26, LEAD CAMPAIGN): her career has been "put on ice". She breaks herself out of a block of ice, mid-break. The form inside is her present self. Line: "Stop waiting to be chosen." This file dresses that idea; it does not replace or reopen it.

## 0. Concept sentence and fixed rules

**Concept sentence:** A working woman, already herself, is cracking her own way out of the ice someone put her career on, and she is not waiting for it to melt.

Rules carried from the decision (non-negotiable on every execution):
1. **It is her, today.** Her own clothes, her own body, her own age. No scrubs-as-promotion, no blazer-as-CEO, no beret-as-author. Tools of the work are allowed because she already uses or wants to use them; they are not a costume of an outcome.
2. **She does it.** Her hands, her shoulder, her tool. No one chips her out, no hand reaching in, no EWU hammer. EWU appears as the date and the mark, never as the rescuer.
3. **Mid-break.** Never fully out (that is the reveal and the "new you" ending). Never fully frozen (that is a victim picture). The sweet spot is 30–70% free.
4. **Ice = Waiting = cool grey/blue.** No red light inside the ice, no red reflections, no warm sunset grade on the block.
5. **Red #B7142E = her move and EWU only.** One red object per frame (glove or mitten, tool handle, the start date), plus the EWU wordmark.
6. **Not "Self Made".** Bobbie Carlyle's bronze shows a man carving himself out of stone with a hammer and chisel: monumental, permanent, self-invention, a future self being made. Ours differs on three counts and we keep all three visible: (a) **material**: ice is temporary and was put there by circumstance; stone is what you are made of. Her block is a holding state, not her nature; (b) **figure**: the form inside is already finished (her present self), she is not sculpting a new person, so we never show chisel marks shaping her body, only fracture lines releasing it; (c) **idiom**: "on ice" is a phrase everyone knows from work (the project got put on ice), so the image reads as a career situation, not a philosophy of self-making. Practical consequence: **no hammer-and-chisel pose on her own torso, no bronze/stone tones, no heroic plinth.**

Clichés to stay off (flag once): Han Solo in carbonite (pose: flat, arms up, face screaming); Frozen/Elsa (sparkle, blue glow, magic); climate-melt art (Néle Azevedo's melting ice figures, Eliasson's *Ice Watch*) reads as global warming if the setting is outdoors and the ice is shown dying rather than broken. Our ice is **broken by her**, not melting away on its own. Melt may appear only as consequence (water at the base), never as the agent.

## 1. Three art-direction routes

### Route A: PRACTICAL. Real ice, real person, one hard decision of light
A real clear-ice block (or real ice shell around her lower body, see §5), photographed in a plain, cold-toned working-adjacent space: a loading dock, a concrete corridor, a cold storage room with industrial light. The physicality is the argument: real meltwater, real breath, real shards on the floor, real effort in her forearms.

- **References:** Nadav Kander (cool, desaturated, patient portraits; *Dust*, *Yangtze*); Rineke Dijkstra (frontal, unflattering-honest bodies, no styling); Andy Goldsworthy's ice works (the honesty of the material, ice as temporary); Alec Soth (the dignity of ordinary people in plain places).
- **Light:** one large hard-ish source from high side (a 4 ft silk at 45°, or a dock door of overcast daylight), plus a backlight behind the block so the ice glows grey-white with internal fractures. No colored gels.
- **Lens:** 50 mm or 85 mm, f/5.6–8 so both her face and the fracture lines are sharp.
- **Grade:** cool neutral, shadows lifted grey-blue (not teal), skin kept true and slightly warm against the ice, fine grain like Kodak Portra 400 pushed one stop. Red object is the only saturated color.
- **What it argues:** this is real effort by a real person. Credible for adults who distrust gloss.
- **Risk:** hard to get the "mid-break" fragment state on set; needs a skilled ice sculptor and composite for the body-in-ice parts.

### Route B: SURREAL HYPERREAL. A studio tableau, impossible but perfectly lit
The ice is a single, impossible monolith on a seamless grey set, carved precisely to her shape; she is half out, the pieces suspended or scattered in a still, clean field. Composited plates (real ice textures + real portrait).

- **References:** Erwin Olaf (cool, controlled, painterly staging); Gregory Crewdson (cinematic stillness, a moment held); Alex Prager (staged ordinariness, a single strange event); Paolo Roversi for soft skin against cold.
- **Light:** soft, large key with a crisp rim; ice lit from inside/below so it reads glass-like.
- **Grade:** steel blue-grey, crushed but not black shadows, very clean.
- **What it argues:** a symbol. Strong stopping power, very ownable for EWU in a category of smiling-campus photos.
- **Risk:** the most "AI-able" look; can tip into Frozen/carbonite cliché and glossy faces; feels like an idea about her rather than her.

### Route C: DOCUMENTARY IN SITU. The ice is where her work already is
The block of ice stands in her real working world: a break room, a hospital corridor at shift change, a library carrel, an open-plan office at 7 p.m. Everything around her is mundane and true; the only impossible thing is that she is half encased in ice and breaking out. Coworkers may pass out of focus, never looking (nobody watches is the point; they are not an audience).

- **References:** LaToya Ruby Frazier and Dawoud Bey (working people, dignity, place); Jeff Wall (staged events photographed as if documentary, e.g. *Mimic*, *Insomnia*); Philip-Lorca diCorcia (ordinary places lit with one deliberate light).
- **Light:** motivated by the space: fluorescent tubes plus one added daylight-balanced key from a window side. Cool-green fluorescent is neutralised to grey, not left green.
- **Lens:** 35 mm, eye level, standing; room visible.
- **Grade:** flat documentary contrast, cool greys, true skin, grain like Kodak Tri-X-era texture in color (Portra 800). Red object pops because nothing else is saturated.
- **What it argues:** the waiting happens at work, in her actual life. The idiom "on ice" lives where careers are put on hold.
- **Risk:** busier frame; the ice must still read at thumb size (see §3).

### Recommendation: Route C, shot practically (Route A methods inside Route C settings)
C is the only route where the image says *career* without a word: the ice sits in her workplace, so "put on ice" reads as a work situation, not a fairy tale or a climate piece. It is also the furthest from Carlyle (no plinth, no stone, no sculptor's studio) and from carbonite (no void). From A we take the production honesty: real ice, real water, real shards, real effort. B is kept as a fallback only for OOH, where one surreal monolith can do more at distance; we do not lead with it because it produces the glossy, generic look Andres wants to avoid.

Settings are generic workplace types, not EWU rooms. The EWU rooms (Catalyst, Get Lit!, MS defense room) belong to the "after" beat: the hub and guide show where her dated act happens (see §4).

## 3. Cold-read check at thumb size (~120 px wide)

Test: shrink each comp to 120 px, look for one second, say what you see.

| Should read in 1 second | Pass condition | Failure we fix |
|---|---|---|
| "A woman stuck in ice" | The ice block silhouette is a single pale geometric mass with her dark head/arms breaking its edge | Busy room competes: drop room exposure 1 stop, keep ice the brightest mass in frame |
| "She is breaking out" | A diagonal: a falling slab or a raised arm at 30–45° breaking the block's rectangle | Static pose reads as "frozen victim"; always a diagonal of force |
| "Something red is hers" | One red dot near her hand | Two reds compete (red sign in room, red chair): remove every other red in set dressing |
| "It is work" | One recognisable work object (laptop, pages, gait belt) at her chest height | At 120 px the program object vanishes: enlarge it, place it against the ice not the room |

Pass rule: if a cold reader says "ice" and "she's getting out" without "Frozen" or "Han Solo", the frame passes. The program is read from the headline, not demanded of the image at thumb size. The bottom 30% of 4:5 is kept clear (floor, meltwater, shards) for the line and the date.

## 4. System

- **Print hero (single page / spread):** KV full-bleed, line "Stop waiting to be chosen." set in the bottom third, in EWU's type, white or near-black on the floor area. Red only in the dated act ("Start Fall 2027") and the EWU mark. Program name small.
- **Social static (4:5):** KV crop tight at the moment of fracture; line + date in the bottom 30%.
- **Social carousel (the crack spreading), 5 cards:** 1) Clean ice block in her office, faint silhouette, no person visible clearly: "Put on ice." 2) A hairline crack, her red glove pressed to the inside: "Next cycle." 3) Crack spreads, arm through: "Big shoes to fill." 4) Slab falls, she is half out: "Stop waiting to be chosen." 5) The empty shape in the ice and water on the floor, her red-dated note on the desk: program, start date, EWU mark. (Told-to-wait phrases stay setup, never rebutted; WordSmither owns final lines.)
- **6s motion:** locked-off time-lapse of the block in the workplace (lights cycling day-night-day = time passing while waiting), then real-time: one hit, crack runs, slab drops at 5s. Card. Sound: room tone and fluorescent hum during the time-lapse, then a single sharp crack and the slab hitting the floor; silence then a short beat for the card. Works sound-off: the crack is visible.
- **15s motion:** 0–5s time-lapse of the frozen room (a calendar on the wall flipping, coffee going cold, a colleague's cubicle getting packed up); 5–12s real-time, close sound: her breath, ice creaking, the red handle hitting, the crack; she shoulders out; 12–15s she steps out of frame (the chair and ice remain: "the title stays, she moves"), line and date. No VO needed; optional VO in her own voice saying one told-to-wait phrase at the start.
- **Hub hero (one per program):** the KV as a wide 16:9 crop with the block on the left third; on scroll, a subtle crack animation (CSS/Lottie, reduced-motion fallback = static) leads into the program's real EWU room photography (Catalyst, Get Lit!, MS defense room): the ice was where she was; EWU is where she goes. Start date as the red CTA.
- **Guide cover (downloadable program guide):** the empty, broken block shape in a plain room, meltwater, a few shards, her red date note on the desk. The reader holds the guide: the next step is hers. No person on the cover, so it works across programs.
- **OOH (Spokane, I-90 boards, skyways):** Route B fallback allowed here: one huge pale block, one dark arm breaking out with the red glove, line. Max 6 words + EWU mark. Night version: block lit cool white, the red glove the only color.
- **Optional real-ice stunt (Spokane):** a 4 ft clear-ice block in a public spot (Riverfront Park near the Pavilion, or a downtown skywalk lobby with permission) with a work object frozen inside (a laptop shell, a notebook, a gait belt) and a plaque: "Put on ice." Countdown to the application deadline in red. Passers-by are given red-handled ice picks to chip at it (safety: blunted picks, gloves, a staff host, barrier, salt mats; check city permits and liability with EWU risk management). It melts or is broken out by the deadline; the time-lapse becomes social content. Climate-misread risk: lowest indoors and with the work object clearly visible; flagged once.

## 5. Practical shoot plan vs generated pitch comps

**Pitch comps (now):** generated with the ChatGPT/OpenAI image model per the 2026-10-26 clearance: pitch/inspiration only, never commercial, each batch costed and approved by Andres first; referenced photographers credited in the deck. Use the three prompts in §6; expect 4–8 generations each to get believable ice and non-glossy faces. Comp the type on top in Figma; do not let the model render text.

**Production (the goal is to shoot for real):**
- **Talent:** real EWU graduate students or recent applicants who consent in writing (model release + a plain-language use statement; payment; right to withdraw before publication). Casting per §2 descriptors; at least one plus-size and one average build. No minors.
- **Ice:** commission a professional ice sculptor (clear-ice block maker) for: (a) one hero block, about 4 x 3 x 2 ft, hollowed into a two-part shell that closes around her legs/torso without touching skin for long; (b) a set of pre-scored breakaway slabs; (c) loose shards and crushed ice for the floor. Real ice in frame; body-in-ice contact handled as a composite of plates when needed.
- **Safety:** ice never in direct prolonged skin contact: talent wears a thin neoprene base layer under wardrobe where hidden, maximum 2–3 minutes per take in the shell, warm-up area with heaters and towels between takes, medic on set; ice picks and chisels are prop-blunted for talent, sharp tools only in the sculptor's hands; non-slip mats under meltwater; electrical gear on raised stands, GFCI everywhere; dry ice not used (CO2 risk). Location must accept water damage (sealed floor, plastic sheeting under the set dressing).
- **Locations:** real workplaces that resemble the program world (an office, a break room/library, a rehab gym), cleared with release. Not identifiable employers.
- **Plates:** shoot a clean plate, an ice-only plate, and the talent plate at each setup for compositing the mid-break moment and the falling slab (high frame rate for motion: 120 fps for the crack).
- **Motion:** time-lapse on a locked-off camera over a real 3–4 hour melt for the 15s, plus real-time break takes.
- **Schedule:** one shoot day per program (3 days) or two days with a compressed MFA/MS split; ice delivered in a refrigerated truck the morning of.
