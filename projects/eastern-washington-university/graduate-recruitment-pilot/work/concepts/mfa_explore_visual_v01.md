## 1. Masterclass, Local (Andres's territory)
**Concept sentence:** The premium-course look, but the teacher is an EWU MFA writer and the lesson is how a book actually leaves the desk.
**Mechanism:** context shift (Pricken). The celebrity-course grammar, transplanted onto working writers from Cheney.
**What carries it:** image and format. Cinematic low-key light, shallow focus, a "lesson" chapter card system (Lesson 03: The Edit).
**Key image:** Shann Ray in a dark room, single practical lamp camera-left, face half in shadow, a marked-up page proof in his hands (not a notebook, not a typewriter). Super, lower third in thin serif: "Shann Ray. American Book Award. EWU MFA." Leyna Krow variant: filmed in a burned-forest edge outside Spokane at dusk, the setting of *Fire Season*, ash on her boots. Kate Peterson variant: on an empty Get Lit! stage, house lights down, one work light.
**The twist that keeps it from imitation:** every "lesson" title is a practicum, not a craft platitude. "Lesson 1: Read 400 submissions. Lesson 2: Say no to 390." The course trailer is really the program.
**Formats:** Social: 15s trailer cuts with lesson cards; 4:5 portrait stills with chapter numbers. Video: 60s "course trailer" with three alumni, each a lesson. Landing hero: dark full-bleed video loop, chapter list as the page nav ("Lesson 1 Magazine / 2 Press / 3 Stage / 4 Your book").
**Risk to keep visible:** borrowed aesthetic reads as imitation without the practicum twist; dark cinematic is now common in premium ed.

## 2. The Masthead
**Concept sentence:** Your name is already printed in the Willow Springs masthead; the ad is the page.
**Mechanism:** literal language / imply don't reveal. The proof is the format itself.
**What carries it:** type and format. A real-looking magazine masthead, with a blank slot or the viewer's name.
**Key image:** a tight crop of a Willow Springs masthead page, editorial staff list in small caps. One line is a pale blue highlight: "Assistant Fiction Editor: you". Margin note in red: "start: year one".
**Formats:** Social: static masthead crop; personalized-name dynamic variant if the platform allows. Video: 6s bumper, names in the masthead scroll, the last slot types itself in. Landing hero: full masthead as the hero, the "you" line links to the application.

## 3. The Rejection Slip
**Concept sentence:** You'll write rejection slips before you stop receiving them.
**Mechanism:** reversal. The writer's most dreaded object, seen from the editor's side of the desk.
**What carries it:** image (the slip) plus one line.
**Key image:** a stack of small printed Willow Springs rejection slips, shot like evidence, flat-lay on a cold steel surface (not wood). One slip is signed by a first-year MFA student. Headline set tiny at the bottom.
**Formats:** Social: carousel, slip 1 (you receive), slip 2 (you send), slip 3 (your acceptance letter). Video: hands sort a submission pile, the camera reveals whose hands. Landing hero: the slip, enlarged, with the program facts printed as its fine print.
**Note:** risky tone; poets may read it as cruelty. Worth a test.

## 4. The Red Pen
**Concept sentence:** The mark-up is the curriculum: every red mark on a page is a skill the MFA teaches.
**Mechanism:** exaggeration. The edited page as an annotated diagram.
**What carries it:** type and annotation. Typeset prose under heavy editorial marks in one red.
**Key image:** an opening page of fiction (a short unpublished excerpt written for the ad, or an alum's with permission) covered in real proofreader's marks. Leader lines run from marks to labels in the margin: "workshop", "line edit", "copyedit", "galley", "reading draft". The final mark, at the bottom: "stet. Read it aloud at Get Lit!"
**Formats:** Social: one page per post, a different practicum labeled each time. Video: marks draw on in real time to the scratch of a pen, ends on a clean printed book. Landing hero: an interactive page, hover a mark to see the practicum behind it.

## 5. The Press Run
**Concept sentence:** Two years, start to finish, like a book going through the press.
**Mechanism:** analogy. The degree's timeline mapped onto a book's production stages.
**What carries it:** format and imagery from book production (signatures, crop marks, color bars, pressroom).
**Key image:** printer's press sheet, uncut, with 8 pages imposed upside-down and sideways. Each page panel is a quarter: Q1 workshop, Q3 Willow Springs reader, Q5 Willow Springs Books intern, Q8 thesis defense. Crop marks and a CMYK color bar along the edge carry "EWU MFA 2027".
**Formats:** Social: carousel unfolding the press sheet panel by panel. Video: macro footage of real offset printing, pages folding into a signature, title page reveals the viewer's book. Landing hero: the press sheet as the program map; tap a panel to open the quarter.

## 6. The Stage (Get Lit!)
**Concept sentence:** The reading is the last draft.
**Mechanism:** context shift. Writing shown where it ends up, in a room full of people, not where it starts.
**What carries it:** image, theatrical lighting, a crowd.
**Key image:** shot from behind a writer at a Get Lit! podium, back to camera, looking out at a full house in Spokane. Their pages are visible in hand, still marked up. Kate Peterson, as Get Lit! director, appears in the wings with a clipboard: the program running the festival is the program teaching it.
**Formats:** Social: vertical video, the walk from wings to mic in one shot, 10s. Static: house lights photo, a poster-style typographic lockup like a festival bill with student names alongside visiting authors. Landing hero: festival-poster hero, lineup typography where "You" is billed.

## 7. Spine Out
**Concept sentence:** A shelf of EWU MFA books is the brochure.
**Mechanism:** omission. No faces, no campus, only spines.
**What carries it:** image (a shelf) and type (the spines).
**Key image:** a row of real alumni books spine-out on a plain shelf: *Fire Season* (Krow), *American Masculine* (Ray), *I'm Fine, But You Appear To Be Sinking* (Peterson), Willow Springs Books titles, then one blank spine in uncoated stock at the end. No headline on the image; the blank spine carries it.
**Formats:** Social: single static, then a carousel pulling each book out with a one-line alum fact. Video: slow track along the shelf, stops at the blank spine. Landing hero: shelf as horizontal scroll, last spine is the "Apply" CTA.
**Check:** cover/trade dress rights for each publisher's book; cleared names do not equal cleared covers.

## 8. Cheney at 7 PM
**Concept sentence:** The writing life doesn't happen in a garret; it happens at 7 PM on a Tuesday in Cheney and Spokane.
**Mechanism:** context shift plus specificity. Evening classes (from fall 2026) become a time-stamped place portrait.
**What carries it:** imagery (real Inland Northwest night places) plus timestamps as type.
**Key image:** a wheat field outside Cheney at blue hour, a lit classroom window the only light in frame, 10–15 silhouettes inside around a table. Timestamp in the corner like a manuscript header: "Tues 7:04 PM, Cheney WA. Workshop, 12 writers." Series: "Sat 11:40 PM, Spokane, layout for Willow Springs", "Thu 6:58 PM, Bing Crosby Theater area, Get Lit! doors open" (venue to confirm with EWU).
**Formats:** Social: timestamped photo series. Video: one evening in time-lapse cuts. Landing hero: time-of-day hero that shifts with the viewer's clock.

## 9. The Slush Pile (rough, strange)
**Concept sentence:** Somewhere in this pile is the next Willow Springs story, and you're the one who finds it.
**Mechanism:** exaggeration. The scale of reading as spectacle.
**What carries it:** image scale.
**Key image:** an overhead shot of a room floor completely carpeted by manuscripts, a single reader lying in the middle of it holding one page up to the light. Shot like an art installation, white walls, hard flash.
**Formats:** Social: the overhead still; reels zooming out from one sentence to the whole floor. Landing hero: zoom-out hero.
