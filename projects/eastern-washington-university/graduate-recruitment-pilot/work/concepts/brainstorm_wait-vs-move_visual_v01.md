# BRAINSTORM: WAIT vs MOVE, visual v01

CLIENT: Eastern Washington University · PROJECT: graduate-recruitment-pilot · BRAND: EWU graduate programs (MS in Management, MFA in Creative Writing, MOT) · SOURCE SCOPE: project-level · AUTHOR: design-champ · DATE: 2026-10-26 · MODE: EXPLORE (no verdicts, rough on purpose)

Built from: strategy/ewu-strategic-assets_v10.md ("Stop waiting to be chosen."; a dated EWU act is the proof; the taped chair she walks past survives; rule 2: tension with the culture of waiting, never bitterness at a person) and concepts/brainstorm_the-chair_visual_v01.md. Andres's references: (1) "I resign from waiting": a resignation letter as her dated act; (2) the *Hamilton* duet structure, "Wait for It" vs "My Shot": two ways of living, neither a villain. **Reference only:** no Hamilton imagery, costume, typography, staging, quill-and-candle period cues or lyric echoes. We borrow the structure (two honest positions side by side), nothing visible.

Described only. No images made.

**Constants across all six.** Real paper, real desks, real light; no stock "quitting" props (box of desk stuff, door slam, fist pump, burning bridge). EWU red #B7142E is only ever the *mark of the move* (a date, a signature, a send) and never a correction, a strikethrough or a red pen on someone else's words. The letter is always addressed to the waiting, never to a person. The taped chair may appear, untouched, never sat in, never torn.

**Program split for the letter idea (Andres):** MOT may be a real, warm resignation (aide to OT, leaving a job she valued on good terms to start the cohort). MS and MFA resign from *the waiting*, not the job: they keep working; the letter is to "next cycle", to "maybe next year".

## 1. THE LETTER (print page)

**Concept sentence:** a real resignation letter, on her own plain stationery, resigning not from a job but from waiting; the only red on the page is the date she starts at EWU and her signature.

**Card one / print page:** full-bleed photographed letter, slightly angled on wood, legible at print size. Formal letter grammar: her address block, a date, "To: Waiting," "Effective [EWU term start], I resign." Two short paragraphs in her voice (WordSmither territory), warm and plain. Signature and date in EWU red ink, handwritten. At thumb size: white page, two red marks. Reads as "a decision was signed."

**Per program:**
- **MS:** "To: Next Cycle." Effective date = MS term start. Small P.S.: "I'm keeping the job. I'm done waiting for the title." (direction, not copy)
- **MFA:** "To: Maybe Next Year." Typed on a manuscript-ish page; effective date = first workshop. Her name in red as the author, the way it'll sit on her thesis.
- **MOT:** a real, warm resignation to her clinic, thanking them (named only as "the team", no person, no logo). Effective date = first fieldwork week or cohort start. The warmth is the point: leaving well.

**What makes it EWU's:** the effective date is an EWU date (term start, first workshop, defense window); a tiny footer line ties it to the program. Needs real program dates from EWU.

**Risks:** walkout energy if the tone sharpens; implying MS/MFA should quit (fix: address to the waiting, P.S. that the job stays); letter copy can sprawl past thumb-readable; MOT letter could read as disloyal to a clinic (keep gratitude in it).

## 2. THE DESK, THE LETTER, THE CHAIR

**Concept sentence:** her desk at the end of an ordinary day: the signed letter squared under her keys, and in the soft background the taped chair she's no longer waiting for.

**Card one / print page:** overhead-ish three-quarter still life. Foreground sharp: letter, red signature, a pen with the cap off, her keys, maybe the EWU acceptance email on a phone face-down (no UI). Background, out of focus: the taped chair, tape label legible only as a shape. Nobody in frame. Reads as "she's left the chair alone and decided."

**Per program:** MS: open-plan desk, the manager's chair down the row with the tape. MFA: kitchen table at night, a notebook stack, the folding reader's chair by the wall. MOT: break-room table, the clinic's aide chair in the hall through the doorway.

**What makes it EWU's:** the dated letter is the hero; the chair is the cast. Red appears twice: the signature and the date.

**Risks:** still-life subtlety may die in feed; desk props drift into lifestyle-stock styling; the chair can read as resentment if lit like a villain (light it neutrally, it's furniture, not an enemy).

---

## 3. THE DUET (split frame, same person)

**Concept sentence:** the same woman twice in one frame, split down the middle: on one side she waits, composed and patient; on the other she moves, in an EWU room on a dated day; neither half is mocked.

**Card one / print page:** vertical split, matched framing and pose height. Left: she's seated in the taped chair's row, coat on lap, phone glowing, a calendar behind her paging "next cycle". Right: she's standing on the red floor band (The Room constant) in the sim lab / reading / presentation room, date visible on a whiteboard or programme. Same clothes, same face; the only change is the light and the red band. Both halves are dignified; the waiting self is not sad, just still.

**Per program:** MS: open office vs her applied-project room. MFA: back-row folding chair at someone else's reading vs Get Lit! stage. MOT: hallway chair beside the therapy gym vs Catalyst sim lab.

**What makes it EWU's:** the right half is always a real EWU room with a real date in it. The structure is borrowed from the duet (two honest ways to live); the content is hers.

**Risks:** before/after diptych is a category and weight-loss cliché; split-screen twin compositing can look cheap; if the left half is too drab it becomes contempt for people who wait (rule 2). Any visual echo of Hamilton (period dress, spotlight on a bare stage, gold-star type) is out.

---

## 4. SEND (the envelope / the send moment)

**Concept sentence:** the half-second where it's decided: her thumb on send, or the envelope leaving her hand at the mailbox, stamped with the date in red.

**Card one / print page:** tight crop. Version A (print): a sealed envelope, hand-addressed "To Waiting", red date stamp across the flap, caught mid-drop into a blue mailbox slot. Version B (social/motion): over-the-shoulder, her thumb a millimetre above a red Submit on the EWU application, outbound timestamp in the corner; the cut is on the press. No reaction shot, no audience: nobody watches, it's done.

**Per program:** MS: lunch break, phone in the parking lot. MFA: 11:52 pm, laptop, manuscript sample attached. MOT: envelope to her clinic manager's tray, or the application, sitting in her car in scrubs.

**What makes it EWU's:** the thing sent is EWU-dated (application deadline, start term). The red is the send button / stamp, the mark of the move.

**Risks:** "click submit" is the most generic higher-ed ad there is (must be the letter-to-waiting, not just the form); a recognizable UI may need EWU's real application screen; mailbox can feel nostalgic-stock.

---

## 5. TWO WEEKS (the notice calendar)

**Concept sentence:** a paper wall calendar where two kinds of dates live: the grey "next cycle"s she's been told, crossed nowhere, just repeating; and one red-circled EWU start date she wrote herself.

**Card one / print page:** flat-on calendar page photographed on a real wall (pinholes, coffee ring). Small grey pencil notes repeat across months: "next cycle", "after review", "maybe Q3". One date circled in EWU red marker, her handwriting: "Start. EWU." Two-weeks variant (MOT): the fourteen days before the cohort start blocked in red, counting down, a warm countdown, not an escape.

**Per program:** MS: office desk calendar, review-cycle notes. MFA: a paper planner, "submit next year" in last year's and this year's pages. MOT: the clinic schedule taped by the time clock; red fortnight before fieldwork/cohort.

**What makes it EWU's:** the only red thing in a grey year is an EWU date. Very literal proof device; works as a carousel (month by month, grey grey grey, red).

**Risks:** grey notes can read as quoting a boss (keep them unattributed, in her own pencil); "two weeks' notice" implies quitting for MS/MFA (use the two-weeks variant only for MOT); calendars are a common deadline-ad trope.

---

## 6. THE SIGNATURE LINE (type-led)

**Concept sentence:** an empty signature line at the bottom of a page, and the only thing on the page that's hers is the red signature and date that fill it.

**Card one / print page:** minimal: off-white page, a long grey paragraph block (unreadable, blurred like the fine print of "waiting your turn"), then a ruled line where she signs in red with the date of her EWU start. Typographic, not photographic; scales to OOH, social, and a printed mailer the prospect could literally sign (an interactive move: the landing page asks for her start term and returns her own "letter").

**Per program:** the date and degree name under the line change: "MS in Management, Fall 2027"; "MFA, first workshop [date]"; "MOT, cohort [date]".

**What makes it EWU's:** the dated EWU act is the signature itself. Bridges into a possible landing-page device (she generates her letter). Interaction idea only.

**Risks:** abstract; a signature line reads as a contract (debt, enrollment pressure); blurred grey text could look like we're hiding terms, which is the wrong trust signal for a cost-anxious audience.

## Notes for Andres
