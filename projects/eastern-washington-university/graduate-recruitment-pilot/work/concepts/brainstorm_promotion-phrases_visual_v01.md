# BRAINSTORM: promotion phrases, visual, v01

CLIENT: Eastern Washington University · PROJECT: graduate-recruitment-pilot · BRAND: EWU graduate programs (MS in Management, MFA in Creative Writing, MOT) · SOURCE SCOPE: project-level · AUTHOR: design-champ · DATE: 2026-10-26 · MODE: EXPLORE (no verdicts, no scoring)

Provocation (Andres, CD): "We're constantly told at work the barriers to get a promotion. Things like 'You got big shoes to fill', self-doubt and anxiety."

Rule carried in: the person is the hero, the phrase is only the setup. Every card one shows the person acting, not the barrier sitting alone. Built against brief v19 and The Room (reverse shot, red floor band = where you stand). Lines in quotes are placeholders for WordSmither. Claims stay inside the brief's reasons to believe; nothing implies a promotion.

---

## 1. THE SHOES SHE BROUGHT

**Concept sentence:** the "big shoes" are on the floor, empty and oversized; she walks past them in her own and stands on the red band.

**Key image:** reverse-shot room (Room camera), watchers in the seats. Foreground floor: a pair of absurdly large, polished men's dress shoes, slightly comic, someone else's. Beyond them, on the red floor band, her own worn work shoes, planted, mid-presentation. We see her only from the shins down. The watchers are looking at her, not at the big shoes.

**Card one alone:** two pairs of shoes, one empty and huge, one filled and on the red line. Reads without copy. Placeholder: "You were never meant to fill them."

**Carousel:** 1 the two pairs · 2 tilt up to the room watching her (reverse shot) · 3 the proof she built there (MS portfolio defense / MFA reading / MOT sim lab) · 4 program fact + sign-off.

**Tie to The Room:** the red band becomes the literal place her own shoes stand; the room is the audience she earned.

**Program fit:** MS: wingtips vs her worn flats at the oral defense. MFA: big shoes beside the Get Lit! stage mic, her boots at the mic. MOT: clinic clogs sized for a giant vs her non-slip shoes beside a manikin in Catalyst. Strongest on MS.

**Risk worth keeping:** the oversized-shoe prop can tip into comedy prop styling. Keep it photographic and dead serious.

## 2. NEXT CYCLE

**Concept sentence:** the deferral note ("revisit next cycle") is pinned to her badge, and she is already doing the work it says she isn't ready for.

**Key image:** close, documentary. A sticky note or calendar reminder reading "revisit next cycle" stuck to her lanyard or laptop lid, while in the same frame she is mid-action: presenting the applied project, reading to a full room, guiding a classmate's transfer in the sim lab. The note is small; the action is big.

**Card one alone:** the note in focus, her hands working behind it, a room partly visible. The gap between the note and what she's doing is the joke and the ache.

**Carousel:** 1 note on the badge · 2 rack focus off the note to the room watching (reverse shot) · 3 the note peeled off and stuck to the back of a program handout, now a bookmark · 4 fact + sign-off. Placeholder: "This cycle."

**Tie to The Room:** "next cycle" is someone else's timeline; the room is now.

**Program fit:** MS best (literal HR language). MFA: the note becomes a rejection-slip echo ("not right for us at this time") clipped to her manuscript at the Willow Springs table, where she is now the one reading submissions. MOT: "need more clinical exposure" on a career-changer's planner, inside 24 weeks of fieldwork.

## 3. THE CHAIR WITH HER NAME TAPED ON

**Concept sentence:** "need more experience" has a seat in the room, and it's empty, because she's standing up.

**Key image:** reverse shot. Front row of watchers. One seat in the row has a strip of masking tape: "MORE EXPERIENCE." It's empty. She is the one standing on the red band they're all watching. The phrase literally lost its place in line.

**Card one alone:** the row of faces with one taped empty seat. Ambiguous at first (who's missing?), then the light tells you everyone is looking at the person you can't see: you.

**Carousel:** 1 the row with the empty taped chair · 2 the reverse: her, on the band, shown as hand and slide/page/patient · 3 the room after, the tape crumpled on the floor · 4 fact.

**Tie to The Room:** the purest extension. The Room already shows watchers; this adds one absent watcher who was the barrier.

**Program fit:** MS (no management experience required: the RTB inverts the phrase directly). MFA (no GRE; the writing sample is the experience). MOT (career changer: the empty seat is the old job title). All three hold.

---

## 4. THE VOICE, TYPESET

**Concept sentence:** the voice in her head is set in tiny type across the room's floor, and the red band is the one line it can't cross.

**Key image:** overhead or high reverse angle on the room floor. Running across the floor in small, quiet, grey type, like a floor decal: "not ready yet · who do you think you are · they'll find out · big shoes · next cycle". The text stops dead at the red band. On the band: her feet. Beyond it, the watchers.

**Card one alone:** a floor of whispered doubt, cut off by a red line with someone standing on it. Type is the image (typography framework: type becomes the idea, not decoration). Very scroll-stopping as a pure typographic static.

**Carousel:** 1 floor of doubt + red band · 2 eye level: what she can hear now is the room (a question being asked, someone writing) · 3 proof moment · 4 fact. Placeholder: "Louder in here."

**Tie to The Room:** the red band gets a job: the threshold where internal noise ends and showing work begins.

**Program fit:** MFA strongest (the voice as a draft being edited; the type could have strikethrough edits in red). MS: imposter-syndrome phrases. MOT: career changer doubts ("too late to switch", "not a science person").

**Risk worth keeping:** must not linger on anxiety; card two has to turn fast, and the voice's type never gets bigger than her.

---

## 5. HAND-ME-UP

**Concept sentence:** someone else's blazer, two sizes too big, hangs on the back of the door; she presents in her own sleeves, rolled up.

**Key image:** reverse-shot room. On a hook by the door, a borrowed, oversized blazer or lab coat or a "big" title nameplate ("Senior ___"). In the foreground edge of frame, her rolled sleeve, forearm, hand pointing at the work. The watchers lean in toward the sleeve, not the jacket.

**Card one alone:** the empty oversized garment in the back of the room, the working arm in front. Big shoes extended to the whole outfit: you don't grow into someone else's clothes, you show up in yours.

**Carousel:** 1 jacket on the hook + her arm · 2 the room watching · 3 the jacket still on the hook as the room empties, she walks out without it · 4 fact.

**Tie to The Room:** reverse shot unchanged; the barrier is a background prop, the person is the foreground.

**Program fit:** MS (the boss's blazer). MOT (an oversized white coat vs her scrubs, sleeves up, hands on the manikin). MFA weakest literal, but can be a famous-author-sized cardigan on the reading-chair: big shoes for writers is "the greats".

---

## Notes for the room

- 1, 3 and 5 are object literalizations (shoes, chair, garment) that all sit inside The Room's camera; 2 and 4 use the words themselves as material (note, floor type). They could combine: the floor type of 4 could carry all the phrases in one system while 1/3/5 rotate as program-specific objects.
- 3 (the taped empty chair) is the closest fit to the Core Creative Idea and needs the least new production.
- Everything shows the work in front of others; no regalia, no campus, no smiling-student portrait. No promotion is claimed or implied; the phrases are the setup, never the promise.
