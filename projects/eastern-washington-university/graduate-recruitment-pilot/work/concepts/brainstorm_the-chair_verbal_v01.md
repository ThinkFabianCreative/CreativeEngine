# Brainstorm round 2: The Chair (verbal v01)

CLIENT: Eastern Washington University · PROJECT: graduate-recruitment-pilot · BRAND: EWU graduate programs (MS in Management, MFA in Creative Writing, Master of Occupational Therapy) · SOURCE SCOPE: project-level · AUTHOR: word-smither · DATE: 2026-10-26 · MODE: EXPLORE (no verdicts, no scores) · STATUS: brainstorm with Andres; nothing EWU-approved.

Built from: concepts/brainstorm_promotion-phrases_verbal_v01.md (round 1), concepts/brainstorm_promotion-phrases_visual_v01.md (DesignChamp idea 3, the taped chair), copy/the-room_copy_v02.md (line "Somebody's going to see this.").

Andres's build: chairs as the hierarchy of work. Stool, task chair, manager's chair, director's chair, and no chair at all if you work retail.

**The turn that makes it ours.** Every office ranks people by chair. The Room reverses it: everyone in the room sits, and the one person who matters is standing. In The Room, nobody's chair outranks the person on her feet. So the chair hierarchy is the setup and standing is the answer. It also keeps us off "get the big chair", which would be a promotion promise.

## 1. The language of chairs at work

| Phrase | Flips? | How |
|---|---|---|
| "Take a seat." | Strong | Said to you for years. In the room, you're the one saying it to them. |
| "Pull up a chair." | Strong (MFA) | The editor's chair, pulled up beside another writer's pages. |
| "The hot seat." | Good | Defense, sim lab: the seat that isn't a seat, because you stand. |
| "You have the floor." / "Take the floor." | Strongest | No chair at all. The floor is where you stand when everyone listens. Bridges retail (on the floor all shift) and the room. |
| "On your feet all day." | Strong | Standing all shift vs. standing with the room listening. Same posture, different room. |
| "Chair the meeting." | Medium | Role-as-chair. Works for MS, edges toward a title claim. |
| "First chair." | Medium (MFA) | Orchestra rank; a bit far from writing. |
| "Endowed chair." | Weak | Professorship. Inside-baseball, and it's faculty, not her. |
| "Musical chairs." | Medium | Reorgs, who keeps a seat. Cynical; could be the setup for "stop circling". |
| "A seat at the table." | Avoid as a line | Cliché. Usable only if we break it: there is no table, she's standing. |
| "Back-seat." / "Back row." | Good | Already card 2 of The Room: you watch from the back row, then you stand. |
| "Keep the seat warm." | Medium | Covering for someone. Cute; small. |
| "Sit tight." / "Sit on it." | Good | Review-speak for wait. Answer: stand up. |
| "Saving a seat." | Good | Ties to DesignChamp's taped chair: the seat saved for "more experience". |
| "Butts in seats." | Avoid | Crude, and it's about headcount. |
| "Chairperson / the chair." | Weak | Committee energy; we've banned committees as the room. |

Best flips: **take the floor, take a seat, sit tight, pull up a chair, on your feet.** Each ends with her standing and the room seated, which is The Room's picture.

## 2. Rough campaign ideas

### A. You Have the Floor
- **Idea:** All the chairs in the room face one spot with no chair in it. That spot is the floor, and it's hers.
- **Lines:** "Every chair faces the floor." · "No chair. You have the floor." · "On your feet all shift. Now on your feet with the floor."
- **Per program:** MS: "Your slide. Your floor." MFA: "Their story. You have the floor." (she reads a writer she championed) MOT: "Your cohort sits. You have the floor, and the gait belt."
- **With the line / The Room:** the purest fit. Card 1 is the chairs and the watchers; card 3 is her standing on the red band. "Somebody's going to see this." over the chairs.
- **Retail note:** the no-chair worker is the hero here without anyone saying "retail". Standing is something she already knows how to do.

### B. Every Chair in the Room
- **Idea:** The watchers sit on a mix of chairs: a stool, a task chair, a leather manager's chair, a director's chair, a folding chair. Every rung of work, watching one person stand.
- **Lines:** "Every kind of chair. One person standing." · "Nobody's chair outranks the person speaking." · "They brought their chairs. You brought the work."
- **Per program:** MS (strongest: cohort of mixed jobs, presenting to people from every rung). MFA: reading chairs, a bar stool at Get Lit!, a workshop chair. MOT: the sim lab already has the chairs (wheelchair, shower chair, plinth stool), see D.
- **With the line / The Room:** it's a casting and props note on The Room's card 1, not a new campaign. Cheap to produce, strong card one.
- **Watch:** the cohort's real mix must be true; do not imply executives in the audience.

### C. Take a Seat / Sit Tight
- **Idea:** The phrases that kept her seated ("Take a seat." "Sit tight." "We're saving that seat.") answered by the one moment she's asked to stand.
- **Lines:** "'Sit tight.' Stood up Thursday." · "For years: 'Take a seat.' This time, you say it." · "They saved you a seat. You took the floor." (folds in DesignChamp's taped chair)
- **Per program:** MS: "Sit tight, said the review. Stand up, said the applied project." MFA: "Pull up a chair. Their pages need an editor." MOT: "Take a seat, you've been told. Here, you help a classmate out of one."
- **With the line / The Room:** this is round 1's struck-phrase device with a single motif. The taped empty chair is the image. "Somebody's going to see this." only on the reverse shot.

### D. In OT, the Chair Is the Work (MOT only)
- **Idea:** OT moves people between chairs: wheelchair to plinth, chair to standing. For MOT the chair is not status, it's the skill your cohort watches you practice.
- **Lines:** "Chair to plinth. Cohort watching." · "Every chair in this room has a reason." · "You've sat in plenty of chairs. Now learn how people rise from one."
- **With the line / The Room:** card 3 already has hands on the gait belt; the chair is now in frame. Classmate in the role, sim lab, under supervision.
- **Watch:** no "patient", no licensure, no "you'll help people walk" outcome claim. Classmate scenarios only.

### E. The Chair You Left
- **Idea:** The empty chair isn't the barrier, it's hers. She got up from it. Back-row seat, coat still on it, she's at the front.
- **Lines:** "Your seat's still warm. You're at the front." · "Back row, then the floor." · "Somebody's going to notice the empty chair."
- **Per program:** all three; it's The Room's card 2 → card 3 move made into one image.
- **With the line / The Room:** quiet, human, cheapest to shoot. Weakest on the hierarchy idea; strongest on her nerve.

## 3. Does "Said in a review. Answered in a room." still fit?

Partly. It fits C (the phrase-led idea), where a review phrase is answered by an image. It doesn't fit A, B, D or E, which are object-led, not phrase-led. Chair alternatives in the same frame:
- "Told to sit tight. Stood up anyway."
- "Seated in the review. Standing in the room."
- "Every chair faces the floor." (sign-off for A/B)

My lean, for discussion: keep the review/room frame only if C survives; otherwise the chair needs no explainer, and "Somebody's going to see this." carries it.

## 4. Risks

- **"A seat at the table."** The obvious read of any chair campaign. Mitigation: no tables in frame; the hero stands. Never write "seat at the table", even ironically, on card 1.
- **Status-chasing.** A ladder of chairs reads as "upgrade your chair": a promotion/title promise and a bit grasping. Mitigation: the hierarchy is only ever in the audience; the reward is the floor, never a better chair. No "corner office", "big chair", "director's chair is next".
- **Retail condescension.** "No chair at all" can read as pity. Never name retail or wages; let standing be a strength she already has.
- **Claims.** No implied promotion, raise or job. MS: "No management experience required" stays factual, not "become a manager". MOT: no licensure, no NBCOT, no "patient", supervised sim lab only. MFA: she edits and champions others' work; never her own publication. Cohort mix in B must be real.
- **Director's chair confusion (MFA).** Reads as film. Avoid for MFA.
- **Banned list scanned:** no "count(s) in", "are you in", "show your work", "doing", "hands-on", "real world".

## 5. PRIORITY: Andres's lines ("Take a seat", "Take your seat at the table.", "Your seat is waiting for you.")

### The arc (not a contradiction)
Stand, then sit. She stands while the room watches and shows the work; afterward she takes her seat, and it's a different seat: in the cohort, at the editors' table, in the huddle. The chair becomes what the standing gives her. Carousel shape: 1 the room seated, one chair empty (hers) · 2 she stands, watched · 3 she sits back down, and the room has turned toward her · 4 the table she's now at · 5 fact. "Somebody's going to see this." stays on card 1 or 2 (the room image). This also answers my status-chasing worry: the seat is a place among peers, not a rung.

### Whose table (the twist on the cliché)
The cliché is someone else's table you hope to be invited to. EWU's version is a specific table, named, that you take by standing first.
- **MS:** the applied-project table, the one your cohort argues across after your slide. (Avoid "the panel's table" as the centre, per rules.)
- **MFA:** the Willow Springs editors' table, where you argue for someone else's pages. Only for students who hold the role; otherwise "the workshop table".
- **MOT:** the debrief table in the sim lab after the scenario. Not "care team" and not "huddle" with patients; fieldwork tables only if EWU confirms wording.

### Line variations

| 3 | "Pull up a chair at the editors' table. Bring someone else's story." | MFA | Medium: only for a student in a real Willow Springs role; generic version "the workshop table". Editing others, never own publication. Low otherwise. |
| 4 | "Your reasoning, out loud. Then your seat at the debrief." | MOT | Low if sim lab, classmate scenario, under supervision. Do not say "care team" or imply clinical practice. |
| 5 | "The seat with your name on it comes after the slide." | MS | Low. Name-taped chair links DesignChamp's image; seat is in class, not an office. |

| 7 | "There's a seat at this table. It gets your name after you read." | MFA | Low-medium. "After you read" from another writer's work she championed. |

### "Your seat is waiting for you": safe use
As written it reads as guaranteed admission or a reserved cohort spot (MOT 32, MFA 12+12 are real limits). Do not run it bare. Safe variants keep the warmth by moving the seat after the work:
- "There's a seat with your name on it. You take it after you stand."
- "Your name on the seat comes after the work."
- "Somebody's saving you a seat. You'll have earned it by the time you sit."
Never with "apply now", deadlines, cohort size or "spots left" in the same placement.

### "Take your seat at the table": safe use
Only with a named table (above) and an image of that table. Never bare on card 1, where it reads as the cliché and as a leadership/title promise. No boardrooms, no executives.

### Fit with the line
"Somebody's going to see this." = the standing half. "Take your seat." = the sitting half. Possible pairing: card 1 line, end-card sign-off "Then take your seat." To discuss whether the sign-off competes with the campaign line.
