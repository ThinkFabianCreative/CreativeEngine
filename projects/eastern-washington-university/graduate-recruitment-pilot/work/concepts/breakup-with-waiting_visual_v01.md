# BREAKING UP WITH WAITING, visual v01

CLIENT: Eastern Washington University · PROJECT: graduate-recruitment-pilot · BRAND: EWU graduate programs (MS in Management, MFA in Creative Writing, MOT) · SOURCE SCOPE: project-level · AUTHOR: design-champ · DATE: 2026-10-26 · MODE: DEVELOP (with Andres)

**Anchor (Andres, final):** WAITING personified as someone she is breaking up with. Line: "Stop waiting to be chosen." Everything below dresses that idea; nothing replaces it.

Built from: concepts/brainstorm_wait-vs-move_visual_v01.md (§1 letter, §2 desk, §6 signature line), strategy/ewu-strategic-assets_v10.md (rules 1, 2, 4; line conditions; red #B7142E only marks her move), reviews/2026-10-25_clearances.md (MFA Willow Springs / Get Lit! and alumni names cleared; comps via GPT-Image only after Andres approves cost; EWU room photos only with EWU permission). No images generated. Letter and text copy below is placeholder direction for WordSmither, not lines.

**Concept sentence:** she writes Waiting a breakup letter, kind, firm and dated; the date is an EWU date, and the only red on the page is that date and her name.

**Guardrails specific to personification**
- Waiting is a *habit she lived with*, not a partner who hurt her. Its voice is soft, reasonable, comfortable ("maybe next cycle", "no rush"), never controlling, jealous, threatening or guilt-tripping. Anything that reads like a toxic or abusive relationship is out (it trivializes real abuse and turns the ad into a grievance, rule 2).
- Waiting has no gender, no face, no body, no pronoun beyond "you". No bad-boyfriend codes (hoodie on the couch, beer, gaming, "u up?").
- Waiting never sounds like a boss or an employer: no reviews, no titles, no "the team", no office jargon in its mouth.
- She is not angry. A good breakup: grateful for the comfort, done with it. Leaving well is the tone (Hamilton dial: two ways of living, neither a villain; reference only).
- EWU never appears as the new partner. She is not leaving Waiting *for* EWU; she is leaving Waiting, and EWU is where her dated plan happens (rule 4; line condition 2).

## The key decision: is Waiting ever shown?

### Option A. ADDRESSED ONLY (Waiting is never on screen)
Waiting exists only as the "To:" on her letter and the contact name in her phone. No actor, no object stands in for it. The audience fills it in from their own life (Barry: imply, don't reveal).
- **Pros:** zero risk of cartoon, creepy or boss reading; every viewer casts their own Waiting; cheapest to produce; type-led pieces scale to OOH and social; the letter stays the hero.
- **Risks:** can feel abstract and wordy; no "thumb-stop" image of the relationship; depends heavily on copy, so a weak letter sinks it; thin for video.

### Option B. WAITING HAS A TRACE, NOT A BODY (the armchair)
Waiting is addressed, and its one physical presence is the place they spent time together: a comfortable, slightly worn armchair, her cardigan over the arm, a dent in the cushion, a lamp left on. Never sat in during the work; never anyone in it. She is shown leaving it (frame edge, coat on), or it appears alone, at peace, with the letter on the seat.
- **Pros:** gives the relationship a warm, specific, photographable home; "comfortable" is exactly the right charge (waiting is cozy, not evil); empty furniture is unisex and faceless; works in print, carousel, OOH; the letter-on-the-seat is a strong single image.
- **Risks:** armchair-and-cardigan can drift to stock-lifestyle "hygge"; reads as "she left home" unless the letter is legible; collides with the existing taped-chair device (two chairs, two meanings). Fix: in this campaign the armchair replaces the taped chair (one chair only); flag for Andres.

### Option C. WAITING AS A FIGURE (actor, silhouette, shadow)
A person or shape plays Waiting: an actor always out of focus or seen from behind, a shadow on the wall beside her, a second coat on the hook.
- **Pros:** most cinematic; strongest for video and for the "relationship" read at a glance.
- **Risks:** any body gets gendered and read as a real ex or a boss within a second; shadows tip into horror, actors into sketch comedy; a figure she leaves becomes a person she's rejecting (rule 2 failure); casting and direction cost; hardest to keep un-cartoonish across 3 programs.

### Pick (superseded by v01b below; kept for history): **B, built on A.** Waiting is *addressed* everywhere (the letter, the texts) and *shown* only as the armchair it leaves behind. Never a person, never a silhouette. Option C is rejected for this pilot: the boss/ex reading is a near-certainty, not a risk. If B's armchair tests as too domestic, fall back to pure A; every piece below survives without the chair.

**The armchair, specified:** a real, lived-in armchair of a modest kind (thrift-store wingback or old club chair, faded fabric, not designer), a cardigan or throw over the arm, a mug on the side table gone cold, warm lamp light. Lit kindly: it was a good place to rest. Never red. Shot as furniture, not as a character: no anthropomorphic angle, no "face" in the upholstery, no tilted head.

## 1. Print page: the breakup letter (hero)

**Layout:** full-bleed, flat-on photograph of a single handwritten-and-typed letter resting on the armchair's seat cushion (B), or on a plain wooden table (A fallback). Letter fills ~70% of the page, slight natural curl, real paper texture, daylight. Thumb-size read: warm-grey fabric, white page, two red marks.

**Stationery:** her own, not EWU's. Plain cream laid or cotton paper, no letterhead, no logo, folded once (fold line visible: she's carried it). Per program the paper changes (below). EWU never supplies the stationery: it's her letter.

**Typography (letter):**
- Body set in a sober serif with a typewriter-adjacent warmth, at a size readable in print (letter text ~11pt equivalent at A4 scale; first line large enough to read from arm's length). Recommended direction: a contemporary book serif (e.g. Tiempos Text, GT Sectra Fine or Source Serif as a free fallback), regular weight, generous leading. Not a script; not a "handwritten" font (fake handwriting is a generic tell).
- Opening "Dear Waiting," set larger (display cut of the same serif), the only hierarchy. Three short paragraphs max: what it gave her (comfort, a reason), why it ends, what she's doing instead.
- **Red marks (EWU #B7142E, real ink, photographed, not set type):** (1) the date at top right, which is her EWU date, written in her hand; (2) her signature. Nothing else red: no underline, no corrections, no stamp.
- **Ad typography outside the letter:** campaign line "Stop waiting to be chosen." set small, bottom margin, in EWU's brand sans (from EWU brand guide; fallback below), black. Program line + URL beneath. EWU wordmark per brand guide, bottom right, not red-filled if avoidable (keeps red exclusive to her marks; check brand rules allow a black/white mark; OPEN).

**Per program:**
- **MS in Management:** paper is a sheet from a good office notepad, typed and signed. Date = MS term start. Direction: "Dear Waiting, you were very reasonable... I'm keeping the job. I'm not keeping you." Armchair variant: her home desk chair corner, laptop bag ready. Proof footnote: real MS start term, format, price (from EWU program facts).
- **MFA in Creative Writing:** letter is typed on a manuscript page (header with her name and page number, like a submission draft), with one handwritten line at the end. Date = first workshop. Her name in red sits exactly where an author's name sits. Claim rule: a draft, never a publication. A Willow Springs issue may sit on the side table (cleared), unopened as a prop for place, not as an accolade.
- **MOT:** two letters on the seat: the breakup with Waiting, and beneath it, folded, a warm resignation to her clinic "team" (no person, no logo, gratitude stated). Date = cohort start / first fieldwork week. Gait belt coiled on the chair arm instead of the cardigan (her tools, already hers).

## 3. Landing hub: she writes her own breakup letter (the inquiry form)

**Interaction concept:** the inquiry form *is* a letter to Waiting. The page opens on a blank sheet of her stationery: "Dear Waiting," pre-set.

- **Fields as letter blanks (inline, not boxes):** "I've waited for ______ long enough." (free text, optional, never stored for marketing beyond consent; or a gentle select: "the right time / next cycle / more experience / someone's OK") · "I'm starting ______" (program select: MS / MFA / MOT) · "in ______" (term select, real EWU start dates, each shown in red when chosen: the red appears the moment she picks a date) · "Sign here:" (first + last name; renders in a signature style) · email/phone as the "return address" block at top.
- **Submit = "Send it"**, not "Request info". On send, the page shows her finished letter with her red date and signature, an option to download it as a PDF/share card ("my letter to Waiting", no personal data on the share version), and the program's next real steps (deadline, info session, cost) on the page below.
- **Accessibility and honesty:** inline blanks have real `<label>`s and a standard-form toggle ("Prefer a regular form?"); clear consent copy; works keyboard-only; red never the only signal (selected state also shown by weight and a check). Cost and deadlines stay plain, not hidden in the letter conceit.
- **Hub structure:** letter-form hero → three program rooms (MS / MFA / MOT: dated act, price, format, proof) → FAQ. Production path is native Instapage (memory: build intent); the inline-letter form needs a feasibility check in Instapage's native form tools. **Fallback:** a styled native form presented on the stationery background with letter-like labels, and the "finished letter" built as the thank-you page with merge fields.

# v01b: Andres's input, "Take a number / Now serving" (added 2026-10-26)

### Option D. WAITING'S WORLD IS THE DELI TICKET
Waiting is still addressed (letter, texts); its physical world is the take-a-number system: the paper ticket in her hand, the dispenser, the "Now serving" display crawling. Being called = being chosen, so **the payoff is never her number being called.**

- **Pros:** the most waiting-specific object there is (the armchair means rest; the ticket means *waiting your turn*, exactly the culture rule 2 targets); universal, ungendered, faceless, not domestic-stock, not a boss; instantly legible at thumb size and on OOH; cheap and fully producible without EWU access; the torn ticket is a perfect physical "breakup" gesture; numbers give the carousel a built-in rhythm.
- **Risks:** (1) **red clash:** real displays use red LEDs. Waiting's display must be amber/green or white-on-black; EWU red #B7142E only on her date. (2) "Now serving [her EWU date]" can read as EWU finally calling her (line condition 2): use it only if she set the date herself, never as a call (see OOH). (3) Deli/DMV jokes drift to comedy-of-bureaucracy and toward admissions-office queues (EWU as gatekeeper): never show an EWU counter or EWU queue. (4) Ticket-in-hand imagery exists in bank/insurance ads; live-ad check.
- **Three ways to show it:** (a) **the ticket left behind** (on the counter, on a chair): she's gone; **recommended**. (b) "Now serving" shows her own EWU start date: only as *her* act (she reprogrammed it / she wrote it), handled carefully. (c) Waiting = the dispenser: good as the "To:" image but edges toward an anthropomorphic character; use as still life only, no face, no voice from the machine.

### Revised pick: **D(a), built on A.** Waiting is addressed everywhere and its only physical trace is **her ticket, left behind** (torn or clipped). The armchair is dropped to fallback: the ticket says *waiting your turn*; the armchair only says *comfort*, and it collides with the taped chair. Ticket also retires the taped chair cleanly (same meaning, a better object, no furniture). Option C (a figure) stays rejected.

**The ticket, specified:** a real pull-tab paper ticket, neutral stock color (pale grey or buff, not red; standard deli tickets are often red, so source or print neutral ones), a plain number in black, e.g. **47**. Same number across all programs and channels (a campaign constant). Display numbers in amber.
