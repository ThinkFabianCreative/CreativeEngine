# Round 1: campaign ideas with a visual engine, EXPLORE v01

CLIENT: Eastern Washington University · PROJECT: graduate-recruitment-pilot · BRAND: EWU graduate programs (MS Management, MFA Creative Writing, MOT) · SOURCE SCOPE: project-level · AUTHOR: design-champ · DATE: 2026-10-26 · MODE: EXPLORE, round one. No verdicts, no scores.

Built from: brief v19, steal-sheet v01 (incl. carousel note), T1 v01, mfa_explore_visual v01, higher-ed-ad-reference (clichés: smiling portrait 11/29, campus backdrop 5/29), clearances 2026-10-25 (MFA imagery and alumni names cleared; generated images are ChatGPT image model, pitch only).

**One job:** persuade working adults considering a master's to request information. **One look across all ideas:** EWU red #B7142E and white, red used as an act (a mark, a stamp, a light, a field), never as decoration.

**Standing rules for every idea below**
- MOT: sim lab or a consenting session; no identifiable patients; patient roles played by classmates, faculty or manikins; faces of patients never shown.
- MFA: no class location claim until EWU signs off (Cheney vs Spokane divergence is OPEN).
- MOT numbers (96%, 95% NBCOT, 82% first attempt) are copy, never the scene, and need EWU wording.
- Lines in quotes are placeholders for WordSmither, not proposals. Keep "count(s) them in" off the ads (Fayetteville "Are You In?").
- OWNABLE fallback = what works with generated pitch imagery or stock-free type only, before EWU grants access.

T1 (The real work, handed over) stands as the lead; it is not repeated here. Ideas 1–8 push past it.

## 1. The Red Mark

**Concept sentence:** every field has a mark that means "you're good enough"; EWU puts that mark on your work in front of others, in EWU red.
**Carries it:** a single graphic act. One red mark on real work: the editor's "stet" or tick on a manuscript, the faculty signature line on a defended portfolio slide, the supervisor's initials on a sim-lab competency checklist. Everything else monochrome. The red mark is the logo of the campaign.
**Key image per program**
- MS: a printed slide from an applied business project, presenter's hand still on the clicker at the edge, a red pen tick and "defended" in the margin.
- MFA: a manuscript page from a real Willow Springs submission flow with one red editor's mark: "yes." (cleared imagery).
- MOT: the competency checklist on a clipboard in the sim lab; behind it, out of focus, a student guiding a classmate through a transfer while an instructor watches; a red initial lands in the box.
**Carousel:** 1 the mark alone, huge, on white (hook) · 2 the work it sits on · 3 the room that watched · 4 who made the mark (title, not face) · 5 fact + public price + request information.
**Single image:** the work, the mark, one line. Nothing else.
**Hub hero:** a full-bleed red mark drawn across the page that resolves into three program cards as you scroll.
**Guide cover:** a white cover with one red tick in the corner, the guide title set like a document under review.
**Real access:** real annotated artifacts (portfolio slide, Willow Springs page, MOT checklist template) and one sim-lab session. **Ownable fallback:** fully type and object based; generated paper and hands, real red marks drawn by our team.

## 2. The Audience

**Concept sentence:** never show the student; show the faces of the people watching them do the work.
**Carries it:** image, reversed. The reverse shot. The camera sits behind the student's shoulder; the frame is the room: a panel leaning forward, an audience at a reading going still, an OT instructor and two classmates watching hands on a manikin. The prospect stands where the student stands.
**Key image per program**
- MS: three faculty and a client partner at a table, one mid-note, one leaning back arms crossed, one nodding. Slide glow on their faces.
- MFA: a Get Lit! audience in the dark, front row lit by stage spill, someone's hand at their mouth (cleared imagery).
- MOT: in the sim lab, an instructor with a clipboard and two classmates watching just off-frame of the student's hands; a classmate plays the client, back to camera.
**Carousel:** 1 the watchers (hook: "who is this for?") · 2 reverse: over-the-shoulder, the work · 3 the watchers react · 4 the student finally seen, from behind, walking off · 5 fact + request info.
**Single image:** the room watching, line: "They're waiting for your [defense / reading / first transfer]."
**Hub hero:** an empty chair in front of an audience; program selector sits where the speaker would stand.
**Guide cover:** an audience photograph, cropped, the guide title where the stage is.
**Real access:** permission to photograph faculty, panels and consenting audiences. **Ownable fallback:** generated audiences (Inland Northwest casting, varied bodies), clearly pitch-only.
**Risk worth keeping:** it flirts with "the institution as judge"; fix by making the audience peers and the public, not only faculty.

## 3. Before the Title

**Concept sentence:** the work already exists; only the title is missing, so the campaign prints the work and leaves the title as a blank red bar.
**Carries it:** type and system. Redaction flipped: a red bar where the job title, byline or credential should be. Underneath, the work is complete and real.
**Key image per program**
- MS: an org chart on a whiteboard, one box drawn by the prospect's hand, its title a red blank; the person is visible only as a marker in hand.
- MFA: a contents page of a literary magazine, one story listed with a red bar where the name goes.
- MOT: a name badge on a lanyard in the sim lab, "[red bar], OT student", clipped to scrubs mid-task.
**Carousel:** 1 the red bar alone (hook) · 2 pull back: the finished work around it · 3 the room where it happened · 4 the bar fills with a real student credit (consenting) · 5 fact + request info.
**Single image:** work + red bar + "You already do the job. Fill in the blank."
**Hub hero:** the red bar is the form field for "your name" in request information. The ad's device becomes the conversion.
**Guide cover:** the guide title redacted except the reader's field: "[ ] Graduate Guide."
**Real access:** one real contents page, one student credit, sim-lab badge. **Ownable fallback:** entirely typographic, needs nothing from EWU.

## 4. The Handover Object

**Concept sentence:** one object passes from a working professional's hand to a student's hand, and each program has its object.
**Carries it:** image of a single gesture, always cropped to hands, always mid-pass, always the same angle and red background. Remix of the steal-sheet handshake, made into a system.
**Key image per program**
- MS: a client lead handing over a printed brief with a sticky note "need this by the 14th".
- MFA: a managing editor handing over a stack of submissions held with a binder clip (Willow Springs, cleared).
- MOT: an instructor handing a goniometer to a student, a classmate's arm resting on the plinth in the background.
**Carousel:** 1 hands mid-pass (hook) · 2 the object close · 3 what was done with it · 4 the object handed back, finished · 5 fact + request info.
**Single image:** the pass on red, one line.
**Hub hero:** three hands, three objects, side by side; tap one to enter the program.
**Guide cover:** the object for that program, alone, on red.
**Real access:** a short hands-only shoot (easy consent). **Ownable fallback:** generated hands and objects; strongest pitch fallback of the set.

## 5. Field Notes from Before

**Concept sentence:** the campaign is made of the margins: the notes, rehearsal scraps and timers students produce the night before they show their work.
**Carries it:** format as an artifact. Scanned, real-scale paper: index cards, a phone timer screenshot reading 11:58, a printed draft with coffee ring, a fieldwork prep sheet. Red is the student's own pen.
**Key image per program**
- MS: defense rehearsal cards, the last one says "they will ask about cost. answer: ___".
- MFA: a printed poem with "read slower here" in the margin before a reading.
- MOT: a hand-drawn transfer sequence on a sticky note on a sim-lab bed rail.
**Carousel:** a swipe through the stack of notes; last card is the room, then fact + request info.
**Single image:** one note full-bleed, the line in the student's handwriting.
**Hub hero:** a desk seen from above, notes scattered, each opens a program.
**Guide cover:** an index card with the guide title handwritten.
**Real access:** current students lending real notes (anonymised). **Ownable fallback:** our team writes and scans the notes; authentic texture, no people needed.

## 6. The Clock

**Concept sentence:** the proof moment has a length, and the campaign shows it to the second.
**Carries it:** type. A giant red timer as the headline: 20:00 for a defense, 07:00 for a reading slot, a session length for a sim-lab assessment. The timer is the moment in front of others.
**Key image per program**
- MS: "20:00" over a dim presentation room; the speaker is a silhouette against the slide.
- MFA: "07:00" over a mic stand in a pool of light.
- MOT: the session timer on the sim lab wall above an instructor and student at a plinth, patient role played by a manikin.
**Carousel:** each card ticks the clock down; the last card is "00:00 · Request information."
**Single image:** the number and the room. Static, works in a 1:1 thumb-stop.
**Hub hero:** a live countdown to each program's next application deadline (real calendars, urgency the brief needs).
**Guide cover:** the time it takes to read the guide, set huge: "12:00".
**Real access:** real durations from EWU (defense length, reading slot, assessment length) must be confirmed; deadlines. **Ownable fallback:** type over generated rooms; durations held as placeholders until EWU confirms.

## 7. Spokane Is the Exhibit (media idea)

**Concept sentence:** the student work goes up where the region already looks, so the proof is literally shown in front of others before the degree is done.
**Carries it:** media and format. Remix of "Shot on iPhone" and Lindenwood: real student work as the out-of-home and the feed. A defended MS portfolio slide as a bus-shelter poster; a Willow Springs page as a transit card; an OT adaptive-equipment design from the sim lab as a window display. Small red credit strip: name, program, "EWU graduate student".
**Key image per program**
- MS: one real portfolio slide blown up on a shelter, credited like an exhibition label.
- MFA: a full page of prose from Willow Springs on a transit card (cleared imagery).
- MOT: a student-built adaptive utensil or splint photographed like a museum object on white.
**Carousel:** 1 the work in the street (hook) · 2 the label · 3 the student making it, mid-work, gaze on the work · 4 people looking at it · 5 fact + request info.
**Single image:** the work in situ with the red credit strip.
**Hub hero:** a gallery wall of current work; each piece is a door into its program.
**Guide cover:** an exhibition catalogue cover: "Works in progress, EWU graduate programs."
**Real access:** consenting students, real work with rights, a modest local OOH buy. **Ownable fallback:** mock the placements in generated Spokane locations with our own stand-in "work"; pitch only.

## 8. Rule Yourself Back In (strange, risky)

**Concept sentence:** the campaign shows the reasons people give for not applying, crossed out in red by the work they already did.
**Carries it:** type collage. The excuse is set in plain black, a real piece of work (or its photo) covers it, a red strike runs through.
**Key image per program**
- MS: "I'm not a manager yet." struck through by a photo of the prospect-type running a stand-up at a whiteboard.
- MFA: "I can't afford it." struck through by the TA stipend fact typed like a contract line (no guarantee implied).
- MOT: "I'm too far from Spokane." struck through by a fieldwork map pinned with rural placements.
**Carousel:** 1 the excuse (hook; the audience recognises themselves) · 2 the strike · 3 the work that struck it · 4 the room · 5 fact + request info.
**Single image:** excuse + strike + one fact.
**Hub hero:** a list of excuses; each strikes through as you scroll and reveals the answer.
**Guide cover:** "Reasons not to apply" with every line struck in red.
**Real access:** fact sign-off (TA wording, fieldwork language). **Ownable fallback:** entirely type; ready now.
**Risk worth keeping:** leans on barriers explicitly, which the brief says to carry inside stories; might be the most effective social hook anyway.
