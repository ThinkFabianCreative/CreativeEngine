## B. THE ROOM (lead)

**Concept sentence:** we never show the student; we show the people watching her work, and the prospect stands where she stands.

**Who carries it:** the image, reversed. The watchers' faces carry "in front of others"; the camera position casts the viewer as the person being watched; copy names the program and the fact. Reading order: faces → the light on them (what they are looking at) → the red base line (where you stand) → line → sign-off.

### B1. The room per program

Rule: **the room is filled mostly by peers and the public. Faculty are present but are never the jury.** Casting ratio: at least two peers or members of the public for every faculty member in frame. Expressions show attention, not verdict: leaning in, a hand at the mouth, someone writing, someone about to ask. No one holds a scorecard. No table of judges facing the lens in a row.

| Program | The room (specific, EWU's) | Who fills it | What lights their faces |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MS** | The room where she presents her applied business project and defends her portfolio (exact room to name with EWU) | Cohort peers in the seats, people she invited from work (a manager, a colleague), one or two faculty readers among them, not in front | Slide glow, cool white |
| **MFA** | Get Lit! reading, from the stage looking out; and a second room for the turn: the Willow Springs submissions table | Get Lit! public audience in the dark, front rows caught in stage spill; at Willow Springs, fellow student editors | Warm stage spill; desk lamp at the table |
| **MOT** | Catalyst Building simulation lab | The cohort (part of the 32), a classmate playing the client (back of head or shoulder only), an instructor at the side, a manikin where a body is handled | Overhead clinical light, flat and bright |

Why a swap school can't run it: the rooms are EWU's and named (Get Lit!, Willow Springs, Catalyst); the shots are taken in them on the day. MS is the weakest on ownability because a presentation room is generic; its ownability comes from the real people she brings (her workplace in her room) and the role credits (B4).

### B2. The reverse shot (the system's one camera rule)

Camera at standing eye height where the student stands, lens about 35mm, slightly wide, so the viewer feels the distance to the first row. The student appears, at most, as a blurred shoulder or a hand at the frame edge. The watchers look just past the lens, not into it (into the lens reads as a portrait and a sales pitch; just past reads as "watching you").

**The Second Reader turn:** in the carousel, card 2 shows the same room from a seat, watching someone else; card 3 cuts to the reverse: now you stand there. MOT (classmates rotate roles in sim) and MFA (she listens, then reads) use it; MS optional.

### B3. Card-one test (thumb size, no copy)

**What a stranger sees** at roughly 2.5 cm square for under a second: five to eight faces at different depths, all oriented toward one point just beside the viewer, lit from that point; behind them, darkness or a clearly specific space (the sim lab's rail and equipment, the stage-dark auditorium). Along the bottom edge, a solid band of EWU red about 8% of the frame height: the floor line where you stand. No logo or headline inside the image area.

**Why it reads without copy:** a group of people all looking at the same point is one of the strongest cues in photography; the brain finds what they are looking at, and it is the viewer. "People are watching me, now" lands before any word. The specific room (sim-lab equipment, stage) tells you what kind of moment it is: a performance of skill, not a lecture.

**Failure modes and fixes:**
- *Reads as a lecture hall / classroom* (category cliché): no desks in rows, no whiteboard, no one taking notes as a student; seats are mixed, light comes from the subject, not ceiling fluorescents (except MOT, where the clinical equipment does the work).
- *Reads as a jury / institution as judge:* peers outnumber faculty; nobody in suits behind a table; expressions warm or absorbed, not cold.
- *Reads as a crowd stock photo:* real people, real ages, varied bodies (average and plus-size), real clothes from their lives; no matching T-shirts, no faked laughter.
- *Too dark at thumb:* faces must be the brightest elements; at least the front three faces at a usable size (each about 10% of frame width).

**Test protocol (gate for BET THE PITCH):** three rough comps (B6) at true thumb size, two seconds, 8–10 people outside the project. Ask "what's happening and where are you?". Pass: most say some form of "people are watching me / I'm about to present / perform". Fail: "a class", "a school ad", "a panel judging".

### B4. Visual system

- **Colour.** EWU red #B7142E and white, red as structure and brand, never as a mark of approval. Three structural uses only: (1) the red floor band at the bottom of every frame, the place where you stand, which becomes the system's constant and its ownable device; (2) the red end card / CTA button; (3) the red program name in the sign-off strip. Photography stays in its real colour, pulled slightly neutral so the red band owns the frame. Contrast: #B7142E with white is about 6.7:1, AA for text both ways, so white type can sit on the band.
- **Type role.** Typeface to confirm with EWU Marketing and Communications. Role, whatever the face: short line set in white on the red band or in black on white, never over faces. A small role-credit caption in each room image, like a stage programme: "Her defense. Cohort 2027, two colleagues from work, two readers." or "Catalyst sim lab. 14 classmates, 1 instructor." The caption names who is watching by role, which keeps the room human and keeps EWU off the judge's chair.
- **The Clock (tested, supporting role only).** As a headline device it reads as a sale countdown, it is generic, and no duration is confirmed. Kept in two places: a small timestamp in the caption ("4 min in") once EWU confirms real lengths, and the hub's live countdown to real deadlines (Nov 15 MOT 2 PM Pacific, Dec 15 MS winter, MFA early March to confirm).
- **Photography direction.** Real EWU people and rooms only in final work. Documentary, available light, shot on the day of a real or consented session. No smiling at the lens, no regalia, no exterior campus or skyline. MOT: sim lab only, no patients, the client role played by a classmate shown from behind or a manikin, no identifiable client faces. MFA: Get Lit! and Willow Springs imagery cleared; no cue for class location (Cheney vs Spokane divergence OPEN). Consent forms for every recognisable face.
- **Layout constant.** Room image full-bleed; red band at the bottom carrying line or program name in white; caption top left; EWU logo per logo guide on the band, right.

### B5. Executions per program

**5-card carousel** (same structure for all three):

| Card | Job | MS | MFA | MOT |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook: the watchers (B3) | Peers and colleagues in slide glow, one leaning forward | Get Lit! audience in stage spill, a hand at someone's mouth | Classmates and instructor around the plinth, looking toward camera |
| 2 | Second Reader: you in the seat, watching someone else | From the back row, a classmate presenting | From the audience, another writer at the mic | From the side, a classmate guiding a transfer on the manikin |
| 3 | The reverse: now you stand there | Over her shoulder: slide edge, the room facing her | From the mic: her hand on the page, the room beyond | Her hands on the gait belt in the foreground, the cohort beyond |
| 4 | The work, close, credited by role | Her recommendation page on the lectern; caption: "Applied business project" | Another writer's manuscript with her editorial notes (never her own publication); caption: "Student editor, Willow Springs" | The checklist and clipboard on the bed rail; caption: "Level I skills session" (EWU wording to confirm) |
| 5 | Fact + access + request information (red end card) | No management experience required; applied project; defended portfolio | Selective TA roles with tuition waiver and stipend, admission doesn't guarantee one; no GRE | 2.5 years, Catalyst Building, cohort of 32; outcomes in EWU wording, kept apart from licensure |

Line placeholders for WordSmither: "Pick the room you want to impress." · "Somebody's going to see this." · "Thirty-two people are about to watch you get it right." T1 sign-off "Before you graduate, you'll have…" is available for card 5.

**Single image per program:** card 1 with the line on the red band. MS: the room in slide glow with her colleagues in the second row. MFA: Get Lit! front rows. MOT: the cohort around the plinth, classmate-as-client from behind at the frame edge.

**Hub hero (one hub per program):** the room, wide, from where the student stands; the red floor band runs across the bottom and holds the request-information action: the form begins on the spot where you would stand. On scroll the camera "steps forward" (one slow push-in; static under reduced motion). The deadline countdown sits in the band. DCO variants swap the room photo.

**Guide cover:** the room cropped to the front rows, title set in white on a deeper red band (about a third of the cover), credit caption top left naming who is in the room. Accessible PDF; alt text describes the room and who is in it.

### B6. Ownable fallback and comp approach before access

1. **Real room plates.** EWU's own photography of Catalyst, Get Lit! and Willow Springs (MFA imagery cleared; request Catalyst images from Marketing and Communications) or permitted scout photos of the spaces. Empty plates are backgrounds for comps only.
2. **Stand-in watchers we photograph.** Our own team, lit to match each plate (front light from the presenter's position), composited in, labeled "pitch comp, stand-ins". This avoids generic generated audiences, the weakness the convergence flagged.
3. **Fallback system that runs without people:** the red band, the caption naming who will watch ("14 classmates, 1 instructor"), over an empty real room with chairs turned toward you. Weaker, but honest and runnable with real EWU photography.
4. **Generated watchers composited on real plates:** only if image generation is later authorized; pitch-only, ChatGPT image model per clearances; casting per standing rules.
5. **Access to lock now, with owner and date at kickoff:** MOT program director for a consented sim-lab session (student and instructor consent forms; no patients; clinical partner permission is not needed for the lab, only for fieldwork sites, which we do not shoot); an MS presentation date and its room, with the student's invited guests consenting; Get Lit! event photography rights for 2027 or archive.

## C. BEFORE THE TITLE (second route, developed visually)

**Concept sentence:** the work is already finished and real; only the title is missing, so we print the work and leave the title as a red blank.

**Who carries it:** type and a real object. The red blank is the device; the real work around it is the proof.

### C1. The blank per program

| Program | Object | The blank | What fills it (never a job) |
|---|---|---|---|
| MS | A real name badge or email signature of someone already running the work, photographed on the job | Red bar over the title line | "MS in Management candidate, applied project lead" |
| MFA | A literary magazine masthead or contents page with editorial credits | Red bar where an editor's credit would be, next to another writer's story she edited | "Student editor, Willow Springs" |
| MOT | Lanyard badge clipped to scrubs, sim lab behind | Red bar on the title line | "OT student" (always; never a credential or licence) |

### C2. Card-one test

At thumb size: a white or near-white field, a recognisable object (badge, masthead) and a solid red bar exactly where the eye expects a title. Reads without copy as "something is missing / name to come", because the bar sits on the known location of the title on a known object. Risk: reads as censorship/redaction (black bars are the redaction code, red is less so; keep the bar flat and clean, no "classified" cues). It does not show anyone watching: C fails "in front of others" unless the room comes in by card 3.

### C3. Executions

- **Carousel:** 1 the red bar on the badge (hook) · 2 pull back: the work around it, mid-task · 3 the room watching (B's reverse shot) · 4 the bar fills with the program credit · 5 red end card, fact + request information.
- **Single image:** object + red bar + one line, e.g. placeholder "You already do the job." (WordSmither; avoid the "fill in the blank" / "count" family if it reads as Fayetteville).
- **Hub hero:** the red bar is the request-information "your name" field.
- **Guide cover:** "[red blank] Graduate Guide", with the program credit as the subtitle.
- **Fallback:** almost entirely typographic and object-based; ready now with our own photographed badges and mocked mastheads, pitch only.

### C4. Sibling or system inside B?

Make it the hub and guide system inside The Room, not a sibling campaign. On its own C has nobody watching, so it misses the core idea, and in market a second red device would compete with the room's red floor band. Inside B it does the one thing B lacks, conversion: the hub's red band, where you would stand, holds the "your name" field as a red blank, and the guide cover leaves the reader's credit open. B brings the prospect into the room; C asks her to put her name where she'd stand. If performance wants a second hook for the cost-led cell or for retargeting, C's badge static can run as a test variant under B's look. Rules kept: the blank fills only with a program credit, never a job title; MOT always reads "OT student".
