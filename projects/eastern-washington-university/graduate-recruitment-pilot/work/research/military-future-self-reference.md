## 3. Translation to graduate recruitment (observation, for creative-strategist to judge)

Likely translates:
- **B, capability shown in action in front of others.** Graduate programs have the same proof points (a thesis reading, an OT student's first patient session, a management capstone pitch). This is the closest match to C2.
- **A, before and after the same person**, if real students are used, as in Royal Navy's real-rating films (12).
- **D, counted in**, when it is a professional field doing the counting, not an institution absorbing you.

Likely off-tone or manipulative for a university (patterns other critics raised, plus my read for the strategist to test):
- **E, reread label**: press coverage of #2 shows it read as provocation toward young people (Guardian, BBC, NYT). Aimed at working adults considering a master's, it risks condescension.
- **C, open-ended future tense** ("be all you can be", "your purpose isn't on this planet"): promises without a proof point; universities face stricter claim scrutiny on outcomes than forces do on purpose.
- **F, trial as threshold**: works for selection-based service, implies gatekeeping that an access-oriented regional university may not want.
- Military's core payoff is belonging to an institution that replaces your identity ("made in", "forged"). Graduate study keeps the student's identity; the field, not the school, counts them in.

## 4. Overlap with EWU C2: does military advertising already own it?

Observed:
- The **ingredients** are heavily used: belonging (British Army, #1, #3), capability in action (Marines, #9, #11), being seen and valued before others see it (British Army, #2: "the army spots" potential). Military advertising clearly owns "belonging" and "forged/made by us" in the UK and US.
- The **specific combination** in C2 (show what you can do, in front of others, *before the field counts you in*) did not appear as a stated proposition in any of the 16. Military work places the institution as the one who counts you in (the Army spots you, the Navy makes you). C2 places the audience's own demonstration first and the field second.
- Closest neighbors to C2: #2 (institution sees capability before rank, but the institution is the judge), #11 (Gen Z "already proving it" against doubt), #9 (character shown off duty in front of others).
- Risk to flag once: "This Is Belonging" is a famous, long-running UK platform; any EWU execution leaning on "belonging" or "counted in" language could read as derivative to UK-aware reviewers. Relevance to an Inland Northwest audience is likely lower; not tested.

## 5. Candidate benchmarks or references for the creatives (3–5)

1. **British Army "Your Army Needs You" / "This Is Belonging" (Karmarama, 2017–19)**: as the benchmark for a platform that owns a human truth for years; also the counter-reference for what C2 must not become (institution as judge).
2. **Royal Navy "Made in the Royal Navy" 2024 (House 337)**: real-person before/after films, place of origin as the opening frame ("Born in Paisley"). Craft reference for mechanism A with real students.
3. **USMC "Battles Won", 2017 long-form (JWT)**: capability shown off duty, in front of strangers; reference for mechanism B in a public setting.
4. **US Army "Be All You Can Be" relaunch (Team DDB, 2023)**: reference for reviving a heritage line toward a new generation; useful if EWU has heritage language to reuse. Also an example of measurement discussed publicly (AUSA paper).
5. **USMC "Battles Won Boot Camp" (JWT, 2019)**: trusted outsiders put through the trial on camera; a possible format reference for showing capability "in front of others".
