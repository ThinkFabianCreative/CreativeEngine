## How students get their work in front of other people outside the university
**MFA:** Get Lit!; Willow Springs Books; Willow Springs Magazines; Writers in the Community event.
Jonathan Johnson (poetry-track director): "One way our current students intersect with the world beyond EWU is through our successful MFA alumni who often return as Visiting Writers. For example, our MFA alumni poet Mary Leauna Christensen, who will be returning to us on March 12th, has been hired as an Assistant Professor at University of Tennessee Knoxville and has a new book coming out on Wesleyan University Press, one of the oldest and most prestigious poetry presses in the U.S. Current MFA students will have the opportunity to have their work read and critiqued by Mary in a workshop, write and deliver the introduction for her public reading, and form what will hopefully be long-term professional and artistic connections with their very successful fellow EWU poet. Another way current students are intersecting with the region is through two experiential-learning classes at EWU--Reading and Writing the Northwest, and Researching and Writing in the Field: Yellowstone. In both of these courses, students journey outside the classroom to write in and about the environment. Every student in the twenty-year history of the Yellowstone class has observed and written about wolves in the wild, and many have gone on to publish their resulting writing in regional and national newspapers, magazines, and literary journals."

## Competitors, program to program
**MFA (Greg):** "Western WA Univ.; Idaho (Boise and Moscow); UW; OSU; UW Bothell; UC Fresno, UC Davis, and UCSF; Arizona State. Mostly west coast places since many of our applicants are looking for something in the region. But also we've been in competition with Sarah Lawrence College (this year we beat them out twice, and last year too), and Columbia University (didn't beat them out)."

**MOT (Danielle):** "Our biggest external competitors are other OT programs in the region. Whitworth, the University of Washington (UW), Pacific Northwest University in Yakima, Rocky Mountain in Billings and Puget Sound in Tacoma are competitors. Particularly UW, given its relatively comparable price point and location is our biggest competitor. I recently did an informal Q&A with several of our first-year students about their application processes, and nearly all had applied to programs regionally. In talking with students, they identified several primary factors that influenced their decision to choose EWU: cost, community involvement, responsiveness and personalization throughout the application process, and their experience during interview day.
We do lose many prospective students because they are interested in doctoral education. The landscape in OT has been shifting for years to offering an entry level doctoral degree. Recent changes to financial aid have made this a little less of a concern but the perception that "doctoral is better" is out there for applicants. Getting the doctorate degree increases loan cost, length of time in the program and does not result in an increase in pay. I discuss this with applicants VERY often.
I think we sometimes focus so heavily on competition between OT programs that we overlook the fact that we are also competing with other healthcare professions for prospective students. I also asked students what other professions they had considered. Several indicated that they had considered nursing or physician assistant programs as alternative career paths. The profession of occupational therapy has made efforts to improve outreach, but we still are a widely unknown or misunderstood profession.
Key Notes:
Cost is a significant consideration. I suspect we may have gained more students than we lost by offering an MOT rather than an OTD. It is difficult to know definitively, but the students who chose EWU seem to value a faster, less expensive pathway into the profession.
Our polytechnic identity and community integration are important selling points. I think our emphasis on applied learning, hands-on experiences, and meaningful engagement with the community resonates with prospective students. This is an important part of what differentiates EWU.
Our personalized application process is a competitive advantage. Several students commented that the application processes at competing institutions felt relatively sterile and impersonal. They appreciated how responsive we were to their questions and felt that we were genuinely interested in getting to know them and identifying students who would be a good fit for our program. Many expressed that they left interview day feeling a strong commitment to EWU. I think this is something we should intentionally preserve and highlight as we grow.
UW is transitioning to the eLOTD which may create a unique recruitment opportunity for us. UW was not accepting students for the upcoming academic year as it transitions to a doctoral program. This could create a temporary increase in our applicant pool, which we may already be experiencing as we are seeing higher numbers than usual. Longer term, UW's transition may also position EWU as the only public MOT program in our immediate region, with the University of Puget Sound remaining a master's-level option at a considerably higher private-university price point. I think this is an opportunity worth exploring from a marketing perspective. We should be thoughtful about how we communicate this, emphasizing the value, affordability, and quality of our program rather than creating the impression that a master's degree offers less value than a doctorate.
I think we have a compelling story to tell. Our strengths are not limited to the degree we offer. Our affordability, polytechnic approach, community partnerships, hands-on learning, and personalized approach to recruiting students are meaningful differentiators. The conversations I had with our first-year students reinforced that prospective students are looking for more than a degree title. They want an education they can afford, a program where they feel valued, and opportunities to make a meaningful contribution to their communities.
I think those are the things we should be highlighting as we continue to position EWU OT for the future."
