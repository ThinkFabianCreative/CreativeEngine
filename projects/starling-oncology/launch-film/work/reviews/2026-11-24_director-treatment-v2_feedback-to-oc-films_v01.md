Subject: Starling Oncology "We're Not Done Yet": treatment v2 feedback

Hi Jorge and William,

Thanks for turning v2 around. The production thinking is solid. Shooting open gate 3:2 to protect the 16:9 master and the social crops is the right call, and I like that the cutdowns and the EN/ES VO pacing are planned into the edit from the start. The grid idea, where portraits multiply into a mosaic and we dive into the grandfather's tile, is the strongest image in the document. The art studio reverse into the clinic hallway is a good one too.

My bigger concern is that I can't see William's film in this yet. The treatment describes camera moves well, but it never says what the film is about or where it ends up. Constant motion is a technique. I need to know what it's in service of.

The writing also reads as generated. Lines like "Momentum Equals Hope," "Nature Over Sterility" and "human-centered care" could sit in any healthcare treatment, and the bios read the same way. There's no first person anywhere, so I don't hear a director. Our client will notice that as fast as I did.

The photography approach isn't defined either. "Natural dawn window light," "soft skin tones" and "tactile lens characteristics" describe almost every film shot this year. Since William comes from cinematography, I expected this to be the strongest part. I want to know how he'll make this look like his, and why that look is right for Starling.

The ending is the clearest gap. Everything builds to the clinic "blooming into a vibrant sky," and that gets one line and no frame. That's the payoff of the spot, and right now I can't picture it.

For v3, could you give me:

1. One sentence from William on what this film is about, in story terms rather than camera terms.
2. The last 10 seconds described and boarded: the sky, the starling, how the line and end card land.
3. Where the film holds still on purpose, and why. Without one quiet beat the momentum stops meaning anything.
4. A one-page director's letter in William's own voice. What did he see when he read the script, and what would he fight for?
5. A clear photography approach, act by act. Camera and lenses (and why those), focal lengths and how close we are to people, the lighting philosophy for home, community, celebration and clinic, the color palette and grade, texture (grain, halation, diffusion), and how framing and movement change as the story turns. Show it with stills from his own work wherever possible.
6. What makes it his. What does William bring here that another director wouldn't? Name the signature choices, whether that's a framing habit, a way of working with non-actors, how he handles light on skin, or anything else he's known for, and point to where each one shows up in the film.
7. Real references (specific films, spots or reel pieces of his) in place of the descriptive lookbook copy.
8. A beat sheet with timings for the :60, and what survives into the :30 and :15. Fourteen beats in 60 seconds feels tight with the grid build in there.
9. A specific detail for each life moment (cake, wedding, coffee, bouquet) so they read as these people and not stock footage, plus the casting approach, the music and sound idea, and what the clinic actually looks like.

None of this means starting over. The bones are here. I just need to see the vision before we take it to Starling.

Happy to jump on a call with William if that's faster.

Thanks,
