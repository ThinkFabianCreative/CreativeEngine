 00B Primer: Edwards, PASCAL and this project in plain words 
 For anyone new to Edwards or heart devices. Read this before the rest of the brief. It covers what Edwards sells and to whom, what PASCAL does, the campaigns around it, and exactly what this project is asking us to build. 
 Edwards Lifesciences is a medical device company headquartered at One Edwards Way in Irvine, a short drive from our office. It focuses on structural heart disease : problems with the heart's valves and structure rather than its arteries. 
 Its history goes back to the first commercially successful artificial heart valve (Lowell Edwards and Dr. Albert Starr, 1960). It became an independent company when it spun off from Baxter in 2000. 
 Today it has three main heart businesses, and we have worked in all three:
 TAVR / THV (transcatheter heart valves): the SAPIEN family, which replaces a narrowed aortic valve through a catheter instead of open-heart surgery. This is Edwards' biggest business and where most of our past work sits (Lifetime Management, S3UR, Pulmonic, TCT, EuroPCR). 
 Surgical: valves surgeons sew in during open-heart surgery (INSPIRIS RESILIA, MITRIS RESILIA, KONECT). We did the MITRIS brochure. 
 TMTT (Transcatheter Mitral and Tricuspid Therapies): the newest and fastest-growing unit (+47% in Q2 2026). It treats the two valves that used to need surgery or had no good option. This pitch is for TMTT. 
 2 · The medical basics, in plain words 
 The two valves The mitral valve sits on the left side of the heart, between the upper and lower chambers. The tricuspid valve is the same job on the right side. Each has flaps (leaflets) that open to let blood through and close to stop it flowing back. 
 What goes wrong Regurgitation means the valve leaks: the leaflets don't close fully and blood flows backward. Mitral regurgitation is “MR,” tricuspid is “TR.” Patients get breathless, tired and swollen, and it can lead to heart failure. Many are older or too sick for open-heart surgery. 
 Repair vs. replace Doctors can repair the patient's own valve or replace it with a new one. Both can now be done through a catheter, a thin tube that goes in through a vein in the leg and up to the heart, with no chest opening. 
 What TEER is TEER (transcatheter edge-to-edge repair) is the main repair option. A small implant is guided up to the heart and grabs the two leaking leaflet edges and holds them together, so the leak gets smaller. “M-TEER” is mitral, “T-TEER” is tricuspid. 
 3 · Who actually chooses the device 
 The operator: an interventional cardiologist who does the procedure in the cath lab. The RFP calls them “TEER operators.” They are the core audience. 
 The imager: a cardiologist who reads the ultrasound images during the procedure and tells the operator whether the leaflets are grasped well. Their confidence matters a lot. 
 The heart team: the operator, imager, cardiac surgeon, heart-failure doctor and nurse coordinator who decide together how to treat each patient. 
 Who this campaign is not for: patients and consumers. Edwards sells the device to hospitals, and the doctors choose which system their program uses. Everything here is HCP (healthcare professional) marketing , and paid media isn't part of the budget. 
 Product What it does Valve Main competitor 
 PASCAL Precision (implants: PASCAL and the smaller PASCAL Ace) Repair (TEER). Grabs and holds the leaflets together. Each side can be grasped on its own, and a central spacer helps fill the gap. Capture Clarity adds markers on the X-ray that confirm when a leaflet has been grasped. Mitral and tricuspid Abbott MitraClip (mitral) and TriClip (tricuspid) 
 EVOQUE Replacement (TTVR). A whole new tricuspid valve, delivered by catheter. Tricuspid No transcatheter tricuspid replacement from Abbott 
 SAPIEN M3 Replacement (TMVR). A new mitral valve, delivered by catheter. Mitral — 
 Edwards says it is the only company with both repair and replacement through a catheter for both the mitral and tricuspid valves in the US. That's why its campaigns never pit one Edwards device against another. 
 5 · The competitive picture 
 Abbott got there first. MitraClip was the first TEER device and has been in US programs since 2013. TriClip was the first US tricuspid TEER device (2024). Many hospitals built their TEER program around Abbott, so MitraClip is the habit. 
 What changed on Oct 21, 2026: the FDA approved PASCAL for tricuspid in the US and approved Capture Clarity for mitral. A next-generation system for both valves is coming. That's why Edwards wants a new campaign now.

 3 TMTT therapies: PASCAL Precision (TEER), EVOQUE (TTVR), SAPIEN M3 (TMVR) 
 The tricuspid news landed after the RFP was written. On Oct 21, 2026 the FDA approved PASCAL Precision for T-TEER: symptomatic severe TR despite optimal medical therapy, intermediate or higher surgical risk, heart-team judged. The same day it approved PASCAL Ace with Capture Clarity for degenerative mitral TEER, which Edwards calls “the first FDA-approved TEER device with fluoroscopic indicators that confirm when valve leaflets have been grasped.” Edwards now says it is “the only company to provide a portfolio of repair and replacement transcatheter therapies for the mitral and tricuspid valves in the United States.” 
 CLASP IITR one-year results (TCT, early Nov 2026): 80% of PASCAL patients at moderate or less TR at one year vs. 30% on medical therapy alone, with an 18-point quality-of-life gain on the KCCQ. Use only Edwards' approved wording in any creative. This is MLR territory. 
 Abbott: MitraClip was first in mitral, and TriClip was first in US tricuspid TEER. US operators now have two T-TEER options. Abbott has no transcatheter tricuspid replacement. 
 Leadership: Daveen Chopra is Corporate VP, TMTT, and was quoted on the approval. CEO Bernard Zovighian. Edwards expected the US tricuspid launch and Capture Clarity approval in Q4 (Q2 2026 results).
