00BPrimer: Edwards, PASCAL and this project in plain words

For anyone new to Edwards or heart devices. Read this before the rest of the brief. It covers what Edwards sells and to whom, what PASCAL does, the campaigns around it, and exactly what this project is asking us to build.

1 · Who Edwards is

  • Edwards Lifesciences is a medical device company headquartered at One Edwards Way in Irvine, a short drive from our office. It focuses on structural heart disease: problems with the heart's valves and structure rather than its arteries.
  • Its history goes back to the first commercially successful artificial heart valve (Lowell Edwards and Dr. Albert Starr, 1960). It became an independent company when it spun off from Baxter in 2000.
  • Today it has three main heart businesses, and we have worked in all three:
    • TAVR / THV (transcatheter heart valves): the SAPIEN family, which replaces a narrowed aortic valve through a catheter instead of open-heart surgery. This is Edwards' biggest business and where most of our past work sits (Lifetime Management, S3UR, Pulmonic, TCT, EuroPCR).
    • Surgical: valves surgeons sew in during open-heart surgery (INSPIRIS RESILIA, MITRIS RESILIA, KONECT). We did the MITRIS brochure.
    • TMTT (Transcatheter Mitral and Tricuspid Therapies): the newest and fastest-growing unit (+47% in Q2 2026). It treats the two valves that used to need surgery or had no good option. This pitch is for TMTT.

2 · The medical basics, in plain words

The two valves

The mitral valve sits on the left side of the heart, between the upper and lower chambers. The tricuspid valve is the same job on the right side. Each has flaps (leaflets) that open to let blood through and close to stop it flowing back.

What goes wrong

Regurgitation means the valve leaks: the leaflets don't close fully and blood flows backward. Mitral regurgitation is “MR,” tricuspid is “TR.” Patients get breathless, tired and swollen, and it can lead to heart failure. Many are older or too sick for open-heart surgery.

Repair vs. replace

Doctors can repair the patient's own valve or replace it with a new one. Both can now be done through a catheter, a thin tube that goes in through a vein in the leg and up to the heart, with no chest opening.

What TEER is

TEER (transcatheter edge-to-edge repair) is the main repair option. A small implant is guided up to the heart and grabs the two leaking leaflet edges and holds them together, so the leak gets smaller. “M-TEER” is mitral, “T-TEER” is tricuspid.

3 · Who actually chooses the device

  • The operator: an interventional cardiologist who does the procedure in the cath lab. The RFP calls them “TEER operators.” They are the core audience.
  • The imager: a cardiologist who reads the ultrasound images during the procedure and tells the operator whether the leaflets are grasped well. Their confidence matters a lot.
  • The heart team: the operator, imager, cardiac surgeon, heart-failure doctor and nurse coordinator who decide together how to treat each patient.
  • Who this campaign is not for: patients and consumers. Edwards sells the device to hospitals, and the doctors choose which system their program uses. Everything here is HCP (healthcare professional) marketing, and paid media isn't part of the budget.
  • How they're reached: Edwards sales reps and clinical specialists who stand in the cath lab; big medical conferences (TCT, EuroPCR, ACC, London Valves); and digital journeys (account-based marketing, HCP email, web, social) between visits.

5 · The competitive picture

  • Abbott got there first. MitraClip was the first TEER device and has been in US programs since 2013. TriClip was the first US tricuspid TEER device (2024). Many hospitals built their TEER program around Abbott, so MitraClip is the habit.
  • PASCAL came second (US approval for degenerative mitral regurgitation in 2022; Europe earlier). It earned a reputation as the device for difficult, complex anatomy. That's a compliment, but it also keeps PASCAL out of routine cases.
  • What changed on Oct 21, 2026: the FDA approved PASCAL for tricuspid in the US and approved Capture Clarity for mitral. A next-generation system for both valves is coming. That's why Edwards wants a new campaign now.

7 · This project: goals and objectives

The goal, in Edwards' words

“Make PASCAL the brand operators think of first for TEER across mitral and tricuspid, today and moving forward.” Success means PASCAL becomes “the default, not just the alternative.”

The four beliefs to change
  • “Abbott is the established name in TEER” → “PASCAL is leading where TEER is going.”
  • “PASCAL is the device for complex anatomy” → “PASCAL is my first choice for TEER.”
  • “Treating the other valve means a new learning curve” → “My PASCAL experience carries across mitral and tricuspid.”
  • “Changing my default costs my team time and effort” → “Edwards makes moving to PASCAL straightforward.”
Where the growth is

Mostly inside hospitals that already use PASCAL some of the time: move operators from occasional to routine use, and get accounts using it on one valve to use it on the other. Then select programs that are new to PASCAL. Focus markets: interventional cardiologists in the US and Europe, with Japan and other regions using the toolkit.

What they want built

One lasting campaign idea and visual system; a messaging framework with tailored messages for each audience segment; key visuals and a style guide; and a launch toolkit and activation playbook the regions can run. It has to stay fresh as new evidence, indications and devices arrive, “not a short launch flight.”

How they'll measure it

PASCAL's share of TEER inside priority accounts, by valve and region; operators moving from occasional to routine use; second-valve adoption; and how operators describe PASCAL: a first choice, not only a complex-anatomy device.

02Our story in one breath

PASCAL doesn't need to win an argument. It needs to become a habit. We're the partner already inside the Edwards brand, ready on November 16, with one lasting idea built in chapters so every piece of news makes it stronger.

Beat 1 · The real competitor is a habit

Abbott got there first. MitraClip is the reflex: the device the team sets up for, the one the imager knows how to read, the one nobody has to defend. Features rarely break a reflex.

Beat 2 · Defaults are set by heart teams

Four hypotheses: a default is a team decision; confidence is earned at the moment of capture; the complex-anatomy label is “a compliment in a cage”; the second valve is the easiest new habit.

Beat 3 · One idea, built in chapters

Chapters, not flights. The tricuspid indication, Capture Clarity, the next-generation system and new evidence each renew the same promise instead of replacing it.

Beat 4 · No learning curve

Twelve years with Edwards: the brand, MLR review (including VAULT), the congress calendar and the people. Our Irvine office is minutes from One Edwards Way.

Beat 5 · Transparent and built to grow

$200K foundation, $400K full, priced in modules, with fees separate from third-party costs and no markup. Ready to extend to TMTT's next two campaigns.
