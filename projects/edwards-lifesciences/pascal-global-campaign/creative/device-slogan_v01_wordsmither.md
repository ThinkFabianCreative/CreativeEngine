# PASCAL device slogan v01 (WordSmither)

CLIENT: Edwards Lifesciences · PROJECT: PASCAL Global Campaign · MODE: DEVELOP · MLR off for the pitch.
Job: a descriptor that sits under the PASCAL name in every territory and says what the device does. It is not the campaign idea. Benchmark: Abbott's "sets the standard". Line to beat: Edwards' live "Grasp the difference."

## 1. Honest read of the candidates

| Line | Truth | Ownable vs "Grasp the difference" | Rhythm | Device line? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Firm grip. Gentle grasp.** | Strong. Independent clasping plus the atraumatic clasp. | Better. It says *what* the difference is, where the live line only says there is one. | Clean 2+2 parallel. | Yes. Two problems. **Grip** and **grasp** are the same action, so the two halves are half-redundant. And opening on "Firm" puts force first on tissue, the same problem that killed "a firm snap". |
| **A clear grasp.** | US-only. Capture Clarity is Ace and degenerative mitral only, so it can't be a global descriptor. | Weak. It reads as a pun on "understand" and sounds like a campaign line. It also overlaps territory B, "The grasp you can see." | Flat. "A" softens it. | No. It belongs in B's headline toolbox. |
| **Grasp. Reclasp. Release.** | Most literal of the four: the procedure in order, with predictable release in the last word. | High. Reclasp is the verb a competitor wouldn't lead with. | Works as a three-beat, though "grasp / reclasp" chimes a bit close. | Yes. The purest descriptor here, but the coldest. It describes steps, not quality. |
| **Holds steady. Lets go clean.** | Real (predictable release). | Medium. | Conversational. | No. "Lets go" reads loose, and "clean" is casual voice. It sounds like campaign copy. |

Verdict: the firm/gentle idea is right but the order and the nouns are wrong. **Gentle** belongs on the grasp (the act on the leaflet), and **firm** belongs on the hold (the result). That split also kills the grip/grasp redundancy.

## 2. Variations (5 words or fewer)

1. **Gentle grasp. Firm hold.**
2. Gentle grasp. Steady hold.
3. Gentle grasp. Certain hold.
4. Grasps gently. Holds firmly.
5. Gentle to grasp. Sure to hold.
6. Gentle grasp. Certain release.
7. Gentle grasp. Clean release.

Clear / sequence family
8. Clear grasp. Gentle hold.
9. Gentle grasp. Clear result. (predictable release, global-safe)
10. Grasp. Reclasp. Release. (keep as is)
11. Grasp gently. Reclasp freely.
12. Gentle grasp. Free to reclasp.

## 3. Top 3

1. **Gentle grasp. Firm hold.** This is Andres's favourite, corrected. It covers tissue care first and secure result second, in four words, with no redundancy. It pairs best with the lead "The grasp you already know."
2. **Grasp. Reclasp. Release.** The most ownable and most literal option. It suits a clinician audience that wants the device described, not sold.
3. **Gentle grasp. Certain release.** This one swaps hold for the predictable-release truth ("what you see is what you achieve"), which is the claim competitors can least match. It overlaps with T2's "What you see is what stays." Choose one of the two.

## How it coexists with "Whichever valve. PASCAL."

They do two different jobs, so keep both. Don't let one replace the other.

- **Descriptor** = part of the product lockup. It sits fixed under the wordmark on every asset, in every territory and every market. It says what the device does.
- **Sign-off** = the campaign end line. It covers the two-valve, one-device story, appears on end frames and closing panels, and can change if the campaign changes.

The rule: never set them as two lines of the same weight. The sign-off's "PASCAL." *is* the wordmark, so the descriptor hangs off it and PASCAL never appears twice.

```
            Whichever valve.

            P A S C A L          <- wordmark (completes the sign-off)
         Gentle grasp. Firm hold.  <- descriptor, small, tracked

                                  Edwards
```

On assets without the sign-off (banners, booth, IFU-adjacent), the lockup is just the wordmark plus the descriptor.

Flag (once): this descriptor replaces "Grasp the difference" in the lockup. That is Edwards' live line, so the pitch should frame it as an evolution, not an accident. The system now uses "grasp" a lot (lead line, B, descriptor). That's fine as a family, but keep "grasp" out of headlines that sit right above the lockup.
