## What the render gives us
4000×4000, black background, paddles open (V shape), clasps open with visible barbs, central spacer, nitinol mesh in white-grey with a blue-violet inner weave. Silhouette reads as a living, open form. Problems for a poster: black background (we must cut it out and rebuild on red or grey, never glow-on-black); blue-violet weave fights Edwards red (grade it toward neutral grey or a warm white for the poster); paddles open is the pre-grasp state. Two of the three directions need a **clasps-closed / paddles-closed render** from Edwards (same CAD, second pose). Ask today.

## The test every direction must pass
4:5 portrait on a corridor wall, 3 seconds from across the hall. Implant fills 60–80% of the frame. One image, one line of at most five words, logo lockup. Nothing to compare, nothing to count, no props. Plainer than Abbott's device-on-gradient because the image says something the gradient does not: a state, a moment, a decision.

## 2 · OPEN, FOR BOTH (rank 2)
**Relation to "Grasp the difference":** moves clearly away. This is about readiness and two valves, not the grasp.

- **Frame:** the existing render, paddles open, upright, centered, 70% of the frame, on dark grey (#2B2B2B, not black). No glow. The open V is the hero shape: a device ready to take hold. One thin red horizontal rule crosses behind the implant at the paddle tips.
- **Line:** above the rule, left: **Mitral.** Below the rule, right: **Tricuspid.** Logo bottom center. One word on each side of one object: the device is the same thing whichever valve sits in front of it. No comparison, one object.
- **3-second read:** "One device, both valves."
- **Render:** uses Pascal.png as is (cut out, weave regraded neutral). Fastest to rough.
- **Award borrow:** ICU Medical "Nursing Nirvana" for the calm single-object confidence; Abbott's device-large layout is the thing to beat. Distance: same scale logic as Abbott, opposite of its gradient and claim; the two words do the job of their paragraph.

- **Top risk:** open barbs read as teeth or claws at scale (the render does look a little like a mouth). Rotate to 3/4 to soften barbs, or grade them low. Second risk: too close to Abbott in kind; the rule and words must be the whole idea.
