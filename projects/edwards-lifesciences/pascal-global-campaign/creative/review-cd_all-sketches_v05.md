### Where I disagree with WordSmither

- **On the pick.** "Reach sooner" is good craft, but its alternate is the stronger line. "First call" is the hub lead belief ("my first choice") in a peer's voice. It turns the "last" Andres found into the reversal of the last-resort habit, and it reads in 3 seconds.
- **On "Don't save the best for last."** I agree it's REWORK, but not because "last can read as last resort." That reading is the insight, and the line should keep it. Its real faults are that it tells peers what to do, and that "best" invites the reader to ask what the rest are.
- **On "It always does."** WordSmither's ADVANCE misses the brief. "Always" is "every case" by another name and implies PASCAL suits all anatomy. I kill it as a headline.

### Picture

### Lockup

It works, with one condition. Stack it as "Whichever valve." (sign-off), then the PASCAL wordmark (which completes the sign-off), then the descriptor small and tracked, then Edwards. PASCAL appears once. On the 3-second ward poster that is headline, then implant, then lockup, and the descriptor is never read at headline weight. Frame the replacement of "Grasp the difference" in the lockup as an evolution: it says *what* the difference is.

### "Grasp" overload

- With the lead now "Don't save the best for last.", the headline is clean.
- The real stack-up is C's support line "The grasp you already know." sitting just above the descriptor "Gentle grasp…". That puts two "grasps" within a few centimetres.
- Rule: no "grasp" in any line set directly above the lockup. On C hero assets, either drop the support line or move it to the body.
- Territory B ("The grasp you can see") keeps its "grasp" as a territory name and headline. Its lockup space is fine as long as no third "grasp" line sits between the headline and the descriptor.
- Across the system, "grasp" becomes the brand's verb, used at most twice per asset.

### T2: "Your first call, not your last."

T2 is not a fourth territory. It is the same insight as the lead. "Don't save the best for last" and "Your first call, not your last" both turn the last-resort habit into the everyday default, so side by side the panel would see one idea twice. That weakens the "three equals" ask; the brief asks for 2–3. Its value is the RTBs: "One leaflet at a time.", "Second grasp, no second guess.", "What you see is what stays." are good, exact, peer proof lines that the lead territory currently lacks. Fold T2 into the lead as its consider-stage proof chapter (the reasons you can make it your first call), and keep "Your first call, not your last." as a body or chapter line inside it. Still three territories: the lead (best for last), A (both valves) and B (the grasp you can see).
