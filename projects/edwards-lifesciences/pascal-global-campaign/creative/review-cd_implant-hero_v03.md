## The convergence test
An image survives only if, with the headline covered, the implant itself carries the thought. A beautiful product shot alone fails that test (it says "here is a device", nothing more). So every direction below puts the real implant in a context that makes the reader complete the idea, and the line adds the second half (1+1=3). Cold reads are **predictions**, to be checked on the roughs.

## Direction 1 · THE DAY'S GRASP
- **Key visual (DC #9 × WS #1):** the cath lab's case board, the day's case sheets (mixed MV and TV TEER, handwritten) held together by a real PASCAL implant acting as the clip, clasps closed on the stack. Edwards product photography of the implant, composited at hero scale; macro, warm neutral, the implant is the sharpest and largest element.
- **Working line:** **First, grasp this.** Sign-off: **PASCAL. First grasp.**
- **Predicted cold read (headline covered):** "PASCAL is what holds our day together, mitral and tricuspid." The line then turns grasp into understand and into default.
- **Chapters:** US tricuspid: the top sheet reads TV, line "First grasp on the tricuspid." · Capture Clarity (US/DMR/Ace only): Ace clip on a mitral-only stack, "First grasp, now confirmed" ⚑ · Next-gen: today's implant clips a sheet headed with a future date, device static, never performing · Europe: the stack's dates run back to 2020, "Both valves, here since 2020" ⚑.
- **Brief points:** lead belief (the habit, whichever valve is next on the board); carries across both valves; switching is straightforward (it's already in the day). Open ground: the moment of capture.
- **Top risk:** the implant as office stationery. If it reads as a gimmick or as device misuse, it trivializes a permanent implant. Fix in craft: real lab board, clinical light, implant at true macro, no whimsy. Kill it if the rough reads "paperclip".

## Direction 2 · ONE SHAPE ON EVERY SCREEN  (lead)
- **Key visual (DC #1 × WS #11, with WS #9 as sub):** two echo monitors side by side, a mitral view and a tricuspid view; every anatomical structure differs, the PASCAL silhouette at the moment of grasp is the same on both, aligned on one line. The implant is the only crisp object. Comp from Edwards' own echo/fluoro imaging, never generated ultrasound.
- **Working line:** **First in view. First in mind.** Sub: *Two valves. One silhouette your imager already knows.* Sign-off: **PASCAL. First in view.**
- **Predicted cold read:** "Different valve, same thing on my screen. I already know this." That is the hub belief almost word for word: whichever valve is in front of me.
- **Chapters:** US tricuspid: the tricuspid screen sharpens, "First in view, now on the tricuspid" · Capture Clarity: single mitral screen, Ace at closure, "First in view, now it confirms the grasp" ⚑ (US/DMR/Ace only, never the two-screen layout) · Next-gen: a third monitor dark, standby, labelled only "next", no device shown · Europe: both screens, "Both valves since 2020" ⚑.
- **Brief points:** lead belief (directly); carries across both valves (by construction); leading where TEER is going (the imager-led lab). Open ground: the imager and one look across both valves, both owned.
- **Top risk:** asset access. Generated echo looks fake to imagers in one second, and that kills credibility with the exact audience. Second risk: echo screens skew dark; hold dark grey, no glow.

## Direction 3 · THE RECORD
- **Key visual (DC #10 × WS #7, line by CD):** a grid of 40 identical PASCAL product photographs, each captioned like a case log, date and valve. Early rows mostly MV, then TV starts appearing. One implant repeated is the whole visual; repetition is the argument.
- **Working line:** *Your record already chose.* (CD working proposal, for WordSmither to beat.) Alt from WS #7: **What you've captured before is why it goes first.** Sign-off: **PASCAL. First choice for TEER.** ⚑
- **Predicted cold read:** "A program that keeps using it, and starts using it on tricuspid." The CCI in its purest form: what the team proved becomes how it works.
- **Chapters:** US tricuspid: the newest row turns TV · Capture Clarity: the latest tiles are Ace, mitral, captioned "captured · indicated" ⚑, US cut only · Next-gen: the final tile is an empty, labelled frame, no device · Europe: captions begin in 2020 with TV present from the start ⚑.
- **Brief points:** key takeout (our own experience is the reason); switching is straightforward (the record shows it already happened); carries across both valves.
- **Top risk:** reads as a catalogue or contact sheet, not an idea, and real case data needs Edwards consent ⚑. Illustrative captions must be labelled as such in the pitch. Also the closest of the three to "every case", so watch the language.

## Rules check
- "First" language: positioning ("first choice", "first in view", "first grasp"), never chronology or first-to-market. All three lines ⚑ MLR; if read as a claim, US-only. Keep away from any date beside "first" except the EU 2020 indication fact ⚑.
- Hard case: not subject or first beat in any direction. Provenance only.
- No Abbott/MitraClip mimicry: no grey-blue clinical palette, no clip-on-gradient hero. Direction 1 "clip" uses the word only internally; never in copy (it echoes the competitor name).
- No EVOQUE/SAPIEN contrast: none in any chapter.
- Clichés: no glow on black, no hand raising the device to light, no blueprint, no arrows/roads/bridges. Direction 2 must stay off pure black.
- Capture Clarity: US, degenerative mitral, Ace only, in every direction; never on the two-valve frames.
- Next-gen: never shown performing; static, empty or unlit only.
- Generated imagery: pitch only. Direction 2 and 3 rely on real Edwards assets; if refused, show process and real people, no generated proof.
