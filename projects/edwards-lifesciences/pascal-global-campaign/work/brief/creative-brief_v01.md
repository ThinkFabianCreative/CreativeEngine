## The problem
In many TEER programs the routine case goes to the device the operator has implanted most, and PASCAL is kept for the hard anatomy where it earned its name. Over the next twelve months US tricuspid approval, Capture Clarity, a next-generation system and new European news reopen the conversation in nearly every account, and Edwards wants to spend that moment changing the default, not running a short launch flight.

Background only, never for copy: Abbott was first to market in mitral and in US tricuspid, which is why MitraClip became the habit in many programs.

## Brand platform
- **Brand promise (proposed):** *PASCAL exists so a heart team's best work goes further: into its everyday cases, across both valves.*
- **Core Creative Idea (proposed, D-A):** *Stories where what a heart team has proved with PASCAL becomes how it works.* Each chapter of this campaign is one episode of that story.
- **Human truth (proposed):** *"I want certainty under pressure, but the certainty I trust is the routine I already know."*
- **Positioning (Edwards house frame, proposed):** **To** interventional cardiologists who perform TEER, **who** already use PASCAL some of the time but default elsewhere when the anatomy leaves the choice open, **PASCAL Precision is** the TEER system for mitral and tricuspid **that** lets the experience their team has built carry into everyday cases and across both valves, **because** one system, with independently operable clasps and staged leaflet capture, works on both valves where indicated ⚑, **so that** their best work becomes how their program works.
- **Umbrella:** it sits under the TMTT portfolio line "Patient-matched & Advancing with every case." PASCAL never uses "every case" as its own language.

## The job to be done

### J1 · The occasional user (core)
- **Persuade:** TEER operators in programs that already use PASCAL some of the time to make it their routine choice where the anatomy leaves the choice open.
- **Behavior to change:** they reach for PASCAL on demanding cases and go back to the device they have implanted most for everything else.
- **Insight (proposed, not voice of customer):** *PASCAL is what I reach for when it's hard. When it isn't, I go with what my hands know.*
- **New belief:** my team's PASCAL experience counts in our everyday cases, not only the hard ones.
- **USP:** the team's own proof. They have already shown themselves what PASCAL does on demanding cases; a competitor cannot claim that experience for them. *Pre-emptive on the device facts; owned through the team's history.*
- **Say this:** the PASCAL experience your team already has belongs in your routine cases.
- **Proof:** independently operable clasps and staged leaflet capture ⚑ (clinician literature); PASCAL Ace's narrower paddles ⚑; CLASP IID, a randomized noninferiority trial in degenerative mitral patients at prohibitive surgical risk ⚑; a US registry of about 2,000 patients reporting no major learning curve ⚑. **Conflict on CLASP IID figures, not resolved:** the pitch hub cites 77% vs 71% at one year; our evidence pack cites MR ≤1+ of 83.7% vs 71.2% at six months. Neither is used until Edwards supplies its approved wording. Contrary data for MLR: one study reports more aborted PASCAL implants for elevated gradient, and some physicians see certain leaflets suiting other devices.
- **Barrier:** Aspirational, through operator comfort. Comfort is rational, never lazy. *Whether the default also sits with the imager and the team is a Phase 1 hypothesis.*

### J2 · The one-valve program
- **Persuade:** programs using PASCAL on one valve to treat the other valve with PASCAL.
- **Behavior to change:** they treat the second valve as a new start, with a new device and a new learning curve.
- **Insight (Edwards' characterization, RFP §3):** treating the other valve means a new learning curve.
- **New belief:** my PASCAL experience carries across mitral and tricuspid.
- **USP:** one device name and one system logic on both valves. *Owned in the US for now: the comparator changes brand name between valves.*
- **Say this:** your team's PASCAL experience carries to the other valve.
- **Proof:** US: FDA approval Oct 21, 2026 for T-TEER, symptomatic severe TR despite optimal medical therapy, intermediate or greater surgical risk, judged suitable by a multidisciplinary heart team ⚑ (Edwards wording; FDA label not yet seen). CLASP IITR one-year: 80% moderate-or-less TR vs 30% with medical therapy alone, +18 KCCQ ⚑ (Edwards-approved wording only). EU: CE Mark for TR since October 2020 ⚑.
- **Barrier:** Credential. Lead with proof.

## By stage: the chapters the campaign has to carry
- **US tricuspid:** "Can I start tricuspid with what I know?" → T-TEER news, US indication only.
- **Capture Clarity:** "Can the whole team see the grasp?" → **US only, degenerative mitral, PASCAL Ace only.** Never on a tricuspid, "both valves" or non-US asset.
- **Next-generation system:** "Where is this going?" → forward-looking, framed by MLR. The team moves, never the device.
- **EU news:** "What's new for a program already on both valves?" → second valve under CE plus real European teams.

## Tone
- **We are:** peer-level, exact, quietly confident, classy and futuristic without sci-fi.
- **Never:** "complex anatomy device" as our description; "every case"; "easy" about the procedure or their skill; anything implying PASCAL suits all anatomy; arrows, roads, bridges or journey imagery for progress; the hard case as the opening beat; the category's stock words (precision, control, confidence, next generation) used as claims.

## The heart team
The operator is the primary audience, with occasional-to-routine users as the core segment and select new programs second. Around them: imagers (co-target if the team-default hypothesis holds, otherwise supporting), cardiac surgeons, heart-failure doctors, who the coverage rule requires in the heart team, and nurse or valve coordinators.
