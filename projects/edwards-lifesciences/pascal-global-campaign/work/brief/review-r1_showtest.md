## Can the key takeout and proposition be said and shown? Three rough ways in (internal test only, not for the brief)

Words only. These exist to prove the brief can produce ideas, not to propose them.

1. **The case log.** Treat the team's PASCAL history as an artifact they already own: the procedural record, the count, the names on the hard days. The idea lives in making that record visible on an ordinary day. Proves J1 can be shown, but only if the brief lets the record be the image.
2. **Same hands, other valve.** One team, one system, filmed on mitral and then tricuspid with nothing changed but the anatomy. The sameness is the proof. Proves J2 visually; still needs a verbal turn the brief doesn't supply.
3. **The quiet day.** Deliberately undramatic: PASCAL in a routine, unremarkable list, where its presence is the news. Works with the "no hard case opener" rule, but risks invisibility unless the brief permits the hard day as contrast later in the piece.

All three were reachable. Two of them pressed against brief lines below.

## Lines that block ideas

1. **Tone, Never: "the hard case as the opening beat."** The hard-to-routine contrast is the most direct dramatization of J1's insight. Banning it as an opener is defensible; written as a tone rule it reads as a ban on the contrast itself. **Fix:** move to Mandatories as "The hard case is provenance, never the claim or the opener in paid formats," and say the contrast is allowed.
2. **Tone, Never: "precision ... used as claims."** The product is called PASCAL Precision. Creatives will avoid the brand's own name. **Fix:** "The product name stays; the word precision is never used as a benefit claim."
3. **Brand platform: "Each chapter of this campaign is one episode of that story"** and **Deliverables: "each [territory] as an episode of the Core Creative Idea."** The CCI is proposed, not approved, yet every territory is forced through it. Three territories built inside one 13-word sentence will not be three territories. **Fix:** "At least one territory is an episode of the proposed CCI; others may test a different reading of the promise."
4. **J1 Proof list.** All device and trial proof; nothing visual the team owns. DesignChamp has nothing to show for "the team's own proof." **Fix:** add one line of visual proof available or to request: the team's own case history, imaging of their grasps, real operators (ties to the Rung 7 dependency). Mark it as an Edwards access ask.
5. **J2 Insight** is Edwards' characterization with no human voice. **Fix:** give the barrier a felt tension (for example, what the second valve costs the team in standing, not only learning curve), labeled as proposed, so the verbal side has something to turn.

## Lines that hand the creatives the answer

1. **J1 "Say this": "the PASCAL experience your team already has belongs in your routine cases."** Usable as a headline as written. **Fix:** state the takeout as a belief, not a line: "Our PASCAL experience is the reason to use it on everyday cases."
2. **J2 "Say this"** has the same shape and near-identical words. **Fix:** same treatment, and differentiate from J1.
3. **The visual anchor from strategic-assets_v03 G1 ("the same PASCAL across contexts ... the shared read")** is not in the brief, which is right. Keep it out; it is a visual answer. Flag it to the director so it is not passed in a work packet either.

## Fixes for brief-machine, in priority order

1. Loosen the CCI-episode requirement (blocker 3).
2. Rewrite both "Say this" lines as beliefs (answer 1 and 2).
3. Add visual proof to J1 (blocker 4) and a felt tension to J2 (blocker 5).
4. Rewrite the hard-case and "precision" rules (blockers 1 and 2).

Round 2 re-tests only J1 visual and J2 verbal.
