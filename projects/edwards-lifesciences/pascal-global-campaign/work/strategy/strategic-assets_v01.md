## 1. Human / professional truth and audience insight

### Universal (professional) truth [S]

**Under pressure, experts repeat what they can read.** Mastery wants the freedom to choose the best tool for each case; live, high-stakes work makes people reach for the setup the whole team already knows how to read. Habit is how a team buys certainty. The tension: the more skilled the team, the more its skill is locked into one routine.

Grounding: RFP §1 names the habit directly ("habits rarely shift on features alone") [F: RFP §1]; TEER is a heart-team procedure with imagers in the core audience [F: RFP §4]; device choice is commonly described as depending on anatomy, mechanism and local experience [F: EP 3.5, paraphrase]. The generalization to "experts repeat what they can read" is ours [S].

### Audience insight (creative target) [S + A]

The occasional user has already proven PASCAL to themselves on the cases that matter most. What keeps it out of routine cases is not doubt about the device; it is that the routine setup belongs to the team (the imager's read, the lab prep, what is on the shelf), and the team's fluency sits with the incumbent.

- "Has already proven PASCAL on hard cases": RFP describes a complex-case reputation that "still holds in some accounts" and the biggest opportunity as programs using PASCAL "some of the time" [F: RFP §1, §4]. That occasional use is concentrated in hard anatomy is [A1].
- "The default is a team decision": DRAFT hypothesis 01 [A2]. Not evidenced; it is the most load-bearing assumption in this document.
- "Fluency sits with the incumbent": Abbott first to market; MitraClip "the habit in many programs" [F: RFP §1]; larger MitraClip evidence base [F: EP 2.11].

**Creative target (PROPOSED):** the operator who uses PASCAL some of the time, and the imager beside them, treated as one pair: the two people who share the grasp. Primary market for messaging: US and Europe interventional cardiologists [F: RFP §5]. Imagers are in the core audience by RFP wording [F: RFP §4]; making them half of the creative target is our choice [S].

**Deficit check.** The insight never says operators are wrong or lazy. It says their habit is rational team behavior, and that their own experience on hard cases already argues for PASCAL. The campaign builds on what they know; it does not correct them.

## 6. The Bar on the Creative Ladder

**PROPOSED Bar: rung 6, FRESH, as the committed level; rung 7, SHOWTIME, as the reach for congress and launch moments.**

- Why not 5 (OWNABLE): RFP says habits "rarely shift on features alone" [F: RFP §1]; ownable work that stays in category comfort is how the incumbent wins. Category verbal codes are generation numbers, "precision", "next-generation", "full potential", "easier at each step" [F: EP 4, LOW, verbal only; visual codes not yet evidenced]. Default rung for the category is likely 4, DEJA VU [S].
- Why not 8 (GAME CHANGER) as committed: regulated HCP category with MLR review [F: RFP §8], $200–400K with no paid media [F: RFP §7], and an April 2027 in-market date "subject to approvals" [F: RFP]. Game-changer needs creative confidence on the client side (`creative-effectiveness.md` §6) that we have not yet seen. Name it as potential, not a commitment.
- Condition for 7: the hard-case stories are told with real teams and real cases (peer voice, ⚑ consent and claims), not product renders.
- RFP weighting supports this: strategic thinking 25%, creative capability ("distinctive, lasting") 20% [F: RFP §10].
