# Review r3: WordSmither show test (verbal)

CLIENT: Edwards Lifesciences · PROJECT: PASCAL Global Campaign · SOURCE: work/strategy/strategic-assets_v03.md §4 · MODE: strategy-loop round 3, step 3 · STATUS: PROPOSED, not approved. No lines written, per brief.

## D-A: "Stories where what a heart team has proved with PASCAL becomes how it works."

**Verdict: SAYABLE.**
**Reason:** the sentence has a built-in from and to (proved → how it works), so every episode knows where it starts and where the team ends up. That is the R2-W1 rule satisfied inside the CCI, not bolted on.

- **Mechanism:** the team's own evidence turned on itself (r1 W1). The reader completes the gap: "if we already did it, why isn't it Tuesday?" The tension is a team's inconsistency, not Abbott's weakness, which keeps it from being a comparative ad.
- **Talking-down risk: low-medium.** "Becomes how it works" can slide into telling experts how to run their lab. Guard: the team narrates its own crossing; the brand never instructs. No "should", no "time to". Second risk: "how it works" is soft and can read as process documentary; each episode needs one concrete artifact (the scheduled list, the log, the shelf) to land.
- **MLR risk of "proved": HIGH as a word, manageable as a frame.** To MLR, "proved" reads as a demonstrated-efficacy claim, and in copy it will be paired, by gravity, with CLASP IID or outcomes. It also tempts the hard case back as opener. Recommendations: (1) keep "proved" in the CCI as internal strategy language; (2) do not let it migrate into the umbrella line or headlines without MLR sign-off (the umbrella in §7 currently repeats it verbatim, which I flag); (3) agree with MLR now a surface substitute that keeps the from-to (the team's experience, what the team has done, its cases) so creative is not blocked if "proved" fails. The idea survives the substitution; the stopping power drops a notch.
- Length (13 words) is fine for a CCI. Do not compress at the cost of the from.

## D-B: "Stories where a heart team's PASCAL experience never has to start over."

**Verdict: THIN.**
**Reason:** strong on the other-valve and new-site chapters, but it is a reassurance, not a crossing; J1 (routine case) has no movement in it, and "never has to start over" frames the story around a loss avoided rather than a team doing something.

- **Mechanism:** negation of a fear (relearning curve). Sayable in one episode (E3), too narrow to generate the system.
- **Talking-down risk: medium.** "Never has to start over" implies the team worried it would; experienced operators may hear doubt about their competence.
- **MLR risk:** lower on "proved", but "never" is an absolute, and transfer of skill across valves is a target belief, not an approved claim (pillar 3 ⚑, A3). "Never" will be struck.
- Use: keep as the E3 episode tension, not as the fallback CCI unless D-A dies at MLR and the substitute loses the from-to.

## Summary for the director
D-A advances verbally; its weak point is one word, "proved", which should be cleared or substituted with MLR before it reaches any line. D-B is an episode, not a CCI.
