## The CCI: is "ownership in proof" enough?

**No, not for a CCI, and not for this pitch.** The strategist's argument is honest and partly right: name-swapped, the sentence is weak for Abbott on substance, because for mitral MitraClip already *is* the default (no "further" to go) and across valves Abbott's experience changes brand to TriClip (EP 1.1). But:

1. **The framework's test is on the sentence.** A CCI that passes only when you add two facts in a footnote is a positioning plus a chapter plan, not an owned idea. Evaluators will not read the footnote; they will hear "takes its experience further" and file it with every medtech "go further" line.
2. **The second fact is soft.** A3 is still unfound (EP 5.3e). If Abbott ever messages MitraClip-to-TriClip transfer, point (b) collapses and only point (a) remains.
3. **"Further" is the weakest word.** It is a direction, not a tension. It risks the category "next-generation" drift the strategist named, and it gives DesignChamp the bridge/path/arrow problem the visual anchor already has to forbid.

What is *strong* and must be kept: team as subject, their experience as the argument (WordSmither's "your own proof turned on yourself"), one movement, the routine case as hero, no anatomy claim.

### Directions for round 3 (strategy sentences, not lines)

The owned truth that Abbott cannot say is the one in the habit itself: **for PASCAL, the experience already exists and is not yet used; for MitraClip, it is already the habit.** Put that reversal in the sentence.

- **D-A (habit reversal):** "Stories where what a heart team has proved with PASCAL becomes how it works." Swap: "what a team has proved with MitraClip becomes how it works" is already true for most teams; there is no story. Owned by the market fact (RFP §1), not by A3. Hosts J1 (proved on demanding cases → routine), J2/J3 (how it works across valves), S2 and E4 (how the whole team works).
- **D-B (nothing restarts):** "Stories where a heart team's PASCAL experience never has to start over." Owns the one-name fact in the wording (case to case, valve to valve, site to site) and carries shift 4 directly. Weaker on J1 and exposed if A3 appears; test it, do not default to it.
- **D-C (keep V1, add the owner):** a V1 variant that names the gap between proof and use, e.g. "Stories where a heart team's PASCAL experience finally gets to work." Test only if WordSmither finds A and B stiff.

Each must be re-run on all seven tests plus swap, with the hard-case rule and R1 boundary unchanged. My lean is D-A: it is the insight stated as a story engine, it survives A3, and it is the clearest reversal for a jury.

## Can this CCI win a pitch criterion weighted 20% on a distinctive, lasting idea?

**As V1: no. It would score mid-pack.** It is lasting (every indication, valve and data release is another chapter) but not distinctive: in the room it sounds like a well-built positioning, and the competing agencies' "precision", "confidence" and "control" ideas will sound equally plausible. Its quality would show in the 25% strategic-thinking section, not in the 20% idea section.

**With an owned reversal in the sentence (D-A or equivalent): yes, it can lead.** The strategy underneath is genuinely ownable: one name across both valves, a reputation the operator has already confirmed with their own hands, and a habit Abbott owns and cannot ask anyone to leave. What wins the 20% is a sentence a jury can repeat that only PASCAL could say, then one or two executions that make it visible (rung 6 committed). The strategy is ready for that; the sentence is one round away.
