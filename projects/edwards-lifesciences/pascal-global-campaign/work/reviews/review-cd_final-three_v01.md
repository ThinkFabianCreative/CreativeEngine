# 1. Concept 02 image: trapeze
The made catch solves the uncertainty. It does not yet solve two other readings.
- Solved: a locked catch is the moment after the risk. No drop, no net, no circus is the right brief.
- New risk A, rescue: a tight crop of two clasped forearms is the stock "helping hand" picture. If the arms hang vertically it reads as someone being saved from a fall, which is the patient-in-danger story.
- New risk B, generic: without any trapeze cue it is a teamwork poster.
Smallest fix (one image):
- Arms horizontal and taut, in mid-swing. Never vertical.
- Chalk on the hands and tape on the wrists, so it reads as trained athletes doing what they do every night.
- Both hands gripping the other's wrist: the double lock. That is the argument. Each side holds independently, like the two clasps.
- Implant beside the lock at the same scale, same angle.
- In the room, call it "the catcher's lock", not "trapeze".
If it still fails on the layout: a mooring line made fast on a dock cleat, a hand pulling it taut, hull closing on the dock. The gap really closes by the hold, "Know it holds" is the literal check, and it is an everyday act, which helps "routine". Weaker on living tissue, so it is the fallback, not the preference.

# 3. Peerless: is the concern real?
Yes, both parts.
- "Peerless", luxury and "Stand apart" all say few and exclusive. The RFP asks for the default, used routinely. A default is what the pack uses.
- The peer wordplay is English only. "Peer-reviewed. Peerless." does not survive Japanese, German or French. "Peerless" also sets the operator apart from the heart team.
- "Taking TEER where no peer has gone before" reads as a Star Trek joke.
Smallest fix:
- Subline on divider and billboard: "Lead where TEER is going." (the RFP's own belief, and it translates).
- Sign-off: "Set the standard." in place of "Stand apart." Others follow a standard; that is how a default is made.
- Rationale insight: cut "keeps great operators in the pack". Use "Habit decides more cases than evidence does."
- Luxury treatment: instrument-maker precision and Edwards heritage, not exclusivity.
- Keep "Peer-reviewed. Peerless." as a US and UK line and say so out loud. Outside English the idea runs on the meaning (second to none), not the pun.

# 4. "Art" and "masters": is the concern real?
Partly. "Art" alone says rare and difficult; "Next-level" says advanced cases. "Made repeatable" already answers it, but it is buried in the brochure.
Smallest fix:

- Billboard second line: "Every case, within your grasp." in place of "Next-level TEER is within your grasp".
- How it extends: add "One craft, mitral and tricuspid." so the idea carries its belief.
