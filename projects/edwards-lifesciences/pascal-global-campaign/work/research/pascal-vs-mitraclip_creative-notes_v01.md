# PASCAL vs MitraClip: notes for creative development

## Facts we can lean on
- **The incumbent and the challenger.** MitraClip was FDA-approved in 2013 and is now on its 4th generation. PASCAL was approved for DMR in Sept 2022. Doctors learned TEER on MitraClip, so their habit is MitraClip.
- **Outcomes are a tie on the big measures.** CLASP IID and meta-analyses show PASCAL is non-inferior on safety, survival and HF hospitalization. Over 90% of patients reach MR ≤2+ with either device. Some data hints PASCAL holds MR ≤1+ better over time (brochure: 79% at 2 years).
- **The real differences are design:**
  - **Spacer.** PASCAL's leaflets close against a central spacer. MitraClip pulls them straight together.
  - **Nitinol paddles.** They are broad and contoured and flex with every heartbeat. MitraClip's arms are stiffer.
  - **Elongation.** PASCAL stretches into a slim profile to get past the chords and to retrieve the implant safely.
  - **Atraumatic clasp, reclasp, predictable release.** The MR you see before release is the MR you get after (brochure: 98% same or better).
  - **Complex anatomy.** PASCAL is favored for wide gaps, large jets and misaligned leaflets.

## Be careful with these
- **Independent leaflet capture is not unique.** MitraClip G4 grasps independently too. Use it as a PASCAL truth, never as "only PASCAL".
- **"Pillow" is a lay word.** The spacer is a nitinol-mesh structure. "Pillow" is fine as a metaphor in a 12-year-old explainer, but never as a device description.
- **Tone (Andres, 2026-11-11): competitive and aggressive.** Knocking MitraClip and the habit of using it is allowed; keep the facts accurate.

## What this means for the work
1. **Challenger, head-on.** Attack the habit and the old clip directly. Outcomes tie, so the fight is on design, anatomy and the operator's craft.
2. **Pinch vs hold** is the cleanest plain-English contrast: a clothes-pin pulls two edges together; PASCAL holds them closed around a spacer. "MitraClip pinches. PASCAL holds." (slide 00b)
3. **Hard cases are PASCAL's home ground.** Wide gaps, messy anatomy, misaligned leaflets. Lines and images can go where the textbook valve isn't:
   - "For the valves that don't read the textbook."
   - "Built for the case you'd rather not get."
4. **Gentleness is part of mastery.** Flex, an atraumatic clasp, tissue preserved. The grip of an expert is firm without crushing. "Gentle grasp. Firm hold." already says this.
5. **Calm control.** Grab one leaflet, check it, adjust, reclasp, see the result before release. The emotional benefit for the operator is composure: fewer surprises, no guessing.
6. **Newer, and designed with hindsight.** PASCAL was built after a decade of MitraClip learning. "The next turn of the screw" (the next-gen chapter) fits.

## Image hooks

- The spacer as the thing that makes the closure soft: two hands meeting around a central object rather than palm on palm.
