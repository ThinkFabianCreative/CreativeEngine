## The test these metaphors must pass

A grab metaphor only earns its place if it dramatizes something PASCAL does that a heart-ward HCP recognizes as PASCAL's: atraumatic grasp of a moving, wet leaflet (gentle) and a secure, durable coaptation (firm), with independent clasps. A generic "things that hold together" image says "closure device", which is MitraClip's category too. The analog has to carry *gentle and firm at once*, or *two edges becoming one*, or it is decoration.

## 1. vGrab3 · Zipper (PASCAL as the slider)

- **Concept sentence:** Two loose edges become one line, and PASCAL is what pulls them together.
- **Cold read (3 s, interventional cardiologist):** Pass. Open V above, closed teeth below, device at the junction: that is a TEER procedure diagram any operator already carries in their head (leaflet edges, edge-to-edge repair). It reads faster than the frog. Risk: at thumbnail it reads "zipper" first and "PASCAL" second unless the implant is large and photoreal.
- **Brief fit:** Strong. Edge-to-edge is literally the mechanism; "one line" maps onto coaptation. Fits mitral and tricuspid equally, so it serves J1 and J2 without labels.
- **Implant hero:** Yes, structurally. The slider is the single active element and sits at the focal point. It is the only drawing where the device *does* the idea rather than stands beside it.
- **Done-before risk:** High in general (zipper for closure, wound care, hernia mesh, "zip up" ASD closure work). The rescue is the specificity: the slider is the implant itself, drawn true to PASCAL's paddles and clasps. Run the "done before" check (ad-reference skill) before comps lock.
- **Line:** It does not carry "Gentle grasp. Firm hold." well: a zipper is firm, not gentle. Its natural line is the tricuspid chapter, "Get a grip on tricuspid.", or retention, "Hold on to what works." The device slogan still sits on the piece as the sign-off per the fixed system.
- **Verdict: ADVANCE.**

Redraw notes:
1. Make the implant photoreal and roughly 40% of frame height; the zipper stays light line work (imagery-light rule), so the device is the only "real" object.
2. Replace mechanical teeth with two soft, slightly irregular leaflet-like edges above that become a clean seam below. Organic above, resolved below: that adds the missing *gentle*.
3. Show the clasps visibly engaged on each edge, one slightly ahead of the other, to imply independent clasp operation without a callout.
4. Kill the V symmetry for one variant: asymmetric edges (tricuspid has three leaflets; consider a three-edge "Y" zipper as the tricuspid chapter execution).
5. Headline top or bottom, never crossing the seam.

## 2. vGrab1 · Tree-frog toe pads on a wet leaf

- **Concept sentence:** A tree frog holds a wet, moving leaf without leaving a mark; so does PASCAL.
- **Cold read:** Partial. Gets "grip on a wet surface" in 3 s. Does not get "leaflet" unless the leaf is read as a pun, and the cartoon frog reads children's book, not cardiology. The subhead does the work, which fails the Barry cold read.
- **Brief fit:** Good on the device truth (atraumatic, wet, moving tissue) and the only one that genuinely carries *gentle and firm* together, so it is the best match for the slogan. Weaker on the key takeout (experience carries over); it is a device-feature ad.
- **Implant hero:** No. Device stands beside the frog as a comparison object; the frog wins the eye.
- **Done-before risk:** High. Gecko/tree-frog adhesion is a biomimicry staple (surgical adhesives, tape, grippers, robotics). Likely seen by this audience.
- **Line:** "Gentle grasp. Firm hold." as headline.
- **Verdict: REWORK.** The truth is right; the staging is wrong.

Redraw notes:
1. Lose the cartoon. Macro photograph of a real toe pad on a wet leaf, cropped tight so the pad and the leaf *edge* fill the frame.
2. Fuse, don't compare: the PASCAL clasp holds the same leaf edge in the same frame, at the same scale as the toe pad, so the eye reads one gesture twice. Or drop the frog entirely and keep only the wet leaf with water beading, gripped by the photoreal implant (the leaf is the leaflet pun; the water says "beating, wet").
3. If after the redraw the frog still outranks the implant, KILL it and keep only the wet-leaf version.

## 3. vGrab2 · Interlocked carabiners

- **Concept sentence:** Two links locked; one system that holds both valves.
- **Cold read:** Fail on PASCAL. Gets "linked / secure" in 3 s; does not get device, valve or grasp. Carabiners are climbing safety, and to a cardiologist read "chain" or "two devices", which argues against "one system".
- **Brief fit:** Aims at J2 (both valves), but the image shows two objects joined, not one system working on both. Logic is inverted.
- **Implant hero:** No. The device is an afterthought with a dotted line to nowhere.
- **Done-before risk:** Very high. Carabiner/chain link is stock "trust/partnership" imagery across finance and B2B.
- **Line:** "Two valves. One grasp." is a fair line but is a new line outside the fixed set; it should not reopen the system.
- **Verdict: KILL.**

## Ranking and system recommendation

Agree with zipper > frog > carabiners.

- **Zipper becomes a chapter execution, not a new chapter.** Use it as the visual for "Get a grip on tricuspid." (three-edge variant) and optionally a mitral twin under "Hold on to what works." It enters through existing lines, so the system does not grow.
- **Frog/wet leaf becomes the device-slogan proof piece,** headlined "Gentle grasp. Firm hold.", only if the rework puts the implant first. It can sit inside "Facts to grasp onto." as the one metaphor in a fact-led chapter.
- **No grab metaphor replaces Lead C.** "Don't save the best for last." is about experience and habit; grab metaphors are about the device. Keep metaphors as chapter texture, so the pitch does not split into two campaigns.
- **Carabiners:** drop. If Andres wants "Two valves. One grasp." it is a body-copy line, not a headline.
