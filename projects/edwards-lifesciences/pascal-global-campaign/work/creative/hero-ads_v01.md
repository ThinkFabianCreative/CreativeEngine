The test for each ad: a stranger gets it in 3 seconds from the image and the headline together. None says "every case", none puts the hard case at the center, none names a competitor, and only Ad C carries an indication claim.

---

## Ad A · Precedent × The Room Remembers (J1, occasional user)

- **IMAGE:** A real cath lab shot as one long exposure: the equipment is sharp, the team shows up as soft see-through figures in the spots where they always stand, and one thin Edwards-red trace glows over the table where the operator's hands work.
- **HEADLINE:** This room already knows what to reach for.
- **SUPPORT:** Your team's PASCAL experience is already part of this room, so let it do the routine cases too.
- **SIGN-OFF:** Your cases set the precedent. + PASCAL Precision lockup
- **WHY IT WORKS:** The photo shows the habit built up in the room, and the headline says that habit should decide the ordinary case.
- **ALTERNATE HEADLINE:** You've already ruled on this.

## Ad B · Tuesday × The Ordinary Board (J1, occasional user)

- **IMAGE:** A flat, documentary photo of a cath-lab whiteboard for an ordinary Tuesday: a handful of handwritten rows (times, room, initials redacted), and on two of the routine rows the word "PASCAL" in Edwards-red marker, in the same plain handwriting as everything else; the rest is dark grey, nothing circled.
- **HEADLINE:** Nobody circled it. That's the point.
- **SUPPORT:** What your team proved with PASCAL belongs on the normal days too.
- **SIGN-OFF:** Bring it to Tuesday. + PASCAL Precision lockup
- **WHY IT WORKS:** PASCAL on an unremarkable schedule, with nobody making a fuss about it, is exactly what "routine" looks like.
- **ALTERNATE HEADLINE:** The best thing on this board is how ordinary it looks.
- **Note:** only two rows say PASCAL, never the whole board, so it can't read as "every case" or all anatomy.

## Ad C · Home Valve × Same Hands (J2, one-valve program)

- **IMAGE:** Two photos side by side, same lab, same framing, same team (operator, imager, nurse) in the same places; the left one is labeled "Mitral", the right "Tricuspid", and only the image on the monitor is different; a thin Edwards-red rule divides the panels.
- **HEADLINE:** Spot the difference.
- **SUPPORT:** PASCAL Precision is now FDA approved for tricuspid TEER in indicated patients ⚑, so your team starts the second valve as the team it already is.
- **SIGN-OFF:** Both valves. Same team. + PASCAL Precision lockup
- **WHY IT WORKS:** The reader looks for a difference, finds only the label, and gets it: on the second valve you aren't beginners.
- **ALTERNATE HEADLINE:** New valve. Nobody new.
- **Notes:** US asset only (US indication ⚑; full indication and ISI per Edwards wording). "Spot the difference" promises the same people and standing, not identical technique ⚑. MLR should confirm it doesn't read as a same-procedure claim. No Capture Clarity on this ad, because it is mitral-only.
