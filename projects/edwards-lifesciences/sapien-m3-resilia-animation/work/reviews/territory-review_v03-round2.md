## 1. Round verdict

The rocks critique is right and the round is a real step forward: all three routes have a verb. But the round produced one A route, one route that duplicates T03, and one emotional route. That is the right shape for a slate only after one KILL and two REWORKs.

## 2. Per territory

### R2-1 The Gather (A) · REWORK

**Keep:** the verb is the picture. "Draws in" becomes one visible motion; tension (irregular material) and resolution sit in one shot; held-then-travels earns the forward move without the glass heart. Ownability is the best of the round: a radial-force competitor's logo on an inward film says something untrue about their device.

**The main session's concern is confirmed, and it is worse than "too tight".** For this audience, a red thread drawn around soft tan material and tightened is not a metaphor. It is a purse-string suture, a cerclage or a tourniquet. Structural heart teams and surgeons tie purse-strings for a living; they will read the literal procedure first and the analogy second, if ever. Red thread on fibre also reads as blood vessel or bleeding. And the cinch makes the red the agent of constriction, so the brand colour stands for squeeze. No amount of slow timing fixes it, because the problem is the object, not the speed.

**Rework directions (design-champ):**
1. **Remove the thread as the agent.** The gathering force is invisible: a slow turning current in sand or water, a field (iron-filing logic), the vortex in a draining basin, grains settling toward a centre. The viewer sees material *coming* in, not something *pulling* it in. Nothing encircles; nothing ties.
2. **Move the red.** The single red is what the gathering makes room for, the point at the centre that everything settles around and that then moves forward. Red = the heart that leads, not the binder. This also gives "heart moving forward" one object to carry from beat 1 to the end card.
3. **Material must yield, not compress.** Fibres and grains settle and nest; the final form is softer and rounder than the start, never tighter or bunched. Test the end state for "knot" and "bundle" reads.
4. **Absorb nature v02's best shots as material options**, not as a montage: the fern curl or a closing anemone are also inward motions without a binder. Keep one world (granular or botanical), not both.
5. **Category check:** sand-swirl and ink-in-water are wellness and luxury-fragrance stock. The type and the single red must make it cardiac within one read; the line "We draw it in." does the linking and stays.
6. Claims (noted only): "draws the anatomy in" stays in R3 wording; the abstract leaflet beat must not imply tissue-behaviour claims (Q29 still open on the evidence line).

### R2-2 Many Hands, One Imprint (B) · KILL as a route

The image is new; the idea is T03's. Two B routes cannot both go to Edwards, and T03 is already in the deck and further developed. R2-2 also carries two problems T03 doesn't: **pressing is pushing**, the exact action proposition A defines Edwards against, so it puts the competitor's mechanism on screen; and a seal pressed into soft material reads to clinicians as forcing tissue to a shape (radial force, crush), not as a standard landing zone. Wax seals and letterpress are also a craft/luxury cliché.

**Salvage into T03:** the rolling procession of identical circles as T03's forward move, and the Becher-style typology grid as a still for the :60 or deck. Not the press.

### R2-3 The Door That Opened (C) · REWORK, advance as the provocative third

**Keep:** the only route whose "heart moving forward" is literal and structural (the camera goes through); the strongest emotional range; the per-door text cascade is a good text-on-screen device.

**Problems:**
1. **Cliché.** Corridor of doors, one opens, light pours in, is hope-and-opportunity stock (and afterlife imagery, which is the worst read in cardiac). Barragán colour must do real work: no glowing light at the end, no Turrell halo.
2. **Audience fit.** As written it is the patient's story, aimed at a physician audience. Reframe for HCPs: the closed doors are the physician's own "no", the patients the heart team had to turn away. Same picture, different owner. Lines read as the team's verdicts, not the patient's feelings.
3. **Red discipline.** A red door is also exit, emergency and stop. Red only on the one opening; the other doors are beige.
4. **Furthest from the mechanism.** It needs the :60 to show why the door opened (the dock). In the :30 the 15–21 s beat must name the reason ("Fully transseptal TMVR" plus a mechanism word) or it is a generic hope film any device could sign.
5. Claims (noted only): "Not a candidate" and "anatomy ruled out" imply eligibility populations that need MLR support; depends on Q36.

## 5. First image tests (before full moodboards)

Run these as small sets (3 to 6 frames each). Each test is a pass/fail cold read: show frames without the brief or copy to someone outside the project and ask what they see. A route goes to full moodboard only after its test passes.

1. **R2-1 suture test.** Same gathering moment in four versions: (a) the original red thread cinch, as the control; (b) invisible current, red point at centre; (c) botanical inward curl with red at the heart; (d) granular settling ring, red at centre. **Must prove:** at least one no-thread version reads as "drawn in / welcomed" with zero reads of suture, tie, knot, wound, bleeding or squeeze. If (a) wins the cold read anyway, it is still out for this audience.
2. **R2-1 forward test.** Three-frame sequence: scattered, settled with red at centre, red-led form departing. **Must prove:** the last frame reads as forward and purposeful (heart leading), not drifting or escaping, and is not mistakable for a wellness, spa or fragrance frame once the single red is present.
3. **R2-3 cliché test.** Corridor with closed beige doors and one open red-edged threshold, three light treatments (flat, warm spill, no light change). **Must prove:** reads as "an opening" with no afterlife, hotel, emergency-exit or hospital read, and the red does not read as stop or danger.
4. **T03 roll-forward test** (lower priority). **Must prove:** identical red circles in procession read as reproducibility moving forward, not as manufacturing or coins.

Generation rule holds for all tests: no Edwards devices, patients, procedures, organs or anatomy renderings; one red; beige ground.
