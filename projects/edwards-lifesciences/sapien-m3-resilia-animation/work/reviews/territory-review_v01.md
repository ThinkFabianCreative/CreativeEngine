## 4. T01 "Nature meets Science"

### Strengths

- **One genuine visual idea.** The wingbeat match-cut into leaflets opening and closing (scene 03) is the best single moment in the deck. It gives R1's mandatory RESILIA moment ("a portion of the animation to highlight the new RESILIA technology… focus on the leaflets") a meaning, not just a render. The leaflets look like something alive.
- **Palette and warmth.** Warm, organic and human, and "never sterile" (Edwards visual direction). The petal heart sits on cream, and the glass-heart image on the script frame sits on beige with speed streaks. That image is the closest thing in either territory to R1's two fixed elements together: the red glass heart, and the heart moving forward on a beige/tan ground.
- **It has a point of view.** It reaches for emotion in a category that defaults to spec sheets.

### Weaknesses

1. **The premise pulls the work toward claims Edwards cannot make.** Built on "the body does this naturally", the territory keeps producing unsupported naturalness claims: "engineered to replicate the heart's natural mitral motion and prevent calcification", "lets the body return to its natural state", "blood now flows effortlessly and efficiently, as it naturally should", "Effortlessly. Efficiently. Perfectly." This is structural, not a copy slip. The only naturalness statement R3 supports is about the **dock**, not the tissue: "Native leaflet-based anchoring is designed to support natural annular motion" [C10]. Unless the premise is re-anchored on that sourced truth, each rewrite will drift back. See the REWORK direction.
2. **The analogy is generic, not specific to this product.** Honeycomb, DNA, tree rings, fingerprints, eyes, lungs, star trails: this is the "wonder of nature / wonder of the body" montage, and it could open a film for any device, drug, car or watch. Narrative-craft's ownability test applies: swap the last frames for a competitor's logo, and scenes 01–05 are still right. The nature images rhyme with "pattern" in general, not with what SAPIEN M3 RESILIA does (a dock that draws the anatomy inward, a valve seated inside it). DNA is also on Edwards' category-codes-to-avoid list. The title itself, "Nature meets Science", and the line "When science meets nature" are the "Where X meets Y" formula on CreativeDirector's exclusion list.
3. **Brand entry is late and the time budget does not hold.** In v1.0, scenes 01–05 are nature and build-up. The product name arrives in scene 06, the mechanism (dock, valve-in-dock, anatomy drawn inward) never appears, and the outcomes are crowded into the end card. At about 6 seconds a scene, that is 25–30 seconds before the product in a 60. In the 30, either the nature section goes (and the territory with it), or the product gets about 5 seconds. The idea does not survive its shortest cut (narrative-craft §10). The INNOVATION pillar is largely missing, and that is R1's most detailed request: "the dock encircling, the dock bringing anatomy inward, valve opening and closing, full valve-in-dock implant".
4. **Written for a voiceover the brief doesn't ask for.** The mood frame proposes "a Morgan Freeman-type VO". The v1.0 script carries the copy in a "Narration" column, and 6 of 10 scenes say "On-screen text · None proposed". R1: "Text on screen would suffice; no narration needed." Narration isn't banned, but in the brief's text-only film, six scenes of v1.0 would be wordless nature footage. VO also adds scope, casting and an MLR surface that nobody has asked for. Separately, "Morgan Freeman-type" asks for a sound-alike of a living actor. That needs a decision, not an assumption.
5. **Patient imagery and promise.** Scene 07: "It's a new day, a new dawn — a new life for heart patients everywhere", with "an everyday outdoor moment with a heart patient". "Everywhere" overreaches the indication, which is narrow (patients "unsuitable for surgery or TEER"). "A new life" is an implied patient-outcome claim. The improvement-montage beat is a narrative cliché. If a patient appears, it has to be approved casting or footage, never generated (Edwards visual direction).
6. **The pillars are not the structure.** OUTCOMES, INNOVATION and LEGACY appear only as stray supers. R1 asks that "the required messaging is around the main points of the 3 pillars", and the pillar icons are to be incorporated when they exist.

## 5. T02 "Precisely Engineered"

### Strengths

- **Product-first.** The device and its making are the film. That suits a launch animation, a text-only format and a 30-second cutdown, and it gives INNOVATION its full weight.
- **A clear payoff.** The device being placed into the red glass heart is a usable climax. It also gives a natural place for the R1 sequence (dock encircling, valve-in-dock implant), even though the territory doesn't yet show it.
- **The outcomes block is closest to R3.** "97% Achieved / MR ≤1+ through 2 years / ENCIRCLE main cohort" keeps R3's qualifiers ("main cohort", "through 2 years"). The attribution under the product name is still a blocker (§7).
- **Pillar coverage.** The script touches all three pillars in order (legacy line, engineering, outcomes), and the title frame names them: "Outcome, innovation, legacy at work."

### Weaknesses

1. **A category cliché, and partly on Edwards' avoid list.** Robotic arms machining a part under blue light, "cinematic sci-fi abstraction", futuristic electronic music: this is the default precision-engineering spot (watches, cars, phones, medtech). Edwards visual direction lists "fake futuristic medicine or labs", "superficial innovation symbolism" and "sci-fi for its own sake" as codes to avoid. "Innovation is in the details" is a generic line. Ownability is low: with another valve maker's logo on the end card, nothing would be wrong.
2. **It may be factually wrong about how the product is made.** The film's argument is that the product's precision comes from robots. My general understanding, NOT verified and not client evidence, is that Edwards tissue valves are hand-sewn by skilled specialists. If that's true, the robotic-manufacturing story misrepresents the product, and it gives away a stronger, truer story about human hands and microscopic stitching. Ask Edwards before developing either version.
3. **The device renders do not depict SAPIEN M3 RESILIA.** On 04_intro4, t2_a and t2_b the device reads as AI-generated: a padded, quilted fabric cylinder, a thick tube coiled around it, a gold disc or leaflets inside, and in t2_b, scaffolding. R3 describes a dock that "captures the anterior and posterior mitral leaflets", "leverages radial force to draw the mitral apparatus inward" and "pulls the leaflets and chordae toward the dock center", with a valve deployed "within the SAPIEN M3 dock". The renders show none of that mechanism, and the valve frame isn't recognizable. (General, unverified understanding: the real dock is a coiled wire structure and the valve a balloon-expandable framed valve. Check against approved reference, brief Q18.) The coil in t2_b may be meant to suggest the dock, but no clinician would read it that way. Client rule (visual-direction.md): "Never generate patients, physicians, clinical procedures, Edwards products or case-study imagery, even in comps." The glass-heart-with-device image on the T01 script frame raises the same issue. Per Andres's standing rule these images stay where he put them. **Flag:** keep them out of anything Edwards sees, or label them unmistakably as mood, not product. Andres decides.
4. **No mechanism story.** "Placing this groundbreaking technology into a glass heart" is a placement, not a demonstration. The dock drawing anatomy inward, which is the system's actual differentiator in R3's elevator pitch ("inward radial force to standardize heterogeneous mitral anatomy"), is absent. The territory shows how the device is made. It doesn't show what it does.
5. **Off-brief palette and the heart is static.** The territory is dark and red-black, with a gradient mood frame. R1: "beige/tan background color as the main color" (t2_b's warm ground is the exception). The glass heart appears, but it doesn't move forward. R1: "The visual of the heart moving forward… This will be our main concept."
6. **A list of supers, not a story.** "Meticulously designed. Exquisitely engineered." "Advancing TMVR like never before." "Setting performance in motion." These are three-adjective positioning lines with no obstacle and no turn (narrative-craft §2). It is a demonstration wearing film clothes.

## 6. Shared problems

- **"Heart moving forward" is a prop in both territories, not the spine.** R1 calls it "our main concept". Neither territory makes the heart's forward motion the film's structure.
- **"Performance in motion" / "Setting performance in motion"** appears in both territories, in the overview and on t2_a. It isn't in R1 or R3. If it is the campaign agency's line, it's useful and should be confirmed. If it's ours, it's a tagline candidate that needs WordSmither's work and MLR. Its source is unrecorded.
- **"Introducing the SAPIEN M3 RESILIA System" and "now with RESILIA tissue."** These are launch and availability language while U.S. approval and launch status are unknown (Q5).
- **"evolve":** neither territory uses it. T02's "From a legacy comes…" carries the lineage without it. Clear.
- **Pillar icons:** neither territory makes room for them. They don't exist yet (Q13, Q14), but the structure should reserve three pillar beats where they can land.

## 11. The strongest route: a fusion, with a new spine

Neither territory alone. The recommendation is to take T02's skeleton (product-first, titles only, pillar order, outcome end) and T01's one real idea (leaflets as a living rhythm), and hang both on the element R1 says is the main concept: the heart moving forward.

**The truth to build on (sourced, R3 C10):** the dock "draw[s] the mitral apparatus inward, rather than expanding outward against the anatomy", and its "native leaflet-based anchoring is designed to support natural annular motion". This is T01's "nature" idea made specific and claimable. The device works *with* the anatomy, not against it. It also makes a visual opposite, which suits a text-only film: **inward and forward.**

```
DIRECTION CONCEPT SENTENCE (working, not a line):
The system draws the heart's own anatomy inward, so the heart can keep moving forward.
```

The second clause is a campaign metaphor, not an outcome claim. WordSmither and MLR must keep it on the right side of that line.

**Beat sketch, 60 s (timing indicative, text on screen only):**

1. **0–8 s.** The red glass heart moves forward on the beige ground (the campaign asset as given), then slows.
2. **8–30 s, INNOVATION.** Push inside the glass heart. In the approved device animation, the dock encircles and draws the leaflets and chordae inward, and the valve is deployed within the dock. Then the RESILIA leaflets open and close. Here, and only here, one T01 rhyme: a wingbeat or a closing petal intercut once with the leaflets, so the "natural" idea lives in the image, not in a claim. Supers in R3 wording, e.g. "Designed to Draw Anatomy Inward".
3. **30–45 s, OUTCOMES.** ENCIRCLE logo (unchanged), data in R3 wording and qualifiers, attributed as Edwards rules on Q29. Pillar icon slot.
4. **45–55 s, LEGACY.** "Trusted SAPIEN Platform. Built for Mitral." RESILIA tissue with "surgical" and the † treatment (Q4, Q33). Pillar icon slot.
5. **55–60 s.** The heart resumes moving forward. End card, indication and footnotes as MLR requires.

**30 s:** heart forward → dock draws inward → valve and leaflets → one outcome beat → heart forward and end card. The cutdown survives because the spine is the heart's motion, not a preamble. Per narrative-craft §10, write the 30 first to prove it.

**Also worth one exploration round (WordSmither + DesignChamp):** a truthful version of T02's craft story, if Edwards confirms its valves are hand-made: human hands and stitching in place of robots. Precision with a person behind it is more ownable and fits "never sterile". Contingent on that fact.
