## 2. Per territory

### R3-2 Footing · BET THE PITCH

**Why I'm backing it.** It is the only route where the client's main concept and our proposition are the same event. The heart cannot move forward until the ground draws in; every scene is one step; three steps is a whole film, so the :30 loses nothing. Repetition makes reproducibility visible as rhythm, which is the HCP want T03 was carrying, now with a forward move. "Landing zone" is Edwards' own vocabulary (R4, §16.2.5) and implanters' working language; "footing" is it in plain words. It is also a system, not just a film: a step is a frame, so stills, booth loops and social cutdowns come free. Ownability passes: the ground comes in to meet the heart; a radial-force story can't sign it.

**What would kill it** (and is why the BET is conditional):
1. **Cute.** A red heart "stepping" is one frame from a mascot. Direction: the heart never hops, bounces or has a gait. It *travels* at an even, weighted glide and *settles* onto each footing; the "step" lives in the cut and the sound, not in the heart's body. No squash-and-stretch. Small in frame, low angle, as procedural as a device animation.
2. **Stepping stones.** Footings must never pre-exist as a path; each forms only just ahead of the heart, from the ground's own edges, and sinks back flush behind it. If the long wide shot (:30 sc5, :60 sc9) shows a row of discs, it becomes stepping stones / coins / manufacturing. Show at most one formed footing ahead and the levelled trail behind.
3. **Ring/coil read (§16.2.5).** Footing must read as a level plane, not a ring, groove or concentric form. No rim.
4. **Who the heart is.** The red heart stands for the valve landing (title slide says so). Clinicians will accept the conflation for a launch film, but keep the line "That is what the dock does for the valve" on the title slide so the analogy is owned, not accidental.

Rework directions from v03 that this absorbs: T03's procession (salvaged, with fix 2), R2-2's rolling identical forms (as the even footings), A's "draw in".

**Heartbeat sound.** Fine as sound (text-on-screen film, sound allowed). Never visualised as an ECG line (category code to avoid). Note: R4 p4's "steady beat of innovation" (Q43) is an unintentional rhyme with Edwards' own language. Record as a talking point for presentation, do not adopt the phrase.

### R3-3 The Second Look · ADVANCE

**Rework check (v03 §2, five problems):**
1. Corridor/light cliché · **landed.** No door, corridor or glow.
2. HCP ownership · **landed, and well.** The team's own verdicts as the subject is the sharpest use of the §16.2 human truth on the slate.

4. Mechanism in the :30 · **landed** (sc3 draw-in on the page, sc4 dock).
5. Claims · noted below; this route carries the most exposure.

**New misreads, must fix before slides:**
- **Payer denial, not clinical decision.** Stamped manila files reading "Not a candidate" look like insurance denials, prior-auth rejections or legal case files to a U.S. audience, which is exactly the legal/insurance stock the brief warned of. Direction: no rubber stamps, no manila tabs, no office desk. The files are heart-team case sheets: a single tan contour drawing (anatomy as line, not render) and the verdict set in typography as a conference note. Ground stays beige. Paper should feel like an imaging printout or a case-review sheet, not a filing cabinet.
- **End line "Reopen the case."** Reads as cold case / courtroom. Replace (see §3).
- **Heart in a folder** can read greeting-card. Keep the heart small and flat-lit on the sheet, same scale and treatment as in the other routes.

**Ownability** is medium by nature; the draw-in beat on the page is what makes it Edwards and must never be cut, including in the :30.
