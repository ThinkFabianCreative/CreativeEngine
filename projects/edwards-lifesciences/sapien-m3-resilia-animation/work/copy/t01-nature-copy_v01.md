## 1. Territory name

**A. Rhyme**
Names the method: every nature shot rhymes with something the device does. Says nothing about the device itself.
- MLR risk: low, since it makes no product statement. The real risk is with the client: on its own it can read as opaque, so it needs the premise line under it.
- R3 source: none.

**B. Drawn Inward**
Takes the territory's one sourced truth and makes it the name.
- MLR risk: it drops "Designed to" from R3's header. That's fine for an internal name, but if it moves into the film it has to go back to "Designed to Draw Anatomy Inward". It also leaves out the forward heart, R1's main concept.
- R3 source: INNOVATION, "Designed to Draw Anatomy Inward" [C10].

**C. Inward, Forward**
The film's two motions: the dock draws in, the heart moves on.
- MLR risk: putting the device mechanism next to "forward" can be read as implied patient progress. It's acceptable as an internal name but would need the same check if it became a super. It's also the spine the review proposed for the fused route (§11), so it may blur T01 with the fusion.
- R3 source: C10 ("draw the mitral apparatus inward"), plus R1 ("The visual of the heart moving forward... This will be our main concept").

## 2. Premise line (territory title slide)

**A. Method first**
> Every nature shot in this film mirrors one thing the SAPIEN M3 RESILIA system does. A tendril coils as the dock encircles; petals fold inward as the dock draws the mitral apparatus inward, rather than expanding outward against the anatomy. Nature gives the picture, and the words on screen come from Edwards' messaging.

- MLR risk: "mirrors" sets nature next to the device, so a reviewer may ask whether we're implying the device is natural. The last sentence answers that. The device description is R3 wording.
- R3 source: INNOVATION, "Designed to Draw Anatomy Inward" bullet 1 [C10]; R1 for "the dock encircling".

**B. Truth first**
> The SAPIEN M3 dock is designed to draw the mitral apparatus inward, rather than expanding outward against the anatomy, and its native leaflet-based anchoring is designed to support natural annular motion. Nature shows what that motion looks like. Edwards' messaging says what it is.

- MLR risk: lowest of the three. Both device statements are R3 verbatim, with "designed to" kept. The word "natural" appears only inside R3's own phrase, attached to annular motion and never to the device or the tissue. A reviewer will check that the dock statements are not put on the RESILIA tissue.
- R3 source: INNOVATION, "Designed to Draw Anatomy Inward", bullets 1 and 3 [C10].

**C. Spine first**
> Inward, then forward. The dock draws the anatomy inward and the RESILIA leaflets open and close, while the red glass heart moves forward on beige. Each nature shot is a picture of one of those motions, never a claim about them.

- MLR risk: the heart's forward motion sits in the same sentence as the mechanism, which a reviewer may read as cause and effect (device, then patient moves on). It's the campaign visual, not a claim, but say so out loud when presenting. "Draws the anatomy inward" shortens R3 and keeps its meaning.
- R3 source: INNOVATION header "Designed to Draw Anatomy Inward" [C10]; R1 for the heart moving forward and the leaflets.

## 3 and 4. Opening and closing supers (scene 1: bird glides forward; scene 10: red glass heart moves forward)

Three different mechanisms: a sentence split across the film, a comparison that ends on R3's own words, and one line repeated over a new subject.

### Pair A. Split sentence

| Scene | Super |
|---|---|
| 1 (bird glides forward) | Forward |
| 10 (heart moves forward) | begins inward. |

The first word hangs for the whole film. The end completes it, and the end card can carry the whole line: **Forward begins inward.**

- MLR risk: highest of the three. A reviewer may read it as causal: drawing the anatomy inward leads to the patient moving forward, which is an implied clinical benefit. It's defensible as a metaphor for the device mechanism and the campaign visual, but expect the question. It also sounds like a saying, so it needs the visuals to earn it.
- R3 source: leans on "Designed to Draw Anatomy Inward" [C10]. R1 for the forward heart.
- 30 s: works. "Forward" can ride the heart if the bird is cut.

### Pair B. Built for

| Scene | Super |
|---|---|
| 1 (bird glides forward) | A wing is built for flight. |
| 10 (heart moves forward) | Built for Mitral. |

The opening states a plain fact about the bird. The close answers it with the device, in R3's own words. It's the territory's method in two lines: nature built for its job, the system built for its anatomy.

- MLR risk: low. The end line is R3 verbatim. A reviewer may ask whether the comparison suggests the device is as well adapted as nature, which would be a superlative by association. The opener says "a wing", not "every wing", so it isn't a false absolute (penguins). Match the case of the two lines in design: R3 sets "Built for Mitral." in title case.
- R3 source: LEGACY, "Trusted SAPIEN Platform. Built for Mitral." [C11, C23].
- 30 s: needs about 2 s of bird to keep the opener. Without it, run "Built for Mitral." alone on the end card.

### Pair C. Same line, new subject

| Scene | Super |
|---|---|
| 1 (bird glides forward) | Watch it move. |
| 10 (heart moves forward) | Watch it move. |

The line doesn't change; what it points at does. At the start it's the bird. By the end, after the coil, the fold, the grip and the wingbeat, it's the heart, and the viewer has spent the film watching the device's motions. It fits a text-only film that is about motion.

- MLR risk: low. It's an invitation to look and makes no product statement. A reviewer may ask whether "move" over the heart implies restored function; it rides the campaign visual, not a claim. It shares the motion theme with "Setting performance in motion" but doesn't sound like it. If that line is confirmed as the campaign agency's (still unconfirmed, review Q7), check the two don't compete on the end card.
- R3 source: none. It describes the film.
- 30 s: works as is.

## 5. Recommendation

- **Territory name: Rhyme.** It keeps the nature angle Andres wants and names exactly how it now works, without the word "nature" or any device statement. Premise B explains it on the same slide.
- **Premise: B (truth first).** Both device sentences are R3 verbatim with "designed to" intact, and it puts "natural" where R3 does, on annular motion. It's the one MLR is least likely to send back, and it shows Edwards we fixed the T01 problem the review named (§4, weakness 1).
- **Bookends: Pair B, "A wing is built for flight." / "Built for Mitral."** It's concrete, ends on R3's approved words, and does the territory's work in two lines. If Andres wants the forward motion in the words as well as the picture, Pair A is the stronger idea, at a higher MLR risk.
