The turn is the dock drawing inward, the opposite of what a device is expected to do (push outward). The heart stops, something happens inside it, and it moves again. ## End line: three options

**A · Drawn inward. Moving forward.**
- WHY IT WORKS: It says the film's structure out loud, in four words: the mechanism, then the heart. The two halves are opposites of direction, so the line explains the dock without a spec.
- IT BETS ON: "Moving forward" reading as the campaign's heart image, not as a promise about patients.
- RISK: MLR may read "moving forward" as implied patient outcome. The answer is that it sits over the campaign heart, never over a patient. If MLR still objects, the line goes.
- CLAIMS: "inward" is R3 C10 (source-stated). "Moving forward" is metaphor, not a claim.

**B · Trusted SAPIEN Platform. Built for Mitral.**
- WHY IT WORKS: R3 verbatim, so it's the safest line through MLR, and it brings the LEGACY pillar into a cut that otherwise has no room for it.
- IT BETS ON: Edwards wanting the platform name to carry the close.
- RISK: It's a header, not an idea. Any SAPIEN film could end on it. It doesn't pay off "inward and forward".
- CLAIMS: C11, source-stated.

**C · Setting performance in motion.**
- WHY IT WORKS: "In motion" matches the heart starting again, and it may already belong to the campaign.
- IT BETS ON: It being the campaign agency's line, cleared for our use. Its source isn't recorded anywhere (review §6).
- RISK: If it's ours, it's an untested tagline that needs MLR. "Performance" points toward a device-performance claim.
- CLAIMS: Source unknown. Don't use until confirmed.

**Recommendation: A.** It's the only one of the three that comes from this film's idea rather than from the brief. B is the fallback if MLR won't accept the metaphor. Hold C until someone confirms where it came from.
