## Part 1. Three directions

### A. CLOSURE (the coaptation line)

**Concept sentence.** Mitral and tricuspid regurgitation is a gap that should close; the deck is built on that one line, two edges that never quite meet until the turn, when TRAFFIK's history and Edwards' next chapter close the gap.

**Why it fits.** It comes from the clinical truth of TMTT (regurgitation = leaflets that fail to meet; therapy restores the seal), not from healthcare iconography. It doubles as the strategic idea: the space between breakthrough therapy and adoption is the gap TMTT's communications must close (evidence -> understanding -> trust -> adoption). One graphic device does the anatomy, the strategy and the turn. Quiet, precise, ownable; reads as engineering and humanity at once.

**Risk.** Abstraction must stay honest: the line is a diagrammatic idea, never a depiction of a device or a procedure, and must not imply a clinical outcome. Mitral has two leaflets, tricuspid three: the device is an abstract seam, not a valve drawing (medical-illustration accuracy is a separate, sourced asset).

| Territory | Reference | BORROW | DO NOT COPY |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medical illustration | Classic anatomical atlas plates (e.g. Netter-style plates; Edwards' own Heart Illustration from the archive, TO FIND) | Line weight discipline, labelling with leader lines, white space as specimen ground | Full-color anatomy as decoration; any plate we lack rights to |
| Editorial design | Swiss-modernist book typography (Müller-Brockmann grid systems) | Strict grid, one focal mark per spread, caption systems | Retro poster pastiche |
| Engineering documentation | Technical drawing and patent-drawing conventions (TO FIND: exemplary patent sheets, public domain) | Dimension lines, tolerances, callouts as a way to "show the gap" | Blueprint blue, fake schematics of Edwards devices |
| Minimalist conceptual art | Lucio Fontana "Concetto spaziale" cuts; also hairline-drawing work generally | The tension of one mark on a field; the gap as subject | The slash/wound literalism (wrong register for a cardiac audience) |
| Science-led brand decks | Annual-report / science editorial from research institutions (TO FIND: a strong institute annual report) | Evidence-first restraint, data set like typography | Stock lab photography, molecule art |

### B. THE PATHWAY (the referral journey as a map)

**Concept sentence.** The deck is drawn as a single route, from data and congress to referring clinician to heart team to treatment, and every TRAFFIK capability is a station where people get on.

**Why it fits.** TMTT's hardest problem as supplied is not awareness but movement: patients not identified, not referred. A transit-diagram language makes the EVIDENCE -> EDUCATION -> ADVOCACY -> REFERRAL -> TREATMENT model visible and makes Act V's "connect the referral journey" feel inevitable.

**Risk.** Transit maps are a well-worn deck trope; it can drift into process-chart PowerPoint, and it flattens the congress and film work into "stations". Better as one hero slide inside A than as the whole system.

| Territory | Reference | BORROW | DO NOT COPY |
|---|---|---|---|
| Information design | Harry Beck's London Underground diagram | 45/90 degree discipline, abstraction over geography | The literal tube look and roundels |
| Wayfinding | Hospital and airport wayfinding systems (TO FIND: a celebrated hospital wayfinding program) | Directional typography, color as route | Generic hospital signage photography |
| Data storytelling | Long-form scroll explainers from serious newsrooms (TO FIND) | Progressive reveal of one route | Fake dashboards, invented numbers |
| Cartography | Swiss topographic maps | Layering, fine line work | Decorative contour textures |
| Documentary photography | Real congress and hospital-corridor reportage from the archive (TO FIND) | People in transit, unposed | Stock "patient journey" smiles |

### C. THE SPECIMEN (engineering precision, the dossier)

**Concept sentence.** Every slide is a specimen plate: one real object, piece of work or fact, isolated on a calm field, measured and captioned, so TRAFFIK's history reads as engineered evidence rather than a portfolio.

**Why it fits.** Matches Edwards' engineering and tissue culture (macro, material, RESILIA-level detail) and the claims rules: everything captioned with its status. Strong for a cautious, regulated room.

**Risk.** Beautiful but cold; it proves rigor, not imagination, and makes Act V hard to lift above Act III. Leans sterile, which the visual direction forbids.

| Territory | Reference | BORROW | DO NOT COPY |
|---|---|---|---|
| Museum cataloguing | Natural-history specimen photography and labels | Object isolation, catalogue numbers, labels | Dusty archival styling |
| Product macro | Real Edwards product and tissue macro photography (archive, TO FIND) | Material detail as hero | Any generated device imagery |
| Editorial | Industrial-design monographs (e.g. Dieter Rams / Braun catalogue design) | Restraint, single-object plates | Retro-modern cosplay |
| Technical | Spec sheets, IFU-style tabular rigor | Status columns, precise captions | Regulatory text density |
| Photography | Irving Penn still lifes | Single-object, controlled light | Fashion/editorial gloss |

### Recommendation: A, CLOSURE, with B's pathway as one hero slide

A is the only direction where the visual idea, the clinical truth and the pitch's turn are the same thing. It gives the deck a built-in arc (gap open -> gap closing -> seal) that makes "and that's what we've already done" land as a physical event on screen, and it lets Act V be visually louder without abandoning the system. C's captioning discipline is folded into A's evidence slides; B survives as the ecosystem slide in Act V.

## Part 2. CLOSURE, full output (editorial-presentation §12)

**VISUAL CONCEPT.** One hairline seam runs through the deck: two edges that approach but leave a gap. The gap is TMTT's challenge (between breakthrough therapy and adoption). At the turn the edges meet, and from then on the seam becomes the structure that holds the future scenarios.

**WHY IT FITS.** Rooted in regurgitation and coaptation, the defining truth of mitral and tricuspid disease; mirrors the strategy (evidence -> understanding -> trust -> adoption); restrained enough for a medical audience, ownable enough that no other agency deck looks like it.

### Design system

- **Type.** If Edwards has an identity face, use it for anything that speaks as Edwards (product names). TRAFFIK voice: one serious grotesk for display and text, used in two weights only (regular, semibold), large display sizes set tight, and a mono for captions, status labels and "THIS PROVES:" tags (the engineering register). Hierarchy: chapter line > single headline > caption. No more than three levels per slide. Final face choice after license check and Edwards guidelines; candidates are for typography.md review, not decided here.
- **Grid.** 12-column, 16:9, generous outer margin; a fixed horizontal "seam axis" at roughly 0.55 height that the device and key headlines sit on, so every slide shares one horizon. Asymmetric layouts; no equal-weight card grids.
- **Photography.** Real only: Edwards product and tissue macro, archive congress photography, archive film stills, TRAFFIK work as it ran. Full-bleed or none; never thumbnails in boxes. Warm, neutral grade, not clinical blue. Labelled future-state mood frames allowed only in Act V and marked as such.
- **Color.** Warm off-white field (paper, not screen white) and a near-black ink. One signal color with one job: the seam and the turn only (a deep arterial red is the obvious candidate; test against Edwards' palette and against "glowing heart" cliché, alternative a quiet oxblood). Act V inverts to the dark field so the future feels heavier and stronger than the archive.
- **Graphic language.** The seam (two hairlines with a measured gap, dimension-line style), leader-line captions, mono status tags (`ARCHIVE EVIDENCE`, `PROPOSED SCOPE`, etc. on internal versions; client versions show only what is confirmed), small catalogue numbers per case. No icons, no ECG lines, no gradients.
- **Motion.** One motion idea: the seam closing. Edges drift toward each other slowly across Acts I-III (a few pixels per chapter in the static version), close at the turn in a single deliberate move, then open into structure in Act V. Everything else is cuts, not transitions. Film plays full-bleed with sound. Must also work as a static PDF.

### Slide behaviours

1. **The seam** (chapter openers): field, seam, one chapter line. Acts I-IV.
2. **Full-bleed proof**: one real image or film still edge to edge, one headline, a `THIS PROVES:` line in mono. Case studies.
3. **Specimen plate**: one artifact (one-sheet, screen, animation frame) isolated, captioned with leader lines and status. Clinical/sales and digital proof.
4. **The statement**: type only, very large, on the seam axis. Thesis, the turn, close.
5. **The map**: single diagram (ecosystem / referral route) in seam line language. Act I challenge, Act V ecosystem.
6. **The scenario** (Act V, dark field): future-state frame plus a verb-led title ("SHOOT THE BRAND FILM"), labelled as future scenario.

### Chapter lines (drafts for word-smither)

- I: "A therapy is only as strong as the gap it closes."
- II: "We've been inside structural heart for years." (exact tenure to confirm, no number until verified)
- III: "The proof." (with five sub-heads, one per capability)
- IV, the turn: "And that's what we've already done." (the seam closes)
- V: "Here's what we could build next."

### Hero moments

1. **Slide 3, the gap.** The seam first named as TMTT's challenge; sets up the whole deck.
2. **Slide 9, the film.** The only moment sound enters; "we can shoot the spot" shown, not said.
3. **Slide 11, the turn.** The seam closes in one move; the room should feel it.
4. **Slide 14, the ecosystem map.** Proves strategic thinking beyond the archive (Direction B lives here).
5. **Slide 17, the close.** Seam as structure: the partnership as what holds.

### Anti-tropes (this deck refuses)

Healthcare blue · ECG/heartbeat lines · glowing or literal hearts · DNA/molecules · HUDs, holograms, screen-surrounded doctors · smiling clinician stock, hospital hallways · logo walls and 12-thumbnail case grids · equal-weight capability cards · gradients and fake dashboards · timeline-of-our-history slides · any invented stat ("X% more...") · generated people, devices or procedures · the seam drawn as a literal valve or anything resembling an Edwards device.
