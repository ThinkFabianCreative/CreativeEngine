## The thought

The industry promises to *drive* traffic. Traffik makes work that *stops* it. The name stops being a pun to apologize for and becomes the claim.

## What makes it more than an idiom

**The brand already carries it.** Traffik's observed palette is black, a stop red (#D91F1F) and a caution yellow (#F4D803). The idea is already sitting in the brand, so it needs no new graphics. Use red for one thing only: the moment of stopping. No traffic lights, cones or road signs.

**Stop, then go.** Stopping people only matters if they then do something. Every case gets two beats:

- **STOP:** the moment people stopped for it. Featherstone: national attention, Forbes and the Chronicle of Higher Education, 2.5M earned media impressions.
- **GO:** what they did next. Featherstone: applications up 9.4%, a CMU record.

This connects to the wayfinding thread from the inventory: stop people, then show them the way. It also gives the case-study template its structure, and it suits regulated clients, where GO is a clear, compliant decision rather than a spectacle.

## Rough directions for the landing page

- **The stop.** The page opens on a single piece of work and nothing else, with no navigation competing for the first second. The stop is in the composition and the restraint, not in scroll-jacking.
- **Stop count.** Each case's stop moment is stated in plain numbers, with the source.
- **Two beats per case.** A STOP / GO rhythm runs down the page and becomes the site's pacing.

## Rough line directions (for WordSmither to beat)

- Everyone drives traffic. We stop it.
- Work that stops traffic.
- Stop. Then go.

These are placeholders to show the idea's verbal range, not proposed copy.

## Risks to test

1. "Stop traffic" is a familiar idiom. Only the work can earn it, so the line must never run ahead of the proof.
2. A literal page-stopping interaction would be scroll-jacking, which hurts accessibility and annoys returning visitors. The stop has to come from design, not mechanics.
3. Health and life-sciences buyers may want reassurance more than spectacle. STOP / GO has to hold up for a Medicare plan, not only for Featherstone.
4. Hero cases without a stop number (Netwatch, SDSU) need one, or they can't lead under this idea.
