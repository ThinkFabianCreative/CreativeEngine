Marble run
scroll storyboard

One bronze marble lays its own road while the category rolls down the same wooden grooves. Eight frames, one continuous run from hero to call to action. Scroll is the clock: the page never fakes a section break, the camera follows the marble from one section into the next.

The fix the director asked for on Nov 22 is built in: our marble is not a body falling down someone else's course. It decides. Its steel rail draws itself ahead of it, and switches throw before it arrives. That is what "We control the road ahead" looks like.

Polished bronzeTraffik. One marble, mirror finish, warm specular. The only object on the page that catches a highlight.
Patinated bronzeThe category. Same metal, same size, gone dull with verdigris. Not losers, just carried along. Rough, so no reflections.
Wood channelsThe road everyone shares: oiled walnut grooves, fixed, heavy, built by someone else. Lanes, funnels, loops.
Steel wire railTraffik's own line. Two thin brushed rails, bent by hand. It appears just ahead of our marble and nowhere else.
RedOnly the road ahead: the rail while it is still being drawn. Once the marble passes, it cools to steel.

How it holds together

One continuous run

Sections are camera moves, not page breaks. The canvas stays pinned behind the HTML, and each section's copy scrolls over a quiet zone of the frame. If any frame needs a hard cut, the idea has broken there.

Scripted hero, live crowd

Scroll-scrubbed motion has to run backwards, and live physics can't. So our marble always rides a hand-authored spline, and real rigid-body physics is spent on the pack (F2, F3, F4, F7), where messiness is the point. Simulate offline and bake wherever a moment must land the same every time.

Refined, not toy

Restraint keeps it premium: one key light, a real bronze material with a roughness map, oiled walnut with visible grain, brushed steel. No neon, no bloom on the pack, no confetti on arrival. The bronze highlight is the page's one luxury.

Mobile and reduced motion

At 390px each frame gets a fixed camera and the marble moves in one direction only. With reduced motion, show eight stills that crossfade per section: the storyboard itself becomes the fallback.

Open questions before the build

  1. Flat 2D or 3D. The Nov 22 style options assumed flat 2D on Webflow. Bronze, wood and rigid bodies mean three.js and real materials. Confirm the switch, and whether it still has to live inside Webflow as an embed.
  2. Where red lives. This board keeps red only for the road ahead. The alternative, a red-lacquered hero marble, is louder and loses the bronze. My pick is bronze.
  3. Section order. The frame order assumes positioning → how we think → process → results → work → clients → contact. If the IA differs, the concepts can re-order, except F1 and F8, which bookend.
  4. Lines. Four slots need WordSmither (F3, F4, F6, F8). F7's run line predates the "road" decision and may need a pass.
  5. Proof. F5 depends on real, reviewed results. Without them, F5 becomes a speed beat with no numbers, which is weaker.
