## 1. What the word carries

| Layer | Meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Root** | Trade. English took it in the 1500s from French and Italian (*traffico*, *trafficare*): to carry goods across and deal in them. The deeper origin is disputed; one proposed reading is Latin *trans-* (across) plus a verb for pushing. | Not verified this session: check a dictionary before quoting it anywhere |
| **Old sense** | Dealings and communication. To "have traffic with" someone meant to exchange with them | An exchange, not a crowd |
| **Modern sense** | People, vehicles or data moving along a route | Flow through a channel with limits |
| **Network sense** | Messages passing through a network; signal against noise | Close to what advertising does |
| **The K** | The name is spelled wrong on purpose and still reads instantly | The brand already breaks a rule and gets away with it |
| **Shadow** | *Trafficking*, meaning illicit trade | Never lean on "smuggling" or "contraband" metaphors |

**Proposed meaning:** *traffic is what gets across.* It covers goods across a border, a message across a network and an idea across a rule. Movement alone is not traffic; movement that arrives somewhere is.

That fits the strategy without strain. Every Traffik client sits behind a rule (Medicare marketing, enrollment, compliance), and the job is to get an idea across it intact. It also fits the new hero line: *Unsaid isn't unsayable* is a claim that something can get across.

## 2. Territories: from meaning to visual metaphor

Each territory gives a metaphor, a visual idea, an interaction idea and its risk. None are judged; this is EXPLORE.

### A. Passage: the idea gets through
- **Metaphor:** a narrow gap in a wall, and something getting through it.
- **Visual:** light through a slit, or water through a crack in stone. On the site, the rule is a solid wall of type, and the idea is the one line that passes through it.
- **Interaction:** the hero opens on a dense block of the category's rule text. On scroll a gap opens and the idea line travels through it. This is the r1 rule → idea animation, given a reason to move.
- **Risk:** "breaking through" is a stock agency image. Ours has to be specific: the wall is made of the client's actual rule.

### B. Flow: organised movement around obstacles
- **Metaphor:** a current that finds the way around a stone without moving the stone. Like a murmuration, it moves as one body and changes shape without breaking up.
- **Visual:** a river around rocks, or a starling murmuration. Thousands of small marks move as one body.
- **Interaction:** a particle field (WebGL or GSAP) flows across the page, and the rule sits in it as a fixed block. The particles bend around the rule and re-form on the other side as the headline. The cursor adds a second, small obstacle. This fits the 3D and GSAP interest; check performance and reduced motion.
- **Risk:** it can turn into a pretty screensaver. The particles have to form words or work, not just look nice.

### C. Signal: the one message that gets through
- **Metaphor:** data traffic. A network full of noise and one clear signal.
- **Visual:** a field of grey, look-alike category ads (the cliché wall), with one piece of work in full color.
- **Interaction:** the first screen is noise, a tiled grid of generic category lines. Scrolling or moving the cursor tunes it, and the noise falls away to leave one case. The work page filter works the same way: tune to Health, Education or Unites.
- **Risk:** mocking the category can feel like a dig at the clients' own past work. Build the noise from generic patterns, not from real competitor ads.

### D. Exchange: trade between two sides
- **Metaphor:** the original meaning, trade. Two parties give something and both leave with more.
- **Visual:** two halves meeting in one frame, such as the rule (client side) and the idea (agency side), or a split-screen case where each half needs the other.
- **Interaction:** cases open as a two-panel exchange. The left panel shows what the client brought (the rule, the brief), the right shows what came back (the idea, the result), and a drag or scroll trades one for the other.
- **Risk:** it's the quietest territory and the least ownable on its own. It's strongest as the case-page structure, not the hero.

### E. The K: a rule broken on purpose that still reads
- **Metaphor:** the name itself. "Traffic" is the rule and "Traffik" is the idea, and nobody misreads it.
- **Visual:** the K as a house device. Elsewhere in the site's type, one letter is a knowing "wrong" glyph (for example, the K's diagonal stroke used as a recurring cut or angle in the layout).
- **Interaction:** hovering the logo swaps K to C and back. The idea is that the correct version is the dull one. The 404 page can say "This page broke the wrong rule."
- **Risk:** it's a small idea that can turn cute fast. Use it as a signature, not a campaign. No red-pen corrections (banned motif).

## 3. Positioning, in one line

**Traffik gets ideas across the rules that stop everyone else's.** *(Positioning statement for internal use, not copy.)*

- It leads with the idea, not the approval. The r1 risk of reading as a compliance shop is answered by "across": the rule stays where it is, and the idea moves.
- It explains the name without a pun.
- It holds for all three divisions. Health has regulation, Education has committees and boards, and Unites has category habits.

## 4. Recommendation for DEVELOP

- **Combine B (Flow) as the site's motion system with A (Passage) as the hero moment.** The particle current carries the work across the page, and in the hero it bends around the client's real rule text and re-forms as the headline. That is one metaphor, used everywhere: the idea gets across.
- **Use E (the K) as a small signature:** the logo hover and the 404 page.
- **Use D (Exchange) as the case-page structure,** not the hero.
- **Keep C (Signal) in reserve** for the Work page filter.

This replaces nothing already approved. It gives Route 3's one-line hero a visual reason: the line is what got across.

## Territory F · Run: working line

word-smither, 2026-11-22, EXPLORE. Tightening Andres's line for Run, where marbles race elaborate tracks, the track is the rule and the forks are decisions. The line has to keep his confidence and foresight. It must not read as luck, it stays inside the run's own words (course, track, line, fork), it keeps "road" out (road imagery belongs to Stop Traffic only) and avoids "control", which a regulated buyer can hear as overriding their legal team. This is a territory line, not the homepage hero.

1. **We control the road ahead.** Andres's original, kept as the benchmark. It has the confidence we want, but "road" belongs to another territory and "control" sounds like we run over the client's reviewers.
2. **We read the course before the run.** Foresight is a skill here, so the win isn't luck, and the client's rules stay intact because we read the track and don't own it. **Pick.**
3. **We know every fork before you reach it.** This makes the decisions the selling point and puts the client in the run with us. It's a little long for a line.
4. **We read the course. Your idea takes the line.** It splits the job in two: our foresight, their idea arriving first. It's the most generous to the client, though the two-part form is a touch staged.
5. **Same track. Better line.** Every competitor runs the same rules and the idea wins on the line it takes. It's short and confident, but it says less about foresight.

Humanizer check (diagnostic only, headline-class lines): 4 and 5 use a two-beat structure. In 5 it's earned, because it states the whole territory. In 4 it's borderline, so I'm flagging it and leaving it unchanged. No stock AI wording, and nothing implies luck or chance.

### Decision, 2026-11-22 (Andres)
"Road" stays: Andres prefers it to "track" or "course". This deliberately overrides the no-road guardrail in `10_traffik_brand_rules.txt` for this territory's line only. The visuals stay marble runs and rivers, never traffic lights or cones. Owner's verb, not observer's: the line must sound confident. Working line: **We control the road ahead.** (alternative: "We own the road ahead.")
Later the same day, Andres compared road, route, line and track. **Road wins.** Route was the stronger fit for the name's meaning; road was chosen for immediacy and confidence. Working line stays: **We control the road ahead.**

### Decision, 2026-11-22 (Andres): belief line
Traffik wants to be known for disrupting things, not facilitating passage. Diversity is not what Traffik is known for, so it's dropped from the credo work. The source for disruption is the live site's own phrase "industry-disrupting creative" (Capabilities).
Belief line (Andres): **Traffic drivers. Not traffic driven.** ("We become" dropped.)
Consequences: Passage, Flow and Exchange rank lower; Stop traffic and Run ("We control the road ahead.") rank higher. Watch: "traffic driven" must not read as "not results-driven".
