**The design problem in one line:** marketing leaders at universities, health plans and life-sciences companies need to see, in the first seconds and in every case study, an agency that leads; the site has to read as ordered, confident and specific, and Andres wants that order to come from Swiss principle, ideally on a dark ground.

## Design thesis

Swiss is a method for making information carry its own authority, so Traffik should take the method (grid, scale contrast, flush-left reading order, objective proof as the image) and apply it to its strongest asset, the evidence in each case, rather than borrowing the costume of Helvetica, white space and a big number.

## Principle

### What actually makes a design Swiss

The International Typographic Style came out of the Basel and Zurich schools from the late 1940s (Müller-Brockmann, Ruder, Hofmann, with Gerstner and others). Its ideas, translated to a browser:

1. **The grid is the argument, not the scaffolding.** A Swiss grid is chosen for the content and then everything obeys it: text, image, captions, white space. On the web this means a column system that also governs vertical rhythm, image crops and where interactive elements sit. If you can delete the grid and nothing changes, it was decoration.
2. **Asymmetry with tension.** Content is placed off-center on purpose, so the empty part of the field does work. Centered heroes with centered buttons are the opposite of Swiss.
3. **Hierarchy through scale and weight, one family.** A grotesk (Akzidenz, Helvetica, Univers and their descendants) in a few sizes and weights does all the ranking. Color, boxes, icons and shadows are not needed to say what matters.
4. **Flush left, ragged right.** One hard left edge per column, a natural rag, no justified text, no centered paragraphs. The left edge is what the eye locks onto when scrolling.
5. **Objective imagery.** Photography that shows the thing plainly, cropped hard to the grid; diagrams, numbers and maps as images. No mood stock, no illustration standing in for proof.
6. **White space as structure.** Space is measured in grid units and separates groups of meaning. It is not "breathing room" added at the end.
7. **Information as form.** The data, the list, the index, the timetable become the visual. A project index set as a well-ordered table can be the most beautiful thing on a Swiss page.

A useful piece of history for an advertising agency: the style was never only for museums and transit. Gerstner, Gredinger and Kutter in Basel used it for advertising (Design Reviewed has an article on their ads for Merkur). Swiss clarity selling things is a real tradition, not a contradiction.

### The pastiche, and how to tell

| Looks Swiss | Actually Swiss |
|---|---|
| Helvetica on white with one giant number | A grid where the number's size, position and caption all follow the column logic, and the number is the client's real result |
| "Minimal": few elements, lots of white | Every element and every gap placed by a system you could write down |
| Thin grey type at small sizes to look refined | Hierarchy readable at a glance; refinement in the spacing, not in faint text |
| Red accent sprinkled on links, tags and buttons | One accent with one job |
| Full-bleed mood video as the hero | A plain, specific image of the work, cropped to the grid |
| Grid shown as visible lines for style | Grid felt through alignment; lines only where they divide information |

The quick test: describe the grid in one sentence (columns, gutter, baseline, how images crop). If nobody can, it is pastiche.

### Dark-ground Swiss: does it hold?

Yes, with changes. Müller-Brockmann's concert and film posters regularly used black grounds (this is from general design history, not re-checked live in this session; the Museum für Gestaltung collection at emuseum.ch is the place to verify). The logic survives because it is structural, not tonal. What changes on screen:

- **Weight goes up a step.** Light text on dark looks thinner and spreads (halation). Body text that is 300 on white often needs 400 on black; Traffik's current Acumin 300 body would likely be too light reversed.
- **Not pure black, not pure white.** Several sites below use near-black (Order #141414, Base #121212, Koto #060606). Pure white text on pure black vibrates at body size. Keep contrast above accessibility thresholds at real size (`visual-craft.md`).
- **Fewer levels of grey.** On dark, three greys read as mud. Use two text tones at most.
- **Images carry more of the light.** On black, the photograph becomes the brightest thing on the page, so it takes over hierarchy. Crop and size images as deliberately as type.
- **An accent gets louder.** Red on black is far more intense than on white. That helps "Stop traffic": one red moment on a dark page will stop the eye. It also means a second use of red anywhere destroys it.
- **Sameness risk is higher.** Dark grotesk studio sites are now a genre. Dark is not a differentiator; the content order is.

### 1. Order
- **URL:** https://order.design (case example: https://order.design/project/lyme-academy-of-fine-arts)
- **Where found:** known reference, verified on the site
- **Size:** small studio; case credits list a Brooklyn office and three named people on one project
- **Ground:** dark, near-black #141414, white type
- **Observed:** one typeface throughout (Pitch Sans, a monospaced-feel grotesk) at very few sizes; home page is a project list where name and discipline tags sit on a shared baseline; an Index page with a filterable discipline taxonomy (Identity, Signage, Strategy, Website and so on) and a search field, rows aligned project / tags / year. The Lyme Academy case (an art school) runs long: institutional history, the founder, place, then the identity, then named collaborators.
- **Inferred:** authority comes from order and patience. The index says "we have done a lot and can find any of it"; the long case says "we understood your institution before we designed for it". That is exactly what an education buyer wants to feel.
- **Traffik could adapt:** an index of all work as a typeset table with filters by sector (education, health, life sciences) and by discipline. Long-form cases that begin with the client's world.
- **Avoid:** Order's text is small and quiet; for a marketing-leader audience that skims, the STOP number has to be big enough to land without reading.

### 2. Base Design
- **URL:** https://www.basedesign.com (case example: https://www.basedesign.com/projects/moca-la)
- **Where found:** known reference, verified on the site
- **Size:** mid-sized; About page states "One network. Five studios." and lists New York, Brussels, Geneva, Melbourne, Saigon and a digital studio
- **Ground:** dark, #121212
- **Observed:** one house grotesk (baseGrotesk) in uppercase titles plus a second line in grey; discipline tags as small boxed labels; each project listed with a one-line idea ("Geometry becomes navigation. Identity becomes interface."). The MOCA LA case is structured CONTEXT, CHALLENGE, SOLUTION, then credits, written in plain, witty prose.
- **Inferred:** the one-line idea per project is what makes a grid of work read as thinking rather than a gallery. The three-part case reads like a strategist talking, which is what "an agency that leads" needs.
- **Traffik could adapt:** the one-line idea under every case title, and a fixed case skeleton. For Traffik that skeleton is STOP / GO, with context before it.
- **Avoid:** the boxed tag style and two-tone uppercase title are Base's signature; copy the structure, not the look.

### 3. AREA 17
- **URL:** https://area17.com (case example: https://area17.com/clients/lucas-museum-of-narrative-art)

- **Traffik could adapt:** cases organized by client relationship, chapters titled as objectives, the "since" date as quiet proof of retention.

## The serif tension

Adobe Caslon Pro is an 18th-century-model old-style serif. It is not Swiss and never was. The current site's mix (Caslon headlines, Acumin body, Acumin Wide labels) is observed, not approved guidelines, and any change to it is Andres's call.

**Option A: Swiss structure, serif voice.** Keep Caslon (or a serif) for headlines and the STOP line; build everything else with Swiss method: a strict column grid, flush left, Acumin for body and data, Acumin Wide for labels, objective images.
- Precedent: OK-RM runs an entire rigorous site in a Times serif on black; Spin pairs its grotesk with Times; Instrument pairs a sans with a serif. In history, Tschichold, the author of the New Typography, later returned to classical serif typography, and Vignelli used Bodoni inside strict grids (both from general design history, not re-checked live here).
- Why it might be right: a serif reads warmer and more literate to university and health buyers, and Traffik keeps an existing brand asset. The serif becomes the human voice inside a rational system.
- Failure modes: Caslon set big and centered becomes "heritage law firm"; Caslon at light weights on black breaks up (thin hairlines at screen sizes); serif and grotesk fighting for headline duty. The serif must have one job only.

**Option B: move to a grotesk.** Acumin (already licensed through Adobe Fonts) or a new neo-grotesk does everything.
- Why it might be right: the most direct expression of Andres's Swiss preference, simpler system, easier on dark grounds.
- Failure modes: Traffik looks like every dark studio site in this list (Koto, Studio Dumbar, Kurppa Hosk); coldness for health and education buyers; the brand loses a recognizable asset with no idea to replace it.

**Recommendation (not a decision):** test Option A first. Keep Caslon for one job, the STOP statement in each case and perhaps the main headline, at large sizes and regular weight or heavier, and let the grid, Acumin and the numbers do the Swiss work. It gives Andres the Swiss structure he likes, keeps a distinctive voice in a genre of identical grotesk sites, and is warmer for these buyers. Build one comparison page in both options with the same case (Featherstone), on dark, and decide on the render. If Caslon breaks up on black at real size, that is the evidence for B.

## Rule for your work

For DesignChamp:

1. **Write the grid before you draw.** One sentence: columns, gutter, baseline, image crop rule. Every page passes the delete-the-grid test.
2. **One family per job, one job per family.** Grotesk for structure and data, serif (if kept) only for the STOP statement, wide caps only for labels. Before: Caslon headlines, Caslon section titles and Caslon pull quotes. After: Caslon appears once per case, where people stopped.
3. **Red is the stop and nothing else.** No red links, tags or labels. On a dark page the single red moment is the loudest thing on screen, which is the territory working.
4. **The proof is the image.** Each case opens on a plain image of the work and a typeset number with its source; no mood video, no stock. The number is set in the grid with its caption, never floating.
5. **An index, not a gallery.** All work as an ordered, filterable table (sector, discipline, year), with flagships promoted above it. Order, Bureau Borsche and Base show this reads as confidence.

For CreativeResearcher: tag each new reference with movement, ground, type (computed if possible), size as stated, and observed vs inferred. "Swiss-looking" is not a tag; name which of the seven principles it shows.

## Risks

- **Pastiche:** Helvetica, a big number and white space with no system behind them. Test: can the grid be written in one sentence, and is the number real and sourced?
- **Coldness for health and edu buyers:** Swiss on black can feel clinical or exclusive. Countered by a serif voice, plain warm copy, real people and places in the images, and readable sizes.
- **Sameness with dark studio sites:** dark, grotesk, reel-first is now the default studio look. Traffik's difference must come from content order (STOP / GO with numbers), not from the color of the background.
- **Legibility on dark:** thin weights, grey-on-black and small labels fail contrast. Check at real size on a laptop and phone.
- **Anachronism:** Swiss is 1950s to 1970s; copying poster-era devices (rotated type, Akzidenz with heavy rules) can read as retro. Take the method, set it in today's type and interaction.
- **Rights:** these sites are reference only. No assets, layouts or proprietary type are to be copied, and any internal screenshots stay internal.
- **Client look:** changing Caslon or adding a dark ground changes Traffik's observed identity. That is Andres's decision, not DesignChamp's or mine.
