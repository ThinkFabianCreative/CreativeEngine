# Route 3 "One line": copy review

CLIENT: Traffik · PROJECT: website-d2c · SOURCE SCOPE: PROJECT-LEVEL · MODE: DEVELOP r2 · word-smither, 2026-11-22
Read: 2026-11-18_homepage-route3_red-antler-direction_v01.md, 2026-11-18_homepage-copy_route1-route2_v01.md, figma-agent-pack/11_traffik_site_direction.txt. Figma frame 695:431 not screenshotted; this review covers the copy as listed in the direction file and the brief. Figma not edited.

## 1. Does the hero carry the page alone?

It carries the attitude, but it doesn't say what we are. "Ideas that survive review" on its own could belong to a compliance consultancy, a UX research firm or a legal-review tool. The r1 who-we-are line existed to stop us reading as a compliance shop, and Route 3 cut it. The nav doesn't help: Work, Services, About and Careers describe any company. The proof row sits below the fold, so it can't do the job either.

Recommendation: add one small strapline under the hero, set well below the hero size so the line still owns the screen. Options:

1. **An advertising agency for health, education and life sciences.** (Recommended. It reuses the footer line, so it adds no new claim.)
2. **Advertising for health, education and life sciences. Idea first, approval second.**
3. **Advertising agency. For categories built to say no.**

If we use option 1, the footer can drop to "Traffik" plus links, which saves 7 words.

## 2. Do the trimmed proof labels hold?

- **77% · lower cost per acquisition.** This one fails. The locked line said 77% *below industry average*. Without the baseline, a reader assumes 77% lower than before, which is a different claim and a bigger one. Use **77% · below industry-average cost per acquisition · PIH Health** (5 words).
- **9.4% · more applications, a school record · Colorado Mesa.** Holds. Spell out **Colorado Mesa University** once on the page; most readers outside Colorado won't know the short form.
- **45,000+ · seniors reached in five languages · WellCare.** Close, but "seniors" isn't what the source said. It said Medicare beneficiaries, and some of them are under 65. Use **Medicare beneficiaries reached in five languages** (6 words).

All three stay `[unv]` until we have a source and a year.

## 3. "The operator's voice" and "Plan explainers"

Neither is acceptable as written. Tiles that pair a client name with a campaign name tell the reader those are real campaign titles. These two aren't, and a CMO checking us (the validation visitor) can catch that. Two fixes, either works:

- Use real campaign names once the account teams confirm them.
- Until then, show the client only, with a plain descriptor set in a different style from the real titles so nobody reads it as a name: **Netwatch** · *live operator audio* and **Care1st Health Plan** · *Medi-Cal and Medicare*.

Also check that "Easy Choice" is the campaign name and not the plan or product name. If it's the plan, the WellCare tile has the same problem.

## 4. Word budget, and what has to come back

At about 120 words we're under the ~150 budget, so there's room for three fixes:

- **Strapline** (section 1): +9 words, or +2 net if the footer is trimmed.
- **What we make.** "Health. Education. Unites." tells an outsider nothing about Unites; it reads like a slogan. Give each one a short line: **Health.** Plans, systems, providers. **Education.** Universities and colleges. **Unites.** Brands, B2B, destinations, nonprofits. That adds about 12 words. Unites scope is still unconfirmed with the team.
- **Careers.** Recruits are one of the three visitors. The footer link has to say **Careers**, and it should be in the nav too (it already is).

Running total with all three: about 140 to 145 words. That fits.

Nothing else needs to come back. The CTA question works without a body line next to a single button. The "How we work" cut to About is fine.

Hero and CTA lines got a diagnostic humanizer pass only (per the department record); nothing flagged. This note got the full pass.

## 5. New hero line (Andres, 2026-11-22: "Ideas that survive review." moves to the sign-off)

Brief: 3 to 5 words, giant condensed caps over one image. It leads with the idea, uses the category's rule as raw material, sits above the strapline "An advertising agency for health, education and life sciences." No approval or traffic words, and nothing that reads as the generic "constraints breed creativity" line (pack 10).

Explored about 25 lines across five angles. Discarded: "The rule is the idea" (it's the strategy said straight, and it sits next to the generic line), "Brave work for regulated categories" (category description), "We read the rules first" (process, sounds like compliance), and anything with "no" or "yes" (traffic-signal vocabulary).

Shortlist:

1. **UNSAID ISN'T UNSAYABLE.** Route 2's "can't say / hasn't said" thought in three words. It argues that the category's limits are habit, and it makes a strong two-line stack. **Pick.**
2. **SAY WHAT THEY HAVEN'T.** Same thought in the imperative. It has more swagger, but "they" is vague over a single image.
3. **THE RULE IS THE BRIEF.** It names the rule as raw material and sounds like an agency talking. It risks the generic-constraints read, so it's safer as a section line.
4. **START WHERE THE RULE STOPS.** It puts the idea on the far side of the rule, and the rhythm suits three lines. It's slightly softer than 1.
5. **YOU CAN'T SAY THAT. WATCH.** Brave and funny, but it opens on the rule (the "can't"), which the brief says to avoid. It's here as the wild card.

Humanizer check (diagnostic only, per the record): 1 is a reversal, and it's earned because it's the whole argument in compressed form. 5 uses a fragment payoff, which is fine for a headline. Nothing reads as an AI default, and none of the lines has been rewritten.

Copy budget: the hero goes from 4 words to 3, so the total stays around 140.
