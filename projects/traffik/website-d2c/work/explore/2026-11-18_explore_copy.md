7. A creative agency for categories where nobody stops for advertising. Until now.
8. Stopping people is the easy half. We also get them to the form.
9. Traffik makes work that stops traffic. Health plans, universities, MedTech.
10. Stop. Look. Apply. *(education cut)*
11. Stop. Look. Choose a plan. *(health cut)*

**Proof under the hero**
- STOP: 2.5M earned impressions for a university's anti-elitism campaign `[unverified]`. GO: applications up 9.4%, a school record `[unverified]`. — Colorado Mesa University
- Stopped: 98% of intruders, talked down by live operators before police arrived `[unverified, client claim, approval needed]`. — Netwatch

**What could kill it.** The idiom is familiar, so it reads as a pun unless the work under it is genuinely arresting; the line can outrun the proof. Health buyers may hear "spectacle" when they want "safe". Netwatch and SDSU have no clean stop number. And it's the only territory that leans on the name, so it competes with the brand rather than describing the agency's actual difference (getting brave work through review).

**Who we are (one-liners)** (most work as a two-part hero: rule above, line below)

2. Rule: you can't tell seniors which plan to pick. Idea: tell them what to ask. *(PIH as hero)*
3. The rule is where the idea is hiding.
4. Somewhere in your compliance binder is your best campaign.
5. Read the fine print with us. That's where we start.
6. Your regulator wrote half the brief. We write the other half.
7. We start with the sentence your lawyer won't let you cut.
8. The thing you can't say is usually the most interesting thing you could show.
9. Rule: no promises about outcomes. Idea: let the operator's own voice do the talking. *(Netwatch as hero; check the rule is real before using)*
10. Traffik. We take your category's rules personally.

*Guardrail note: the brief bans the general "constraints breed creativity" claim. Lines 1, 3 and 8 come close to it; they only pass if a named case rule sits right next to them on screen. Lines 2, 4, 6 and 9 are safer because they're specific.*

**Proof under the hero**
- The rule / the idea / what it did: Medicare marketing limits what a plan can tell a senior → a campaign built on the questions to ask → CPA 77% below industry average `[unverified]`. — PIH Health
- A rotating strip, one per sector, each in the same three beats (PIH for health, Featherstone for education, Netwatch for brands). Featherstone's rule still has to be named and checked.

**What could kill it.** Only PIH and Netwatch clearly pass the remove-the-rule test today, so the territory runs on two cases. It depends on Traffik naming a real rule correctly in public; get one wrong and it's a credibility problem in exactly the categories it's for. And opening on a rule may read as a compliance shop to a CMO hoping for creative ambition.

## Case-template voice (deck fields)

The deck's fields: client; three tags; **challenge (two lines)**; **what we did (three lines)**; how / media; three results; one-line quote; related; next step. The director's note adds candor fields (the rule, what review changed). This is a voice note for the two prose fields.

**Challenge, two lines.**
- Line 1 is the business problem, said as the client would say it at lunch. No "In an increasingly competitive landscape."
- Line 2 is the catch, the thing that made it hard. Where there's a real rule, name it here, concretely (the regulation, the review step, the thing they couldn't say).
- No adjectives about difficulty ("complex", "challenging"). Show the difficulty with a fact.

**What we did, three lines.**
- Line 1: the idea in one sentence a stranger could repeat.
- Line 2: where it ran and what it looked like, in nouns (trolley wraps, a radio spot, a seminar, a mailer).
- Line 3: the brave part or what review changed. If nothing brave happened, say what was hard to get right. Never leave this line for process filler ("we leveraged insights").
- Past tense, active voice, "we" only when Traffik did the thing. Numbers belong in Results, not here.

**Worked example: PIH Health, "Ask the Right Questions"** (facts from the current case page; rule wording to be checked with the team)

> **Challenge**
> Seniors wanted PIH doctors, but most didn't know which Medicare plan would let them keep one.
> And a health system can't tell a senior which plan to buy.
>
> **What we did**
> We gave them the questions to ask instead of the answer we weren't allowed to give.
> Print, digital and community seminars, all built around a short list of questions to bring to any plan.
> The question list was the campaign, so the rule that blocked a recommendation became the reason to show up.
>
> **Results** CPA 77% below industry average `[unverified, benchmark source needed]` · seminar attendance up `[number needed]` · third result `[none on file]`

Note: the deck's "three numbers" assumes results that this case doesn't fully have. Leaving the third slot visibly empty is more honest than padding it; design should allow one or two results.
