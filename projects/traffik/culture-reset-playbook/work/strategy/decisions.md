## Diagnosis

- Work often starts with no brief, or a 16-page AI-written brief that makes no choices.
- No creative strategy function. Media plans from budget; Cayley is acting strategist.
- No time for discovery or ideation. Dates are set by PMs asking designers "when will this be done?". The Task Hub has 593 standard times nobody uses.
- No round limits in SOWs. RFPs carry no production budget.
- QC (Judy, Katie) checks grammar only. Nobody owns brief fit, claims, or whether changes were made.
- Creative is often sent to clients cold, without a presentation.
- Designers log no hours, so the Hub's capacity numbers are invented.
- Task data, Apr 28 – Oct 9 2026 (710 tasks): Creative 54% on time against the original date vs. Digital 34%, agency 44%. 30% of creative requests come with one day or less. 31 creative tasks were due on a weekend. About 23% of creative tasks are rework by name. Blockers are almost never logged.
- Culture today: disconnected departments, under-resourced and disheartened teams, account-led budget culture. Target: high-achieving, award-winning, ambitious, entrepreneurial.

## Decided

**Briefs and insight**
- One-page brief; the research pack is separate. Andres signs off every brief within one business day; the client signs before work starts.
- Briefs built on a truth with a source (Pete Barry). Truth hunt before the brief: account lead, Cayley, Connor, ACD. Evidence labels: Verified / Observed / Hypothesis.

**Process**
- Two tracks. **Light:** edits, resizes, variants, template changes; ticket and change log; build → QC → send. **Full:** brief, gates G0–G3, presented.
- Rounds: 1 at territories, 2 at concept, 2 at final; beyond that is a change order.
- Review by job size: Light-track web work reviewed by Jordan; Full track by Andres. Andres spot-checks Jordan's reviews for 6 weeks.
- Maker is never checker.
- QC gate: five yes/no sections (brief, brand, delivered as asked, mistakes, presentation ready), each with a signer. QC team: Judy and Katie, given the brief, guidelines and change log.
- Creative is presented, never just sent. A short recorded walkthrough counts.
- No weekend work by default; no client sends after 3 PM Friday; "ASAP" becomes a dated deadline.
- Dates come from the plan (standard times), not from asking designers.
- Daily 9:45 stand-up, PM-led, on the board: blocked → with client → review/QC → each person → ready → close. Three questions per designer.
- Daily time logging for everyone, including Accounts and PM.
- Media decides who and where; creative decides what and how; Accounts gets approval.

**Craft (the designer reset)**
- Full track: discovery 15% + ideation 20% of the timeline. A discovery share-out opens every G1.
- Weekly 45-minute crit; work shown before every G2.
- Making the case: ADLOB, rationale, the insight's source, competitive map, lines, stealing well, Whipple-inspired copycraft.
- One award-target project per quarter; one personal craft goal per designer.

**AI**
- Agency position: AI for production only after a validated pilot with a named QA owner. Brent's DOT demo edited a review PDF, not the InDesign production file.
- Client contract clauses on responsible AI use and human-led feedback (drafted).

**Pilot**
- Variant Matrix on the CMU Applications Open Meta campaign: Figma masters plus a variant sheet; Andrea owns QC and export.

**Rollout**
- Month 1: the five rules, the board, stand-up, weekly crit, designer reset session.
- Month 2: daily hours, dates from standard times, Light and Full tracks.
- Month 3: round limits in new SOWs, the Variant Matrix pilot, the AI pilot.
