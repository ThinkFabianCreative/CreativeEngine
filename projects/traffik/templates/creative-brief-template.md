## The problem
> Ask: Why are we being asked to make anything at all? What is going wrong, or what opportunity is being missed?
> Write: two sentences. First, what people do today that keeps them from the brand. Second, why it matters now.
> Good: "Prospects inquire, then stall before applying. To them every program looks the same."   Weak: "Raise awareness of our programs."
> No problem, no brief.

## Brand platform
> Copy these from the client's approved brand house; don't rewrite them for one brief. If one is missing, write MISSING. Don't invent it here.
- **Brand promise:** *[Brand] exists to [satisfy a need].*
> Test: could a competitor sign it? If yes, it isn't a promise yet.
- **Core Creative Idea:** *Stories where [Brand] [does what].*
> Test: the work you're asking for should be one episode of this story.
- **Human truth:** *"I want ___, but ___."*
> Test: it needs a "but". A truth with no tension is a platitude.

## The job to be done
> Ask: Who do we need to move, from what they do today to what we want them to do, and what belief stands in the way?
> Write one block per job. More than three blocks is more than one brief. One job, one brief.
### [Offering or audience]
- **Persuade:** *[who] to [do what]*
> The large group we could recruit, broader than current buyers, and the single action we want.
- **Behavior to change:** *what they do today instead*
> This is the real problem, e.g. "They default to the bigger-name program."
- **Insight:** *"…"*
> What they believe deep down, in their words, that explains the behavior. It needs a tension the brand can resolve.
> Fact: "Students compare programs online."   Insight: "Every brochure says hands-on. None show me what I'd do on a Tuesday."
- **New belief:** *what we want them to think*
> It has to fit the brand promise.
- **USP:** *what only we can offer*
> The product or offer truth no competitor can claim. Test: swap in a competitor's name; if it's still true, it isn't unique. If there's no real USP, write "parity". Then the distinctiveness has to come from the idea and the brand assets, not the claim.
- **Say this (single-minded proposition):** *one sentence*
> The single most compelling thing to say, usually the USP turned into a benefit for the person. Not a tagline, and no "and". If you need an "and", you need another brief.
- **Proof:** *facts only*
> Mark each one ⚑ if it needs client or legal approval before it runs. No claims without evidence.
- **Barrier:** Credential / Aspirational / Higher order
> Credential: they doubt it works, so lead with proof. Aspirational: "not for me", so lead with emotion. Higher order: values, so lead with what the brand stands for.

## Ambition
> Agree this with the client before work starts. It's the yardstick in every review (see the judging card).
- **Rung:** *_ of 10 on the Creative Ladder*
> 5 is the floor. Higher rungs take more time; check the timing below can support the rung.

> Ideally from outside the category. Client and agency agree on them.
- **Test:** *one question the work must pass*
> E.g. "Would they share it?" or "Could a competitor run this?"

## Brief Standard check
> Before it goes out, every line must be ✓. One ✗ and the brief goes back.
- ☐ **1 Length:** three pages at most, prompts deleted
- ☐ **2 Complete:** every field filled, or MISSING / parity / N/A
- ☐ **3 Real problem:** names what people do, not a marketing goal
- ☐ **4 Single-minded:** one proposition per job, no "and", three jobs at most
- ☐ **5 True insight:** their words, with a tension
- ☐ **6 Tested USP:** proposition, unique, matters; or honestly parity
- ☐ **7 Connected:** behavior → insight → new belief → proposition
- ☐ **8 Ownable:** a competitor's name breaks the proposition
- ☐ **9 Honest evidence:** claims sourced, ⚑ marked, assumptions in italics
- ☐ **10 Ambition:** rung, three benchmarks, timing that fits
- ☐ **11 Creative space:** few mandatories, media open, no executions prescribed
- ☐ **12 Showable:** the creatives can see an ad in it
