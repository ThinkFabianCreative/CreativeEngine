## TENSION
All year, Orange County behaves; for 23 days it doesn't. The deck now states this cleanly (slide 3). The problem: after slide 4 the tension disappears. The nation becomes an inventory of props, and nothing on the work side says "the rules are different here."

## CREATIVE PERSPECTIVE
The idea is right and the opening four slides sell it. Slide 2 (a decade of "fun" themes) is the best argument in the deck: it makes "a place, not a promise" feel inevitable. From slide 6 on, the deck is a concept document, not a campaign. It shows what the country contains; it barely shows what the country says to people in channels. The RFP gives 3 points for channel translation, and we have one billboard, whose line ("A land you've never been to before") is the weakest line in the deck and does not carry the tension.

## POST-IT IDEA
A country that's only open 23 days a year. Get there before it disappears.

## VERDICT
**ADVANCE the idea · REWORK the deck.** Not BET THE PITCH yet: the idea could be, the deck cannot defend it.

**Fatal flaw:** the deck proves the idea is *generative* but not that it *advertises*. No headline system, no campaign arc in media, one weak OOH. Section 4(c) is unearned.
**Unfair advantage:** a 23-day country gives the District urgency, a passport-sales mechanic and a 2030 kit no competitor will match. And "Year after year of fun" is a slide only we will show.

**Residual risk, noted once (Andres's call stands):** the slide 1 emblem (purple velvet zeppelin, gold script) is the closest thing in the deck to Wonka's live trade dress, and slide 20 itself lists "the zeppelin" and "purple-and-gold" under *Leave*. The deck contradicts its own rule. Purple can stay; the velvet zeppelin plus swirl script lockup is what needs its own shape before the District sees it.

## Top 5 pushbacks
1. **Story: the deck peaks at slide 5 and then becomes an appendix.** Slides 6–14 are inventory. Reorder so the work arrives as a campaign: idea → hero film/launch → OOH/social/digital → tourism tactics → onsite → calendar. The nation's paperwork belongs after the audience has seen the ads.
2. **No headline system carrying the tension.** "Visa not required. Stretchy pants recommended." / "Pledge allegiance to the corn dog." / "The only country you can visit in Costa Mesa." / "Founded 1890. Open 23 days a year." are in the concept doc and missing from the work. Build a 6–8 line system on one rule (country language, Fair content, a closing date), and put it on the OOH. Replace "A land you've never been to before" (generic, any destination ad).
3. **Channel translation is almost absent (3 pts at risk).** One bulletin. Needed: social (vertical video + carousel), digital/display, the website/ticket flow as a tourism site, radio/audio (anthem), email, onsite, earned (troupes as a news story), Spanish-language media. One slide that shows the same idea moving across six channels is worth more than slides 6, 9 and 11 combined.
4. **Placeholders are doing the copy's job.** Slide 12 (two of three populations blank), slide 14 (seven of ten things blank), slide 17 (all dates blank). Internally fine, but they make the strongest tactics look unfinished. WordSmither fills them this round; dates can stay bracketed, things-to-do cannot.
5. **Craft hygiene.** Footer page numbers don't match slide positions (slide 11 reads "10", 21 reads "19"); slide 21 says "v02 · INTERNAL DRAFT"; slide 15 has no notes and flags its own wordmark as wrong; slide 9 notes say the Ticket Guard is "still to generate" while slide 8 calls it "the best visual idea." The best visual idea needs a picture.

## What's still missing to win the 10 points
- **(a) Ownability:** an emblem that is not Wonka's; a line about why only the OC Fair (1890, 150 acres, 4-H, Centennial Farm) can be this country. Show the competitive "fun" slide as the distinctiveness proof.
- **(b) Concept strength:** a hero execution, e.g. a 30s "tourism film" script or the Independence Day launch stunt. The deck has no single piece of work that makes the room laugh.
- **(c) Channels:** the cross-channel slide; social and video frames; Spanish media executions (not just a translated title); the ticket/passport purchase flow.
- **Sales and date mechanics:** passport as the ticket product (pre-sale, stamps = return visits), midweek "OC Fairlandia on a Wednesday," closing-week "last days of the nation." Show at least one with a price/date placeholder.
- **Headline system** carrying "the rules are different here" and "while it lasts."
