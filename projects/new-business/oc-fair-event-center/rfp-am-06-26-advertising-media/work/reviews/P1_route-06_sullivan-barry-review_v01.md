## The unlock: Opposite Tool + truth + conflict

The harmony version ("everyone's welcome") is the expected one. Flip it. **The real Fair day is a negotiation.** Dad wants the pig races, the kids want the rides, abuela wants the quilts, the teenager wants to be anywhere else, and somebody's still in the corn-dog line. Everybody in your group wants a different Fair. The truth is that the Fair is big enough to give every one of them theirs.

- **Human truth:** no family or friend group ever agrees on what to do first.
- **Product truth:** 150 acres, livestock to headliners. The Fair actually has everyone's thing.
- **Cultural truth:** a county of 3 million very different people that shares one Fair.
- **Enemy:** the compromise. At the Fair, nobody has to settle.

Fun comes first, because the joke is the family argument, and it never reads as a diversity statement.

## Sharper lines

| English | Spanish (native direction) |
|---|---|
| **Everybody's Fair. Nobody agrees on why.** | **La Feria de todos. Y a cada quien la suya.** |
| One Fair. No two the same. | Una Feria. Ninguna igual. |
| Everybody wants something different. Perfect. | Cada quien quiere algo distinto. Perfecto. |

Recommendation: **Everybody's Fair. Nobody agrees on why.** The second half adds the smile, the tension and the specificity the first half lacks. *"A cada quien la suya"* lands on its own in Spanish instead of being translated.

## What changes in the work

- **Platform:** *Everybody's Fair* becomes the platform; *Nobody agrees on why* is the engine. "What's your Fair?" drops to a participation mechanic (street interviews, button maker, planner).
- **Executions become two- or three-person arguments,** not single portraits. The :30 is a family that splits up at the gate into five different Fairs and meets again at sunset, each with a different button. Radio is a group chat that can't agree.
- **The Button** now settles arguments: everybody wears their own.
- **Headline pattern:** *[Person A] wants X. [Person B] wants Y. Everybody's Fair.* For example: *Dad wants the pig races. The kids want the Zipper. Grandma wants to judge your jam.*
