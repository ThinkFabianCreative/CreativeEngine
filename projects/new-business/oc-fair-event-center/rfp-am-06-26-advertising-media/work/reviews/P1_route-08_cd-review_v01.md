Brand ownership at 4 is below the JUDGE gate (6). That is the identity, not the idea.

**Fatal flaw:** the look is Wonka's, not the Fair's. Purple velvet zeppelin + gold swirl script is Ferrero's current trade dress, live on wonka.com this fall. **Unfair advantage:** a country generates everything: passport, flag, guard, map, regions, citizenship. No other bidder will hand the District a whole world.

## Push back

1. **The identity fails rubric (a).** "Distinctive, ownable" is 3 points, and RFP objective 3 is to "strengthen and protect the distinct brands of the OC Fair." A committee member who has seen the Wonka relaunch reads "they put our name on a candy brand." Swapping the Fair's orange for royal purple also walks away from the Fair's own equity. Keep the craft (velvet, brass, gold, the zeppelin as a vehicle) and take back the color: an **OC-orange velvet envelope with the leaf at the nose**, or a different vehicle that only the Fair owns. Purple can stay as royal accent and night.
2. **Too many names.** A country of its own, OC Fairlandia, Extra is Normal, Republic of Extra, Come out and play, the Grand Guide, the Ticket Guard, the Court of Extra, the NORMAL stamp. The committee scores one theme. Lock a hierarchy: **platform** *A country of its own* → **name** *OC Fairlandia* → **motto** *Extra is Normal*. Retire "Republic of Extra" and "Come out and play" from this route.
3. **"Fairlandia" sits next to Disneyland.** A *-land* name in Orange County invites the comparison, and the Fair loses it on spectacle. It works only if the work keeps saying what Disney can't: real farms, real kids, real neighbors, public land. Keep it, but never let the world look like a theme park.
4. **The tension has gone quiet.** "A country of its own" is a premise. The funny truth ("OC behaves all year") lives in the films and manifesto. Make sure the headline system carries it, or the route becomes pretty world-building.
5. **The world is outgrowing the Fair.** Zeppelins, castles, guards and courts are costumes. The RFP scores practical application and sales: dates, capacity, real attractions. Every frame needs a real guest, a real attraction, or a reason to buy a day (passport stamps, midweek regions).
6. **Production honesty.** AI lettering, velvet CGI and costumed guards against roughly $100K a year of TV production. Show the District a version they can afford: illustration and photography, with costumes for a handful of staff, not a CGI universe.
7. **What's working, keep it:** the passport-and-regions system (it sells return visits), the Ticket Guard (Alice's card soldiers in ride-ticket tabards is the best visual idea in the route), the map, bilingual by design, and welcoming-never-screening.
