### Visual source: fairytale lore (Andres, 2026-10-22)

The look is drawn from shared fairytale and carnival lore, not from any one brand: **Victorian-era carnival, The Wizard of Oz, Alice in Wonderland, Dr. Seuss, Disney's Sleeping Beauty**, with Wonka, Tim Burton and Peter Pan's flying ship as mood. Purple regalia, gold foil, satin, crowns, banners, seals and pageantry are a shared visual language (royal purple long predates Wonka; see Cadbury). No other county fair or event owns it, and neither does Wonka. Ownership comes from what only the Fair has inside that language: 1890, 23 days, the orange and leaf, 4-H, Centennial Farm, the Passport.

**Texture layer (Andres, 2026-10-22):** a little steampunk and retro-futurism, with a touch of real-life (live-action) *Howl's Moving Castle* and *One Piece*. What each adds to the country:
- *Howl's Moving Castle*: a nation that arrives, stays, and moves on, which is the 23-day country. Ramshackle-magical architecture; warm, lived-in wonder.
- *One Piece* (live action): the voyage and crew spirit; the flying-ship sail; a joyful band of misfits as citizens.
- Steampunk and retro-futurism: the nation's machinery and craft (the airship, brass fittings, gauges, mechanical wonders on the Midway), a 1890-meets-tomorrow feel.
These dress the nation; they do not replace it. The steampunk "Extra-ordinary Works" factory stays retired as a frame.

Working rule (Andres, 2026-10-22): the swirl display lettering and the zeppelin are part of the system, alongside the passport stamp. Decision made; not to be re-raised.

### Tourism tactics

**1. Postcards.** "Greetings from OC Fairlandia," one per region and one per tradition. The system runs as print mailers, social templates, onsite postcard stations (write one, the Fair mails it), and the post-visit email. Each card names a region with a silly population: *"The Barns. Population: 400 goats."*

**2. Touring travel troupes.** Before and during the Fair, small troupes from OC Fairlandia travel Orange County like a visiting delegation: the Grand Guide, two Ticket Guards, a 4-H courier, a bilingual ambassador. They appear at farmers markets, malls, beaches, ballparks and community events, hand out passports and postcards, swear in honorary citizens, and stamp the first page. Clips feed social; local news covers the "foreign delegation." Warm and welcoming only: no checkpoints, no papers.

**3. "Things to do."** The campaign speaks like a tourism guide for a country you can only visit 23 days a year:
- *Top 10 things to do in OC Fairlandia* (web, social carousel, print, email)
- *OC Fairlandia in a day / an evening / with kids / with grandparents* (itineraries that steer date and daypart: "OC Fairlandia on a Wednesday")
- *Only in OC Fairlandia* local experiences: shear a sheep, judge a pie, watch the national championship
- Search and the planner answer like a travel site: "best day to visit," "what to eat," "how to get there"

These sell days, not just the idea: every guide ends with a date and a ticket.

For 23 days a year, 150 acres in Costa Mesa become a country of its own: its own flag, motto, passport, currency, dish, sport, anthem, guard, court and regions. Everyone in Orange County is a citizen. Nobody needs a visa.

## Tone: warm and touristy, never screening

**In:** passports, flags, banners, mottos, sigils, postcards, stamps, a court, kingdoms and regions, ceremonies, honorary titles, a tourism board.
**Never:** customs, checkpoints, inspections, "papers, please," border walls, deportation or citizenship-test jokes. In Orange County that lands on real families. Every border moment is a welcome.

### Wayfinding (a map you can walk)

- **The national map:** an illustrated storybook map of the 150 acres, every region with its sigil, little Seuss hills, a rabbit-hole "you are here." Printed on the Passport's inside cover and on giant boards.
- **Leaning signposts:** Seuss-curved posts with stacked arrows, each in its region color: *"Pig Races, this way. Funnel cake, that way. Normal, nowhere."*
- **Region gateways:** an arch at each region with its banner and sigil: *"You are now entering the Barns. Population: 400 goats."*
- **Distances that make no sense:** *"Corn dogs: 47 steps. Your diet: 2,000 miles."*
- **Bilingual by design:** every sign in English and Spanish, equal size.
- **Accessibility:** clear plain-language layer under the whimsy (big type, high contrast, pictograms, accessible routes marked). The joke never costs anyone their way.

## How it plays (carried from the original frame)

- **Tourism-board films** (VisitOSLO premise): a glossy travel film for a 150-acre nation, hosted by the Grand Guide. *"Experience the ancient tradition of the pig race."*
- **Passport stamps:** each region is a stamp, and collecting them is the trail (the scavenger-hunt idea with a reason to exist). Collect them all to become an honorary citizen.
- **Onsite:** the welcome arch at the gate ("You are now entering a country of its own"), Citizen of the Day, the sunset anthem led by the Grand Guide, the Court of Extra at the competitions, the Ticket Guard on parade.
- **Every property is a region,** year-round: the Amphitheatre as the nation's concert hall; Centennial Farm and The Ranch as the countryside; Imaginology as the academy; Heroes Hall honored quietly.
- **Engines carried from Route 07:** "Extra is Normal" as the motto and the NORMAL stamp; the Is-This-Normal Line; the Extra Awards, presented by the Court.
- **National calendar:** Independence Day (reveal), Passport Season (presale), The Nation Is Open (Fair), See You Next Year (recap).
