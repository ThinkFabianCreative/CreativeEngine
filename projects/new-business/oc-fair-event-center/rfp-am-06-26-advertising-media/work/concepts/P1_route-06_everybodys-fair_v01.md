## The line

# Everybody's Fair.
### La Feria de todos.

## The idea in one sentence

Orange County is 3 million people who love completely different things, and once a year there's one place that has all of them. Every execution is one person and their very specific Fair obsession, signed off by the button they wear for it.

## Why it's fun first

The joy is the obsession, not the demographic. We never say who someone *is*; we show what they *can't miss*. The variety of the county shows up through casting and through how weirdly specific everyone's favorite thing is. Nobody in an ad is a representative of anything. They're the guy who's done the giant slide 40 times.

**Rule:** a headline names a *thing*, never a group. "For Danny, it's the pig races" is in. "For the Latino community, it's…" is never in.

## The truth underneath (from brief v02)

- Everyone arrives with a different reason: the same county, a hundred different Fairs.
- It literally is everybody's: public land Orange County fought to keep in 2009–11, and a mission built on access for the whole community. This is the proof story for PR and the proposal, not the ad headline.
- 140 years since 1890: 140 years of people finding *their* thing here.

## The device: the Button

A big round pin-back button, OC Fair orange, with the orange-and-leaf mark at the top and a hand-written fill-in:

```
        ( OC FAIR mark )
          MY FAIR IS
     ___________________
   [ "THE PIG RACES" in marker ]
      EVERYBODY'S FAIR · 2030
```

Why a button:
- **Personal by design.** Same badge, a different word on every one. The system is the variety.
- **Ownable.** The phrase can be common; this button, with this mark and this fill-in, is ours. It becomes the theme logo, the sign-off on every ad, the onsite activation and the merch.
- **Not 2028.** Deliberately not a ribbon, so it doesn't repeat "It's a Blue Ribbon Summer."
- **Cheap, real and collectable.** Button makers onsite, trading culture, a 140-years commemorative set.
- **Hand-written** is the fun signal: marker, imperfect, personal; never a typeset template.

Visual direction:
- Palette: OC Fair orange, sky blue, cream, with a marker black for the hand-writing.
- Type: a bold friendly display face for the master line; real hand-lettering on the buttons (shot from real guests where possible, never fake "handwriting fonts" in hero work).
- Photography: tight, joyful, mid-obsession. People caught in the moment of their thing: mid-bite, mid-scream, mid-cheer at the pig race. Button visible on chest or bag.
- Layout: one person, one obsession, one button, one line. Grids of many buttons for the "everybody" moments (crowd, OOH, finale of the spot).

## Executions

**TV / CTV :30, "Everybody's Thing."** Rapid, funny, one-second portraits, each person mid-obsession with their button: the goat girl, the funnel-cake dad, the folklórico dancer, the teen on the 12th ride, the abuela judging the jam, the couple at the concert, the kid asleep on a giant stuffed banana. VO counts them off fast ("Pig races. Quilts. Third corn dog. The slide. The slide. The slide."). It speeds up into a wall of hundreds of buttons that pulls back into the Fair's skyline at sunset. Super: *Everybody's Fair.* End card: dates and tickets. Spanish version cast and written in Spanish, not dubbed. Cutdowns: :15 and :06 as single obsessions.

**Radio / streaming audio, "Ask Anyone."** Real-sounding vox pops: "What's your Fair?" Thirty seconds of wildly different, very specific answers that overlap and argue ("The pigs." "No, the pig races are second to the—" "Fried butter."). Tag: *Everybody's Fair. Get your day now.* A Spanish version recorded with Spanish-speaking guests, not translated.

**OOH.** Freeway bulletin: one giant button, one obsession, six words max. *My Fair is the giant slide.* Variety comes from the rotation, not the board. Transit shelters: a grid of 12 buttons, each a different obsession, one left blank: *What's yours?* Spanish-language bulletins in Spanish-dominant corridors.

**Paid social and short-form.**
- "What's your Fair?" street interviews across OC in spring; answers become in-flight ads with the person's own button.
- AR button maker: write your thing, wear it, share it.
- Creator brief: "Your one thing, done 10 times." Creators pick an obsession and go all in.

**Web, search, display.** The landing page is a button maker that turns into a planner: pick your thing → we suggest the best day with room, the times and the ticket. Search answers intent first (tickets, dates, things to do); the theme supports. Display: rotating buttons, each linking to the thing it names.

**Email and CRM.** Last year's purchasers get "Your Fair was the pig races. They're back." (only where the data is authorized). Post-visit: "Here's what your Fair looked like," plus next year's save-the-date.

**Partners and POP.** Vendors get their own button: "Our Fair is the best tamales on the midway." Local businesses get window clings: "Ask us what our Fair is."

## At the Fair

- **Button press stations** at the gates: write your thing, press your button, wear it. The single most visible proof of the theme onsite.
- **The Button Wall:** thousands of guest buttons on a wall that grows all summer. The finale shot of the post-Fair film.
- **"Everybody's" daily lineup:** each day, one guest's obsession becomes the "Fair of the Day" on the big screens.
- **Theme exhibit, "140 Years of Everybody's Fair":** 140 years of what people loved here, from the 1890 horse race to the latest fried food. The fight to keep the fairgrounds public appears here as part of the story.
- **Competitions:** a "My Thing" open category, anything you're obsessed with, judged for passion.
- **Merch:** the 140-years commemorative button set, blank-button tees with a marker, trading pins.

## Across District properties

The button flexes without changing the property brands:
- Pacific Amphitheatre: *My Fair is the show.* / "Everybody's got a show."
- Centennial Farm and The Ranch: *My Fair is the barn.*
- Imaginology: *My Fair is the robot I built.*
- Heroes Hall: a quieter, respectful use only ("Everybody's history"); no jokes.
- Venue rentals: *Everybody's venue*, for the year-round sales team, only if the District wants it.

## Risks and how we handle them

- **Reads as a diversity statement.** Guardrail: name things, never groups; casting carries the range. Test the spot with the line removed: if it still plays as a funny montage, it's right.
- **Plain phrase.** Run the USPTO search (entertainment and event classes) before Oct 26. Lead every piece with the button.
- **Fill-ins written for people we invented.** In the pitch, mark the people as casting placeholders. In production, real guests and their real obsessions.
- **Heroes Hall.** Kept out of the comedy.
- **Spanish.** Linguist review of every line; "carreras de cerditos," "resbaladilla" and "banderilla" vary by region, so choose by the OC audience, not a dictionary.
