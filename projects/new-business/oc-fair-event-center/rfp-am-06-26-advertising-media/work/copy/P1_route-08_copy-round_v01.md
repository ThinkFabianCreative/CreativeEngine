## 1. Headline system

**The rule.** Every headline is written in the voice of a tourism board for a real country, about something true and specific to the Fair, and it has to turn on one of two hinges: **the rules are different here** (something OC never does the other 342 days becomes normal) or **while it lasts** (the country closes). If a line could sell any destination, or names no Fair thing, it is out.

Shorthand for the team: *country voice + Fair fact + (rule flip or closing clock).*

## 5. :30 tourism film

**Choice: VisitOSLO-style deadpan contradiction.** The Dundee fake trailer needs a big star and a reveal; we don't have the star and the reveal is the whole idea, which the audience gets in one frame. The deadpan narrator suits the tension better: a sober, official voice describing a country that refuses to be sober. Every line is a straight tourism claim; every picture shows real guests breaking the OC rules. The joke is the gap, and the gap is "the rules are different here."

**Title:** *Visit OC Fairlandia*
**VO:** calm, official tourism narrator (could be the Grand Guide off-screen, revealed at the end)

| Time | Picture (real guests, real Fair moments) | VO / SFX |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:04 | Wide, golden hour: flags on the grounds, the Ferris wheel. Super: *OC Fairlandia* | VO: "OC Fairlandia is a nation of quiet tradition." |
| 0:04–0:07 | Pig race crowd erupts, a grown man screaming at pig number 4 | SFX: crowd roar. VO: "Its people are reserved." |
| 0:07–0:10 | Grandmother biting a giant turkey leg, eyes closed | VO: "Its cuisine is light." |
| 0:10–0:13 | 4-H kid, serious, walking a steer bigger than her family car | VO: "Its citizens are mostly human." |
| 0:13–0:17 | Couple on a ride, mid-scream; teen asleep on a hay bale | VO: "Life here moves at a gentle pace." |
| 0:17–0:21 | Ticket Guards in tabards bow a family through the gates; the Grand Guide tips a hat | VO: "And visitors are always welcome." |
| 0:21–0:26 | Sunset, crowd singing the anthem, phones up | VO: "It has been this way since 1890. For 23 days a year." |
| 0:26–0:30 | Lockup on purple. Super: *A Country of Its Own. Open [dates]. Get your Passport at ocfair.com* [confirm URL/product] | VO: "The OC Fair. A country of its own. Visit while it lasts." |

**:15 cutdown.** Keep the pig race, the turkey leg and the steer (three contradictions), then the end: "OC Fairlandia. Open 23 days a year. Visit while it lasts." Three beats plus the clock is the minimum that still lands the joke.

**Spanish version.** Write a new VO, don't dub. The deadpan works in Spanish in a slightly more formal, documental register ("OC Fairlandia es una nación de costumbres tranquilas"). Same pictures, picked so the Spanish cut can swap in one moment native to Latino families at the Fair (*cast from real guests; never stage it as a token shot*). End: "La Feria es otro país. Visítalo mientras dure." Linguist review.

## 7. Self-critique (copycraft stress test: specific, true, compressed, could only be the Fair, carries the tension)

**Strongest.**
- *Founded 1890. Open 23 days a year.* True, short, owns both history and scarcity. No competitor can run it. Should be the OOH workhorse.
- *Pledge allegiance to the corn dog.* Highest stopping power. Risk: "pledge allegiance" plays with a national symbol; mild, but test it with the District.
- *Here, a goat can outrank you.* Specific and true to livestock judging; nobody else can say it.
- The :30 deadpan. The structure is proven (credited as VisitOSLO-style), and every contradiction is a real Fair moment, so it shoots as documentary, not staging.
- *"After that, it's just Costa Mesa."* Best closing device; reusable on every guide and email.

**Weakest / cut candidates.**
- *In OC Fairlandia, you're allowed to want the giant one.* Soft; "allowed to" is a hedge. Cut unless a visual makes it.
- *Dinner on a stick is the law of the land.* "Law of the land" edges toward the rules/enforcement language we avoid. Cut or rewrite.
- *A whole country. Gone by mid-August.* Good, but unusable until dates are confirmed.
- *Population: 8,000 people who know every word.* Reads as a capacity claim; use the no-number version unless confirmed.
- Spanish #6 ("¿me da otro?") is fun but may read as translated wit. Linguist decides.
- *Visa not required.* Funny, and the nearest thing in the set to the forbidden territory. Keep off large OOH.

**What's still missing.** One line that carries T3 (the county fought to keep this land) in the country voice. It could be the strongest emotional line in the system ("The county voted to keep its country" would need fact-checking against how the 2009–11 fight actually resolved: the court, not a vote).
