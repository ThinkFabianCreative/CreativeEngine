## 1. "Perfectly Normal" — TV/CTV :60 / :30 / :15

*Premise borrowed (VisitOSLO): the narrator plays down exactly what the viewer finds irresistible, and the contradiction is the joke.*

**Our twist:** the voice insists the Fair is completely, boringly normal, in the flat tone of a county public-information announcement. The picture shows the most extra things in Orange County. No on-screen spokesperson (to stay clear of Six Flags' "Funsultant").

**Voice:** calm, mildly bored, polite. Think a county parking-information recording.

| Picture | VO |
|---|---|
| Wide, golden hour: the Fair's midway, rides spinning, thousands of people. | "Welcome to the OC Fair. Please don't get excited. It's a fairly normal place." |
| A man takes a first bite of a deep-fried [placeholder]. His eyes close. He has a religious experience. | "The food is food. You eat it. That's about it." |
| A grandmother in the front car of a ride, screaming, arms up, grandkids screaming next to her. | "The rides go around. Some of them go up. Nothing to write home about." |
| A 4-H teen blow-drying her steer with total focus. The steer looks fantastic. | "Some local kids have brought cows. Cows are common." |
| A pig race. The crowd loses its mind. | "There are pigs. They walk quickly. Moving on." |
| A dad on his ninth attempt at a carnival game, sweating, deadly serious. | "There are games. You probably won't win. So there's really no point." |
| A kid pulling a giant carrot out of the ground at the farm, holding it up like a trophy. | "You can touch some vegetables. If that's your thing." |
| Night: the Amphitheatre crowd singing every word. | "In the evening there's music. People seem to know the words." |
| The whole family walking out under the lights, sticky, sunburned, exhausted and grinning. | "Honestly, you could skip it." |
| Super: **EXTRA IS NORMAL.** OC Fair mark. | "Or, you know. Pick a day." |
| End card: ocfair.com · [dates] · *Pick your day.* | — |

**Why it isn't the reference:** the original plays down a city as underwhelming. Ours turns the Fair's own theme line into the joke: everything the voice calls "normal" is the definition of extra. The NORMAL stamp lands on each shot as the visual punchline.

**Spanish :60, "Nada del otro mundo."** Its own script and voice, not dubbed. VO in the same deadpan register: *"Bienvenidos a la Feria de OC. No se emocionen. Es un lugar bastante normal… La comida es comida. Te la comes y ya."* Close: *"La verdad, ni vale la pena. O bueno… escoge tu día."* Super: **AQUÍ LO EXAGERADO ES NORMAL.**

**Cutdowns:** :15s as single contradictions (*"There are pigs. They walk quickly."* over the pig race). :06s for social and pre-roll. Radio uses the same voice over Fair sound: the audio contradiction (screams, crowd, sizzling) does the job the picture does on TV.

## 2. "The Is-This-Normal Line" — social, radio, PR, onsite

*Premise borrowed (The Swedish Number): real people from the place demonstrate its character through a real, live interaction.*

**Our twist:** a phone and text line where anyone in Orange County can ask the Fair whether something is normal. A real Fair person answers: a 4-H kid, a corn-dog vendor, a competition judge, a ride operator, a Centennial Farm educator, a grandmother who has entered the jam contest for 30 years.

**How it works**
- **Launch (spring):** OOH, radio and social carry one thing: a number and a question. *"Is it normal to want a corn dog for breakfast? Ask the Fair. [number]."*
- **Calls are answered by real people, on a schedule,** in English and Spanish. Every answer ends the same way: "Yes. That's normal here."
- **Answers become content,** with permission: short clips of real calls (*"Is it normal to cry at a goat show?" "Ma'am, I cried yesterday."*) for social, radio spots and the PR story.
- **During the Fair** the line moves onsite: an "Is This Normal?" booth near the entrance, staffed by rotating Fair people.
- **Every call ends on a next step:** a text with the planner link and the days with room.

**Example calls (scripted for the pitch; real calls in production)**
- *"Is it normal to plan my whole summer around one funnel cake?"* Vendor: "Sir, I plan my whole life around it. Yes, that's normal."
- *"¿Es normal que mi abuela quiera ganarle a todo el condado con su mole?"* Judge: "Señora, aquí eso es lo más normal del mundo."
- *"Is it normal for my kid to want to show a rabbit?"* 4-H member, age 12: "Yes. Mine is named Kevin."

**Spanish:** a full Spanish line staffed by Spanish-speaking Fair people, with its own launch: *"¿Esto es normal? Pregúntale a la Feria."*

**Why it isn't the reference:** the original connected callers with random citizens to show a country's openness. Ours is about a specific attitude (extra is normal), answered by the people who make the Fair, and every call converts to a visit.

**What it needs from the District (for the pitch to flag):** staffing, a consent and recording process, safeguarding for any minors (the RFP requires it), and a human-only rule: no AI voices answering.

## 3. "EXTRA" — the summer blockbuster that's actually an invitation

*Premise borrowed (Dundee): entertainment that looks like something else, then turns into a destination invitation with a practical next step.*

**Our twist:** a fake summer-blockbuster trailer for a movie called **EXTRA**, released in spring like a real tentpole, teased on social, cut in a real trailer grammar. At the end it turns out the "movie" is the OC Fair, and the release date is the Fair's opening day.

**Trailer :90 / :60 (online), :30 (TV), :15 teasers**

| Picture | Sound |
|---|---|
| Black. Studio-style logo animation, but it's the OC Fair mark, rendered like a film studio ident. | Low brass hit. |
| Text card: *THIS SUMMER* | Trailer drone. |
| Slow-motion: a 4-H teen walking into a show ring with her steer, like a hero walking into battle. | Trailer VO: "In a county where everyone behaves…" |
| A grandmother cracking her knuckles at the pie table. | "…one woman will do whatever it takes." |
| A dad stares down a carnival game. Extreme close-up of his eyes. | "One man. One ring toss." |
| Rapid montage: ride drop, fryer flare-up, pig race photo finish, the Amphitheatre crowd. | Trailer music builds. |
| Text card: *140 YEARS IN THE MAKING* | Hit. |
| A kid holds up a giant stuffed prize in triumph, in slow motion. | Choir. |
| Title card: **EXTRA** | Full hit. |
| Card: *EXTRA IS NORMAL.* | Silence. |
| Card: *Now playing at 88 Fair Drive. Opening [date]. Pick your day.* | VO, normal voice: "Tickets at ocfair.com." |

**System**
- **Teaser posters** in movie-poster style: one-sheets for "EXTRA" with real Fair people as the stars, billed with titles like *"The Pie Judge"* and *"The Kid With the Rabbit."*
- **The reveal** happens on social and in PR: "the OC Fair just made a blockbuster trailer."
- **Ticket CTA in the movie's own language:** "Choose your showtime" = pick your day; matinee (early entry), late show (evening). It turns date management into a movie-ticket ritual everyone already understands.
- **Spanish version:** a Spanish-language trailer in the style of a Mexican action-comedy trailer, with its own VO and cast: *"Este verano… lo exagerado llega a la Feria."*
- **Pacific Amphitheatre** gets the "double feature" line: the Fair by day, a headliner by night.

**Why it isn't the reference:** Dundee faked a film sequel with movie stars to sell a country. Ours uses trailer grammar with real Fair people as the stars, and the "showtimes" do real work for date mix.
