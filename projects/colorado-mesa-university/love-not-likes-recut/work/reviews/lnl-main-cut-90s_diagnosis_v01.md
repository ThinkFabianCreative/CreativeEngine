## Diagnosis: the footage is good, the film is set in the wrong genre

The cinematography is clean, well cast and well lit. That is the problem. It is shot and graded as a sunny lifestyle spot (green trees, warm daylight, the hero smiling at the sky), so the world he walks through never feels wrong. The idea needs a world that has gone strange and a hero who is the only one awake. Right now a pleasant town has a few people on phones.

1. **No dread.** The look stays the same from the first frame to the food drive. There is no visual line between the phone world and his world.
2. **Flat escalation.** Each vignette runs 10–25s at the same intensity. The zombie section (24–44s) is the longest yet weakest: the hero just looks around at people on phones.
3. **The climax costs nothing.** At the skatepark he pushes between them, and it's over in a few shots. There's no hit, no risk and no mark. The black-eye ending you described doesn't exist in this cut.
4. **The payoff is borrowed.** The ending is about a food drive and a mascot, not the kid he protected. The relationship from the climax is dropped at about 70s. The kid walks with him (good), then disappears.
5. **Supers carry the message the images don't.** "While some pursue likes, others stand for love" explains what the film should have made us feel.
6. **Admin:** runs 99s, not 90. The board's CTA ("Are you a Maverick at heart? Apply to CMU today.") is missing.

## Recut plan (existing footage first)

- **Grade as two worlds.** Phone world: desaturated, cool, lifted shadows, faces lit cyan by screens (add phone-glow power windows on faces). The hero, and later the campus, get warmth back. The temperature returns only when he acts.
- **Sound does the horror.** Remove the lifestyle music from the first 70s. Build a bed of taps, notification pings, shutter clicks and muffled voices that thickens into a drone, with a heartbeat or low pulse under the crosswalk. Hard silence on the shove. Score enters only after he steps in.
- **Compress and escalate:** open 0–8s → 4s (cut the sky look). Sidewalk + filmed donation → 14s. Zombies → 10s, rebuilt from the split-screen filming faces as a rising montage (speed ramps, the near-collision). Skatepark gets the time saved, about 25s: slow the ring of raised phones, hold on the hero's face before he moves.
- **Restore the cost.** Look for a take where he takes a hit. If none exists, this is the pickup.
- **End on the two of them.** Bridge from the skatepark walk-off to the food drive with the kid beside him. Final laugh is hero + kid (black eye), food drive behind them. Keep the redhead and mascot as texture, not payoff.
- **Supers:** cut "While some pursue likes, / others stand for love." Keep only "Do it for love, not the likes." Restore the CTA card. Land at 90s, then cut :60 / :30 / :15 from it.

## Pickups (small live shoot, same actors)

## Casting change (Andres, 2026-11-11): gentle giant hero

The hero must be a big gentle giant, the CMU lineman from the original idea. The current actor is slight, so the recut can't deliver this. Recasting means a reshoot of every hero shot, and the existing footage survives only as world and crowd material (phone zombies, filming faces, the fake donation, campus).

Why it's stronger: size sets up a misread. The audience expects muscle and gets tenderness, and the person who can end the fight in a second is the one who would rather not. "Enough" from someone his size works without a punch. The black eye lands harder: he could have hit back and didn't.

Spec: 6'3"+ and 270 lb+, a real lineman build, not a gym physique. Soft eyes, slow movements, a shy smile, carries a small backpack that looks tiny on him. Age reads 19–21. Ideally a real CMU football player, which also gives authenticity. Casting language per house rule: name ethnicity specifically, not "Latino".

Shot implications: low angles and wide lenses to give him scale; cramped frames (bus seat, crowd) where he has to fold himself in; the bullied kid small beside him in the final laugh.
