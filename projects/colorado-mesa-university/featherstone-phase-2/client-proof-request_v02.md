Phase 2 of "Welcome to Featherstone" will show who CMU is, not only who it isn't. We have read CMU's public website and would like to build on what CMU already says about itself. Before any of it appears in advertising, we need CMU to confirm it and to supply what the website does not cover.

## 1. Please confirm these published facts for advertising use

We found these on coloradomesa.edu. Please confirm each is current and approved, or correct it.

**Figures that disagree on the site. Which is right?**
- Academic programs: "255 programs and 51 minors" (homepage, future students) vs "200+ academic programs" (Why CMU).
- Financial aid: "$88 Million awarded in financial aid last year" (homepage) vs "$77 million total aid awarded last year" (financial aid page). Which year?
- Campus housing: "Nearly 2,300 students" in "14" halls (future students) vs "More than 3,200 students" across "more than 17 residence halls" (September 16, 2026 news release).
- Tuition ranking: "3rd lowest tuition in Colorado" (homepage) vs "3rd lowest tuition and fees among other four-year Colorado colleges" (financial aid page). Which wording is approved, and what is the source of the ranking?

**Facts we would like to use:**
- "More than 10,000 students", 15.4% from outside Colorado. Which year?
- "60% of first-time students received a scholarship last year." Which year?
- "Small classes with professors that know you by name." Is there a class-size or student-to-faculty figure we may cite?
- 2026-27 cost of attendance figures. Will 2027-28 rates be available before launch?
- Career outcomes: "74% of 2025 graduates have found success in their career path…". How is "success" defined, and may we cite it?
- CMU Tech: "We reduced tuition for career and technical programs by 40%." From what baseline, and when?
- Athletics, campus and founding facts (NCAA Division II, 28 teams, 141 acres, founded 1925, accredited by the Higher Learning Commission).
- Mission, values and "Human Scale University" language: may the campaign quote them?

## 2. Enrollment and application figures

- Verified enrollment and application figures, with baseline year and source. Needed before any work states or implies that "CMU enrollment is up", even inside fiction. The website does not state a current increase.
- Confirmation or correction of the agency-published Phase 1 results ("9.4% growth in applications since 2024", "new application record"; impressions and engagement figures), and permission to cite them.
- Permission to quote the Phase 1 press coverage (Forbes, The Chronicle of Higher Education) and President John Marshall's quoted remark.

## 3. People

- Real student stories: current students or recent graduates willing to appear or be described. For each: signed consent and release covering advertising use, channels, duration and markets, plus a named contact.
- The students named on the website (for example testimonial speakers and the Mass Communication students who interviewed Gov. Polis) may appear in advertising only with their own consent.
- Faculty or staff willing to appear, with the same releases.
- Any rules on depicting students: minors, athletes, identifiable people in crowd shots.

## 4. Campus places, events and traditions

- Campus locations we may film or photograph, with location permissions and restricted areas.
- Permission to portray real events in a comic context, including a fictional character trying and failing to disrupt them: for example Homecoming, Stampede Week, the Student Showcase, Mesa Experience, New Mav Days.
- The 2027 Student Showcase date, and the filming calendar.

## 5. Brand and mascot

- CMU brand guidelines, logo files and lockups.
- Mascot usage rules: whether the mascot may appear in a comic scene with Featherstone characters, and costume availability.
- Approval of the Phase 2 platform line: "We care about what you know, not who you know." Phase 1 aired "We care about who you are, not who you know." Please confirm which line CMU approves for Phase 2.

## 6. Sensitivities and approvals

- Featherstone satirizes family money buying access. CMU has just announced its largest-ever gift, and its Homecoming is sponsored by a bank. Are there donor, sponsor or partner sensitivities the campaign must respect?
- Who approves creative, claims, and legal or compliance review, and how long review takes.
- Any limits on satire: referencing other real institutions, real people, or real higher-education issues.
