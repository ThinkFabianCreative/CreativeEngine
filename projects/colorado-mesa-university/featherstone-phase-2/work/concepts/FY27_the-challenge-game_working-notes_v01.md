## The idea (Andres)

Year 3. Featherstone University's admissions are failing and it blames CMU, so F.U. challenges CMU to a game. Fiction becomes reality: a real game with an uncontrolled outcome. A documentary-style serial (30 for 30 *genre*, not ESPN's brand) covers the making of it. Whatever the score, everyone joins a big open bonfire: the Code of the West and what makes us Mavs. Phase 1 told the world who we're not (antagonist: privilege); Phase 2 shows who we are: values and place. Content feeds the asset library.

## Satire target (Andres, 2026-11-22, firm rule)

We target **privilege, nepotism and corruption. Never wealth.** Full list of what Featherstone stands for and we're against (Andres): entitled behavior, nepotism, elitism, corruption, shallowness, fakeness, discrimination, and silencing views: CMU values all voices; conservative and liberal values are both treated with dignity and respect ("democracy" in Andres's list). Featherstone silences whoever disagrees with it; it is never coded left or right. Featherstone's sins are buying access, legacy and family-name advantages, and rigging the game. Money itself, wealthy people, donors and success are never the joke.

Emotional core (Andres): many students believe "the game is rigged" and success isn't an option for people like them. We're for the thousands who are scared or overwhelmed and don't think there's a choice; not trying to turn an Ivy-bound student. Must resonate across Colorado and beyond, not only the Western Slope. (To be backed with research before it carries the deck.)

## Strategy decisions (Andres, 2026-11-22)

- Leading feeling: **"rigged."**
- Competition is **both** other colleges and not going to college at all; the opportunity is that one message serves both. Working strategy line (proposed): "Prove, in public, that at CMU the game isn't rigged."

## Go-to-market (Andres, 2026-11-22)

Funnel: awareness → series engagement → event RSVP (first-party lead) → pivot to CMU (quiz, Promise check) → segmented nurture → application → yield. Paid ads, including 30-second TV/streaming spots, are **tune-in ads**: they promote the story ("watch it unfold", episode promos, watch game day live), never a standalone college pitch (Andres: lands more genuine). Paid targeting: **parents** as a primary paid audience, plus **lookalikes** (built from adult seed audiences). Teens reached via age/location, organic, creators and owned follow-up after RSVP.

## Competition attitude (Andres, 2026-11-22, supersedes "rigged")

No rigging, no cheating. Both sides play fair and square; CMU never claims prowess over the Ivies. The difference is **attitude toward competition**: Featherstone NEEDS to win (entitlement, ego, image, fragility). CMU stands for fairness: if it loses, it loses, with grit, composure and grace. "Rigged" is dropped from the plot, the student voice and the strategy line. Insight stays: "I want it. Nobody showed me how."
- **Why Featherstone needs to win:** it lives on appearances and society's validation, so it can't afford to lose. **CMU is authentic and doesn't need that validation.** (Links to Marshall's promise: "free to be authentically you.")
- **A loss to Featherstone does not define CMU.** Values define it, not the scoreboard.
- **Back to Marshall's question:** we know what we don't want. We know who we don't want to be: sore losers, people who need to win. Featherstone is the answer to "What don't you want?", which ties the client's premise and The Challenge into one campaign.
