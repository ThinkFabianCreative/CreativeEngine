## The problem as I see it visually

Phase 1 gave privilege a full wardrobe: pastel, gilt, crest, feathers, the giant check, the bound books. CMU got a building, a mascot and maroon. The director's tension is right and I am not pushing against it, with one amendment: merit should not get its own *prettier* code that competes with F.U.'s. It will lose on production value, and a second polished world is a sequel. The strongest merit codes below are ones F.U.'s world **cannot produce**: wear, marks, blanks, unlocking, things made by hand. Privilege is visible because it is displayed. Knowing is visible because it leaves evidence. That distinction runs through most of these territories, in different ways.

Two families are here on purpose:
- **Merit gets a mark** (1, 2, 3, 4, 5): what "what you know" looks like.
- **F.U. gets out** (6, 7, 8): F.U.'s artifacts escaping into the real world as media and press moments.

## 1. WEAR MARK

**Concept sentence.** At F.U. everything is pristine because nothing is ever used; at CMU knowing shows up as wear: the cracked spine, the chalk-dusted sleeve, the scuffed boot, the burn on the glove.

**What the image argues.** Privilege is spotless. Knowledge leaves marks. You can tell who did the work by looking at their stuff.

**F.U. / CMU.** F.U. is the museum of the untouched: uncracked books still shrink-wrapped, a lab coat with the fold lines from the packet, a varsity sweater that has never been sweated in, a piano with the protective film on the keys. CMU is the same objects, used: the same book with a broken spine and forty sticky tabs, the same coat with a stain from something that went wrong and was fixed. [NEEDS APPROVED CMU PROOF] if the worn objects are tied to named CMU programmes or real students.

**How the line lands.** Mechanism: omission and contrast. The pair of objects says it before any words: the pristine one is "who you know", the worn one is "what you know". The line sits under a diptych and only confirms it.

**Hero film premise.** The headmistress gives a tour of F.U.'s "Collection of Unused Things" with museum reverence (a cello with the tag still on, "donated by the Wexford-Smythes, never played"). The student in flannel sets her own backpack on the display plinth; the camera lingers on its contents in the same museum light: wrecked notebook, taped calculator, worn-through knee. The museum lighting finally has something worth lighting.

- **Social:** "Spot the difference" diptychs: two identical objects, one pristine, one worn. Swipe reveals whose is whose. UGC: people post their most worn tool of learning.
- **OOH:** Macro photography of wear at billboard scale, shot like luxury product (a pencil chewed to a stub lit like a watch ad). F.U. panels beside them: the same object, mint, in a vitrine.
- **Owned/PR:** "The Wear Archive": a physical exhibition of real students' most-used objects, labelled like museum pieces with what the wear taught them. [NEEDS APPROVED CMU PROOF] for real students and stories.

**Borrows / breaks.** Borrows F.U.'s luxury lighting and museum reverence. Breaks the pastel: the worn objects bring in real material color (graphite, rust, denim, oil).

**Biggest risk.** Wear reads as "poor" rather than "skilled". It must read as mastery, never hardship porn. And close-up object photography can drift into a generic "real grit" brand look.

## 2. BLACK BAR

**Concept sentence.** Two applications side by side: F.U. redacts everything except your surname; CMU redacts only your surname.

**What the image argues.** What an institution blacks out tells you what it cares about.

**F.U. / CMU.** F.U. documents (application forms, transcripts, recommendation letters) are solid black bars with one legible line: the family name, gilded, in the crest typeface. CMU documents are fully legible (the essay, the project, the grades, the portfolio) with one black bar where the name was. As a system: black bar = the brand device. It works as a logo lock-up, a transition in film, a sticker, a filter.

**How the line lands.** Mechanism: reversal. The line maps exactly onto the bars: "what you know" is what is visible, "who you know" is what is blacked out. The bar can also physically redact "who" in the line on the end card.

**Hero film premise.** F.U.'s admissions committee reviews files in a candlelit room; every page they turn is black except the surname, and they nod gravely ("Ah. A Whitcombe."). Hard cut to a reading room where the only black is a strip across the top of each file, and a reader actually reads. Final frame: the student's file, her name redacted, her work legible.

- **Social:** A redaction filter: upload your résumé or a class project, the tool bars your name and highlights what you did. F.U.'s account posts its own version: all black, one surname.
- **OOH:** Huge single documents at transit scale, 90% black, one gilded surname. Next panel: a fully readable page with one bar. Almost no copy needed.
- **Owned/PR:** A "name-blind" gesture in public, e.g. a talent showcase where work is exhibited with names redacted. Any claim that CMU admissions is name-blind or that legacy status doesn't count: [NEEDS APPROVED CMU PROOF]. Without proof, the bar stays a metaphor.

**Borrows / breaks.** Borrows F.U.'s crest typeface and gilt for the surname. Breaks everything else: black, white paper, no pastel, no feathers.

**Biggest risk.** Redaction has true-crime, surveillance and legal-document connotations and is a common design trope. Also the most claim-sensitive idea here: it implies an admissions policy.

## 3. SKILL TREE

**Concept sentence.** F.U. shows its family tree, gilded branches of surnames all flowing from one founder; CMU shows a skill tree, the node-and-line map from games, where every branch unlocks only by doing.

**What the image argues.** One tree you are born into; the other you climb. Same diagram form, opposite logic.

**F.U. / CMU.** F.U.: an oil-painted genealogy in the great hall; every leaf a Featherstone, every node pre-filled at birth. F.U.'s "skill tree" parody has one node: BE BORN, already unlocked, glowing gold. CMU: a skill tree where nodes light up as they are earned, the branches spreading unevenly and personally. It gives merit a youth-literate visual language that already means "earned".

**How the line lands.** Mechanism: fusion of two tree diagrams. Family tree = who you know. Skill tree = what you know. The line reads as a caption for the split image.

**Hero film premise.** The headmistress unveils F.U.'s new "Skill Tree" to show F.U. is modernising: a gilt frame, one gold node, "Inherited". Intercut with the student's life as a skill tree drawn in real space: lines of light connect a night shift, a failed exam, a retake, a workshop, each node clicking on as she does the thing. The final wide shot shows her tree beside F.U.'s.

- **Social:** Interactive carousel/reel format where each swipe unlocks the next node. Template: "build your tree"; F.U.'s account posts a one-node tree daily.
- **OOH:** DOOH where a tree grows node by node over the day. The F.U. static poster next to it never changes.
- **Owned/PR:** Microsite skill-tree generator; CMU programmes as branches. [NEEDS APPROVED CMU PROOF] for any programme, pathway or outcome shown in the tree.

**Borrows / breaks.** Borrows F.U.'s portrait-hall gilt for the family tree. Breaks into game UI and light for CMU, which is new territory for the campaign.

**Biggest risk.** Game UI is a borrowed aesthetic and can feel like an edtech app or cheap gamification. It may skew young and lose the policymakers.

## 4. PORTRAIT HALL

**Concept sentence.** F.U.'s gilded oil portraits of ancestors, repainted with the same reverence, but the sitter is replaced by what someone knows: a perfect weld bead, a solved proof, a sutured practice pad, a hand-drawn circuit.

**What the image argues.** Merit deserves the frame too. The institutions that hang ancestors are honoring the wrong subject.

**F.U. / CMU.** F.U. owns the ancestral portrait (Phase 1: "proof of ancestral portrait ownership"). CMU steals the format: heavy gilt frame, Old Master oil treatment, brass plaque, but the subject is a skill, and the plaque names what was learned, not who begat whom. Merit gets the crest-level treatment the director asked for, in F.U.'s own medium.

**How the line lands.** Mechanism: context shift. A weld bead in a Rembrandt frame is funny and dignifying at once. Line as plaque text or end card.

**Hero film premise.** The headmistress leads the tour down the ancestor gallery, each portrait a pompous patriarch. Near the end the portraits change: an oil painting of calloused hands on a lathe, a still life of a whiteboard proof. She is appalled: "Who commissioned these?" The student: "I did." [Or the CMU voice gets a face here: the painter.]

- **Social:** Commission real painters to paint students' skills live; time-lapse reels from blank canvas to finished work (process is itself "what you know").
- **OOH:** Gilded frames as physical billboard builds: the frame is 3D, the painting inside is a skill. F.U. equivalents hang next to them: a smug ancestor.
- **Owned/PR:** A real exhibition, "The Other Portrait Hall", at a real gallery or at the state capitol. Portraits of real students' work. [NEEDS APPROVED CMU PROOF] for students, programmes and any venue.

**Borrows / breaks.** Borrows directly from Phase 1's portrait wall and microsite. Breaks it by changing the sitter; no pastel needed.

**Biggest risk.** It can become a tasteful art project that loses the satire's rudeness and press punch. Oil-painting parodies are a known internet format.

## 5. THE EARNED LETTER

**Concept sentence.** F.U.'s varsity F and U are sold to anyone with the right surname; CMU releases a varsity letter that cannot be bought, only earned by showing what you know.

**What the image argues.** A letter on a sweater used to mean something was earned. F.U. turned it into a purchase; CMU turns it back.

**F.U. / CMU.** F.U. drops real merch: the pastel F and U sweater, with a checkout that asks for "Surname" and "Referring family member" and no price, because price is vulgar. CMU's letter is a physical patch whose only checkout is a real task (a problem, a build, a piece of work), reviewed and awarded. The patch design is the merit crest the director asked for: stitched from something that took skill, e.g. a letter whose chenille is visibly hand-finished, or whose shape is only complete when earned. [NEEDS APPROVED CMU PROOF] if the challenge is tied to real admissions, credit or scholarships.

**How the line lands.** Mechanism: reversal of a known symbol. The two checkout screens are the line in UI form: one asks who you know, the other asks what you know.

**Hero film premise.** A sweater fitting at F.U.: tailors fit the letter onto a student while his father signs a check. Cut to the flannel student at 2 a.m. finishing something difficult; a letter arrives by post, no name on the envelope, just what she did. She puts it on over the flannel.

- **Social:** The drop itself is the content: waitlist, unboxing, people posting what they did to earn it. F.U. "sold out to legacy buyers in 4 seconds."
- **OOH:** Letters at billboard scale: one pastel F, pristine; one earned letter, fraying where it was worked. "Available to: [surname]" vs "Available to: [task]".
- **Owned/PR:** The drop mechanism on the microsite. Press story: "The college sweater you can't buy."

**Borrows / breaks.** Borrows F.U.'s varsity letters, the most recognizable asset Phase 1 built. Breaks by making the letter a real, wearable artifact with a rule attached.

**Biggest risk.** Execution: a skills challenge needs judging, fulfilment and fairness rules. It risks feeling like a promo mechanic. Also merch-as-idea can look like a marketing stunt without enough cultural point.

## 6. VELVET ROPE (risky)

**Concept sentence.** F.U. starts roping off ordinary public things (a bench, a drinking fountain, a library table, a bike rack) with brass stanchions, velvet rope and a plaque reading "Reserved for Legacies"; CMU's mark is the hook unclipped.

**What the image argues.** Privilege is a rope around something that should be open. The simplest gesture of access is unhooking it.

**F.U. / CMU.** F.U. places the ropes and plaques in the real world (with permits; no real institutions named). The rope is F.U.'s cheapest, most photographable icon. CMU never removes the rope; it unclips one end and lets it hang. The dangling brass hook becomes a visual signature that can shrink to a favicon and grow to a building wrap.

**How the line lands.** Mechanism: context shift and literal language ("gatekeeping" made physical). Rope = who you know. Unclipped hook = what you know gets you in. The line goes on a small tag tied to the hanging end.

**Hero film premise.** A day in a real-looking town where F.U. ropes appear overnight around everything useful. The headmistress personally stands guard at a public library table. Throughout the film the flannel student simply unhooks each one and gets on with her work. The last rope is around F.U. itself.

- **Social:** Spotted-in-the-wild posts; F.U. account announcing "new members-only amenities"; people photographing themselves unhooking ropes.
- **OOH:** Physical stanchions built into billboard bases, rope running across the ad face and around a bus shelter bench. CMU panels: the rope lying on the ground.
- **Owned/PR:** The overnight roping stunt is the press photograph. Ropes later auctioned or donated to libraries.

**Borrows / breaks.** Borrows F.U.'s brass, gilt and signage typography. Breaks the pastel campus by leaving the castle for real public space.

**Biggest risk.** Permits and public-space law; roping off public amenities may anger people before they get the joke. Could read as mocking a local town. Legal limits unknown (Q8).

## 7. ESTATE SALE (risky)

**Concept sentence.** After Phase 1 exposed it, F.U. is quietly liquidating: every artifact of privilege gets an auction lot tag, and the sale becomes the campaign.

**What the image argues.** These trappings are worth less than they look. Status is for sale; knowing is not.

**F.U. / CMU.** F.U. under pressure, as the director flagged. Everything from Phase 1 (the Bank of Family Trust check, the portraits, the crest banners, the DON'T WORK, NETWORK books, the champagne tower) now wears a paper lot tag with an absurd reserve price and a provenance note ("Lot 14: One legacy. Lightly inherited."). CMU is the buyer who bids on nothing, or buys one lot and repurposes it: turns the portrait frames into bench tops, the banners into shop rags. Any link to real scholarships or donations: [NEEDS APPROVED CMU PROOF].

**How the line lands.** Mechanism: exaggeration plus context shift. The auction catalogue is the medium. The line is the final lot: "Lot 0: What you know. Not for sale."

**Hero film premise.** An auction house hushed and candlelit; the headmistress is auctioneer, grimly smiling as lots go under the hammer ("Lot 22: A father's phone call. Do I hear a board seat?"). The flannel student sits in the back and never raises a paddle. As the room empties, she's still there, studying.

- **Social:** Daily lot reveals from the F.U. account. Fake bidding in comments.
- **OOH:** Posters as auction catalogue pages: object photograph, lot number, estimate, provenance. Transit DOOH shows "live" bidding.
- **Owned/PR:** A real auction of real Phase 1 props (if the client owns them) at a real auction house, proceeds per approval. Press will photograph a check being auctioned.

**Borrows / breaks.** Borrows every Phase 1 artifact directly, which honors the audience that remembers. Breaks the fiction's confidence: F.U. is shrinking.

**Biggest risk.** It is more sequel than any other territory, and CMU's side is the thinnest again unless the repurposing becomes the real story. Prop ownership and charity law unknown.

## 8. PRE-GRADUATED (strange)

**Concept sentence.** F.U. now issues diplomas at birth: a framed degree hanging over a crib, dated the day the child was born; CMU's diploma stays blank until it is filled in by what you actually learned.

**What the image argues.** For some, the outcome is decided before anything is known. Merit is the only kind of credential that has to be filled in.

**F.U. / CMU.** F.U.: the maternity ward with a crest, newborns in pastel F onesies, diplomas issued with the birth certificate ("Magna cum Legacy"). CMU: a diploma template that is blank, and in the hero visual it slowly fills with handwriting, notes, sketches, formulas: the student's learning literally writing the document.

**How the line lands.** Mechanism: exaggeration taken to the literal. The crib shot is "who you know" at its absurd limit: born knowing the right people. The filling diploma is "what you know". The line sits between the two.

**Hero film premise.** Opens in F.U.'s Legacy Maternity Wing: the headmistress presents diplomas to swaddled newborns while fathers clap. One baby cries; "He'll grow into it." Cut to eighteen years later: the flannel student, and a blank page that fills as we watch her learn.

- **Social:** F.U. birth announcements in its account ("Welcome to the Class of 2048, Tripp Whitcombe V, graduated today."). Parents can generate their own "pre-graduated" parody.
- **OOH:** A single image: a baby in a crib, a gilded diploma on the wall. Almost no copy.
- **Owned/PR:** F.U. "graduation" ceremony for infants, staged and photographed like a real commencement; the press photo is caps on babies.

**Borrows / breaks.** Borrows F.U.'s crest, pastel and ceremonial tone. Breaks the campus location and the age of everyone in it.

**Biggest risk.** Babies in satire can land as mocking parents or real families. The joke is so strong CMU risks disappearing again; the blank diploma has to be as compelling as the crib.
