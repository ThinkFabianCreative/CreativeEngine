## Ground rules applied

- **Fixed line, exact wording:** "We care about what you know, not who you know." (S6). User direction; client approval pending (Q18). It is a different line from Phase 1's aired "We care about who you are, not who you know." Nothing here treats them as the same.
- **No CMU facts.** Cost, aid, access, outcomes, rankings, programmes, admissions practice, student demographics, Phase 1 statistics and press: all out (Q6, C2–C11). Any territory that would show or imply a CMU fact is marked **[NEEDS APPROVED CMU PROOF]**. The most common trap: implying how CMU admits students, or that a skill was learned at CMU.
- **No real institutions or people** named or mocked (Q8). Genres (the hearing, the professional network, the deposition) are satirized; nobody real is.
- **Characters:** the headmistress (name uncertain, Q19), Dr. Augustus Sinclair Featherstone III (owned-only in Phase 1, unused on screen), the flannel student (whether she is an F.U. character is Q24).
- Channel lengths and placements are not specs. "Hero film" means the flagship film, whatever its length turns out to be.

## Where I push on the director's tension

I agree with the read: Phase 1 made "who you know" vivid and left "what you know" abstract. Three cautions before the territories:

1. **Merit made visible drifts earnest fast.** The moment the campaign starts celebrating "real people with real skills", it becomes every other higher-ed ad, the exact script Phase 1's case film mocked. Merit has to stay funny. The best route I found: what people actually know is weirdly, delightfully specific ("I know which cow is faking it"). Specificity is where the jokes are on the merit side.
2. **Don't make F.U. stupid.** The satire targets a system where connections replace knowledge, not rich people's intelligence. F.U. people don't need to know anything, and that is the joke. "Rich kids are dumb" is a cheap shot, and it lets the real argument off the hook. Several territories below carry this risk, and I flag it where it bites.
3. **CMU's best face may not be CMU's facts.** Claims are barred, and even if they weren't, an institution listing its virtues is the voice from nowhere again. CMU can get a face through a person (a student, a counsel, a docent) or through the audience's own knowledge, without asserting anything about itself.

## 1. HER SIDE OF THE TOUR

**POST-IT:** The same F.U. tour, replayed from inside the head of the student who walked out, where everything she knows quietly takes apart everything F.U. pretends.

**Tension / truth:** Pretense only works on people who don't know things. Knowing things is a way of seeing through.

**F.U.:** Unchanged. Oblivious, performing the tour. **CMU:** Her knowledge. Optional retcon: the Phase 1 "voice from nowhere" was her all along. GUIDE: "Who said that?" STUDENT: "Me."

**Line lands:** The guide finally turns to her: "And whom do you know here?" "Nobody." Hard cut to the line.

**Hero film:** Rewind Phase 1 to the start. This time we hear her thinking, with pencil annotations drawn over the frame. The ancestral portrait is a print (she can see the dot pattern). The rowing crew is holding the oars backwards. The $150,000 check isn't signed. GUIDE: "Our library holds forty thousand volumes." HER (VO): "Spines never cracked. They're wallpaper." Each cutdown is one debunk.

- **Social:** "Added context." She annotates F.U.'s own in-character posts in pencil, in the style of community notes. "Added context: this horse is rented."
- **OOH:** F.U. posters, marked up in her pencil in the margins.
- **Owned / PR:** The F.U. microsite gets an "annotated" toggle. Press story: the student from the ad talks back.

**Assumes:** Phase 1 is remembered well enough for a replay to read. The actress is available (Q24). Her knowledge can be shown without saying where she learned it.

**Biggest risk:** A sequel to a sequel, with F.U. filling most of the screen again. Her knowledge must feel ordinary and earned, not like trivia. A know-it-all is unlikeable.

## 2. WEAR

**POST-IT:** Privilege looks brand new, and knowing things leaves marks, so the campaign makes merit visible as wear.

**Tension / truth:** Nothing at F.U. has ever been used: uncracked spines, a lab that has never been lit, a violin never played. Learning leaves evidence: dog-ears, graphite on the side of the hand, a calculator with its most-used keys worn blank, burn marks on a glove, coffee rings on notes.

**F.U.:** The showroom. Everything in mint condition, some of it still in plastic. **CMU:** The evidence. Wear becomes CMU's visual language. This one is DesignChamp-led.

**Line lands:** Over a single macro shot: a page softened by being turned a thousand times.

**Hero film:** Two inventories. An F.U. estate appraiser moves through the university cataloguing: "One library, unread. One science wing, sealed. One Stradivarius, never played." APPRAISER: "It's never been used. That's why it's so valuable." Then one backpack is emptied onto a table. Every object in it is worn from use. Almost no dialogue.

- **Social:** "Show your wear." People post the most worn-out object of their learning. The participation is the argument.
- **OOH:** Billboard-scale macro photographs of wear. Next to them, an F.U. counter-board: a shrink-wrapped textbook, "MINT CONDITION."
- **Owned / PR:** An F.U. estate sale of books sold by the metre, a real interior-decor practice: "Never read. Never opened. Priced accordingly."

**Assumes:** People read wear as effort, not hardship.

**Biggest risk:** It aestheticizes poverty. Worn objects can imply that CMU's people have less, which is condescending to the audience it courts. It is also quieter than the others and may not generate talk on its own.

## 3. THE HEADMISTRESS HAS BEEN LET GO

**POST-IT:** F.U. fired the headmistress for losing a student to CMU on camera, and now, for the first time in her life, she has to know something.

**Tension / truth:** Out in the world, a surname stops working faster than anyone expects. And Phase 1 has consequences: someone at F.U. has to pay for that walkout.

**F.U.:** The institution that drops her. Statement: "Ms. [surname] is no longer with Featherstone. She remains, of course, with her family." **CMU:** The place, or at least the people, who don't care who she's related to. **[NEEDS APPROVED CMU PROOF]** for any depiction of her enrolling at CMU, the courses she takes or the campus.

**Line lands:** Her first day among people who know things. She starts name-dropping. Nobody reacts. Line.

**Hero film:** She hands in her clipboard. A résumé montage: SKILLS: "Knows the Vandermeers." Interview after interview: "What can you do?" "I know people." It ends at a workbench next to the flannel student from Phase 1. STUDENT: "You're holding it upside down." HEADMISTRESS: "I was told there would be staff."

- **Social:** "Learning" episodes. One real thing per episode, learned badly: jump-starting a car, a spreadsheet, eggs. F.U.'s account posts passive-aggressive statements about her.
- **OOH:** Her job-seeking ad: "SEEKING POSITION. Qualifications: excellent connections. References: everyone."
- **Owned / PR:** F.U.'s formal departure press release, followed by her "tell-all" interview.

**Assumes:** The actress is available and audiences liked her. CMU is comfortable with a former snob as its most visible face.

**Biggest risk:** CMU becomes a rehab centre for privileged people, and the protagonist is someone learning humility rather than someone who already knows things. A redemption arc softens the satire.

## 4. GHOSTWRITTEN

**POST-IT:** Every F.U. achievement was quietly done by someone who actually knew how, and the campaign turns the camera on the ghost.

**Tension / truth:** Behind a lot of privileged success stands an uncredited competent person: the essay "consultant", the tutor, the engineer who built the family's namesake building, the assistant who wrote the speech. The public already suspects this about elite admissions (READ, not a verified claim; name no real case).

**F.U.:** The credited: the valedictorian heir, "his" thesis, the donor wing. **CMU:** Where the name on the work is the name of whoever did it. The line lands as a reallocation of credit.

**Line lands:** As the ghost steps out of the wings and the credit moves to her.

**Hero film:** F.U. commencement. The heir gives the valedictory address and reads it cold. "…and as I argued in my thesis on (squints) thermodynamics…" He reads the stage direction aloud: "Pause for applause." In the wings, a young woman silently mouths every word. Closing credits roll over the ceremony: "Speech written by… Thesis by… Bridge engineered by…"

- **Social:** "Credits." F.U. posts each gain an end-credit roll showing who actually did the work.
- **OOH:** An F.U. dedication plaque, and beneath it a smaller plaque: "Actually built by…"
- **Owned / PR:** "Featherstone Essay Concierge", a parody service offering to write your application essay, which redirects to the line. Flag: it parodies a questionable real service category and needs legal review (Q8).

**Assumes:** Anger at purchased credentials is still live in the culture.

**Biggest risk:** The ghost is a victim, which makes it sad rather than funny. It can slide into class condescension ("the help"). Merit ends up exploited rather than triumphant, and the story doesn't strictly need CMU to be told.

## 5. 500+ CONNECTIONS · STRANGE / RISKY

**POST-IT:** F.U. moves onto the one platform where "who you know" is literally the score.

**Tension / truth:** Professional networking platforms count connections and endorsements. The whole grammar of professional life measures who you know, and politicians, press and executives all live there. That is this audience.

**F.U.:** A brand page posting in-character thought leadership: job listings ("VP, Strategy. Requirements: must be the Chairman's godson"), alumni endorsed for "Being Related" and "Yachting". **CMU:** The reply underneath: "3 connections. 41 skills."

**Line lands:** As the pinned post on CMU's page, set against F.U.'s.

**Hero film:** A mockumentary about F.U. Career Services. COUNSELOR: "What are your skills?" HEIR: "Skills?" COUNSELOR: "The section under your name." HEIR: "That's where my name goes again." A networking mixer where the business cards carry only surnames.

- **Social:** F.U. parodies the humble-brag leadership post, the genre, not any real author. "Today I inherited a company. Here's what it taught me about grit."
- **OOH:** Business-district and statehouse-area boards: "Endorsed for: Knowing Someone (99+)."
- **Owned / PR:** An F.U. Career Fair where every booth is somebody's uncle.

**Assumes:** The platform permits satirical organization pages. Most professional networks prohibit fictional personal profiles, so this is **UNVERIFIED**: check current official policy before planning around it.

**Biggest risk:** Platform takedown. Inside-baseball humor that only office workers get. Prospective students are mostly not on the platform (acceptable while students are secondary, Q23).

## 6. PROUDLY REJECTED

**POST-IT:** F.U. opens its application to the public, and being rejected by F.U. becomes a badge of honor.

**Tension / truth:** Almost everyone is spectacularly unqualified for privilege and oddly, specifically qualified for something. Everyone has sat through an application or interview.

**F.U.:** The gatekeeper, with a form made only of who-you-know questions: "List three people who owe your family a favor." "Name of your godfather's boat: ______." "Essay (optional; your consultant may submit on your behalf)." **CMU:** Gives the rejected somewhere to say what they do know. Careful: this must not describe CMU's admissions. It is a celebration, not an application. **[NEEDS APPROVED CMU PROOF]** for any line implying how CMU admits students.

**Line lands:** Under the rejection letter, as CMU's reply to it.

**Hero film:** The F.U. admissions panel interviews a parade of applicants, played by real people or cast to feel real. Asked "Who do you know?", each answers with what they know. APPLICANT: "I know when a calf's faking it." PANEL: "But who do you know?" APPLICANT: "…My grandma?" PANEL: "Is she on a board?" APPLICANT: "The church board." (The panel leans in.)

- **Social:** The application itself as an interactive quiz. Everyone scores 0 out of 10. Then a stitch and duet prompt: "They asked who I know. Here's what I know." The public becomes the protagonist, and merit becomes visible at scale.
- **OOH:** The form at billboard size, with fill-in blanks: "Name of your family's wing: ______."
- **Owned / PR:** A microsite application that ends in a personalized, shareable rejection letter: "We regret to inform you that we have never heard of you." Press story: the public, proudly rejected. (Any count of rejections cited later would need real data.)

**Assumes:** People enjoy being rejected by F.U. and will share it.

**Biggest risk:** Admissions-claim creep toward CMU. Collecting data from minors (the microsite needs privacy review). Quizzes are a familiar format, so it lives or dies on the writing of the questions.

## 7. THE UNCONNECTED COLLECTION

**POST-IT:** F.U. hangs oil portraits of whoever you descend from, so CMU commissions the same grandeur for people and captions them by what they know.

**Tension / truth:** Societies build monuments to lineage and money, almost never to competence. Portraiture is status. Phase 1's microsite asked for "proof of ancestral portrait ownership", so this continues it.

**F.U.:** The Ancestral Gallery. **CMU:** Patron of a new portrait tradition.

**Line lands:** On a gallery placard: "MARISOL T. Can rebuild a transmission, read Latin and calm a room. Related to: nobody you've heard of." Then the line.

**Hero film:** An F.U. docent walks the ancestral gallery: "Our founder, who founded us. His son, who inherited us. His son, who inherited that." She opens a door she has never opened onto an equally magnificent gallery of people, each placard listing what they know. DOCENT (reading, baffled): "'Can suture.' But who are her people?"

- **Social:** Portrait-reveal reels, plus an effect that renders you as a gilded oil portrait with a placard of what you know. The effect needs a platform policy check (UNVERIFIED).
- **OOH:** Gilded frames on billboards and in transit. The placard is the headline.
- **Owned / PR:** A real exhibition that tours, including somewhere policymakers walk past.

**Assumes:** The budget allows real painted or convincingly painted portraits, with consent from every sitter. If the sitters are CMU students, their stories become CMU claims: **[NEEDS APPROVED CMU PROOF]**. Portraits of real people generated with AI raise a separate consent and disclosure question for DesignChamp and legal.

**Biggest risk:** "Celebrating everyday heroes" earnestness. The satire dies the moment the placards turn inspirational. They have to stay specific and dry.

## 8. DO YOU KNOW WHO MY FATHER IS?

**POST-IT:** F.U.'s heirs meet the one place connections don't work: reality.

**Tension / truth:** Gravity, engines, weather and a patient's pulse don't check surnames. "Do you know who my father is?" is already the culture's shorthand for entitlement.

**F.U.:** Its alumni out in the world, stranded and name-dropping at problems. **CMU:** The person who knows how. **[NEEDS APPROVED CMU PROOF]** if the film says or implies that the fixer learned it at CMU.

**Line lands:** After the fixer leaves, with the problem solved and the heir still mid-name-drop.

**Hero film:** Vignettes. A flat tire on a two-lane road with no signal. HEIR: "Do you know who my father is?" STUDENT: "Do you know what a lug wrench is?" An heiress's live board presentation crashes. A heir's canoe drifts toward a weir. Every "Do you know who my father is?" gets the same answer from the universe.

- **Social:** Built for :06: one vignette, one beat. The phrase works as a format: "Do you know who my father is?" / [reality].
- **OOH:** "Gravity has no legacy admissions." "The river doesn't check your last name."
- **Owned / PR:** F.U. launches an alumni emergency concierge. "Dial-a-Daddy."

**Assumes:** A meritocracy of physical reality is believable and funny.

**Biggest risk:** Class coding. If the rich are helpless and the fixers are always in service roles, the ad says "CMU grads will serve F.U. grads", or implies CMU means trade school. The fixer needs agency and should outrank the heir in the scene. It also overlaps with 4 (a competent person rescuing a privileged one) and carries the "rich are dumb" risk.

## 9. FEATHERSTONE v. COLORADO MESA · STRANGE / RISKY

**POST-IT:** F.U. sues CMU for defamation, and the deposition requires its students to state, under oath, what they know.

**Tension / truth:** Privilege answers criticism with lawyers, not reflection. And an oath is the one place where you have to answer the question you were asked.

**F.U.:** The plaintiff, wounded in its dignity, with forty attorneys who are all nephews. **CMU:** A calm defendant with a face at last: a plain-spoken counsel character whose whole defense is asking questions.

**Line lands:** As CMU's one-line closing argument, or as its statement on the courthouse steps.

**Hero film:** A deposition room. COUNSEL: "Please state for the record what you know." Long silence. F.U. LAWYER: "Objection. Relevance." COUNSEL: "Mr. Featherstone, what is fifteen percent of eighty?" (the heir whispers to his lawyer) F.U. LAWYER: "My client invokes his uncle."

- **Social:** A running fictional trial: "Exhibit A" posts (the $150,000 check), courtroom-sketch illustrations, in-character live coverage from both accounts.
- **OOH:** Boards in the style of a legal notice: "NOTICE: Featherstone University has filed suit." Then CMU's reply.
- **Owned / PR:** A parody complaint published on the F.U. site. Never filed anywhere, visibly fictional. Legal must set the limits (Q8).

**Assumes:** A litigious F.U. reads as funny, not as CMU being petty. Legal review allows mock legal documents.

**Biggest risk:** Parody legal documents mistaken for real ones. The "rich are dumb" cheap shot. The film is talky. And CMU as a defendant plays on defense.

## 10. YOU CAN'T INHERIT IT · STRANGE

**POST-IT:** Dr. Augustus Sinclair Featherstone III tries to leave his heirs everything, including what he knows, and discovers the one asset that won't transfer.

**Tension / truth:** You can inherit money, a surname, a house, a seat on the board, a legacy admission. You cannot inherit calculus. What you know is the only asset that is entirely yours. This makes the line's "what you know" concrete by contrast: it is whatever can't be handed down.

**F.U.:** The founder's family, frantically trying to make knowledge heritable: trusts, transplants, a "Knowledge Transfer Endowment". **CMU:** Cares about the one thing on the ledger that belongs to the person holding it. CMU's role is smaller here, which is a weakness.

**Line lands:** As the estate lawyer's last page: the one item that won't pass. Then the line.

**Hero film:** An estate-planning session in the Featherstone library (the president is alive; this is planning, not a death). FEATHERSTONE: "To my son Thaddeus, the house in Newport. The seat on the board. And my Latin." LAWYER: "Sir, the Latin is non-transferable." FEATHERSTONE: "Then buy it from me and give it to him." LAWYER: "It isn't… it doesn't work like that." FEATHERSTONE: "What doesn't?"

- **Social:** F.U. launches heritable-knowledge "products": a leather-bound "Inherited Wisdom" volume that is blank inside, and an "Heirloom Degree" certificate made out to "Bearer".
- **OOH:** A two-column board: "THINGS YOU CAN INHERIT: a boat, a surname, a seat. THINGS YOU CAN'T: [a single worn equation]." It pairs naturally with 2.
- **Owned / PR:** F.U. announces the "Featherstone Knowledge Transfer Endowment", seeking scientists who can make knowledge heritable. The press release does the joke.

**Assumes:** The president character is ready for his first on-screen appearance (he was unused in Phase 1). Audiences get the abstraction quickly.

**Biggest risk:** It's clever rather than warm. Merit is defined by absence, so the director's "make merit visible" is only half met unless it is paired with a people-led layer (6 or 7). The territory gives CMU the least to do.

## Considered and folded (not shown as territories)

- **F.U. Is Sorry** (crisis PR, rebrand as "Featherstone Unites", a merit scholarship awarded to the chairman's nephew). Strong for social, but it only reacts. Folded into 9 as an opening beat: F.U. apologizes, badly, before it sues.
- **The Offer** (F.U. tries to buy CMU, or mails back the $150,000 check). A good PR stunt, but a plot rather than a truth. Available as a stunt inside 9 or 10.
- **The Exchange Heir** (a Featherstone IV is sent to CMU for a semester). Too close to 3; the headmistress has Phase 1 equity.
- **Statement from the Office of the President** (Featherstone III newsjacks the higher-ed news cycle; CMU replies). This is an always-on content engine that any territory can use, not a territory itself. It carries the highest risk of touching real institutions or people (Q8).
- **First-generation legacy.** Depends on CMU demographic facts. Barred (Q6).
- **The Hearing** (F.U. testifies before a fictional committee). This is the policymaker stage. It can sit on top of 9 or 3 as a PR moment and is too thin alone.

## Combinations worth noting (not recommendations)

- **10 + 2**: can't inherit it / it leaves marks. A truth, paired with the picture that proves it.
- **6 + 7**: the public's "what I know" answers from the quiz become the placards in the gallery.
- **1** could open any of them as a short bridge from Phase 1.

## What WordSmither would back

1. **PROUDLY REJECTED (6).** It answers the director's tension at scale: the public, not CMU, supplies the merit, in their own specific and funny words. It keeps F.U.'s world at full strength and gives the press a story ("people are proudly failing F.U.'s application"). It also dodges the claims ban naturally, because CMU asserts nothing about itself. The work is writing questions sharp enough to carry it.
2. **YOU CAN'T INHERIT IT (10).** It holds the sharpest truth in the set, and it defines "what you know" precisely: whatever can't be handed down. That makes the line feel inevitable rather than asserted. It brings the unused president on screen and fits the F.U. world completely. Its weakness, that merit is shown by absence, is the one fix I'd attempt in DEVELOP, most likely by pairing it with 2 or 6.

Dark horse: **HER SIDE OF THE TOUR (1)**, if the student can be made the lead without becoming a know-it-all.
