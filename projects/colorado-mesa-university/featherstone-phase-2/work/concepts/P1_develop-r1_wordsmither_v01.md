## The Headmistress: where I agree with the director's rule, and three amendments

The rule is right: her schemes fail **because of what someone knows**. It turns every gag into a demonstration of the line, and it also fixes the weakness the director named in A. Merit was only visible by its absence. Now every failed scheme needs someone on screen who knows something specific. The antagonist brings the protagonist with her.

Amendments:

1. **She is excellent at her own game.** Favors, money, surnames and intimidation work on everyone in F.U.'s world, instantly and every time. They fail only against people who know things. If she is a buffoon, the satire loses its teeth and the argument becomes "rich people are silly". The argument is that connections have no purchase on knowledge. (This is the same caution I raised in EXPLORE: don't make F.U. stupid.)
2. **The knowers' signature response is generosity.** When she tries to buy, steal or block what someone knows, they offer to teach her. Knowledge is the one asset you can give away and still keep, and that is incomprehensible to her. It keeps CMU's side calm and warm without anyone making a speech, and it gives her a reaction to play: horror at "free".
3. **She spies on places and objects, not on young people.** Whiteboards, bulletin boards, an empty front row, the back of a lecture hall. A grown woman watching teenagers through binoculars reads as creepy, not funny, and the new student audience makes that worse. Her disguises are obvious (pearls with the camouflage, a topiary that is clearly her), so the comic distance never collapses. In D, where watching CMU people is the premise, she watches them at work (the project, not the person), the people are clearly adults, and every observation is about what they are doing.

**Phase 1 voice to keep.** Clipped, condescending, self-correcting. "No, honey." "Keep your comments to the end." "Efficient. I meant efficient." "Please don't touch it." And the last thing she said in Phase 1: "Who said that?"

## The bridge from Phase 1 (applies to all four)

Phase 1 ends with an unseen voice and the Headmistress asking **"Who said that?"** Phase 2 starts from that line. She has been trying to find out ever since, and F.U. has taken it personally. Chapter two is her investigation.

- **Continuity cues:** same actress, teal plaid suit, clipboard, the F.U. crest and pastel world, the flannel student if Q24 allows.
- **Chapter device (F.U. voice):** a crest title card reading **FEATHERSTONE UNIVERSITY · PART THE SECOND**. F.U. would never say "Part 2".
- **Callbacks, one per film at most:** "Who said that?" becomes "Who told her?" / "Who unclipped this?"; "Keep your comments to the end" becomes "Keep your qualifications to the end."
- **The difference from Phase 1:** in Phase 1 CMU was a voice from nowhere. In Phase 2 she has found where the voice comes from, and it is people who know things.
- **Flag:** Phase 1 wording comes from machine transcripts (D2). Confirm against the approved scripts before any callback is locked.

# A. NON-TRANSFERABLE

## 1. Post-it and campaign rule

**POST-IT:** F.U. can hand its heirs everything except what they know, so it sends the Headmistress to acquire it, and it will not transfer.

**CAMPAIGN RULE:** Every execution shows F.U. trying to buy, copy, inherit or install what someone knows, and failing because knowing only lives in the person who did the learning.

## 2. The Headmistress in A

- **Mission: acquisition.** Dr. Augustus Sinclair Featherstone III (on screen for the first time) learns that "what you know" is apparently valuable. At F.U., anything valuable is acquired. She is sent to get some for his heir, Thaddeus.
- **Running gag: she always acquires the container and never the contents.** The notebook, the photographed whiteboard, the chewed pencil, a worn calculator, a hired tutor. They pile up in Thaddeus's study like trophies in vitrines, labelled like museum pieces. This is where WEAR lives: she buys the most worn objects because they look the most valuable, and the wear is the one part that doesn't come with them.
- **Why she fails:** the knowers hand things over freely because it costs them nothing. What they know stays with them, and Thaddeus gets paper. Her tools (money, a word with someone's father, a stare) work on everything except the one thing she was sent for.
- **Best lines:**
  - "Name your price, dear. Everyone has one. Or a godfather."
  - "I'd like to purchase whatever it is you know. Wrapped, please."
  - "Install it by Tuesday. He has a regatta."
  - "It depends on *him*?"
  - To Featherstone III: "Sir, I have the notebook, the whiteboard and the girl's calculator. He still can't do it." / "Did you buy the girl?" / "She offered to teach him. For free." *(A long, appalled silence.)*

## 6. Audiences

- **Prospective student:** they recognize themselves in the student who says "Sure. Keep it." What you know is the thing nobody can take off you, including someone with more money. They also recognize Thaddeus's panic from the other side: the moment it depends on you. The risk is that it's more watched than lived. They see a knower, but they aren't inside the premise the way they are in B.
- **Press:** a founder on screen for the first time, a trust announcement that writes its own story, and a logic that journalists can repeat in one sentence ("you can't inherit what you know").
- **Policymakers:** inheritance, legacy and transfer of advantage, stated as a law of nature rather than a policy position. It gives them a quotable idea without asking them to take a side on any real institution.

## 7. Proof flags, risks, cuts

- **Proof flags:** [C12] campus exterior, classroom window, study table. CMU TRUTH A1–A3 (beat 2) and A4 (the whiteboard subject in social 1). The student must not be identified as a CMU student by name or programme without approval and consent.

  3. **Spying and buying from young people.** Keep her transactions brisk and absurd, never lingering, and her surveillance aimed at objects.
- **What I'd cut:** the diplomas-at-birth idea from DC8. Babies plus a spying antagonist overloads the world. It's a strong :06 on its own later, not part of this round.

# B. PROUDLY REJECTED

## 1. Post-it and campaign rule

**POST-IT:** F.U. opens its application to the public, the Headmistress rejects everyone, and the rejection becomes the proudest thing on your record.

**CAMPAIGN RULE:** Every execution puts someone in front of F.U.'s gate, has them answer "Who do you know?" with what they know, and turns the rejection into a badge.

## 2. The Headmistress in B

- **Mission:** after Phase 1, F.U.'s board decides it must "appear accessible" and opens applications to the public for the first time. The Headmistress runs admissions. Her private orders: let nobody in, and make sure the rejected never find their way to "that voice".
- **Running gag: every applicant she rejects has just fixed something in the room.** The stopped clock, the panel's projector, a translation, the crest. She stamps REJECTED on people who have just made her afternoon better, and she stamps harder each time.
- **Why she fails:** a rejection only works on someone who needed your approval. What these people know doesn't need hers, so they leave prouder than they arrived. Her second scheme, stopping them from finding CMU (detour signs, a "closed" road, a map with F.U. at the centre of everything), fails because they know how to get places: the trucker reads the road, the ranch kid reads the sun, the kid who takes the bus knows the routes by heart.
- **Best lines:**
  - "Keep your qualifications to the end."
  - "Fixing it is not an answer to the question."
  - "We regret to inform you that we have never heard of you."
  - "Please stop being useful. It's unsettling the board."
  - Watching them all leave in the same direction: "Where are they going?" / ASSISTANT: "They seem to know." / "Who told them?"

## 6. Audiences

- **Prospective student:** B is the most direct recognition of the three. They are in the application season. "Who do you know?" is their private fear ("I don't know anyone"), and being asked what they know is the relief. The stitch prompt lets them put themselves in the work. Being rejected by F.U. is a flex they can post.
- **Press:** the public, not CMU, supplies the story, at scale, with a funny artifact (the letter) that people share themselves.
- **Policymakers:** a satire of legacy and connection-based gatekeeping told through the form itself. The "who do you know" questions are the argument, and no real institution is named.

## 7. Proof flags, risks, cuts

- **Proof flags:** [C12] college fair. CMU TRUTH B1–B2 (beat 6b). **[NEEDS APPROVED CMU PROOF]** for anything implying how CMU admits students. That includes the beat-6 destination if it reads as "CMU will take them", any "apply to CMU" CTA placed next to the rejection, and any name-blind visual (DC2 Black Bar) used literally.
- **Top 3 risks:**
  1. **Admissions-claim creep.** The whole direction sits next to admissions. CMU's replies must stay at "Congratulations on your rejection" and never become "We'd take you."
