## What the round established

**Independent convergence (strongest signal).** WordSmither and DesignChamp each arrived at WEAR (WS2, DC1) without seeing each other's work: privilege looks brand new, and knowing leaves marks. Both also arrived at the ancestral portrait (WS7, DC4). Wear is the first real answer to the gap in Phase 1: it gives "what you know" a visual language that F.U.'s world cannot produce. Its limit is that it's a code, not a premise. On its own it won't make the press talk, so it belongs **inside** a direction as CMU's visual evidence, not as the direction itself.

**The test that sorted the set.** Does the direction make "what you know" as specific and visible as Phase 1 made "who you know", while F.U. stays at full comic strength and CMU never has to make a claim about itself? Most territories do only one half. Some give F.U. new jokes and leave CMU thin (WS1, WS3, DC7, DC8 alone). Some make merit visible but earnest (WS7, DC4).

## Carry forward to DEVELOP (three directions)

### A. NON-TRANSFERABLE
*(WS10 You Can't Inherit It + DC8 Pre-Graduated + WEAR as its proof image)*

```
TENSION:              You can inherit a surname, a seat, a house and an admission, but not one thing you know.
CREATIVE PERSPECTIVE: Absurdist satire with a hard-logic spine
POST-IT:              F.U. can hand its heirs everything except the only thing that counts.
```

- **Why it leads.** It turns the line from a slogan into a law of nature: "what you know" is whatever can't be handed down, so the line lands with an "oh, of course". It keeps F.U. at full strength. The Featherstone family's frantic attempts to make knowledge heritable (estate planning, a Knowledge Transfer Endowment, diplomas issued at birth) are a comic engine that produces many different executions. It also brings the unused president on screen for the first time.
- **The fix it needs.** Merit is shown only by its absence. DEVELOP must give the other side a picture and a person. WEAR supplies the picture: the one thing on the ledger that is visibly used, worn and owned. The student, or real people, supply the person.
- **Strongest channel moves:** the estate-planning film ("Sir, the Latin is non-transferable."); a two-column OOH board of what you can and can't inherit; F.U.'s Knowledge Transfer Endowment press release as the PR moment; pre-graduated birth announcements on F.U.'s social.
- **Risk:** clever rather than warm. Babies in the satire need care.
- **Predicted taste fit (uncertain):** high. A compressed proposition, humor that makes the argument rather than decorating it, and a premise that generates executions.

### B. PROUDLY REJECTED
*(WS6 + DC2 Black Bar as its document device)*

```
TENSION:              Almost everyone is hopelessly unqualified for privilege and oddly, specifically qualified for something.
CREATIVE PERSPECTIVE: Participation as the argument
POST-IT:              F.U. opens its application to the public, and failing it becomes a badge of honor.
```

- **Why it matters.** It is the only direction where **the public supplies the merit**, in their own specific and funny words, at scale. That gives the broad audience a role, the press a story, and CMU no claim to make. The application form (made only of who-you-know questions) and the rejection letter are strong artifacts. Black Bar can be the form's visual code: F.U.'s documents are black except the surname.
- **The fix it needs.** The quiz format is familiar, so it lives or dies on the questions. It must never describe CMU's admissions (Black Bar especially implies a name-blind policy: [NEEDS APPROVED CMU PROOF] or keep it as metaphor). A microsite that collects data from minors needs a privacy review.
- **Predicted taste fit:** high on participation, which carries the idea. Some risk that it reads as a social mechanic unless the film and OOH stand alone.

### C. RESERVED FOR LEGACIES
*(DC6 Velvet Rope)*

```
TENSION:              Privilege is a rope around something that should be open.
CREATIVE PERSPECTIVE: Brand act, gatekeeping made physical
POST-IT:              F.U. ropes off ordinary public things "for legacies", and CMU simply unclips the hook.
```

- **Why it matters.** It has the best press photograph in the set. It's the most relevant direction for policymakers (access to public goods), and it takes F.U. off campus into the real world, which Phase 1 never did. The dangling brass hook is a simple, ownable mark that scales from favicon to building wrap. CMU's gesture, unclipping rather than tearing down, is a character: calm, not angry.
- **The fix it needs.** The unclip shows access, not knowledge. DEVELOP must connect the rope to "what you know", not only to "open to all", or it drifts toward an access line the brief doesn't hold. Permits, public-space law and local sensitivity (Q8).
- **Predicted taste fit:** high. It is a conceptual visual where the action contains the argument.

## Director's recommendation

Develop **A** as the lead platform candidate, with **B** and **C** developed in parallel as genuine alternatives, not supporting tactics. A and B combine naturally: the public's rejected applications become the evidence of what can't be inherited. Before DEVELOP, the one strategic check worth putting back to BriefMachine is Q23 (prospective students as a secondary audience). A and B change shape depending on whether a 17-year-old has to recognize themselves in the work.
