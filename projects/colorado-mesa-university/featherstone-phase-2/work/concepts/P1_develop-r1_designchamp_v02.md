## 1. The CMU world: designing who CMU is

Phase 1 gave F.U. a castle, a horse, a rowing shell carried like a trophy, a champagne tower, a portrait hall, a wardrobe and a color palette. CMU got a brick exterior, a mascot hug and an end card. Phase 2 gives CMU **the same density of decided specifics**. The wear code (v01 §0.1) supplies the surfaces. This section supplies the places, people, light and rituals.

### 1.1 The richness rule

**Every CMU frame carries as many decided specifics as an F.U. frame.** If an F.U. shot has a crest, a chandelier, a marble floor and three wardrobe details, the CMU shot opposite it needs an equal count: a real place, a real tool worn at the point of use, a specific task in progress, a wardrobe detail that comes from the work (safety glasses pushed up, a pencil behind the ear, chalk on a cuff). "A student walking on a campus" is never a CMU shot. It's the Phase 1 gap again.

### 1.2 Place and light: the landscape F.U. can't have

F.U.'s world is green lawns, clipped hedges, soft overcast light and tungsten halls: old East Coast money. CMU's name holds its strongest visual asset: **the mesa**. The site now states the setting: main campus in Grand Junction, "nestled between mountains and high-desert canyons", with "300+ days of sunshine a year" [SOURCE-STATED: about_index]. It sits at the convergence of the Colorado and Gunnison rivers, in the "Heart of the West" [SOURCE-STATED: admissions_why-cmu]. Colorado National Monument is "within minutes from campus" and the Grand Mesa is nearby [SOURCE-STATED: about_local]. What remains open is [CMU TRUTH: CT1 which of these landforms are actually visible from campus locations, and the best times of day to shoot them: a location recce].

The table assumes the source-stated setting:

| | F.U. | CMU |
|---|---|---|
| Light | Soft, diffused, shadowless; flatters and hides texture | **Hard, clear, dry sun with long raking shadows and big sky.** Raking light is also what reveals wear, so the landscape and the wear code become one look |
| Ground | Lawn, gravel drive, marble | Real campus surfaces [CT2] and native ground: rock, dust at the edges, desert planting [CT13] |
| Horizon | Enclosed: walls, hedges, towers | Open: long lenses compress people against distant landforms |
| Color | Pastel, cream, gilt | Warm stone, sky blue, brick [CT10], CMU maroon and yellow on the things in use (v01 §0.2) |

That contrast is the key to the look: **F.U. is lit so nothing shows. CMU is lit so everything shows.** "300+ days of sunshine" is CMU's own number, and it makes hard sun a brand fact as well as a lighting choice. It must not appear as a claim in copy without approval.

**Scale.** CMU describes itself as aspiring to be a "Human Scale University" [SOURCE-STATED: admissions_why-cmu]. That becomes a composition rule. **F.U. is framed to dwarf people**: wide lenses, towering halls, figures small against architecture. **CMU is framed at human scale**: eye level, people filling the frame, architecture behind them and never above them. The landscape is the one thing allowed to be bigger than the people, and it sits behind them as a horizon, not overhead as a monument.

### 1.3 People

- **Casting rule:** people are shown mid-competence, never posed. No eye contact and smile at camera (stock tell, visual-craft §9). The camera finds them already doing the thing.
- **Wardrobe from the work:** what they wear is set by the task: gloves, glasses pushed up, rolled sleeves, a lanyard, a field vest, and CMU colors where they would naturally appear [CT11 for any official CMU apparel].
- **An ensemble, not a mascot.** F.U. had a cast of types (the rower, the rider, the tea-drinker). CMU needs its own cast with the same specificity: the one who fixes, the one who calculates, the one who performs, the one who works outdoors, the one who teaches [CT7 real students, CT8 real faculty and staff]. Types are defined by what they know, never by background, and the jobs shown do not signal class (WordSmither's caution on fixers in service roles applies).
- **The Phase 1 student in maroon flannel and yellow straps** continues as the ensemble's anchor if she is available [CT17; Q24].

### 1.4 Rituals

F.U.'s rituals were display: the champagne tower, the horse, the shell carried over the heads. CMU's rituals are **doing, and the moment something works**: the first successful test, the performance, the late-night review round a table, fieldwork, the fix that holds. Real CMU traditions are the most valuable material here, because they are ownable and no other school has them [CT5 CMU's real traditions, annual events and rituals: names, dates, whether they can be filmed]. Until CT5 comes back, rituals are shown as unnamed types, which is C12-safe.

**What the website names** (every item needs client approval before it is depicted or named):
- **The annual Student Showcase**, the 17th in 2026: "presentations, posters, performances, demonstrations and exhibits", open to the public, every project with a faculty mentor, check-in at the University Center and a celebration in the Meyer Ballroom [SOURCE-STATED: showcase_index]. **It is the single best CMU truth for this campaign:** a real public day on which what students know is literally on display. It is the ideal target for her sabotage (§5).
- **Stampede Week**, the welcome week: BBQ at The Plaza, movie night and paint night on Elm Quad, a glow party at Grand Mesa Field [SOURCE-STATED: welcome-week_index].
- **Homecoming**, whose 2026 theme is "Make Your Mark", nodding to "1980's hard rock" [SOURCE-STATED: homecoming_index]. This is a notable coincidence: the client's own language already says what the wear code says (knowing leaves marks), and it already reaches for the 80s. That's a reason to believe the wear code and D are ownable by CMU. It is **not** an asset to co-opt. Homecoming has a named bank sponsor, and the 2026 edition falls before this campaign runs.
- **The Outdoor Program's free weekly outings**: Monday Yoga, Tuesday Town floats, Wednesday Fly Tying, Thursday Hikes, Friday Rocks [SOURCE-STATED: outdoor-program_index]. These are ready-made CMU rituals of doing. Fly tying and climbing are knot-knowledge, which feeds C's "The Knot" and D.
- **The Mavericks** identity: athletics, the Maverick Center, the Hotel Maverick "in the heart of CMU's main campus" [SOURCE-STATED: about_index, homecoming_index]. The phrase "Maverick at heart" does not appear in the captured pages, so it is not used here.
- **CMU Tech**, at the Tilman M. Bishop Campus: career and technical programmes, and a steel-industry workforce partnership in the news [SOURCE-STATED: about_index, tech_index, now_…rk-steel]. **This is the wear code's natural home.** The captured pages do not name welding or specific trades, so any specific trade shown stays [CMU TRUTH: CT2].

### 1.5 The CMU grade and camera

This builds on v01 §A6. The camera is steady handheld at 35–85 mm, observing across space, and goes to macro at the point of contact. The grade keeps true color and hard highlights with no pastel lift and no grunge desaturation. In the edit, CMU scenes run slightly longer than F.U. scenes. F.U. is cut like a brochure. CMU is given time, because what people are doing takes time.

## 5. D. ENROLLMENT IS DOWN (working title)

```
PREMISE:     F.U.'s enrollment is falling, and F.U. is convinced CMU is to blame, so the Headmistress goes undercover on CMU's campus
             in camouflage to find out what CMU has. Everything she photographs as a threat is exactly who CMU is.
GENRE:       The 80s campus comedy (Caddyshack, Animal House, Revenge of the Nerds, Dirty Dancing): snobs vs everyone else.
CLAIM:       "CMU enrollment is up" is a factual claim even inside fiction [NEEDS APPROVED CMU PROOF; CT12].
             Claim-safe framing until proof arrives: F.U.'s decline is F.U.'s own fictional fact; that CMU is the cause is only
             F.U.'s BELIEF, dramatized by her panic. No CMU figures, arrows or charts.
```

### Genre read: what to borrow and what to leave

Borrow the **grammar and the structure**: the underdog ensemble; the establishment trying to spy on it and stop it; the montage; the era's film look; the painted ensemble one-sheet; the freeze-frame ending with typed captions.

**CMU is not the slobs.** In Caddyshack and Animal House the other side of the snobs is chaos. Here it has to be **Revenge of the Nerds and Dirty Dancing**: the underestimated people who turn out to be brilliant at something, and whose skill is what beats the establishment. The energy of a party, with competence at the center.

Leave the era's politics and its vices. No alcohol, hazing, voyeurism, "panty raid" tropes, or ethnic or gender caricature. Prospective students are an audience, so these are hard exclusions, not preferences.

### D1. Key visual

**Concept sentence.** The Headmistress lies in a pastel plaid ghillie suit, pearls over her face paint, photographing CMU through a lens the length of her arm. Every photo she pins to F.U.'s evidence board, circled in pink grease pencil and labelled as a threat, is a portrait of someone doing what they know.

**What carries the idea.** Visual-verbal. Her annotations are F.U.'s voice and they get the reading exactly wrong. The photos are CMU's truth. The gap between them is the joke and the argument.

**Key visual layout: "The Evidence Board" (horizontal, 3:2 master).** It is F.U.'s situation room, a panelled office at night lit by a banker's lamp. A gilt easel holds a cream corkboard in a picture frame. Pinned to it are 12–15 of her surveillance photos in hard daylight with the full CMU richness of §1: people at a bench [CT2], a late study table [CT3], a performance, fieldwork against the landscape [CT1], and one ritual moment [CT5]. They are joined not with red string but with **strands of pearls**. Every photo has a pink grease-pencil circle and a copperplate note: [COPY: e.g. "SUBJECT KNOWS HOW TO WELD. ALARMING."; "TEACHING EACH OTHER. FOR FREE?!"]. On a side table sits a chart of F.U.'s own enrollment, a single line going down, with no axis values and nothing about CMU. The Headmistress stands at the right edge in teal plaid with the ghillie hood pushed back and camo paint still on under the pearls, pointing with a riding crop.

**Reading order.**
1. The wall of bright, rich photographs (stop: this is the most color and life in the frame).
2. Her pink circles and notes (premise: she thinks this is a threat).
3. Her, pointing (character: F.U. has a problem).
4. The line, bottom left on the dark panelling in CMU type (resolution: what she's circling is what we care about).
5. The lockup.

Her own photos are the campaign's CMU imagery. The antagonist is the vehicle that shows CMU.

| | F.U. | CMU |
|---|---|---|
| Role | The spy: stiff, luxurious, ridiculous | The subject: an ensemble, busy, unaware, generous |
| Camouflage | Absurdly F.U. (below) | None. CMU never hides anything |

| Palette | Pastel, gilt, pink grease pencil | §1.2 plus v01 §0.2 |
| Type | Copperplate annotations; engraved caps on dossier covers; a 1980s-style display face for the title lettering (the one-sheet's fat, slanted, dimensional lettering) | Line and lockup in CMU identity type [CT11] |

**The camouflage wardrobe** is always brand new with the tags on, because the disguise fails for lack of wear:
- **The pastel plaid ghillie suit:** thousands of strips in her teal-and-pink plaid, with a pearl necklace worn over the face paint.
- **The crested topiary:** a perfectly clipped boxwood ball with a gilt F.U. crest on it, opera glasses poking through. It's the wrong plant for the landscape [CT13].
- **The "student":** a hoodie with the price tag swinging, a backpack with the cardboard stuffer still inside, sneakers blinding white, and a "HELLO MY NAME IS: Student" sticker in copperplate.
- **The maintenance worker:** a hi-vis vest with packet creases, a hard hat with its sticker, and a spotless tool belt holding a silver letter opener.
- **The sprinkler head** (for social): a brass pop-up with a pearl on top.

**Signature device: the surveillance circle.** A hand-drawn pink grease-pencil ring with a copperplate note beside it, the mark of F.U. misreading CMU. It sits over real CMU imagery everywhere: stills, DOOH, social and film, where it draws on in post. It scales down to a pink circle icon. Unlike the hook, **it always points at CMU**. Generative rule: **wherever CMU is doing something it knows, she circles it and calls it a threat.**

### D6. Film look

- **Overall:** a 1980s campus-comedy grammar. 1.85:1, a 35mm film look (warm stock, soft halation on highlights, visible grain), zoom lenses used the period way: slow zoom-ins, **snap zooms onto her reactions**, a Steadicam walk through campus, and a needle-drop montage (music licensing flagged). Avoid the lazy retro signals: no VHS glitch, no scan lines, no synthwave neon. This is a film-era look, not a tape-era look.
- **F.U. (situation room, the board, the chancellor on the phone):** stiff, symmetrical, lamp-lit, like an 80s boardroom scene where the villains plot.
- **CMU:** the heroes' grammar of the era, energetic and in the sunlight, with the §1.2 hard light, crowds, overlapping action and people doing things in the background of every shot.
- **She on CMU ground:** stakeout grammar. Her POV through branches and ghillie strips on a long lens, a telephoto grain boost, her pink key on the leaves around her, the shutter sound of a motor-drive camera, and circles drawing onto the frame.
- **Ending:** **the freeze-frame with typed captions** (the Animal House ending). The action freezes on each ensemble member and a caption names what they know [COPY]. The last freeze is the Headmistress in the topiary: [COPY: her fate]. Captions must never state real outcomes such as jobs, salaries or graduation [NEEDS APPROVED CMU PROOF].

### D7. What a 17-year-old sees

They don't need to know Caddyshack. The painted poster, the 35mm look and the freeze frames are already current through the shows and filters they watch. What they recognize: the spy-on-your-rival bit and "caught in 4K" surveillance humor; a Where's-Waldo hunt; the underdog ensemble. Above all, they see the answer to the question every campus ad dodges: **what does it actually look like there, on a normal day, and who would I be next to?** They get to see it through the eyes of someone horrified by it.

### D8. Proof flags and risks

Flags: the enrollment premise and any number, arrow or chart about CMU [NEEDS APPROVED CMU PROOF; CT12]; every campus place, ritual and person [CT1–CT10, C12]; filming real students under "surveillance" framing (releases, and a clear fiction frame); music licensing; illustrator commission; the Stakeout site; talent continuity for the Headmistress.

## 7. Comparative note (updated)

**Should D lead? D should be the narrative frame, not a competing premise.** D is the best answer so far to the user's governing principle. It is the only direction whose engine produces CMU imagery by necessity: she can't spy without showing us what she sees. It gives the antagonist a motive (enrollment panic), a wardrobe (the camouflage) and a genre with a rich poster and film language. But D is a plot and a look. It needs a law that makes her circles mean "what you know" and not just "campus is fun". A supplies that law.

**Recommended structure: D carrying A.** F.U.'s enrollment is falling. The Headmistress goes undercover to find out what CMU has and to take it back for F.U.'s heirs, and she discovers it's the one thing that can't be collected, bought or inherited. "The Collector" (v01 A3) is already a D scene. The estate tag and her surveillance circle work as a pair: she circles it, tries to tag it, and it's NON-TRANSFERABLE. B folds in as a social and PR layer (her rejection letters). C's rope becomes one of her failed schemes, and the "Unclipped" reel survives as a social format.

**Does the hook still lead the visual system? No.** v01 chose the hook for its image and its scale. Under the new principle, the lead device must point at CMU, and the hook only points at a gap. **The surveillance circle leads**, because every time it appears it frames who CMU is. The estate tag is the secondary device, the argument. The hook keeps its click and its press photo as one scheme inside the story.

**Visually strongest single image:** C's roped piano is still the cleanest press frame. **Strongest campaign:** D + A. It has the richest CMU world, the best OOH (the one-sheet), the best social mechanic (Where's the Headmistress?), and the line proved by what she circles. Everything in v01 §0 still holds across it: the wear code, the color and end-card split, and her own pink light.

**What the website pass changes.** It strengthens D + A. The Student Showcase gives D a real public stage where what students know is on display: the densest CMU frames available, and a sabotage target that turns every failure into evidence. CMU's own words ("Human Scale", and Homecoming's "Make Your Mark" with its 1980s nod) show that the wear code and the 80s shell sit close to how CMU already talks about itself. The one correction: **no donor money as F.U.'s gag** (§2), because of the recent largest-ever gift.
