### 1.1 Lighting: DECISION (Q29)

**CMU is lit at golden hour, with raking light, and interiors take soft, diffused, warm light. I withdraw "hard raking daylight". Midday light is not CMU's key.**

Why this is right, beyond compliance:

1. **It is what S12 asks for.** "The golden glow of a sunset illuminating a study session or the soft, diffused light in a campus gathering" (S12 p.71). Exteriors take the first half of that sentence and interiors the second.
2. **It keeps what hard light was doing for us.** A sun 5–15° above the horizon rakes harder than noon. It pulls texture out of every surface, throws long shadows and backlights the dust. The wear code, the dust on the boots and the energy of a crowd all read better at golden hour than at midday.
3. **It puts CMU's secondary palette in the camera.** S12 says its gradients "represent the colors of western Colorado throughout the day and night" (p.40), and the Mavroon-to-Monument Sunset gradient *is* a high-desert sunset. At golden hour the landscape paints Terracotta, Sandrock and Monument Sunset onto rock and brick, and Colorado Sky holds overhead. The brand colors arrive as light, so nobody has to lay them on the photograph.
4. **It makes the F.U. contrast structural.** F.U. is lit by lamps, behind curtains, at night. CMU is lit by the sun going down while people are still working. *F.U. has never watched a sunset. CMU works through it.* That's a story about effort told in light, and it is warm, which S12 asks for and my round 1 look wasn't.
5. **The Headmistress's pink key reads better against it.** Against warm raking sun, a flat, shadowless pink front light looks obviously wrong, and it is the only thing in the frame not lit by the sun.

**What stays outside the key.** Clear high-desert daylight appears in S12's own location photography (p.68, blue-sky exteriors). I allow it in two places only: wide establishing landscape, and her **midday stakeout suffering** (heat shimmer on the long lens, and she is baking in a ghillie suit). CMU people are never keyed at noon. Flat overcast is F.U.'s light and never CMU's.

**Production cost, stated.** Golden hour gives roughly 60–90 usable minutes at each end of the day. Exteriors are scheduled into two windows a day, and interiors (the Showcase, classrooms) fill the middle of the day under soft, diffused light. The Showcase blackout (§4) is the one interior scheduled *for* sunset, and it needs west-facing windows [FR-T02]. Which landform catches which golden hour depends on orientation and sightline. That is a recce question [FR-T02], not an assumption.

### 1.2 How CMU's system and F.U.'s world share a frame (Q-b)

Every piece is built in **three layers that never trade places.**

| Layer | Holds | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · The world** (photo or footage) | CMU at golden hour. She may intrude at ≤15% of the frame | CMU colors appear only on things in use (a flannel, a jersey, a strap, a painted tool handle), per v01 §0.2. CMU apparel with real marks needs [FR-T13] |
| **2 · F.U.'s annotation** | The flamingo circle, cream evidence labels, Polaroid and dossier frames, pearl strings, the estate tag | Never touches layer 3's clear space. **Never circles, tags or covers a CMU mark**: no CMU logo, monogram or wordmark ever sits under a pink circle or inside an F.U. object. No PF Grand Gothik in this layer |
| **3 · CMU's sign-off** | Mavroon field or band, the line in the S12 lockup, the logo, the tagline, the URL | Built on the S12 grid. Logo clear space "x" (S12 p.28) and minimum sizes held: interlocking > 0.5 in, wordmark > 0.375 in (p.30). Logo white or yellow on Mavroon, maroon or black on light (p.20). Never recoloured, textured, faded, rotated except per p.21, or combined with other graphics (p.31). **One branding mark per piece** (p.24). The seal is never used (p.18). No F.U. type, pastel or gilt in this layer |

**Mavroon in every piece.** Every CMU-published piece carries layer 3, so Mavroon is always present as the sign-off field or band. **F.U.-account, in-world pieces** (its carousels and posts) cannot carry CMU's sign-off without breaking the fiction, and the licensing position is open (Q31). Until Q31 is answered, the conservative design is that **Mavroon appears diegetically in layer 1**: every surveillance photo she files contains a visible Mavroon object in use, often the very thing she circled. The client decides whether that satisfies S12 p.38 or whether the F.U. account also needs a sign-off frame (§7 gives both).

**Pink vs Mavroon.** Mavroon's hue is about 335°. A hot china-marker pink sits at the same hue and would read as a Mavroon tint. That makes F.U. look CMU-adjacent and fails the brand-blind test. F.U.'s pink is therefore **Flamingo #FF6F91**: warmer (about 346°), much lighter and chalkier. It is Palm Beach pink, country-club pink. The two never touch at equal weight, and the pink never enters the sign-off.

### 1.3 The genre as images (S13)

- **CMU's energy is cheering at competence.** Its loudness comes from a crowd shouting because a weld held, a proof closed, a note landed or a climber topped out. **No cups, cans or bottles in any crowd frame. No togas. No pranks on people.** The Deltas' spirit without their vices.
- **The ring.** CMU crowds form a ring around the person doing the thing. Her grease-pencil circle then **circles what the crowd is already circling**. The device and the composition rhyme, and she's always late to what everyone else already knows.
- **Every field, sleeves rolled** [FR-T01]: a welder, a nursing student in the sim lab, a physicist at a chalk wall, a violinist or actor in rehearsal, a geologist with dust on her boots, a film student with a camera, an ag or environmental scientist in the field, an athlete, the Outdoor Program. Trades are one of these fields, not the image of CMU. **The spread is the point.**
- **CMU never serves F.U.** No caddies, waiters, valets, groundskeepers or anyone carrying her bags. No CMU person holds a tray, a door or an umbrella for an F.U. person. When CMU people offer to teach her (the generosity beat), they do it **as equals**: they keep working and show her by doing, at the same or a higher eyeline. She is usually the one in the bush.
- **Caricature.** F.U. is exaggerated in posture and manner (nose in the air, finger guns, the Shooter strut), never in body or ethnicity. CMU is never exaggerated. Casting follows S12 p.69: "accurately representing our student body and campus community."

### 1.4 Hard rules carried forward

- **The circle lands on the task, never the person.** Hands, tool, instrument, page, the thing being made. Never a face or a body.
- **No residence halls.** She never aims at, stands near or photographs where students live. Lenses aim at teaching and public spaces.
- **Eye contact is reserved.** CMU people don't pose for camera. The one exception is when they **catch her**: a CMU subject looks straight down her lens and offers to show her how. That is the "caught" beat, and it's the only eye contact in the system.
- **No donor money as F.U.'s gag.** Legacy, surnames, favours and membership are the targets (director review, "not yet good enough", point 6).
- **The richness rule** (v02 §1.1). Every CMU frame carries at least as many decided specifics as an F.U. frame.

### 1.5 The wear code, updated for S13

v01 §0.1 holds, with one relaxation. **Clothes may now carry today's work**: rolled sleeves, chalk, sawdust, rosin, fresh dust on boots, scrubs pushed up. Rule 2 already allowed fresh working residue, and S13 extends it from tools to people. What remains prohibited: holes, patches, stains from neglect, anything that reads as hardship. **Unpolished means mid-effort, never poor.** F.U.'s inverse is unchanged: tags on, packet creases, protective film, unbroken spines, nothing touched.

### 1.6 The estate tag (secondary device, from A)

It is a cream shipping tag with a gilt eyelet and a string, stamped **NON-TRANSFERABLE** in engraved caps with an F.U. inventory number. It appears **only when she has tried to take something**: tied to the notebook she bought, or to a welding helmet on a marble bust in Thaddeus's study. In layer 1 it hangs on the object. It is never tied to a person. It is the argument (it won't transfer) after the circle has done the premise (she wants it).

## 2. Key visual: "The Evidence Board", final composition spec

**One-line read.** In F.U.'s lamp-lit situation room, a wall of golden-hour photographs of CMU people being good at things is circled in pink as threats. It is the warmest, loudest, most alive thing in the room.

**Masters.** 3:2 landscape at 7200 × 4800 px (print and spread), plus derived 16:9, 4:5 and 9:16 crops, each composed and not just cropped (see the end of this section). Built on the S12 **6×8 landscape grid**: 6 rows, 8 columns, 1 row = 800 px.

## 3. End card and lockup spec (Q30)

### 3.1 The problem, restated precisely

The line is 10 words. S12 p.35 recommends its high-contrast dynamic styling for headlines of 8 words or fewer, and p.58 limits headlines to 3 rows in 3 fonts. **Useful precedent:** S12's own showcase headline on p.33/p.35, "BE A MODEL OF THE WORLD WE WANT TO CREATE.", is also 10 words. It's set in 4 rows and handles its length by putting the connective words (BE, OF THE, TO) in the light Condensed styles and the heavy styles on the words that matter. The 8-word note is advice about how much *heavy* type a line can carry. The solution follows the brand's own example and keeps the heavy styles for the words that carry the argument.

### 3.2 The lockup: the type makes the argument

```
   WE CARE ABOUT                 ← Condensed Book
WHAT YOU KNOW,                   ← Extended Black Italic (widest row; the loud one)
   NOT WHO YOU KNOW.             ← Condensed Book Italic
```

- **3 rows, 3 styles, exact wording.** The heavy style lands on three words, so the dynamic styling carries 3 words and not 10.
- **The type makes the argument (Barry §7).** "What you know" is the widest, heaviest, loudest row. "Who you know" sits in the thinnest, narrowest style in the family. Strip the treatment and the words still work. Keep it and the layout *says* the line. That earns the treatment (typography §7).
- **Proportions.** All three rows share one cap height. Row 2's measure sets the lockup width. Rows 1 and 3 are centred on it and naturally run at about 55–65% of its width, which gives the lockup a shape that swells in the middle. Leading 0.95 × cap height (S12 sets headlines solid, 32/32). Tracking 0 for Extended Black Italic; +10 for the Condensed rows at display sizes; optical kerning in the comma space after KNOW.
- **Colour.** White on Mavroon (default); Mavroon on white or light (secondary). Never gold on white, and never on photography without a Mavroon field behind it. The sign-off never sits on the image.

### 3.3 How the line and "MAVERICK AT HEART" coexist

**The line is the argument; the tagline is the signature. They never compete as two dynamic headlines.**

- **Static (every format).** The line takes the dynamic lockup in the headline safe area. "MAVERICK AT HEART" is set in **Wide Bold, single style, white** (the setting S12 itself shows on p.36 as H1) in the URL safe area, above COLORADOMESA.EDU, at about **28–32% of the line's cap height**. The logo sits in the logo safe area (S12 p.59). Reading order: line → logo → tagline → URL.
- **Film (:90 and :30).** They're **two cards, because the tagline is a punchline.**
  - **Card 1: the line** (:90 at 1:23.0–1:27.0). The dynamic lockup on a Mavroon field. Rows build on the beat: row 1 cuts on, row 2 slams in at full width (the one moment the end card moves with energy), row 3 settles.
  - **Card 2: the signature** (1:27.0–1:30.0). The horizontal signature centred, white on Mavroon, with "MAVERICK AT HEART" beneath and the URL.
  - **Why two cards.** If the Headmistress's report has earlier said [COPY: "They call themselves Mavericks, sir. Unbranded."], the tagline lands as the payoff to her line. CMU's own name for itself answers her. This is subject to client approval of the Maverick usage [FR-T12]. If it isn't approved, card 2 stays and simply signs off.
- **:15 and :06.** One combined card: the lockup in the headline area, and the logo, tagline and URL stacked below at the static proportions. Minimum 2.5 s on screen for :06, 3.5 s for :15.

### 3.4 The background and the transition

- **The field** is Mavroon, **or** the S12 **Mavroon → Monument Sunset gradient** (p.40), angled so the warm end sits at the bottom like a horizon. A core-values combination background (p.49) is also on-system. If one is used, I'd propose **Curiosity + Courage + Power** (Power is the Maverick horns, p.48, which fits the "Unbranded" payoff), cropped so the whole lockup sits on flat Mavroon. Final template files come from CMU Marketing [FR-T14].
- **Transition (film).** The last epilogue freeze frame is a golden-hour wide. **Its sunset sky dissolves into the Mavroon → Monument Sunset gradient**, so the sun sets into the end card. CMU's gradient is literally what western Colorado does at dusk (S12 p.40). Nothing from F.U. crosses into the card: no circle, no label, no pink.

## 4. Film look: the :90 "Field Report"

Script and beats are WordSmither's. This section is the look. Beat names follow the director's round 2 brief: boardroom → arrival → surveillance montage → the Collector beat (from A) → the Showcase blackout → the classroom turn → epilogue → end card.

### 4.1 Format and capture

- **1.85:1**, delivered in 16:9 with thin letterbox for CTV and YouTube. Protect a centred 4:5 and 9:16 in every CMU shot so cutdowns can be recomposed.
- **35 mm film preferred** for CMU exteriors: **Kodak 250D**, printed toward the 80s daylight-stock look (the 5247 era), warm, with fine grain and halation on highlights. F.U. interiors on **500T**, where lamp flames and practicals bloom. If digital, grain and halation are emulated from a film scan reference, never from a preset LUT. **No VHS, scan lines, synthwave or neon.** This is a *film* era, not a tape era.
- **Lenses.** A period cine zoom (the Angénieux 25–250 class) for slow push-ins and **snap zooms onto her reactions**. Spherical primes for CMU at 35–50 mm. **Split-diopter** for her signature shot (below).

### 4.2 CMU at golden hour vs F.U. lamp-lit

| | F.U. | CMU |
|---|---|---|
| Key | Practicals and Willis-style top-light; faces fall into shadow; candle and green banker's-lamp glow; curtains always shut | Low sun, 5–15°, raking or backlit; dust and breath in the air; bounce only to hold faces, never to flatten the light |
| Camera | Locked off, symmetrical, wide, slow | Steadicam and handheld inside the crowd, eye level; moves *with* the work |
| Cutting | Slow and formal, like a brochure | Longer takes than F.U.; overlapping action; the crowd reaction is part of the shot |
| Sound bed | Clock tick, porcelain, hush | Crowd, tools, instruments, laughter |

### 4.3 Stakeout grammar (her POV)

- **Opera-glass matte.** Not a generic binocular double-circle. The frame is her mother-of-pearl opera glasses, with the gilt rims soft at the edge of frame. It's specific to her, and the gag is in the prop.
- **Through the camouflage.** Foreground ghillie strips or boxwood leaves out of focus, lit **flamingo pink** by her key, while CMU beyond is golden. The mismatch in the light is the joke.
- **Long lens** (300–600 mm equivalent) with heat shimmer on midday stakeouts, a telephoto grain boost and slight breathing.
- **"Taking a photo" is a unit of grammar.** A motor-drive shutter burst; **freeze 6 frames**; the flamingo circle **draws on in 10 frames around the task**; a cream label slaps on in 2 frames with her note; then the shot unfreezes. It's the same unit every time, in film, DOOH and social.
- **Split-diopter depth staging** (her signature frame): the Headmistress sharp in the near foreground, mid-scheme; the CMU task sharp in the background, already undoing her scheme without looking at her.
- **She is never the POV onto living spaces**, and the lens never rests on a body.

### 4.4 The 80s grammar

- Crest title card: **FEATHERSTONE UNIVERSITY · PART THE SECOND** [COPY], engraved serif, gilt on black, with a slow push-in.
- **The Steadicam crowd walk** through the Showcase or a game-day crowd: one unbroken move, the camera threading between people, everyone mid-something.
- **The montage** (surveillance) on an original score written in the era's style. Licensed needle-drops are a production rights question.
- **Snap zoom** to her face at every "Impressive. I meant suspicious." [COPY].
- **No quoted scenes.** Grammar is borrowed; specific sequences, lines and music are not.

### 4.5 Key lighting beats

- **Arrival (morning golden hour).** A crested boxwood crosses the quad in long shadow; the dust it kicks up is backlit.
- **The Collector beat (from A).** At the study table, **soft, diffused warm window light**. She buys the notebook; the estate tag goes on in Thaddeus's study, which is lamp-lit and airless.
- **The Showcase blackout** (the spine; the strongest shot in the campaign). The room is soft and even under projector light. She pulls the breaker. **The projectors die, and the sunset floods in through the west windows**: the room goes from blue screen-light to deep gold in one cut. The presenter keeps going, better, lit by the sun. The shrub is silhouetted against a window, betrayed by a head torch. *She cut the power; the light came in.* This needs west-facing glass and a sunset slot [FR-T04, FR-T02].
- **The classroom turn** (with the director's harder snap-back). Morning window light. She is asked "What do you think?" [COPY]. As she answers, **her pink key fades and the room's warm light takes her face**: for one second she is lit like CMU. Then **an audible lamp click and the pink key snaps back on**. The lighting delivers the snap-back, so the performance doesn't have to over-sell it.

### 4.6 Epilogue freeze frames

A 2 px, 60% black drop shadow, as on 80s film captions. The era's yellow caption becomes CMU's gold.

## Taste fit, reported separately

Predicted fit with Andres's profile is high. The type does the argument (§3.2), each piece has one focal point, richness carries the idea rather than decorating it, and the humour is inseparable from the observation. The risk to his taste is density: the Evidence Board and the one-sheet are maximal by design. That is argued (the richness rule, the F.U. vs CMU density contrast), and it has to be protected by strict hierarchy in the build. This is a prediction, not his approval.

## Top risks

1. **Golden hour becomes a filter.** If it's graded in rather than shot, CMU turns into a warm stock campus. It has to be scheduled and shot at the real hour.
2. **The spread of fields collapses back to trades.** If FR-T01 returns only CMU Tech, the campaign shrinks "blue collar" to a job. Hold the spread.
3. **The loud crowd reads as a party.** Every crowd needs a visible task at its centre. No task means no crowd.
