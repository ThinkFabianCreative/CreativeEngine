# "What Don't You Want?" :60 scripts: director review, v01

CLIENT: Colorado Mesa University. PROJECT: Featherstone Phase 2 / FY27 brand campaign. SOURCE SCOPE: PROJECT-LEVEL. Reviewed: `work/copy/FY27_what-dont-you-want_scripts_v01.md` (word-smither, 2026-11-22) against the client's `raw/2026-11-22_client_what-dont-you-want_brief-and-scripts.docx`. MODE: DEVELOP leaning JUDGE. Reviewer: creative-engine-director, 2026-11-23. Scripts file not edited.

Standing rules checked: satire hits privilege, nepotism, corruption and silencing, never wealth, money, donors or success; no rigging plot; CMU never claims to be better than the Ivies; audience is kids who think the game is rigged; no school named; claims ungated but flagged.

## Headline verdict

All four beat the client's scripts. The client's three explain the question (a counselor speech, a janitor speech, a girl's speech followed by a slow clap) and aim their jokes at money (rich athlete, sorority, crystal glass). Ours mostly dramatize it and aim at last names and enforced agreement. Two problems run across the set:

1. **Donor leakage.** "Library named after a man whose grandson..." and "his grandpa has a building" both put the joke on a building, which means a gift. That is the donor line Andres has drawn. Move the joke to the last name, not the building.
2. **Back halves explain.** In Honest Tour and Interview the satire half is dramatized and the CMU half turns into supers and tidy answers. The CMU half has to be a scene too, or it reads as "we're better than them."

| 3 | The Custodian | REWORK | Last line contradicts the idea; sushi and building gags lean on money |

## 1. The Honest Tour: ADVANCE

The best idea in the set. The question works as truth serum, the kid is the smartest person on the quad, and the jokes are about who gets in, not who has money. It is the one an audience would send to a friend.

**Biggest weakness:** the opening gag points at a named building ("library's named after a man whose grandson got in with a 2.1"). A name on a library reads as a donation. That is mocking a donor, which breaks the rule, and it is also the line most likely to make CMU's own advancement office nervous.

**Line fixes**
- 0:04 Deshawn's question shifts the campaign question to "What don't you want me to know?" That is a clever bend, but it is not the line on the end card. Set it up so the bend is visible: GUIDE: "Questions? What do you want to know?" / DESHAWN: "What don't you want me to know?" Now the flip is the joke and it rhymes with the end card.
- 0:10 replace the library: GUIDE: "...See the guy with the lacrosse stick? Fourth generation. Got in with a 2.1." Keep 0:14 ("He's in the building now. He has a podcast" becomes "He has a podcast."). The joke is now inheritance, not a gift.
- 0:24 "The waitlist is a vibe. It's mostly last names." Keep. Strongest line in the set.
- 0:28 "It's not what you know here. (Pause.) You know?" Keep; it pays off the Phase 2 platform line without saying it.
- 0:38–0:54 the back half explains. Three supers plus a VO line in 16 seconds is too much and turns satire into a claims list. Replace with a scene: CMU, daylight. A CMU student guide, walking forward. Deshawn: "What don't you want me to know?" CMU GUIDE (shrugs): "Parking's bad." Beat. End card. One honest, small answer does more than three supers and keeps CMU from claiming superiority. Keep at most one super ("Your last name isn't a credential.") if the client wants a proposition on screen.
- Cut "At Colorado Mesa, we care about what you know, not who you know" VO or move it to the end card. Choose one platform line per spot.

**Checks:** dramatized, yes until 0:38. Money mocked, yes at 0:10 (fix above). End beat: not earned as written (supers), earned with the "Parking's bad" scene. Timing: front half is tight but filmable; at 0:07 the "long beat" and 0:28 "long pause" together cost ~3s, keep both, they are the comedy. **Legal:** the old campus must not resemble any real school (no real gate, crest, Gothic landmark, colors or mascot; build on a set or composite). The "2.1" is fictional and about an invented student, low risk once no real campus is identifiable. "This class is great if you agree with the professor" is a general jab, fine unnamed. Any CMU super about legacy admissions or price needs [PROOF].

## 2. The Interview: ADVANCE

The cleanest structure: three propositions become three questions a 17-year-old would actually ask, and the other side answers by dodging. "Thank you for your time. I know it's expensive." is a great button. It jabs the price of the place, not the people's wealth, so it stays inside the rule.

**Biggest weakness:** the CMU half is a mirror scene where CMU gets every answer right. That is a direct "we're better than them" comparison against a polished elite school, which Andres has ruled out, and it is less funny than the first half.

**Line fixes**
- 0:40–0:50 six lines in ten seconds will not fit at a natural pace. Cut to two answers and let the third be a look. Suggested: JO: "Last name?" / ADVISOR: "Whose?" (genuinely confused). JO: "Disagree with the professor?" / ADVISOR: "Which one? They argue with each other." Then the cost sheet slides over with no line. Humor instead of correct answers removes the scorecard feel.
- "Please do. Bring evidence." is good but sounds like a brochure. If kept, it needs CMU to stand behind it [PROOF].
- 0:50 "Huh." Keep. That is the earned end beat: she is almost disappointed it was simple.
- End VO "Ask us anything" is a new line competing with the campaign question. Drop it; end on "Colorado Mesa University." over the card.
- 0:17 "what happens to my grade?" implies elite schools punish viewpoints. Unnamed, so low legal risk, but it is the most politically loaded line in the set. Keep it as a question, never answer it on the elite side with anything that sounds like confirmation.

**Checks:** dramatized, yes. Money mocked, no. Timing: front half fine; back half over by ~5s until trimmed. **Legal:** cost figure must be a real approved number with conditions in legal super (C18 candidate) [PROOF].

## 3. The Custodian: REWORK

A clear improvement on the client's C: Carlos drops from a speech to ~40 words, the room says the truths, and "DAD: I don't want her to do what I did" is the best emotional line across all four. The mop knock on the glass is kept and earns its place.

**Biggest weakness:** the last line, "Best thing I didn't want to do," says the opposite of the campaign: that you should do the thing you didn't want. It muddies the idea at the moment it should land.

**Line fixes**
- 0:48 replace with silence, or: CARLOS: "Six years. Nobody asked me my last name." That pays off the nepotism thread and keeps him dry.
- 0:00 "MARKETERS eat catered sushi" is a luxury gag; it mocks money. Swap for behavior: they tally answers on a whiteboard under "WANTS," or one of them is mouthing the script along with the facilitator who isn't there.
- 0:25 FARM KID: "lose to some guy because his grandpa has a building" is the donor gag again. Change to: "I don't want to lose my spot to some guy because of his last name."
- 0:21 KID 1: "my mom paying for me till she's seventy" stays; it is about debt, not wealth.
- 0:38 "They heard you." Keep.
- The wise-janitor device is the client's and a familiar trope. That is fine for relationship reasons, but this is why it ranks below the two originals.

**Checks:** dramatized, yes. Money mocked, yes twice (fixes above). End beat: not earned until the last line is fixed. Timing: realistic. **Legal:** "Night classes, six years" is a CMU program claim [PROOF] (or "Took me a while"). The CMU sweatshirt is the only branding moment; fine.

## 4. Cross It Out: REWORK

Most flexible for cutdowns, lowest production risk, and the act of crossing out is a genuinely visual version of elimination. As a hero :60 it is the weakest.

**Biggest weakness:** a teenager's wall of college mailers is the category's own picture, and the end beat ("Ask me something easier") has her refuse a question instead of finally answering the right one. The question is never asked out loud in the story; it appears only as a super.

**Line fixes**
- 0:50 end: MOM: "So what do you want?" / MAYA: "Wrong question." / MOM (looks at the wall, beat): "...What don't you want?" / Maya hands her the Sharpie. The mom gets to the question herself; that is the earned beat.
- 0:45 the one plain mailer left is ambiguous (CMU? any school?). Either it is CMU and visible, or there is no mailer left and the wall is all black marker. The second is braver and avoids "pick us" logic.
- 0:35 cut "I don't want 'maybe if you're lucky'"; two lines in ten seconds plus a montage is dense.
- 0:22 "pay for a name until I'm forty" is about price, fine. Keep.
- 0:04 swap the "AD: Find what you really want" voice for something sharper and less generic.

**Checks:** dramatized, partly. Money mocked, no. Timing: fine. **Legal:** mailer headlines must be invented, generic phrases and fonts; no real school's mailer design.
