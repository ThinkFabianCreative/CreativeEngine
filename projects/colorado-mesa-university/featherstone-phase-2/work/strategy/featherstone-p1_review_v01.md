## 1. What is actually in the work (SEEN)

**The spot (:90).** A campus tour of Featherstone University: a castle-like campus, pastel preppy uniforms, varsity F and U sweaters, a rider on horseback, a rowing crew carrying a shell, a champagne tower, books titled DON'T WORK, NETWORK and HOW TO BUY FRIENDS, and a father handing over a $150,000 "Bank of Family Trust" check ("Hi, Daddy."). The guide is a woman in a teal plaid suit with a clipboard. The microsite names her headmistress, Arabella Wexford-Smythe [name uncertain, read from a small frame]. She is not Dr. Augustus Sinclair Featherstone III, who appears in none of the footage reviewed.

**The audience stand-in.** The student wears a plaid flannel shirt and a mustard backpack, the only earth tones in a pastel world. She reacts with no lines of commentary until "F. me?" and "Yes."

**The joke engine.** The guide is a straight-faced tour guide who says the quiet part out loud, then corrects herself: "Why work hard when you can just be privileged? … Efficient. I meant efficient." The F.U. name joke gets its own exchange at the top.

**The CMU turn.** An unseen voice interrupts the tour: "Looking for something more real?" The student says "Yes." The voice says "Come to Colorado Mesa University." The guide asks "Who said that?" The voice answers: **"We care about who you are, not who you know."** The student walks out. A hard cut lands on the real CMU campus (University Center, the mascot, students in maroon), then the CMU end card.

**Timing.** The satire runs from about 0:00 to 1:08. The CMU turn and real campus take about 1:08 to 1:30. About 75% of the spot is Featherstone.

## 2. What made it work (READ)

1. **A complete world, not a sketch.** Production design, costume and the microsite make F.U. feel like a real institution. The satire lands because the world is played straight and never winks. This is the campaign's most valuable asset, and the hardest to rebuild.
2. **Exaggeration of a real grievance.** Legacy money, buying grades and networking over work are genuine public complaints (the case film sets them against real tuition and confidence headlines). F.U. exaggerates them just enough to be absurd yet recognizable.
3. **The name.** "F.U." did much of the earned-media work. It is a joke people repeat, and it lets CMU be rude to elite higher ed while keeping deniability.
4. **The audience has a seat on the tour.** The flannel student turns a lecture into a point of view: we watch her decide.
5. **The media system extended the fiction.** F.U. posted as itself, and CMU interrupted it. The campaign was structured as two institutions talking, not only as a spot with cutdowns.

## 3. What Phase 1 leaves thin (READ)

These are Phase 2's openings, not criticisms of Phase 1's job. Phase 1's job was to get noticed, and it did.

1. **CMU is the weakest part of the story.** About 20 seconds of real campus and a mascot high-five follow 70 seconds of richly specific satire. F.U. has specific details (ancestral portraits, Versailles); CMU gets general words: "real," "who you are." The satire proved what CMU is against. Almost nothing proves what CMU is for.
2. **The turn is a voice from nowhere.** The unseen voice is efficient but has no character. CMU has no face, place or person in the world.
3. **The best jokes have been told.** The F.U. name reveal, "Hi, Daddy" and the check only surprise once. Another tour with new gags risks a weaker sequel.
4. **The student has no story.** She observes and leaves. We never learn what she knows, can do, or wants, which is the "what you know" half of the premise.
5. **The platform line isn't settled** (see §4).

## 5. Director's read for Phase 2

Phase 1 won attention by building the villain. The natural Phase 2 question is not "what else is ridiculous at F.U.?" but **what CMU can now say or show that attention bought the right to say**. That points toward giving the "what you know" side its own specificity. It could also mean showing F.U. under pressure: a place that got called out and is now reacting.

I am not choosing a direction here. EXPLORE should cover the full range, from pure continuation to a real evolution. I am flagging that continuation alone has the lowest ceiling, and that the first answer to settle (F1) is which line the campaign stands on.
