## Five-line summary (what the evidence supports)

1. Some universities report application gains after distinctive, entertainment-led or content-series campaigns (Victoria Uni +25%, UEL +7%, Central Michigan +25%). The figures are self-reported or come from award entries, and none is a branded-entertainment docuseries plus live event.
2. The closest analogue to a docuseries (*Last Chance U*, EMCC) shows a "dramatic" rise in interest in anecdotes only. No application data is public.
3. Small-budget stunts can reach national earned scale (KFC Canada "Fixed It": $30K, 18x earned to paid, +5.1% store sales, per an Effie-cited entry).
4. Gen Z spends less time on linear TV than any other cohort (ARF, Deloitte, Nielsen). Their viewing is moving to streaming and YouTube/TikTok, though some data shows ad tolerance in streaming is rising.
5. Earned media is trusted most (Nielsen, but the data is from 2012–13). Binet & Field find fame campaigns more efficient, but earned media almost never works without paid support.

## B. Evidence: teen / Gen Z viewing

- ARF DASH (June 2025): Gen Z spends the least time with TV of any generation. 59% of Gen Z use a TV set to watch, vs 88% of Boomers. https://thearf.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/ARF-DASH_TV-DECONSTRUCTED_JUNE-2025.pdf
- Deloitte, as reported in the search excerpt: Gen Z spends 1.3 h/day on streaming services vs 0.8 h/day on traditional or live TV. I didn't open the primary source.
- Nielsen: streaming is 66.7% of ad-supported TV time for ages 18–49 (reported March 2026), https://senalnews.com/en/data/usa-streaming-now-commands-66-of-1849-ad-supported-viewing/amp. About two-thirds of 6–17s watch streaming ahead of conventional TV (Oct 2025), https://senalnews.com/en/data/usa-teens-embrace-traditional-tv-and-prioritize-friendships-in-streaming-choices/amp
- Activate (via search excerpt): 43% of Gen Z prefer YouTube/TikTok to traditional TV or paid streaming. UCLA Teens & Screens: YouTube beats TV shows and movies for Gen Z/Alpha, https://cordcuttersnews.com/?p=254688
- eMarketer: nearly 40% of US 18–26s use no ad-supported streaming platform. https://www.emarketer.com/content/gen-z-doesn-t-like-streaming-ads--should-marketers-worried
- Counter-evidence: Hub Research finds Gen Z will accept heavier ad loads for lower prices and is more aware of TV ads during breaks. https://www.advanced-television.com/?p=287328 ; https://www.tvtechnology.com/insights/analysis/study-younger-viewers-more-receptive-to-ads-and-more-distracted
- Not found: a credible Gen Z ad-skip rate. None is stated here.

## C. Evidence: earned vs paid

- Nielsen Global Trust in Advertising (2012): 92% trust earned media (friends/family recommendations) above all other forms; 70% trust online reviews. Later Nielsen pages repeat the 2012 sentence, so cite it as 2012 data. https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2012/global-trust-in-advertising-and-brand-messages-2/
- Nielsen 2013: word of mouth most trustworthy for 84% (58 countries, 29,000+ respondents). https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2013/under-the-influence-consumer-trust-in-advertising
- Binet & Field (IPA): fame campaigns are about 4x as efficient at generating ESOV, per Ipsos. https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/knowledge/media-brand-communication/we-dont-need-talk-about-ads
- WARC summary: earned adds about 26% to paid campaign effects and owned about 13%. Binet: earned is "a consequence of paid media, by and large". The IPA Databank has almost no cases of earned effects without paid. https://www.warc.com/newsandopinion/news/why-scale-still-matters-for-brands/42085 ; https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/the-greatest-hits-of-binet-field/
- Caveat: the 26%/13% dataset and year are unconfirmed. Check the IPA primary before citing.

- Source: Effie Australia 2025 entry #1239, Noisy Beast, category Challenger Brands. https://advertisingcouncil.org.au/effies/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/1239-B02-Defying-a-Category-in-a-State-of-Decline-to-Deliver-Breaking-Results.pdf (opened 2026-11-22)

- Context: entrant says media spend rose only 3% (2023 to 2024), and the category was in decline (school leavers continuing to higher education fell from 81.1% in 2008 to 67.9% in 2023). **5. Binet & Field: fame about 4x, and earned rarely works without paid: VERIFIED through near-primary sources**
- 4x: Ipsos (Mar 2021) quotes *The Long and the Short of It*, saying fame campaigns are "four times as efficient in generating Excess Share of Voice". https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/knowledge/media-brand-communication/we-dont-need-talk-about-ads . I could not open the IPA book itself, so cite it as "Binet & Field, IPA, via Ipsos".
- Earned needs paid: Binet's own deck *Marketing Effectiveness in the Digital Era* (2017), slide "Earned media: no free lunch". Of IPA Databank cases from 2014–16 that generated earned media online, 4% used earned only and 78% used earned, owned and paid together. https://www.aktv.cz/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Marketing-Effectiveness-in-the-Digital-Era_Les-Binet.pdf . Supporting: an IPA report (Oct 2016, reported by The Drum and Research Live) found earned media raised a paid campaign's effectiveness by 26% and owned media by 13%. https://www.thedrum.com/news/2016/10/31/brands-use-paid-media-grow-three-times-faster-those-just-rely-earned-and-owned-finds . This settles the dataset caveat in section C.
- CORRECTION: the IPA "greatest hits" blog (2018) makes neither claim. Drop it as a source.
- Slide-ready: "Fame campaigns are about 4x as efficient (Binet & Field, IPA). But only 4% of campaigns that earned media online did it without paid support (IPA Databank 2014–16)."
