# FY27 "The game is rigged": evidence file v01

CLIENT: Colorado Mesa University (CMU). PROJECT: Featherstone Phase 2 / FY27 brand campaign. SOURCE SCOPE: PROJECT-LEVEL (public secondary research, gathered 2026-11-22 by creative-researcher). Status: evidence only, unverified against full source documents unless noted. No strategy, no verdicts.

Belief under test: many high school students, especially rural, small-town, first-generation and lower-income students in Colorado and the US West, feel "the game is rigged" and that college "isn't for people like me."

Confidence key: HIGH = official data or peer-reviewed / large probability sample, read in source excerpt. MED = reputable survey, non-probability panel, or secondary report. LOW = single anecdote, older data, or not read in full.

## What the evidence supports / doesn't

1. Supports: Colorado has a persistent, measurable access gap: about half of grads enroll, FRL students ~20 points lower, rural lower, Western Slope districts (Montrose, D51) below Front Range suburbs, FAFSA among the nation's worst.
2. Supports: "Hidden rules nobody explained" is well evidenced: information gaps (Hoxby), misinformed cost beliefs, thin counseling, and first-gen voice that clusters on "nobody told me."
3. Supports: Young people broadly feel systems are unfair or out of reach (Harvard, UCLA, CNN, Chetty on elite admissions), but no source found asks teens about "rigged" directly.
4. Doesn't support: That these students believe college "isn't for people like me." Aspiration is high; the gap is between wanting it and getting there. Their doubts are mostly about cost and value.
5. Complicates: The proven "rigging" sits at elite private schools, not regional publics, and skeptic audiences (often conservative, rural) cite cost and politics. "Rigged" may fit the frustration better than the audience's own words do.
