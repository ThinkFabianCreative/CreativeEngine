# "What Don't You Want?" :60 scripts, v01

CLIENT: Colorado Mesa University. PROJECT: Featherstone Phase 2 / FY27 brand campaign. SOURCE: client brief and scripts `raw/2026-11-22_client_what-dont-you-want_brief-and-scripts.docx`. MODE: DEVELOP. Author: word-smither, 2026-11-22. Status: DRAFT for Andres, not client-approved.

## Why rewrite

The client's three scripts have the right question and the wrong delivery.

- **Someone explains the idea.** The counselor and the custodian give speeches about the question. Here, the kid gets to the answer herself, on camera, and we watch it happen.
- **The villains are cartoons of money.** A rich athlete and dancing sorority girls are easy jokes, and they mock wealth. Our target is rigging: legacy, connections, being told what to think. A kid who thinks the game is rigged recognizes that right away.
- **The endings are slow claps.** The audience doesn't believe applause. Every script below ends on a small, dry beat.
- **Some claims will get killed in legal.** "Half a million in debt" and "pays the coach more than all the professors" are exaggerations about other schools. These scripts go after behavior and only claim things CMU can prove.

Rules for all four: we never name another school, CMU never says it's better than anyone, and the kid is always the smartest person in the room. Claims marked **[PROOF]** need sign-off.

## 1. "CROSS IT OUT"

A literal take on elimination. The quietest of the four and the easiest to cut down.

| Time | Picture | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | A 17-year-old's bedroom wall, covered with college mailers, all glossy smiles and sunsets. MAYA (17) sits on the floor with a fat black Sharpie. | Room tone. A phone keeps buzzing. |
| 0:04 | Montage of grown-ups asking, straight to camera: an aunt, a coach, a guy at the gas station, a YouTube ad. | AUNT: So what do you want to do? / COACH: What do you want, Maya? / GAS STATION GUY: Where you wanna go? / AD: Find what you really want. |
| 0:12 | Maya uncaps the Sharpie. She crosses out one mailer's headline, "A LEGACY OF EXCELLENCE." | MAYA (V.O.): I don't know what I want. |
| 0:16 | Another one: "WHERE CONNECTIONS ARE MADE." Cross. | MAYA (V.O.): I know I don't want a place where who your dad knows counts more than what I know. |
| 0:22 | A glossy brochure with a price sheet tucked in. She flips to the number, reads it, puts the whole brochure face down. | MAYA (V.O.): I don't want to pay for a name until I'm forty. |
| 0:28 | A mailer: "JOIN A COMMUNITY THAT SHARES YOUR VALUES." She looks at it, then writes "MINE?" in the margin. | MAYA (V.O.): I don't want to be told what to think. I can do that myself. |
| 0:35 | Faster now. Cross, cross, cross. The wall turns into black marker. | MAYA (V.O.): I don't want a lecture hall where nobody learns my name. I don't want "maybe if you're lucky." |
| 0:45 | One mailer left, plain and unflashy. She holds the Sharpie over it and waits. Then she caps the pen. | Silence. |
| 0:50 | Her mom leans in the doorway, looks at the wall. | MOM: …So what do you want? / MAYA: Ask me something easier. |
| 0:55 | Super over the wall: **What don't you want?** CMU logo. coloradomesa.edu | V.O.: Colorado Mesa University. Start with what you don't want. |

**Why it's stronger:** The idea is the action. Each cross-out turns one of the three propositions into a thing she rejects, so nobody has to say the propositions out loud. Cutdowns are easy: a :15 is one cross-out plus the end card, and every cross-out works as a :06.

## 2. "THE HONEST TOUR"

Satire aimed at privilege.

| Time | Picture | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Old ivy-covered quad, unnamed and generic. A peppy TOUR GUIDE walks backwards in front of a group of families. | GUIDE: Okay! Questions, questions, anybody? |
| 0:04 | DESHAWN (17, hoodie, from a small town) raises his hand. | DESHAWN: Yeah. What don't you want me to know? |
| 0:07 | The guide's smile stays on. Something behind it breaks. A long beat. | A distant bell. |
| 0:10 | The guide points to a building. | GUIDE: …That library's named after a man whose grandson got in with a 2.1. |
| 0:14 | Families freeze. A dad slowly lowers his phone. | GUIDE: The grandson's in the building now. Ground floor. He has a podcast. |
| 0:19 | Past a seminar room through a window. | GUIDE: This class is great. You'll love it if you agree with the professor. |
| 0:24 | Past the admissions office. | GUIDE: The waitlist is a vibe. It's mostly last names. |
| 0:28 | The guide stops walking backwards and turns around for the first time. | GUIDE: Honestly? It's not what you know here. (Long pause.) You know? |
| 0:33 | Deshawn nods, takes off his visitor lanyard, puts it gently on the guide's neck, and walks out of frame. | |
| 0:38 | Hard cut: CMU, Grand Junction, wide daylight, the Monument in the distance. Real students. No tour guide. A professor talking to two students at a picnic table. | Music in, low and steady. |
| 0:44 | Supers, one per beat: **Your last name isn't a credential.** / **Your opinions are yours.** / **The price won't follow you for twenty years.** [PROOF] | V.O.: At Colorado Mesa, we care about what you know, not who you know. |
| 0:54 | End card: **What don't you want?** CMU logo. coloradomesa.edu | V.O.: Colorado Mesa University. |

**Why it's stronger:** The kid asks the campaign question, and it works like truth serum. The jokes hit legacy admissions, connections and enforced agreement. Nobody gets mocked for having money, so there is no donor problem. It also uses the platform line from Phase 2 ("what you know, not who you know") without a speech. **Watch out:** the old school must not look like any real campus. Build it from a set or a mix of locations.

## 3. "THE INTERVIEW"

A role reversal. The kid interviews the colleges.

| Time | Picture | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | An admissions interview room. Long table. Three ADMISSIONS OFFICERS from an unnamed, well-funded school, very polished. Across from them, one chair. | OFFICER 1: So, Jo. Tell us what you want. |
| 0:04 | JO (17, ranch kid, clean boots) sets a spiral notebook on the table and flips to a page. | JO: Actually I made a list of what I don't want. That okay? |
| 0:08 | They exchange looks. OFFICER 2 smiles too much. | OFFICER 2: Love that. Go for it. |
| 0:11 | JO reads. | JO: Number one. Does my last name help me here? |
| 0:14 | OFFICER 3 coughs. OFFICER 1 shuffles papers. | OFFICER 1: We consider the whole applicant. |
| 0:17 | JO makes a mark in the notebook. | JO: So yes. Number two. If I say something in class my professor disagrees with, what happens to my grade? |
| 0:23 | A long, careful pause. | OFFICER 2: We're a very… welcoming community. |
| 0:26 | Mark. | JO: Number three. What's this going to cost my mom? Total. With everything. |
| 0:30 | OFFICER 3 opens a folder, closes it again. | OFFICER 3: There's a net price calculator on our— |
| 0:33 | JO closes the notebook. | JO: Thank you for your time. I know it's expensive. |
| 0:37 | Smash cut: the same notebook, opened on a table at CMU, in a regular office with a window onto the mountains. A CMU advisor across from her, relaxed. | |
| 0:40 | Quick answers, one per question. | JO: Last name? / ADVISOR: Doesn't matter. / JO: Disagree with the professor? / ADVISOR: Please do. Bring evidence. / JO: Cost? / ADVISOR: (slides a sheet over) About this. [PROOF] |
| 0:50 | Jo reads the sheet. She looks up. She's almost annoyed at how simple it was. | JO: Huh. |
| 0:54 | End card: **What don't you want?** CMU logo. coloradomesa.edu | V.O.: Colorado Mesa University. Ask us anything. |

**Why it's stronger:** The three propositions turn into three questions a kid would actually ask, and the other side gets them wrong by dodging. CMU's short answers are the proof of "teach you how to think." **[PROOF]**: the price sheet should show a real, approved number (candidate: 2026-27 resident tuition after COF, with residency and full-time conditions, from `cmu-proof-candidates_v01.md` C18). "Please do. Bring evidence." needs CMU to stand behind it.

## 4. "THE CUSTODIAN" (rewrite of the client's script C)

The client's favorite, tightened: he has fewer words, the kids say more, and the reveal lands harder.

| Time | Picture | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | A slick focus group room. Two-way mirror. Behind the glass, MARKETERS in quarter-zips eat catered sushi. Students and parents around the table, waiting. The facilitator's late. | Wall clock ticks. |
| 0:05 | CARLOS (60s, custodian, cart) backs in through the door, sees everyone, stops. | CARLOS: Oh. Sorry. Wrong night? |
| 0:08 | A PARENT waves at the stack of glossy folders. | PARENT: It's about picking a college. They keep asking what our kids want. |
| 0:12 | Carlos looks at the kids. They look tired. He leans on his mop. | CARLOS: Huh. How's that going? |
| 0:15 | Silence. Nobody has an answer. | |
| 0:17 | Carlos pulls out a chair, sits, takes off his gloves. | CARLOS: Try the other one. What don't you want? |
| 0:21 | Beat. Then a kid in a hoodie, almost to himself: | KID 1: I don't want my mom paying for me till she's seventy. |
| 0:25 | It opens the room up. Fast cuts: | GIRL: I don't want to keep my mouth shut for four years so I don't lose a grade. / FARM KID: I don't want to lose to some guy because his grandpa has a building. / DAD: (quiet) I don't want her to do what I did. |
| 0:36 | Behind the glass, a marketer stops chewing. | |
| 0:38 | Carlos knocks twice on the mirror with his mop handle, without looking at it. | CARLOS: They heard you. |
| 0:41 | A GIRL, sincere: | GIRL: Is there anywhere that's not like that? |
| 0:43 | Carlos stands, puts his gloves back on, pushes the cart toward the door. | CARLOS: I went to one. Night classes. Took me six years. |
| 0:48 | He turns to leave. His jacket hangs open on the cart: an old CMU alumni sweatshirt underneath, faded. | CARLOS: (over his shoulder) Best thing I didn't want to do. |
| 0:53 | The door clicks shut. Behind the glass, someone writes down "what don't you want?" on a notepad. | |
| 0:55 | End card: **What don't you want?** CMU logo. coloradomesa.edu | V.O.: Colorado Mesa University. |

**What changed and why:**
- Carlos went from a 120-word speech to about 40 words. The kids and the dad deliver the truths, so the truths sound like people talking.
- He doesn't list the three propositions. The kids' answers cover them: debt, being silenced, family-name advantage.
- The client's best image, the mop knocking on the glass, is kept and pushed further: the people being watched finally talk back.
- "I used to work at a place like that" didn't make sense (working there isn't a reason to choose it). Now he's an alum who did it the hard way. It's more honest, and it makes "opportunity for a better life" true without a slogan. **[PROOF]**: CMU's night or adult programs need to be confirmed, or that line changes to "Took me a while."
- The dad's line is the emotional turn, and it's the only one in the room that isn't a joke.

## Recommendation

Lead with **"The Honest Tour"** if we want earned media and argument. It's the most shareable, and the question works as a weapon in it. Lead with **"Cross It Out"** if we want the most flexible cutdown system and the lowest production risk. Present **"The Custodian"** as the client's own idea, made better: that protects the relationship and shows how far it can go.

Open items: approved cost figure, the "bring evidence" teaching claim, the custodian's night-class history, and the brief's platform line ("What don't you want?" vs. Phase 2's "What you know, not who you know"). The scripts use both. Andres to decide which one leads.
