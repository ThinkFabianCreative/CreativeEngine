The spine is the idea in one read, for a jury or a university president. It is not a tagline. "Looking for something real?" stays the end line, unchanged.

## Candidates

1. A fake university rigs a real game against CMU in public, and CMU shows every student who was never shown how.
2. Featherstone rigs every game it plays; CMU plays it in the open so students who feel cheated can watch it fail.
3. **Strategy line.** Prove, in public, that at CMU the game isn't rigged.
4. CMU accepts a rigged challenge on camera, then shows the students who gave up what a fair shot looks like.
5. Featherstone is the rigged system with a mascot, and CMU takes it on where the students who stopped trying can see it.
6. A fake Ivy buys the umpire, the roster and the rules; CMU plays anyway, and shows how it's done.
7. For every kid nobody showed how: a real game against a school that cheats, played straight in front of the whole state.
8. CMU puts the rigged game on the field, loses nothing it earned, and hands students the rulebook nobody gave them.

## Recommended

**A fake university rigs a real game against CMU in public, and CMU shows every student who was never shown how.**

The insight is "nobody showed me how," so the line has to end on showing, not on winning. It also covers a loss: if CMU loses a rigged game, it has still shown how, so the spine holds whatever the score.

Note on #3: it is the right line for the strategy slide and the brief. As the spine it states the goal and says nothing about Featherstone or the game, so a president would get the aim without the idea.

## Spine test on deck v2

Slides that don't serve "rigs a real game in public, CMU shows the students nobody showed how":

1. **05 What the game is, close (11:402).** "At CMU, you play on what you know" is vague and misses the insight. Change to something like "At CMU, someone shows you how." Keep the Promise fact in the note so it isn't empty.
2. **06 What students told Marshall (9:221).** The title is about fear of getting stuck, which is the Promise engine's ground. Keep the evidence, retitle toward the insight: they want it and nobody showed them how. Marshall's quote can stay as the close.
3. **07 Who we're for (9:256, 9:265–9:266).** Opens on who we skip. Lead with the student nobody showed how; cut or shrink the "Who we skip" column. One clause is enough to say the Ivy-bound kid isn't the target.
4. **08 Why entertainment, "Featherstone proved it" (9:306–9:308).** A claim with no results yet, and off spine. Hold the column until CMU confirms Phase 1 numbers; in its place, say why showing in public beats telling in an ad.
5. **11 Two engines, "Where they meet" (9:380–9:381).** Make the Promise the "how": The Challenge shows the game is rigged elsewhere; the Promise is the first rule nobody told them. Small rewrite, same structure.
6. **22 Risks, "We lose on camera" (10:557–10:558).** Answer with the spine, not tone: losing a rigged game honestly still shows how. Add what the Promise clip says if the loss travels alone (carries over note 5 from the copy pass).

Slides 13–19 and 23–25 serve the spine as written. 21's "festivals and award shows" is fine for the agency but reads as vanity to a president; optional cut.

## Satire check

Every candidate targets rigging: bought umpires, rosters by last name, rewritten rules. None makes money, donors or success the joke, and none codes Featherstone left or right.
