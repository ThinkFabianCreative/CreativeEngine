## Evidence base
6 interviews: Autumn Strier (CEO), David LaBonte (Marketing Director), Quy Nguyen (Board Vice Chair), Tracy Keyser (External PR, 360 Circle Leader), Anthony Geisler (Major Donor), Janina Dorman (Marketing Manager).
Data: Blackbaud, Mailchimp, web analytics, YouTube, Meta, volunteer feedback, Charity Navigator, competitors, audited financials, the brand guide.

## Findings (as stated)
- Mission belief is universal. Charity Navigator 93% (4-star). "84¢ on every dollar goes to programs." 20+ years, same founder.
- Autumn is "the soul": every stakeholder centers her. "$5.7M organization built through personal relationships." The St. Jude / Danny Thomas analogy: the founder's story scales.
- Family stories are the most distinctive content: 6 min average time on a family story page (4x site average), 6x YouTube views versus other content, 10.1% click rate on a post-gala email with family photos.
- 360 Miracle: 290+ members (14 in 2020), $274K revenue in FY2025, 20x member growth, 7K+ email subscribers. Request: put 360 on the website.
- Five audiences, one story: individual donors, corporate sponsors, the 360 community, hospital partners, volunteers.
- One voice: the mission is worded slightly differently across the brand guide, homepage and meta description. Pillars is the differentiator (Autumn spent the most time on it). Volunteers describe the mission only broadly.
- Draft positioning: "For those who want their generosity to mean something real, Miracles for Kids is the only children's health nonprofit that stays with critically ill families from crisis through long-term stability, because for over two decades, one founder has treated every family's emergency as personally as her own."
- Website implications: family stories become the homepage; 360 joins the site and funnel; one brand voice, codified; a Pillars spotlight; conversion tracking (48,000 annual visitors).
- Plan: Phase 1 Foundation (website strategy, brand messaging, IA, tracking, email authentication; deliverable: wireframes and a messaging guide), Phase 2 Build, Phase 3 Launch and Grow. No visual identity phase.
- Appendix: email authentication. MFK emails stopped reaching Gmail in Feb 2026.

- Charity Navigator 93% matches the "4-star" in C13.
- The "only children's health nonprofit…" positioning is a stronger comparative claim than C1. It needs MFK approval.

## Why it matters for the brand pitch
- The April review already established brand messaging as Phase 1, but scoped it as words only (a messaging guide). Our ask, "let's look at your brand, not just your site," extends that to the visual brand (color, type, shape, imagery, logo), on top of work MFK has already accepted.
- It backs our direction with MFK's own evidence: stories first (6 min, 6x), one voice, Pillars, 360.
- It adds a pillar we haven't used: Autumn as the brand's soul, and the founder story at scale.
