## 10. Brand, tone & voice

- **Current brand assets (S3, S5):** script "Miracles" wordmark with a blocky light-blue "FOR KIDS"; magenta, navy and light blue; program icons; real family photography mixed with Adobe Stock. No MFK brand guidelines were supplied (Q5).
- **Tone direction (S1, S2):** childlike, whimsical, inspirational, warm, hopeful, never condescending. Not like a bank, health plan, SaaS product or "vibecoded."
- **System direction (S1, decided):** follow Gillette's color *philosophy* (about 6 colors, fixed ratios, white dominant, at most 3 brand colors per layout, a combinations chart, body type in navy or white). Use graphic shapes derived from MFK's own mark, with rules. The primary face is a sans serif that is geometric, rounded or friendly, "interesting yet legible." Gillette's actual colors, fonts and shapes are excluded.
- **Brand system structure (Andres, 2026-10-16: "read gillette brand, add branding elements in the same way").** The small MFK brand system mirrors the *structure* of the Gillette Children's Brand Guidelines 2022, with MFK's own content. Elements, in Gillette's order:
  1. **Brand house:** vision, mission, purpose, positioning, target, benefit, differentiators, personality, values. Built from MFK's sources; no invented values (MFK hasn't published a values list).
  2. **Boilerplate:** one approved paragraph about MFK.
  3. **Voice & tone:** about 4 traits, each with "Including / But not" lists and 2–3 example lines.
  4. **Writing style guide:** person-first language (child before diagnosis), naming rules (Miracles for Kids / MFK, program names), numbers and claims rules.
  5. **Logo usage:** primary and secondary lockups, color versions (full, one-color, reversed on photo), anatomy, clearspace, minimum size. Uses the existing logo; no redesign (Q5).
  6. **Brand architecture:** masterbrand, programs (Lifeline, Safe Haven, Balance, Boost, Pillars), 360° Miracle, events (Basket and Box of Miracles, Night of Miracles), partner co-branding.
  7. **Color:** palette with primary, secondary and tints; ratio; approved combinations; type-color rules.
  8. **Typography:** primary and accent faces, alternates (web-safe), hierarchy.
  9. **Photography:** lifestyle, program and moment photos; rules for dignity and consent.
  10. **Icons:** program icons redrawn on one grid, with solid and line sets.
  11. **Graphic shapes:** a shape family derived from MFK's mark, with application rules.
  12. **Grid system.**
  13. **Application examples:** the landing page, plus a few collateral moments (social post, donation card, event card) as design only.
  Not copied: Gillette's emoji system (internal use), lower thirds and presentation template unless asked.
- **Visual references Andres likes (S6, SOURCE STRATEGY · agency, 2026-10-16): "we like those colors and layouts."** The children's charity board (Figma MFK-DRaft 5:25) is the colour and layout direction:
  - **Make-A-Wish** (wish.org): saturated royal blue with coral CTAs, big photo heroes with the child's own words as a caption, stats as colored blocks in sentences, star motif.

### 16.2 Strategy improvement
Method: `shared/frameworks/brief-strategy.md`, applied lightly; Barry base. Everything here is DERIVED.

- **Observation:** MFK's work is unglamorous and recurring: bills up to $500/month, rent, groceries, counseling (C2, C5, C6). The families have about $31K incomes (C7). Stories on MFK's own pages are about a car payment, a snack at home, siblings at the beach (S3).
- **Interpretation:** what a donor buys isn't a cure or a miracle in the medical sense. It's the ordinary life a family keeps while a child is in treatment.
- **Insight:** when a child gets critically ill, the household falls apart before anything else, and the child loses a childhood along with their health. MFK protects the part of life that medicine doesn't treat.
- **Tension:** "Miracle" sounds like a cure, but MFK's miracles look like a paid electric bill. The brand name and the work seem at odds until the name is redefined.
- **Proposition (one plain sentence):** Your monthly gift pays a family's grown-up bills so their sick child can still be a kid.
- **Competitor check:** a hospital foundation can't own this, because it funds medicine. Make-A-Wish owns the one big wish, and Ronald McDonald House owns a place to stay. Monthly bills plus housing plus counseling (C1) is MFK's lane. Medium-strong ownability, pending confirmation of C1's wording.
- **Desired response:** *Think:* "I didn't know a sick kid's family could lose their home over bills." *Feel:* warmth and a little delight, not pity. *Do:* start a monthly gift.
- **Why this fixes the pushback:** emotion comes from specific daily-life moments (the Gillette lesson); vibrancy comes from the child's world; trust comes from the blunt, plain adult world, which is the opposite of the bank tone.
