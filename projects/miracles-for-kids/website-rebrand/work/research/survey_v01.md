## Why a survey

It reaches many more donors, workers, staff and families at low cost and without a moderator. Its scales measure **how much** people prefer each site. A handful of 30-minute interviews then explains **why**.

## Tool

- **Recommended: Lyssna** (formerly UsabilityHub). It has built-in 5-second tests, first-click tests, preference tests and randomization, so we can measure "easy to find events or donate" without moderators.
- **Free alternative: Google Forms**, in the work Workspace. It handles ratings and preference questions with screenshots, but has no click tracking or timing.

## Who and how many

- Aim for **at least 30 people per group** (donors, workers and volunteers, staff, families), so the groups can be compared. That's around 120 or more in total.
- Distribute through:
  - MFK's donor email list
  - the volunteer list
  - an internal staff link
  - a family-liaison link for families (optional, with no giving questions)
- Before the survey starts, show the NDA notice and require a consent checkbox.
- First screen asks the participant's group, which routes families past the giving questions.

## Structure (about 10 minutes)

Everyone rates the **live site (E) first**. Then A, W and J appear in **randomized order**, which the tool handles.

Participants only ever see "Site 1, 2, 3, 4." There are no names, and no "new" or "old."

### Part 1: About you (30 seconds)
1. How are you connected to Miracles for Kids? *Donor · Volunteer or event worker · Staff · Family we've served · Other*
2. Device: *Phone · Computer · Tablet*

### Part 2: Blind fonts and colors (1.5 minutes)

Run this before any site is shown, so photos and copy can't sway the answers.

3. **Fonts.** Show four type boards, each with the same sentence in a different site's fonts, black on white, in random order.
   - "Which feels most like a charity that helps families?" *(pick one)*
   - "Which is easiest to read?" *(pick one)*
4. **Colors.** Show four palette boards, each as proportioned swatches, in random order.
   - "Which feels most hopeful and trustworthy?" *(pick one)*
   - "Which feels least right for this charity?" *(pick one)*

### Part 3: Each site (about 1.5 minutes per site, 6 minutes total)

Repeat this block for E, then A, W and J in random order.

5. **5-second test.** Show the top of the page for 5 seconds.
   - "What three words describe it?" *(open text)*
6. **First-click tasks** on a full-page screenshot. *(Lyssna; in Google Forms, ask "Where would you click?" as a multiple-choice question)*
   - "Where would you click to **donate**?"
   - "Where would you click to find an **upcoming event**?"
   - "Where would you click to learn about a **family's story** or watch a **video**?"
7. **Ratings** on a 7-point scale, from *Strongly disagree* to *Strongly agree*:

| Code | Statement |
|---|---|
| S1 | This feels like Miracles for Kids. |
| S2 | I would trust this organization with a donation. |
| S3 | The text is easy to read. |
| S4 | The colors suit the organization. |
| S5 | It would be easy to find what I'm looking for. |
| S6 | It invites me to give without pressuring me. *(not shown to families)* |

8. **Brand scales**, 7-point semantic differential:

| Left | ← 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 → | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Cluttered | | Clean |
| Plain | | Elegant |
| Corporate | | Human |
| Distant | | Friendly |

### Part 4: Compare (1.5 minutes)

All four screenshots appear side by side.

9. "Which site best shows the mission?" *Show the mission text.* *(pick one)*
10. "If Miracles for Kids could launch only one new site, which should it be?" *(pick from A, W or J)*
11. "Compared with Site 1 [the live site], your pick is: *Much worse · Worse · About the same · Better · Much better*"
12. "In a sentence, why?" *(open text, optional)*

### Part 5: Thanks
- Optional: "Would you join a 20-minute follow-up chat?" *(email field)*
- Families get a thank-you.

## What we'll report

| Question | Measure |
|---|---|
| Is a new site better than the live one? | Mean S1–S6 and brand-scale scores for each new site against E, by paired comparison for each respondent, plus Q11 |
| Which new design is preferred? | Share of Q10 votes, overall and by group |
| Fonts | Q3 votes, then S3 in context |
| Colors | Q4 votes, then S4 in context |
| Ease of use | First-click success for donate, events and families on each site, plus S5 |
| Clean and elegant, yet human and friendly | The four brand scales for each site. The target is high on **all four** together |
| Values and mission fit | S1, Q9 |
| Giving without pressure | S6 |
| The why | Q5 words as word clouds per site; Q12 themes; follow-up interviews |

**Reading the numbers**
- Because everyone rates E, each new site can be compared with E within the same respondent. That's a fair test of "better than the live site."
- Report results by group as well as overall. If donors and families disagree, that matters more than the overall average.
- With about 30 or more per group, differences of roughly 0.5 points on the 7-point scale are worth acting on. Smaller ones are noise unless the interviews back them up.

## Watch-outs

- **Screenshots.** Lyssna works from screenshots, so animation, the donation widget's motion and the video don't come through. Give a "visit the site" link as an optional extra, or cover the interactions in the interviews.
- **Order.** E always comes first, by your choice. Randomizing A, W and J protects comparisons between the new sites, but every new site still gets the "after E" effect.
- **Staff and families know the live site,** so expect them to rate it from memory. Compare their results with donors'.
- **Length.** Keep it to 10 minutes, or dropout climbs. If needed, show each person E plus two of the three new sites, randomized. That brings it to about 7 minutes.
- **No real giving.** Screenshots only. Nobody reaches a payment page.
