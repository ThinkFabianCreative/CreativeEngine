# MFK brand presentation — discovery addendum v02

CLIENT: Miracles for Kids. PROJECT: website-rebrand. DATE: 2026-10-18.
STATUS: Proposed internal presentation copy. Live observations supplied by the parent agent from its October 18 discovery. Design rationale is qualitative; no engagement or conversion uplift has been established. Copy supplements v01 and supersedes its palette/type statements where the direction has changed.

## Discovery — A warmer system starts with what is already there.

The current website already uses program colors, family photography and handwritten accents. The proposal builds on that recognition. It gives type, color and imagery clearer roles, then applies those rules across the homepage and mobile experience. The change is a more coherent expression of MFK's existing character.

## Current type — The ingredients are familiar. The roles can be clearer.

The live site uses Open Sans for everyday reading and Jenna Sue for handwritten expression. Body copy is 16px at weight 300; sampled headings are 32px or 42px at weight 300. Program headings combine colored script with black category labels. The proposal makes hierarchy and emphasis more consistent.

Token labels: Body / Open Sans / 16 / 300. Heading samples / Open Sans / 32–42 / 300. Accent / Jenna Sue. Program headings / 19px.

## Before and after — The same mission, easier to follow.

The current page introduces MFK through several visual treatments. The proposed page brings them under one idea: everyday life kept intact. Family stories appear early, programs follow a shared format and Donate remains the primary action. The comparison shows intended clarity; usability testing will establish how visitors respond.

## Logo — Give the signature room to be read.

The current navigation displays the logo at approximately 99 × 60px. At that scale, the fine script and small stacked lettering appear delicate. Preserve the familiar mark, give it clear space and test larger or simplified approved applications at real device sizes. This is a visual assessment, not a legibility study.

Presenter note: source image asset is 240 × 146px. Asset dimensions do not alone establish display quality or justify a logo redesign. Obtain approved vector artwork where available.

## Palette evolution — Keep recognition. Define the relationships.

MFK already assigns a color to each program. We retain green, magenta, teal and sky blue, while proposing yellow in place of Boost's orange. Shared neutral text gives these surfaces a consistent reading voice. Program names and icons reinforce identity so color never has to carry the meaning alone.

Palette labels:
- Lifeline: green #31B744, retained.
- Safe Haven: magenta #A92061, retained.
- Balance: teal #27BBC9, retained.
- Boost: orange #F99400 → proposed yellow #FFD23F.
- Pillars: sky #79BDE9, retained.
- Reading text: charcoal #242424; secondary gray #595959; reversed white #FFFFFF, subject to contrast checks on each surface.

## Yellow and magenta — Warmth with a clear hierarchy.

Yellow gives Boost and selected highlights a brighter, lighter presence beside magenta. Magenta remains both Safe Haven's program color and the shared primary-action color. A Donate label, button shape and consistent placement distinguish the action. Program identity comes from the complete combination of name, icon and color.

Presenter note: this is a proposed palette relationship, not evidence that yellow increases optimism or magenta improves conversion. Avoid claiming magenta belongs exclusively to either Donate or Safe Haven.

## Font choice — Keep the handwriting. Strengthen the reading voice.

Anago is proposed for everyday reading and interface text, with Playpen Sans reserved for brief accents. Their roles give the page warmth without asking handwriting to carry long passages. Bogart with Anago remains an editorial alternative. Final selection should follow actual-size checks for hierarchy, spacing and mobile reflow.

## Five programs — One system, five clear reasons to care.

### Lifeline — Help with the bills that keep arriving.

Lifeline's green stays recognizable. The story leads with practical support: up to $500 a month toward household bills. A plain-language description and shared row structure make the offer easy to understand.

### Safe Haven — A place for the family to stay.

Safe Haven keeps magenta and centers the housing itself. Show the place and the people it supports. Its name and housing icon distinguish the program from the shared magenta Donate action.

### Balance — Support for the hard days.

Balance retains teal and makes counseling and family wellness visible. Photography should show connection and participation. The program description explains the support without promising recovery or a particular mental health outcome.

### Boost — Everyday essentials, brought into focus.

Boost's proposed yellow distinguishes food, clothing and essentials within the system. Show the practical contents and the people preparing them. The program name and icon preserve clarity as the palette evolves.

### Pillars — Help with what comes next.

Pillars retains sky blue and introduces employment, financial literacy and education referrals in plain language. Its visual identity should suggest opportunity without implying guaranteed work, income or a specific outcome for families.

## Text and accessibility — Let color support the reading.

Proposed text uses charcoal, gray or white, selected for each background. Color remains on surfaces and icons, supported by visible names and labels. Grayscale text is a design choice. Accessibility still requires contrast checks, recognizable states, keyboard access and testing at different screen sizes and text settings.

## Measurement note — Be precise about what has been checked.

The earlier audit inspected 89 visible text nodes in mobile frame 39:136 and found a minimum measured solid-fill contrast of 5.55:1 after repairs. That result does not certify this revised palette, new typography or other frames. Recheck the chosen design and its interactive states before implementation approval.

### Copy and claim flags

- Retain “up to” with $500 and confirm the current amount before publication.
- Existing program color coding is an observed feature, not an invention of the proposal.
- The five program case descriptions explain design intentions and source-stated service categories; they imply no measured outcome.
- Do not describe grayscale text as a WCAG requirement. Gray #595959 is not universally accessible; test every pairing and state.
- Do not characterize the existing logo as proven illegible, or its raster source as inherently defective.
- “Easier to follow” is a proposed communication objective in the headline; the slide body expressly states testing remains outstanding.
