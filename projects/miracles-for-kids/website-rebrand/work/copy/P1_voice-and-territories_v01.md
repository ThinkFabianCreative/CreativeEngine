### Voice & tone: four traits

**1. Plainspoken**
- Including: honest, direct, concrete, numerate
- But not: blunt to the point of cold, clinical, grim, preachy
- Write about real things: bills, rent, groceries, the car payment. Use the number, not the adjective.
- *"Rent's due on the first. Chemo doesn't care."*
- *"We pay the bills. Up to $500 a month, every month."*

**2. Warm**
- Including: kind, close, personal, family-sized
- But not: sentimental, pitying, saccharine, sanctimonious
- Write about the small moments of ordinary life a family keeps: a snack at home, a ride to treatment, a birthday.
- *"Dinner at the table. Same as last Tuesday."*
- *"Somebody's got this. So you can hold her hand."*

**3. Kid-hearted**
- Including: playful, curious, light, a little silly
- But not: childish, cutesy, jokey about illness, condescending
- The child's world gets the color and the fun. Let kids speak in their own words, never words written for them.
- *"I wish my mom didn't have to worry about the lights." (placeholder, real quotes only)*
- *"Surf camp. Sand in everything. Worth it."*

**4. Steady**
- Including: reliable, monthly, here, accountable
- But not: corporate, institutional, salesy, urgent for the sake of urgency
- Show up the way MFK does, every month. Proof is calm and sourced.
- *"Every month since [year]."*
- *"Audited every year. Open to anyone who asks."*

**Say:** "families with a critically-ill child" · "your monthly gift" · "pay the bills" · "a kid being a kid"
**Avoid:** "vital relief," "stability," "secure," "impact by the numbers," "solutions," "empower," "journey," "warriors," "battle" used as decoration, and any pity framing ("poor," "suffering," "victims")

### Writing style guide (starter)

- **Person-first language.** "Dailani, 2, who is fighting cancer," never "cancer patient Dailani." The child's name and age come before the diagnosis.
- **Real words only.** Quotes from children and parents are real and consented. Never write a line and attribute it to a child.
- **Names.** Miracles for Kids on first reference; MFK after that only in internal or casual contexts. Program names are capitalized: Lifeline, Safe Haven, Balance, Boost, Pillars, Miracle Manor, Basket of Miracles, Box of Miracles, 360° Miracle.
- **Numbers.** Numerals for amounts and counts ($500, 12 families). Every public number needs a dated source (claim ledger). "Up to" stays where MFK uses it.
- **"Miracle."** Use it for what MFK actually does (the paid bill, the kept home), never for a cure or a medical outcome.
- **Headlines.** Sentence case, short, with a period when it's a statement.

## Part B: landing page territories

Four genuinely different routes. Each gives the idea, the hero, and how it carries through the page. The page structure (from the brief) is: hero with donation card → the truth → the five programs → family stories → proof → other ways to help → events → footer. Copy for each section is sketched only in the lead territory for each route.

### T1: "The miracle is a paid electric bill."
**Idea:** redefine MFK's own word. The miracle isn't a cure; it's ordinary life kept intact. It honors Autumn's name instead of dropping it.
- **Hero:** *Some miracles look like a paid electric bill.*
  Sub: *When a child is critically ill, the bills don't stop. We pay them, up to $500 a month, so families can focus on their kid.*
  CTA: *Give monthly*
- **Device:** each program is shown as a "receipt" with a stamp: *PAID. Rent, March.* *PAID. Groceries, this week.* *PAID. Counseling, Thursday.*
- **Stories header:** *Small miracles. Every month.*
- **Close:** *Be the reason the lights stay on.*
- **Risk:** the "paid bill" is less emotional than a child's face. It needs the photo to carry the child's world beside it.

### T2: "We handle the bills. They handle being kids."
**Idea:** a division of labor. Two registers stated outright: the adult column and the kid column.
- **Hero:** *We handle the bills. They handle being kids.*
  Sub: *Miracles for Kids pays the rent, the lights and the groceries for families with a critically-ill child, so childhood keeps happening.*
  CTA: *Join us monthly*
- **Device:** split modules. Left (plain, navy): *Rent, $1,140: handled.* Right (bright, photo, kid quote): *Birthday party, Saturday: handled.*
- **Programs header:** *Five ways we keep a family's life running.*
- **Close:** *You handle one thing: $[25] a month.*
- **Risk:** "handle being kids" can read flip about kids in treatment. Photos have to show real joy, not just health.

### T3: "Childhood, uninterrupted."
**Idea:** the quietest and most ownable. Illness interrupts everything; MFK keeps the rest of life going. Implied, never stated.
- **Hero:** *Childhood, uninterrupted.* over a kid mid-play (round 2's running boy)
  Sub: *Treatment interrupts a lot. We make sure rent, dinner and a little fun aren't on the list.*
  CTA: *Keep a childhood going*
- **Device:** "uninterrupted" as a running line through the page: *Birthdays, uninterrupted. Dinner, uninterrupted. Home, uninterrupted.*
- **Close:** *Help a kid stay a kid.*
- **Risk:** elegant but quiet. It could feel brand-y rather than urgent; it needs the waitlist line nearby for urgency.

### T4: "Somebody's got this."
**Idea:** the parent's relief. Told from the parent's side: the moment a parent realizes they don't have to carry everything alone. The most emotional, warmest route.
- **Hero:** *Somebody's got the bills. You've got her.*
  Sub: *For families with a critically-ill child, we cover the rent, the lights, the groceries and the hard days, so parents can be parents.*
  CTA: *Be somebody, monthly*
- **Device:** "Somebody's got…" as a repeatable line for each program: *Somebody's got the rent (Lifeline). Somebody's got a home near CHOC (Safe Haven). Somebody's got your back on the hard days (Balance).*
- **Close:** *Be somebody.*
- **Risk:** "somebody" is anonymous; it must land on the donor ("be somebody") or it gives MFK no credit.

### Lines worth keeping across routes
- *Helping the families who don't have time to ask for help.* (Jordan's footer line, the best line from his rounds)
- *Rent's due on the first. Chemo doesn't care.* (a manifesto or truth-section opener)
- *Every family is referred by a children's hospital. Every dollar is audited.* (proof, pending C4 and audit wording)
- *[6+] families are waiting. Your monthly gift moves one off the list.* (urgency, pending C3)

### Writer's view (not a verdict)
T1 and T4 are the strongest. T1 owns the brand word and the bill truth no competitor can take. T4 has the most heart and the most whimsy room. A likely combination for DEVELOP is **T1's idea with T4's warmth**: lead with the redefined miracle, carry it through the page with "somebody's got…" moments. T3 is the most elegant fallback. T2 is the most literal; it's fine but expected.

### Ways into the programs (the landing page supports the existing content)

1. **Program grid:** five cards, one per program, each with its miracle name, one line, a real photo and a link to its existing page.
2. **Story tags:** every story card and story page shows the programs that helped that family ("Lifeline · Safe Haven"), each a link to the program page. This is the main bridge: story → program → gift.
3. **Truth section:** "How we help" leads to the programs overview.
4. **Nav:** a "Programs" menu with the five miracle names plus the real program names (for example "The lights-on miracle · Lifeline").
5. **Footer:** the five programs listed.

Rule: the miracle name is the hook; the real program name always appears beside it, so the existing pages still make sense when people arrive.
