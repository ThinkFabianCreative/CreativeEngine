## 01 — Miracles look like a normal Tuesday.

A proposed brand system for Miracles for Kids.

Rent paid. Lights on. Dinner on the table. The everyday things a family needs while their child is in treatment. This direction makes that work visible through warm photography, expressive type and a clear invitation to help.

## 02 — Build the brand around the work.

MFK describes five programs: Lifeline, Safe Haven, Balance, Boost and Pillars. Lifeline provides up to $500 a month toward household bills. Families receiving financial assistance are pre-screened and referred by children's hospitals. Those specifics give the brand a concrete story to tell.

Source note: MFK program and Lifeline pages; source-stated, checked October 17, 2026. Confirm current amount before publication.

## 03 — Keep the warmth. Give it direction.

Jordan's second direction brings real children, joyful photography and welcome energy. We keep that progress. The proposal gives it a more specific headline, one clear Donate action and shared rules for color and emphasis. Each section now contributes to the same story about everyday support.

Visual annotation: “Help families in crisis to find Miracles” → “Miracles look like a normal Tuesday.” Heart Donate + Take Action → one primary Donate button.

## 04 — A miracle you can picture.

“Miracle” becomes tangible when it means a bill paid or a meal at home. That gives MFK a repeatable creative idea, grounded in its services. Family stories provide the human detail; plain language explains what support can do. The proposed tone is hopeful, specific and respectful.

## 05 — Every color has a job.

Navy anchors text and structure. Magenta signals the primary action. Sky blue supports secondary surfaces; yellow highlights a moment of warmth. Cream gives photographs and copy room to breathe. Assigning roles keeps the palette recognizable while allowing each section a different rhythm.

Swatch labels: Navy #002855 · Magenta #A92061 · Sky #79BDE9 · Yellow #FFD23F · Cream #FBF6EE.

## 06 — A clear voice, with a human accent.

Anago is the proposed everyday voice for headlines, body copy and interface labels. Playpen Sans adds a handwritten accent to selected words. Bogart with Anago offers an editorial alternative for longer stories. A small set of shared type roles keeps expression deliberate and reading comfortable.

Production note: Adobe web project supplied; desktop font activation remains required for the unavailable Figma families.

## 07 — Friendly shapes. A consistent language.

Soft blobs, arches and rounded bursts give the system a playful rhythm. Photo masks accommodate 4:3 imagery and protect faces; hearts stay out of photo crops. Gentle waves connect sections. Repeating a small shape family makes the page feel related from its first screen to its last.

## 08 — Let families be themselves.

Photography should show relationships, play and ordinary moments, with the child's dignity intact. Use approved MFK imagery and consented stories, with captions that explain what we are seeing. Borrowed prototype photographs are placeholders and must be replaced before public use. The families carry the emotion.

## 09 — Expression with a purpose.

Material 3 Expressive informs the connected story filters and compact program rows. Surface color establishes hierarchy; subtle elevation identifies actionable cards. Quotes and broad sections remain flat. These shared rules help visitors recognize choices while leaving room for MFK's photography, typography and shapes to lead.

## 10 — Make room for the next story.

On mobile, family stories form a horizontal rail, programs become compact rows and secondary content takes less vertical space. Donate remains the primary action. The intention is to make stories easier to reach and the page easier to scan; implementation testing will confirm scrolling and keyboard behavior.

## 12 — Approve the direction. Resolve the details.

Confirm the creative idea, palette and primary type pairing. Before publication, approve family imagery, validate claims and legal details, and confirm logo permissions. The next build should test responsive behavior, keyboard access and donation journeys.
