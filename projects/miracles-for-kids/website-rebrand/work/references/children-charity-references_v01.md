## Andres's brief on the board (paraphrased)

Bold type, strong photography, playful and whimsical shapes and icons, rounded friendly UI, handwritten elements. Warm, inviting voice that is compelling and hopeful, **never condescending**. Starlight AU's story grid is flagged "Cool Grid."

## Patterns across the set

1. **The child is the hero, as a person and not a patient.** Kids playing, running, being silly. The illness is named in the caption, not shown in the image. (Jordan's round 2 running kid fits this. Round 1's staff-at-desk doesn't.)
2. **Each headline carries one human tension.** None says "vital relief" or "stability." Every one is a short sentence a parent might say.
3. **Whimsy has a single source.** Each brand owns one playful device (Wish: stars; Starlight: the star and handwriting; Hyundai: hand-drawn wings and confetti; RMHC: the heart-house). They don't use five at once. Jordan's round 2 used all of them.
4. **Bold flat color blocks with real photos, not gradients.** It's the opposite of the "vibecoded" navy glass look.
5. **Proof is turned into story:** numbers in sentences, sourced ratios, rating badges styled as design.
6. **A specific ask** with amounts that name what the money does.

## Implications for MFK (feed into the brief)

- MFK's whimsy device should come from its own mark. The script "Miracles" is the obvious candidate, used only for **the family's or the child's own voice** (handwritten), never as decoration on random words.
- The emotion comes from copy that turns the grown-up problem MFK takes away into the childhood that gets kept. Ronald McDonald House proves that territory works for a family-support charity. MFK's version is about **money and the household**, which is more specific than RMHC's (a place to stay).
- Treat Hyundai Hope on Wheels as a regional competitor in look as well as for donors. Avoid yellow-plus-cut-out-kid as the only idea, or MFK reads as its neighbor.
- Not yet checked: whether MFK has photo consent to feature kids prominently and name their diagnoses in captions.
