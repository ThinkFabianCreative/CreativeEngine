# Among the Hunted — VFX / Motion Graphics Brief

*Source: "Among the Hunted" pitch deck (Gia Chacón & Anthony Trimino). Prepared 2026-11-05.*

## Elevator pitch

**Among the Hunted** is an investigative documentary by Gia Chacón and Anthony Trimino. Its argument: when ISIS killed Christians and Yazidis in Iraq and Syria, the world called it genocide. In Nigeria, an ISIS affiliate has spent more than a decade killing, abducting and driving Christians from their land, and the same violence gets called something else.

Filmed in camps for displaced survivors in Abuja, the film follows survivors who have lived in displacement for over twelve years. Three survivors of persecution carry the story, with Gia (investigator) and Anthony (in the field) alongside. It measures their testimony and the perpetrators' own record against the legal definition of genocide. It also follows the powers behind relabeling it as "farmer-herder clashes," "banditry" or "poverty," and asks what that language lets the world avoid. Then it puts the question to the people with power to act.

**Tone:** urgent, investigative, in the vein of Netflix's *American Manhunt*. Survivor testimony, archival news, perpetrator propaganda and interviews with policymakers, cut with momentum.

## Look references

Two references, one system: ***American Manhunt*** (Netflix) for the investigative look, and ***Explained*** (Vox / Netflix) for how information is taught on screen.

**From American Manhunt (the investigation):**
- **Org-chart dossier.** Photocopied, high-contrast B&W portraits on distressed paper. Typewriter name labels with underline and role ("LEADER — ...") and boxed connector lines. A "?" box for unknowns. Handwritten pencil notes ("Second in command"), loose circled groups, scribbled arrows. Use it for perpetrator structure (Boko Haram / ISWAP leadership), built only from public, verified identifications.
- **Evidence table.** Real objects photographed top-down on a dark surface: documents, photos, passports, case labels. Use it for chapter openers built from our own evidence (documents, maps, field notes). No weapons staged as props.
- **Red marker circle.** A thick, layered hand-drawn red circle over a halftone portrait. This is the film's signature mark. Use it on perpetrators and evidence, **never on a survivor**.
- **Texture:** grain, photocopy noise, ink bleed, flicker, letterboxed 2.39:1 framing.

**From Vox Explained (the teaching):**
- Highlighter sweep on document text (the Genocide Convention device).
- Maps with callout lines; numbers that build with a source line always on screen.
- Steps animated at 12 fps for a hand-made feel.

**How they combine:** Manhunt is the default surface (dossier, evidence, red marks). Vox moments explain the law, the numbers and the geography inside that surface, on the same paper and in the same palette. Survivors are never treated as evidence-board items. Their portraits stay clean and dignified, with consent.

Reference frames and a mood board are on the FigJam story wall ("Explainer style — references").

## Hard rules

1. **Protect people.** Never show camp coordinates, routes or anything that locates the camps more precisely than "Abuja." Blur or omit minors and anyone not cleared on consent. No identifying details on survivors without a signed release.
2. **Sourced numbers only.** Every statistic, date and quote on screen ships with source and date. Production supplies a locked fact sheet; don't source figures independently.
3. **Propaganda is evidence, not spectacle.** No re-staged violence, no glorifying treatment of flags or footage. Graphic material is cleared by the producers before use.
4. **"Genocide" is the film's argument.** On-screen text presents it as the case being made, attributed and tested against the definition. Final wording follows legal review.
