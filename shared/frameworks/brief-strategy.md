# Brief Strategy

How BriefMachine writes, sharpens and reviews the strategy inside a brief: insight, tension, proposition and desired response. It produces briefs that are credible enough to trust, focused enough to guide and interesting enough to inspire. CreativeDirector keeps readiness and creative judgment.

## Where it sits

- **Normalization comes first.** `brief-normalization.md` owns intake, provenance, the claim ledger, questions and the schema. This framework starts after it and repeats none of its rules.
- **It runs only on request.** A request to write, optimize or strengthen strategy authorizes the DERIVED strategy-improvement layer (schema §16.2). A request to normalize or extract does not. In review mode, diagnose and recommend; do not implement strategic changes.
- **Barry is the base.** Barry's brief rules are CreativeEngine's default (`shared/knowledge/barry-concept-book-reference.md`): one benefit, credible support, one defined person, the real problem, the binary choices. The three lenses below sharpen that base; they do not replace it.
- **No creative output.** No territories, lines, headlines, scripts or visual concepts. BriefMachine defines the problem and direction; the departments find the expression.
- **Current client only.** Carry source, claim, question and pattern IDs through the work.

The three lenses are an original synthesis informed by Jon Steel, Howard Ibach and Mark Pollard: interpretive adaptations, not quotations or official frameworks. Apply the principles; do not perform a persona.

## Three lenses

### 1. Human understanding (informed by Jon Steel)

Look for the behavior, circumstance, contradiction or unmet need that makes the communication problem matter to a person. Connect evidence to a creative opportunity through interpretation, not by summarizing everything collected.

Keep three steps apart:

- **Observation:** what the evidence actually shows.
- **Interpretation:** what it may mean, with its basis and confidence.
- **Implication:** what the communication could address because of it.

An insight changes how the problem is understood and has a consequence for communication. An interesting fact, a demographic label, a universal aspiration or an evocative sentence is not enough on its own. A functional or situational insight may beat an emotional one. Do not manufacture tension.

A vivid anecdote is not automatically representative. State who the evidence concerns, its limits, plausible alternative explanations, and what would weaken the interpretation. Evidence levels for audience claims are in `brief-normalization.md` §12: targeting alone never establishes a human truth.

### 2. Focus (informed by Howard Ibach)

Reduce the assignment to the decisions a creative team needs. Objective, audience, tension, proposition, desired response and support must form one coherent chain. Every element earns its place by changing the work or protecting a real requirement.

For one creative problem, find one organizing proposition: the central meaning the communication should establish, not a tagline. Say why the audience should care and which eligible proof supports it.

Do not force incompatible audiences, objectives or decision stages into one proposition. Recommend separate problem IDs and packets when the communication jobs differ; do not split merely because the formats differ. A filled template is not evidence of a sound strategy.

### 3. Precision (informed by Mark Pollard)

Use writing to test the thinking. Describe a specific problem, a revealing insight and a strategic choice in language a creative team understands at once. Prefer concrete verbs and recognizable situations to abstractions such as "engagement", "empowerment" or "innovation".

Write the reasoning plainly before compressing it. A striking line cannot validate an insight: restate the strategy in ordinary language, and if its logic disappears, fix the thinking. Familiar but accurate thinking beats invented provocation.

## Claim strictness follows the mode

| Mode | Claims in strategy work |
|---|---|
| EXPLORE, territory work | Record each claim's ledger status next to the proposition. Do not soften or narrow the strategy for claim reasons; the client calls claims out. Client mandatories still apply |
| DEVELOP, once a route is chosen | Run the credibility check. Flag what the route depends on that is not substantiated |
| JUDGE, going to the client or MLR | Full credibility check. Unsupported claims are restricted or removed |

User direction for a project can loosen or tighten this. Record it with its date (BriefMachine `AGENT.md`, reviewer preferences).

## Working method

Scale the work to the assignment. A narrow revision does not need a full restart.

1. **Establish the job.** Client, project, mode, affected problem IDs. Separate the business outcome from the communication's contribution. Do not invent objectives, metrics, approvals or deliverables.
2. **Inspect the evidence.** Offering truths, audience evidence, proof, constraints, open conflicts. Keep supported findings, source hypotheses and your own interpretation apart. Missing substantiation stays missing.
3. **Map the category.** What does the category usually say, show and promise? Name the conventions in two or three lines, with their basis. This is what the proposition has to escape.
4. **Build the chain.** Real problem → audience understanding → tension → insight → proposition → desired response → support. If several directions are plausible, compare only the real alternatives, on evidence, relevance, brand credibility and creative usefulness. Never manufacture options for a quota.
5. **Sharpen.** Make the choice explicit, remove competing messages, use plain language. Say what the communication must achieve, never the creative solution.
6. **Challenge.** Apply the checks below. Repair what the evidence supports; expose the rest as questions or hypotheses. Do not keep polishing an unresolved premise.
7. **Hand over.** Record the derived changes, their basis, affected fields and packets, evidence limits and questions. CreativeDirector decides readiness. A strategy is not approved because it reads well.

Ask only the questions whose answers could change the direction, scope or permissible claims, and name exactly which problem each gap blocks. If no defensible insight exists, give an evidence-led provisional direction with its limits. Never invent research or call a hypothesis a human truth.

## Quality checks

Reasoned judgments, not scores or thresholds.

| Check | Question | If weak |
|---|---|---|
| Human grounding | Is the audience understanding specific, supported and relevant to the decision? | Narrow it, label the hypothesis, or name the research needed |
| Commercial relevance | Can the communication plausibly contribute to the objective? | Repair the causal link, or flag a problem advertising cannot solve |
| Focus | Can a creative name the primary job and the proposition? | Remove competing directions or recommend separate packets |
| Ownability | Could a competitor run this proposition with their name on it? Does it escape the category conventions mapped in step 3? | Find what only this offering can claim or credibly mean |
| Credibility | Can this offering deliver the meaning, with eligible support? Strictness per the mode table | Revise the proposition or restrict claims, as the mode allows |
| Strategic choice | Does the direction say what to prioritize, and why? | Replace generic ambition with an explicit choice |
| Creative usefulness | Can different executions serve it without the brief dictating one? | Add stimulus or remove execution prescriptions |
| Language integrity | Does the reasoning hold without stylish phrasing? | Fix the logic before the words |

## Output

### Strategy improvement (schema §16.2)

For each material proposal:

- the affected problem or field, and what the source currently establishes
- the proposed DERIVED framing
- its basis: source, claim, pattern and question IDs, and the reasoning in a few lines
- confidence and evidence limits (confidence never changes claim status)
- what it changes, what needs validation, and what it means for packets

Use only the elements the assignment needs: problem, audience, category conventions, tension, insight, proposition, desired response, support, creative stimulus questions, boundaries. Never fill a missing element with invented facts. The recommended direction is carried into the handoff's DERIVED fields (`brief-normalization.md` §14): PRIMARY TENSION, INSIGHT, PROPOSITION, DESIRED RESPONSE.

### Brief review

Lead with the strongest usable element, then the weaknesses with the greatest downstream consequence. For each: the evidence, the consequence for the creative work, a concrete correction. Keep writing problems, strategic problems and evidence gaps apart. Preserve unresolved conflicts; do not pick a convenient resolution.

### Handoff

Recommended direction if it is supportable, why it deserves development, remaining uncertainties, and the decision needed from CreativeDirector or the user. Use the verdict vocabulary only when the mode and the role allow it.
