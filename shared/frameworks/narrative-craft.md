# Narrative Craft

> CreativeEngine shared framework. Owner `word-smither`. Used by `design-champ` for film, motion and sequential formats, and by `creative-engine-director` in review.

## Provenance

In-house CreativeEngine framework. It is not derived from a single named book, screenwriting method or curriculum, and it reproduces no institution's course material. It synthesizes ordinary professional practice in dramatic writing as it applies to advertising.

- Do not attribute its shapes, beat counts, labels or tests to any named source or author.
- Do not imitate or recreate specific published commercials as ideas for a client.

## Purpose

`hey-whipple-copycraft.md` finds the thought and sharpens the line. This framework answers what happens when the work has to **unfold over time**:

```
IS THERE A STORY, OR ONLY A SEQUENCE OF EVENTS?
WHAT DOES SOMEONE WANT, AND WHAT IS IN THE WAY?
WHERE IS THE TURN?
WHAT DOES THE BRAND'S ENTRANCE DO TO THE MEANING?
DOES IT SURVIVE ITS SHORTEST CUT?
```

It covers scripts, film, social video, radio and audio, sequential formats (carousels, stories, scroll narratives) and long copy carrying a narrative.

It especially fights:

- events in order mistaken for a story
- voiceover narrating what the picture already shows
- a film that would work just as well for a competitor
- a 30 cut down to a 15 by deleting the middle
- emotion applied at the end rather than earned

## What this framework does not cover

- **Motion craft** — timing, easing, kinetic type, transitions, loops — belongs to the planned `animation-craft.md`. This framework covers what happens, not how it moves.
- **Format specifications** — durations, aspect ratios, caption limits, safe zones — come from verified knowledge in `shared/knowledge/`, never from here or from memory.
- **Object classification.** In `creative-object-taxonomy.md`, a film or script is an **execution** (§10) of a parent idea, not an object type of its own. A beautiful film of a weak idea is still a weak idea.

## 1. When not to tell a story

Story is a tool with a cost: it spends time before it delivers. Do not reach for it by default.

A story is probably **wrong** when:

- the idea is a single visual-verbal gap that lands instantly (`pricken-idea-mechanisms.md`)
- the demonstration *is* the idea, and narrative would only delay it
- the format gives you one beat (a six-second bumper, a static, a banner)
- the story would be a vehicle for a message that could simply be said better

A story is probably **right** when:

- the claim is easier to experience than to assert
- the audience holds a belief that has to be dismantled rather than contradicted
- the product's value only appears inside a situation
- feeling is the point, and the feeling needs duration

Deciding not to tell a story is a legitimate narrative decision. Record it as one.

## 2. The minimum story

```
SOMEONE WANTS SOMETHING.
SOMETHING IS IN THE WAY.
```

Without an obstacle there is no story, only a sequence. "A woman opens the app, books a table, enjoys dinner" is a demonstration wearing narrative clothes.

Before writing a frame:

```
WHO:       the person, specifically enough to picture
WANTS:     what they are trying to get, in plain words
OBSTACLE:  what is in the way
STAKES:    what it costs them if they don't get it
TURN:      the moment the situation, or its meaning, changes
BRAND:     what the brand has to do with any of it
```

If OBSTACLE is empty, stop. If BRAND is answered with "it appears at the end," see §5.

## 3. Story shapes

Seven shapes that work in advertising. Each has a job and a characteristic failure.

| Shape | How it works | Needs | Fails as |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem → escalation → resolution** | An obstacle worsens until the product or truth resolves it | Escalation that genuinely escalates | A complaint, then an ad |
| **Reversal** | The final beat retroactively changes what everything before it meant | A first reading that is complete, and wrong | A twist seen coming, or one that changes nothing |
| **Vignette chain** | Repetition with variation; many small units, no single arc | A rule strong enough to generate the next vignette | A list; a montage |
| **Held moment** | No plot. One situation, sustained, tension unresolved | Something genuinely at stake in the stillness | A mood film |
| **Demonstration as drama** | The product's function is the plot's resolution | A real obstacle the product actually defeats | A feature list with actors |
| **Testimony** | One voice, direct address; the interest is the speaker | A voice worth listening to, and specificity | A scripted person reciting brand copy |
| **Absurd escalation** | Comic logic pushed past the reasonable, consistently | Rules the absurdity obeys | Random events called funny |

Shapes are not genres, and they mix. Choose the one the idea needs, not the one the format usually gets.

## 4. The turn

The turn is the moment meaning changes. Most advertising narrative lives or dies here.

- **Every story needs one.** If nothing changes, it is a description.
- **Place it deliberately.** Too early and there is no tension left to change. Too late and there is no time to land what it meant, or to land the brand.
- **One turn, usually.** A second turn inside thirty seconds tends to erase the first.
- **The audience completes it.** A turn explained immediately afterwards has been spent twice (`hey-whipple-copycraft.md` §2).

Test: describe the piece to someone in two sentences and see whether the turn is what you naturally say second. If it doesn't survive the retelling, it isn't the structure — it's a detail.

## 5. Where the brand enters

When the brand arrives changes what the story *was*.

| Entry | Effect | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **As resolution** | The brand is what changes the situation | Feels bought if the obstacle was manufactured |
| **As revealed cause** | The brand was responsible all along; the reveal is the turn | Only works if the reveal reframes rather than merely informs |
| **As narrator** | The brand tells the story; voice is the brand | The voice has to be genuinely theirs, not generic warmth |
| **As participant** | The brand is in the world, not commenting on it | Easy to lose; needs a reason to be noticed |
| **As tag only** | The story runs; the brand signs it | The "whose ad was that?" failure |

Tag-only is the most common entry and the most dangerous. Apply the audit's ownability question (§4.3): **if the last four frames were replaced with a competitor's logo, would anything be wrong?** If not, the story is not this brand's, however good it is.

## 6. Time budgets

Duration is not a container. It is a constraint on how many beats can exist.

| Length | Beats available | What it can be |
|---|---|---|
| **6s** | One | A single turn, or one image with a line. Not a story |
| **15s** | Two to three | Setup and turn. One obstacle, stated fast, resolved once |
| **30s** | Four to five | Setup, obstacle, escalation, turn, brand. The standard dramatic unit |
| **60s+** | Six or more | Room for a character to be someone, and for a turn to be earned rather than announced |
| **Vertical / scroll** | One, then optional more | The first frame is the premise. Assume the audience leaves at any moment and never arrives at an ending |
| **Audio only** | Two to four | No picture to carry exposition. Voice and sound do everything |

**Vertical and scroll are not short film.** Nothing guarantees the ending is reached, so value must be delivered continuously rather than banked for a payoff. The hook is the first frame, not the first three seconds, and sound-off is the default state. Format craft for these lives in `.claude/skills/format-copywriting/`.

**Audio has no establishing shot.** Everything the listener needs about who, where and when must arrive through dialogue, sound or VO without sounding like exposition. Write for the ear: read it aloud, at pace, against a stopwatch.

## 7. Character

Advertising has no time to develop a character. It has time to make one specific.

- **Specificity beats relatability.** A person with one exact, slightly odd detail is more recognizable than a person constructed to be everybody. The everyman is nobody.
- **Behavior, not description.** What someone does in the first three seconds is who they are.
- **The want is visible.** The audience should be able to name what the character wants without being told it.
- **Casting is writing.** Casting, styling and location are arguments (`barry-concept-art-direction.md` §6), not dressing.

## 8. Dialogue, voiceover and silence

- **Dialogue is behavior.** People in ads state their feelings and their product benefits; people in life do neither. Write around the point.
- **Read it aloud, always.** Copy written to be read and then performed as speech is immediately detectable.
- **VO must add.** Voiceover describing the picture is the time-based form of the caption failure (`hey-whipple-copycraft.md` §2). Give VO what the picture cannot show: the interior, the contradiction, the time jump, the joke the image sets up.
- **Silence is a beat.** A held silent moment before a turn is usually stronger than a line, and costs less.
- **Count the words.** Roughly two to three words per second is comfortable; more is rushed. Write the 30 to fit, then cut ten percent.

## 9. Sound

- **Sound-on and sound-off are two different pieces.** Decide which is primary, then confirm the other still works. Supers are not a transcript; they are a second script, with their own reading order (`barry-concept-art-direction.md` §5).
- **Music carries meaning or covers a gap.** A track doing emotional work the story did not earn is a patch. Test the cut without music: if it collapses, the narrative is not there.
- **Sound design can be the turn.** A sound arriving before its source appears is one of the cheapest reveals available.
- Recording, mixing and audio production craft belong to execution, not to this framework.

## 10. Cutdowns and adaptation

A cutdown is a rewrite, not a deletion.

- **Decide which beat survives.** A 30 reduced to a 15 usually cannot keep setup, escalation and turn. Normally the turn survives and the setup is compressed or implied.
- **Write the short one first, at least once.** Writing the 6 and the 15 before the 60 reveals whether the idea needs duration or is merely using it.
- **Do not cut down to a trailer.** A 15 made of the 30's best images without its structure is a montage of a story, not a story.
- **Sequential adaptation is restructuring.** A film becoming a carousel loses time control: the audience sets the pace, and every frame must earn the next swipe.
- **Record which version is canonical**, so feedback attaches to the right one (`WordSmither/AGENT.md`, feedback and rounds).

## 11. Narrative clichés

Retrieved structures rather than found ones:

- **The improvement montage** — lives visibly getting better over rising music.
- **The diverse-faces sequence** — many people, one product, no idea.
- **The rhetorical-question opening** — "What if we told you…", "Ever wonder why…".
- **The problem-person** — an exaggeratedly incompetent human so the product can look capable.
- **The last-second wink** — an unearned joke after the logo, buying back charm.
- **Piano-and-slow-motion sincerity** — production signaling an emotion the story never created.
- **The reveal-it-was-an-ad** — the twist is that it is advertising.
- **The manufactured obstacle** — a problem that exists only so the product can solve it.
- **Voiceover as glue** — narration holding together shots that do not connect.

Warnings, not bans. A structure used knowingly and visibly turned can be the idea. Count each weakness once, under its most precise diagnosis.

## 12. By operating mode

| Mode | Use |
|---|---|
| **EXPLORE** | Try different shapes (§3) on the same truth — the same idea as reversal, as vignette chain, as held moment is three different pieces. Write premises and beat lists, not scripts. Do not apply cliché filters yet |
| **DEVELOP** | Fix the turn and its placement, the brand entry, the beat count against the duration, and VO against picture. Write the shortest cut to test whether the structure holds |
| **JUDGE** | Is there an obstacle? Does the turn survive retelling? Does the brand entry make the story theirs? Does it work sound-off, and in its shortest cut? Name clichés precisely |

## 13. Framework output

When useful:

```
SHAPE:        [problem-escalation / reversal / vignette / held moment / demonstration / testimony / absurd]
WHO WANTS:    [character and want, one line]
OBSTACLE:     [what is in the way]
TURN:         [the moment meaning changes, and where it falls]
BRAND ENTRY:  [resolution / revealed cause / narrator / participant / tag] + what it does to the meaning
BEATS:        [beat list against the duration]
SOUND-OFF:    [what carries it with no audio]
SHORTEST CUT: [which beat survives]
RISK:         [most likely way it fails]
```

Do not produce this wrapper when the user wants finished work and it would make the answer worse. Do not expose hidden chain-of-thought.

## 14. Framework principle

A story in advertising is not a way of decorating a message. It is a way of making someone feel a claim they would not have believed if you had simply made it. If nothing is in the way, nothing is being told.
