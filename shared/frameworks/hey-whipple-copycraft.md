# Hey Whipple Copycraft

> CreativeEngine shared framework. Used by `word-smither` and `creative-engine-director`, and by other agents when relevant.

## Attribution

This is an operational CreativeEngine framework. It is inspired by principles associated with advertising copycraft, including ideas the user associates with Luke Sullivan's *Hey Whipple, Squeeze This*.

- The techniques, labels, tests and examples below are CreativeEngine terminology unless a source is explicitly named.
- Do not claim that any of them appear verbatim in Sullivan's book.
- Do not quote the book or invent quotations from it.

## Purpose

This framework helps CreativeEngine **generate, develop and critique** advertising ideas and copy. It is a toolbox, not a scoring rubric and not a mandatory checklist.

It helps agents get from:

```
STRATEGY
  ↓
IDEA
  ↓
LANGUAGE
```

It especially fights:

- literal advertising
- strategy restatement
- generic AI copy
- category cliché
- over-explanation
- cleverness without an idea
- copy and visual saying the same thing

Use techniques selectively, according to the creative object and the active operating mode. Do not require every technique on every task.

## 1. Own the problem

Do not automatically hide, soften or euphemize the product or category problem. Sometimes the weakness, objection, embarrassment, contradiction or category tension contains the creative opportunity.

Ask:

- What is the thing we are tempted not to say?
- What does the audience already know but the category pretends isn't true?
- What product truth looks like a liability?

Could acknowledging it create honesty, humor, tension, confidence, memorability or differentiation?

- Do not manufacture weaknesses.
- Do not use confession as a gimmick.
- The problem must be strategically relevant.

## 2. 1 + 1 = 3

For visual-verbal advertising, image and copy should ideally create meaning neither contains alone.

**Avoid redundancy:**

```
IMAGE:     person running
HEADLINE:  Run faster.
```

**Seek:**

```
IMAGE:          provides one piece of information
COPY:           provides another
AUDIENCE:       makes the connection
THIRD MEANING:  the idea occurs in the audience's mind
```

Use the reusable 1 + 1 = 3 test in `creative-trajectory-evaluation.md` (section 10) when appropriate. This framework adds the copycraft interpretation: the copy's job is to supply the piece the image withholds, not to describe the image.

Do not force this onto copy-only work.

## 3. Radical positioning reduction

As a forcing device, complete one of:

```
THIS BRAND = __________
THIS PRODUCT = __________
THIS CAMPAIGN IS REALLY ABOUT __________
```

The answer is not necessarily final strategy. Its purpose is to expose whether the team actually knows what territory it is trying to own.

Then challenge it:

- Is that distinctive?
- Is it emotionally interesting?
- Is it ownable?
- Is it a category generic?
- Can a competitor write the same answer?

Reduction is a diagnostic, not a substitute for strategy.

## 4. Shake out the ugly

Early ideas are allowed to be bad. Do not demand polished copy too early.

Use staged divergence:

| Round | Focus |
|---|---|
| **A · Obvious** | Write the expected category answers. Get them out of the system. |
| **B · Opposite** | Invert assumptions. What would the category normally never say? |
| **C · Human** | Write from actual behavior, contradiction, embarrassment, desire, ritual, fear, pride, laziness, status or social truth. |
| **D · Product** | Find demonstrations, consequences, peculiarities, proof and unexpected uses. |
| **E · Culture** | Look at language, rituals, memes, behaviors, norms, taboos and tensions surrounding the problem. |
| **F · Form** | Explore different verbal structures and visual-verbal relationships. |

The goal is not a giant visible list. The goal is to move beyond the first available idea.

- Do not require "100 headlines" as literal output.
- If extensive divergence is useful, it may happen in working artifacts.
- Surface only the strongest useful material unless the user requests the full exploration.

## 5. Structural copy devices

Keep a toolbox of rhetorical and structural devices, for example:

- reversal
- antithesis
- parallelism
- rule of three
- misdirection
- understatement
- overstatement
- omission
- callback
- double meaning
- juxtaposition
- zeugma
- paraprosdokian

**A rhetorical device is not an idea.**

- Do not start with "let's write an antithesis."
- Start with the strategic or human idea, then ask whether a structural device sharpens it.
- If the technique becomes visible before the idea, abandon it.

## 6. Truth + conflict

```
TRUTH + CONFLICT = CREATIVE ENERGY
```

- Truth without conflict often becomes an observation.
- Conflict without truth often becomes a gimmick.

Useful conflict may exist between:

- desire and behavior
- product and perception
- aspiration and reality
- individual and society
- convenience and consequence
- identity and action
- expectation and experience
- what people say and what they do

Do not require negativity. Conflict may be funny, affectionate, absurd, emotional or subtle.

## 7. Product truth × human truth × cultural truth

Strong creative territory may emerge where three things intersect:

| Truth | Question |
|---|---|
| **Product truth** | What is concretely true, useful, strange, distinctive or demonstrable about the product? |
| **Human truth** | What recognizable behavior, desire, contradiction or emotion surrounds the problem? |
| **Cultural truth** | What larger norm, ritual, tension, language or conversation gives the idea relevance now? |

Do not require all three to be equally strong. Use the intersection as a search tool.

## 8. Find the enemy

Sometimes a campaign becomes clearer when it knows what it opposes. The enemy may be:

- a category convention
- a behavior
- a belief
- an inconvenience
- an outdated assumption
- a social pressure
- a ritual
- an emotional obstacle

The enemy does not need to be another brand. Avoid manufactured hostility.

Ask: **what are we fighting?** Then: **is that fight actually relevant to the brand?**

## 9. Copy compression

After finding an idea, compress it. Remove:

- setup the audience already understands
- duplicated meaning
- throat-clearing
- adjectives doing no work
- strategy language
- corporate qualifiers
- explanation of the joke
- explanation of the visual
- unnecessary claims

Ask repeatedly: **can another word disappear without losing the idea?**

Compression does not mean "always short." Long copy may be exactly right. The goal is density of meaning, not minimum character count.

## 10. Cliché graveyard

Actively detect language that feels retrieved from advertising memory rather than discovered from the specific problem.

**Word-level examples:**

- unleash
- elevate
- seamless
- revolutionary
- reimagine
- unlock
- game-changing
- possibilities
- the future of
- made for every moment
- where X meets Y

**Structural clichés:**

- generic empowerment
- inspirational manifesto with no distinctive belief
- feature followed by vague emotional benefit
- "meet the new..."
- generic challenger language
- empty category disruption
- purpose language any brand could claim

Do not ban words mechanically. Context can redeem or deliberately subvert a cliché.

The question is: **did this language come from this idea, or could the model have written it for almost any brand?**

## 11. Headline stress test

When the object taxonomy identifies a HEADLINE, test:

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| **Stopping power** | Would I notice it? |
| **Idea** | Is there a thought, or merely phrasing? |
| **Specificity** | Could this belong to another brand? |
| **Tension** | Is there friction, surprise or unresolved meaning? |
| **Compression** | Can anything disappear? |
| **Rhythm** | Does it sound good spoken? |
| **Visual relationship** | If paired with an image, does it create 1 + 1 = 3? |
| **Reward** | Does understanding it give the audience a small payoff? |
| **Memory** | Is there anything worth remembering? |

Do not turn this into a mandatory numeric scorecard unless the active task requires scoring.

## 12. Campaign generativity test

When evaluating a campaign idea or platform, ask whether it can produce:

- multiple headlines
- multiple visual ideas
- different stories
- different media behaviors
- different tones
- different audience situations

without merely repeating the same joke.

A platform should create a **family of ideas**, not a pile of executions wearing the same template.

Use `creative-object-taxonomy.md` to distinguish campaign platform from advertising idea from execution.

## 13. Smile-in-the-mind test

Ask whether the audience gets the pleasure of completing something. Possible sources:

- recognition
- reversal
- implication
- understatement
- juxtaposition
- delayed understanding
- visual/verbal connection
- surprising specificity
- elegant compression

The audience should sometimes be allowed to arrive at the meaning rather than having it completely explained.

Do not confuse obscurity with intelligence. If nobody can understand it, that is not sophistication.

## 14. Strategy is not copy

Reject copy that merely turns the proposition into a sentence.

**Failure pattern:**

```
STRATEGY:  Product makes mornings easier.
BAD COPY:  Make mornings easier.

STRATEGY:  Designed for ambitious people.
BAD COPY:  Made for ambition.
```

The copy needs a creative transformation.

Ask: **what happened between the strategy and the line?** If the answer is "we shortened it," there may be no creative idea.

## 15. Idea before language

Separate **the thought** from **the wording**. A beautifully written sentence can conceal a weak thought.

Before polishing language, ask:

- What is the idea?
- Can it survive paraphrase?

If changing the exact wording destroys everything, determine whether the work is:

- **A.** intentionally a verbal idea, or
- **B.** merely dependent on phrasing because there is no concept underneath.

Both can be legitimate, but know which one you have.

## 16. Copy modes

Apply copycraft differently depending on CreativeEngine's operating mode.

| Mode | Bias toward |
|---|---|
| **EXPLORE** | Volume of territories, strange thoughts, opposites, rough lines, provocative possibilities, different mechanisms. Do not over-edit. |
| **DEVELOP** | Strongest territory, compression, brand ownership, structural craft, visual/verbal interaction, campaign generativity, tonal consistency. |
| **JUDGE** | Originality, strategic relevance, memorability, ownability, conceptual strength, craft, cliché elimination. |

Do not judge EXPLORE work by final-copy standards.

## 17. Object-aware copycraft

Consult `creative-object-taxonomy.md` before applying copy criteria to serious work.

Do not evaluate a tagline, headline, slogan, body copy, manifesto and campaign platform as though they have the same job. For example:

| Object | Copycraft emphasis |
|---|---|
| Tagline | compression, memorability, ownability, longevity |
| Headline | stopping power, idea density, visual relationship |
| Body copy | voice, progression, rhythm, persuasion |
| Manifesto | worldview, conviction, emotional progression |
| Campaign platform | generativity, strategic tension, extensibility |

## 18. Framework output

When useful, WordSmither or CreativeDirector may surface:

```
OBJECT:             [creative object]
CORE THOUGHT:       [idea beneath the language]
TENSION:            [if relevant]
COPYCRAFT MOVE:     [what verbal/conceptual mechanism is doing the work]
WHY IT WORKS:       [concise]
WEAKNESS:           [concise]
STRONGEST VERSION:  [copy]
```

- Do not expose hidden chain-of-thought.
- Do not produce this diagnostic wrapper when the user simply wants final copy and it would make the answer worse.

## 19. Framework principle

The purpose of copycraft is not to make advertising sound clever. It is to make the idea:

- clearer
- sharper
- more human
- more surprising
- more memorable

Technique serves the idea. The idea serves the strategy. The strategy serves the real problem.
