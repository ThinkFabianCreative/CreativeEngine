# Pricken Idea Mechanisms

> CreativeEngine shared framework. Used by `design-champ` and `word-smither` together, and by `creative-engine-director` in review. Mainly a generation framework.

## Attribution

This is an operational CreativeEngine framework. It is inspired by the systematic idea-generation approach the user associates with Mario Pricken's *Creative Advertising* and its catalogue of creative "kick-start" questions.

- It was built from a secondary summary supplied by the user, not from the book. The mechanism names, directives, tests and examples below are CreativeEngine terminology.
- Do not claim that any mechanism, label or example appears in Pricken's book, or that the book organizes its techniques this way.
- Do not quote the book or invent quotations from it.
- Do not imitate or recreate specific published ads as if they were ideas for a client.

## Purpose

`hey-whipple-copycraft.md` helps find the thought and sharpen its language. This framework answers a narrower question:

```
WE HAVE A TRUTH.
WHICH MECHANISM TURNS IT INTO SOMETHING THE AUDIENCE HAS TO COMPLETE?
```

A mechanism is the move that creates **productive friction**: a gap between what the audience sees or reads and what they realize a moment later. The realization is the idea landing. The mechanisms are search tools for that gap. They are not ideas.

It especially fights:

- literal product demonstration with nothing to decode
- image and copy saying the same thing
- the first available visual metaphor
- "clever" executions with no strategic truth underneath
- AI-default visuals: the product floating in a void, the generic surreal composite

## 1. The ground rule: truth first, mechanism second

```
STRATEGIC TRUTH  →  MECHANISM  →  EXECUTION
```

Never start with "let's do an exaggeration ad." Start with one plain sentence of what must be understood or felt, drawn from the work packet. Then ask which mechanism dramatizes *that* truth.

Test: **remove the mechanism. Is there still something true and relevant left to say?** If not, the mechanism was the idea, and it is a trick.

Test: **could this mechanism, unchanged, sell a different product?** A stain remover so strong it cleans the next shirt can become any cleaning product. Specificity comes from the truth, not the mechanism.

## 2. The mechanisms

Each mechanism has an art direction move, a copy move, a social and motion adaptation, and its own failure mode. Pick by the truth, not by preference.

### 2.1 Omission: remove what is expected

Strip away the product, its main component, its function or the expected visual anchor, so the audience has to supply it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Art direction** | Show the aftermath, the empty space, the shape of what is missing or only a fragment. Negative space must point somewhere. |
| **Copy** | Don't say the punchline. Leave the last step to the reader, or name what the image withholds. |
| **Social / motion** | Hold the gap before the reveal, or never reveal. The comments can complete it. Works as a text-only post in a busy feed. |
| **Best for** | Brands with a recognizable product, shape or consequence. Benefits felt as absence: silence, relief, speed, cleanliness. |
| **Fails when** | The missing piece can't be inferred without brand familiarity the audience does not have. Emptiness can look like a poster with nothing on it. |

Ask: *What would still prove the benefit if the product disappeared from the frame?*

### 2.2 Reversal: make the opposite true

Name the category convention or expectation, invert it and build the work around proving the inverted version.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Art direction** | Show the expected outcome upside down: strength shown as delicacy, luxury as mess, the winner losing, a perspective flipped. |
| **Copy** | Say what the brand should never say. Present the weakness as the benefit, advise against the product, argue the category's opposite. |
| **Social / motion** | The setup follows the platform's familiar format and the payoff breaks it. Self-mockery of the category travels well. |
| **Best for** | Categories full of sameness. Products with a real trade-off. Challenger positions. |
| **Fails when** | The inversion is not backed by a real product truth, or it confirms the negative it meant to flip. Check the Whipple §1 rule: do not manufacture weaknesses. |

Ask: *What does every brand in this category show, and what if the opposite were true for us?*

### 2.3 Fusion: two things in one space

Take the product or benefit (domain A) and an unrelated object, place or system (domain B) that shares a meaningful property, and merge them into one object or scene.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Art direction** | One believable object, not a collage. The join should look inevitable. Craft carries it: bad compositing kills the reading. |
| **Copy** | Often none, or a short line that tips the reading. Don't name both domains; the image already did. |
| **Social / motion** | A transition or morph between A and B, a match cut or a loop that makes the link on its own. |
| **Best for** | Abstract or technical benefits that need a physical equivalent. |
| **Fails when** | The shared property is shallow (a round product merged with a round thing), or the viewer notices the technique before the meaning. |

Ask: *What property is the benefit really about (grip, protection, flow, patience), and where else in the world is that property most visible?*

### 2.4 Analogy: borrow a world

Explain the benefit through a parallel domain, such as nature, mechanics, sport, rituals or everyday objects, without necessarily fusing images. Fusion merges objects. Analogy transfers a logic.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Art direction** | Show the parallel world clearly enough that the transfer is effortless. The product may appear only at the end, or not at all. |
| **Copy** | Carries the bridge: one phrase that makes the parallel apply to the product. |
| **Social / motion** | Serial content: the same analogy across several situations. Also explanatory formats where a complex feature must be understood in seconds. |
| **Best for** | Complex, invisible or boring benefits: software, finance, insurance, services, B2B. |
| **Fails when** | The analogy needs explaining, or it is the category's standard metaphor (security as a lock, growth as a plant, speed as a rocket). |

Ask: *Who, outside this category, already solves the same problem, and how does it look when they do?*

### 2.5 Exaggeration: push a truth past plausibility

Take one real benefit, feature, consequence or problem and magnify it to an absurd, surreal or cinematic extreme, presented with a straight face.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Art direction** | Commit fully. Realistic rendering of an unrealistic consequence. Scale, physics or social reaction pushed until it breaks. |
| **Copy** | Deadpan. Treat the small benefit with total seriousness, or understate the huge event. Don't wink. |
| **Social / motion** | Escalation across seconds or a series. Meme-native hyperbole. The absurdity must read in the first frames, sound off. |
| **Best for** | Parity products with one clear benefit. Humor-permissive brands. |
| **Fails when** | The exaggeration is not tied to a specific real benefit, or it reads as a literal claim. Hyperbole about safety, health, finance or results can become a misleading claim; check the claim ledger and flag it. |

Ask: *If this one benefit were a thousand times more true, what would it break?*

### 2.6 Context shift: the wrong place

Place a familiar object, product or behavior in an unexpected environment, scale, time or social situation, so its value is seen fresh.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Art direction** | One precise dislocation, not general weirdness. Wrong scale, wrong setting, wrong era, wrong owner. |
| **Copy** | Explains nothing about the image; names why it is there, or lets the setting speak. |
| **Social / motion** | The product dropped into an unexpected creator format, platform culture or real-world location. Out of home and experiential work can make the shift physical. |
| **Best for** | Familiar products whose value has become invisible. Repositioning a use case, audience or occasion. |
| **Fails when** | The new context is random rather than revealing, or so strange nobody connects it to the brand. |

Ask: *Where would this product look absurd, and what would that absurdity reveal about it?*

### 2.7 Literal language: take the phrase at its word

Take an idiom, common expression, piece of category jargon or audience slang and execute it with literal accuracy, so the figurative phrase gains a new meaning in the brand's context.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Art direction** | Illustrate the phrase exactly and seriously. The more carefully executed, the better the reading. |
| **Copy** | The phrase itself, positioned so its second meaning belongs to the product. |
| **Social / motion** | Native to meme culture and audience slang. Needs the audience's own vocabulary, not the brand's. |
| **Best for** | Brands with a playful voice. Audiences with a shared vernacular. |
| **Fails when** | The idiom is the most obvious one in the category, or the work doesn't travel. Idioms are language- and market-specific: never assume one translates, and never pick an idiom by frequency; pick it by fit. |

Ask: *What does this audience literally say about the problem, and what if it were physically true?*

## 3. Splitting the load between image and copy

Every mechanism works through a gap. Decide where the gap is.

```
IMAGE CARRIES:     [which part of the puzzle]
COPY CARRIES:      [which part, or nothing]
AUDIENCE SUPPLIES: [the realization]
```

- The audience's contribution is the point. Neither image nor copy should supply it.
- There is no fixed ratio. Some ideas are image-only, some copy-only, some depend on both. Judge the result with the 1 + 1 = 3 gate in `creative-trajectory-evaluation.md` §10.
- If the audience can't supply the realization within the format's attention window, the gap is too wide. If they don't need to, there is no gap.

## 4. Social and digital

Mechanisms from print carry into digital, but the format changes the gap:

- **Time replaces the double take.** A print ad hides the gap in one frame. A video or carousel can open it and close it over seconds or swipes. Decide what is shown first, what is withheld and when it lands.
- **The first moment carries the mechanism.** The strangeness has to show before truncation, in the first second, with sound off.
- **Audiences can finish the idea.** Polls, comments, duets, stitches and remixes can be the place the realization happens. Participation must serve the idea, not be engagement bait.
- **Platform specifications, safe zones and policies** come from verified knowledge (`shared/knowledge/`), never from this framework.

## 5. Mechanism tells

Mechanism-led advertising has its own clichés. Watch for:

- **The award-print visual pun:** a clean fusion on a white background, a small logo in the corner and no line. The genre is instantly recognizable and often skipped as "an ad."
- **The standard category metaphor:** a lock for security, a lightbulb for ideas, a rocket for growth, a mountain summit for ambition.
- **Generic AI surrealism:** oversized products in dreamlike landscapes, melting objects, floating composites. It looks like a mechanism, but nothing is decoded.
- **Same mechanism, every execution:** a campaign where each ad fuses a different object with the product is a template, not a platform. Check Whipple §12.
- **Explaining the gap:** a headline that tells you what the image means.
- **Borrowed famous ads:** reproducing a known execution's mechanism and subject. Published advertising is reference, not raw material.

These are warnings, not bans. Deliberate, visible subversion of a tell is craft.

## 6. By operating mode

| Mode | Use |
|---|---|
| **EXPLORE** | Use mechanisms as divergence prompts. Run the same truth through several mechanisms to get genuinely different territories, not variants of one image. Allow rough and strange. Don't apply the tells as filters yet. |
| **DEVELOP** | For promising ideas, test whether a different mechanism dramatizes the truth better. Fix the image/copy split (§3). Check the failure mode for the chosen mechanism, and check the tells. |
| **JUDGE** | Ask whether the mechanism serves the truth or replaces it (§1). Name the mechanism precisely when it is the weakness. Don't reward a mechanism for being visible. |

Mechanism variety is not divergence by itself. Two ideas using different mechanisms on the same thin truth are still one idea. Use `creative-trajectory-evaluation.md` §5.

## 7. Choosing a mechanism

No mechanism is better in general. Useful starting points:

| If the truth is... | Try first |
|---|---|
| A benefit felt as absence or relief | Omission, exaggeration |
| Hidden, technical or abstract | Analogy, fusion |
| Contrary to category convention | Reversal |
| Familiar and taken for granted | Context shift, omission |
| One clear parity benefit | Exaggeration, reversal |
| Owned by the audience's own language | Literal language |

These are starting points, not rules. The client's voice, category sensitivities, claims and mandatories limit which mechanisms are appropriate. For example, exaggeration and reversal may be wrong for regulated claims or for a brand in crisis.

## 8. Division of labor

The mechanism is shared property of the creative pair, not a handoff.

- **DesignChamp** owns whether the mechanism reads visually: composition, scale, rendering credibility, what the eye finds first and whether the craft makes the gap legible.
- **WordSmither** owns whether a line is needed at all and, if so, which part of the gap it holds.
- **CreativeDirector** challenges whether the mechanism is serving the truth, and whether the campaign repeats one mechanism as a template.

## 9. Framework output

When useful, surface:

```
TRUTH:              [plain sentence from the packet]
MECHANISM:          [omission / reversal / fusion / analogy / exaggeration / context shift / literal language]
IMAGE CARRIES:      [...]
COPY CARRIES:       [... or "nothing"]
AUDIENCE SUPPLIES:  [the realization]
FAILURE RISK:       [the mechanism-specific risk most likely here]
```

Don't produce this wrapper when the user wants finished work and it would make the answer worse. Don't expose hidden chain-of-thought.

## 10. Framework principle

A mechanism earns its place when the audience's small moment of work turns into understanding of something true about the brand. If the work leaves people admiring the trick and forgetting the brand, the mechanism won and the idea lost.
