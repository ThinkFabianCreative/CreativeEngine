# Creative Effectiveness

How CreativeEngine sets creative ambition before the work, and assesses work against brand, strategy and creative level during and after it. It gives the department and the client one shared vocabulary for "how good is this, and is it good enough for this job?" CreativeDirector owns it.

Source: the Cannes Lions Creative Effectiveness Program (Lions Advisory: Nicola Perrozzi, Cinzia Morelli-Verhoog), delivered as a two-day client workshop in October 2022, with its "3 pitstops / 10 questions" one-pager and the Cannes Lions *State of Creativity* 2023 trends report. This is an interpretive capture of the program's method. Quoted statistics are the program's own citations and have not been independently verified here; cite them as "cited in the Cannes Lions program", never as CreativeEngine findings. The program was taught to one client; none of that client's facts belong in this file.

## Where it sits

- **Method, not verdict.** The Creative Ladder and the 10 questions are decision aids. Verdicts (KILL, REWORK, ADVANCE, BET THE PITCH) stay CreativeDirector's (root `CLAUDE.md`). The program says it too: the ladder "will never replace your judgement".
- **Mode behavior.** EXPLORE: set the ambition, then stay out of the way; no ladder scoring of rough territories. DEVELOP: use the questions to diagnose what is missing, not to kill. JUDGE: full assessment; an agreed ambition rung may act as a selection gate.
- **Strategy upstream.** Questions 1–4 test the work against a Brand Promise, a Core Creative Idea, an insight and a Job to Be Done. Those come from `brand-strategy.md`. If they do not exist, say so; do not invent them to complete a scorecard.
- **Specificity wins.** Copy craft stays in `hey-whipple-copycraft.md`, story in `narrative-craft.md`, visual craft in `visual-craft.md`, ceiling and PUSH in `creative-direction-audit.md`, defense in `selling-the-work.md`. This framework asks whether each is present and pulling its weight, not how to fix it.

## 1. The Creative Ladder

Ten rungs from damaging to culture-shaping. Its purpose is shared calibration: "in order to be better, you need to know what you have to be better than" (Dave Trott, cited in the program).

The program used two sets of rung names. CreativeEngine uses the one-pager names as canonical and keeps the workshop names as aliases.

| # | Rung | Workshop alias | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | **CULTURAL MOVEMENT** | Cultural phenomenon | A brilliant interpretation of a social theme that sets a new socio-cultural trend |
| 9 | **CONTAGIOUS** | Contagious | Disproportionate attention, earned outpacing paid; enters pop culture, memes, references |
| 8 | **GAME CHANGER** | Game changer | Breaks the category clichés and unbolts new codes; a new creative perspective for the category |
| 7 | **SHOWTIME** | Creative | Spectacular work people enjoy for its artistic value, originality and entertainment |
| 6 | **FRESH** | Heads up | Distinctive, innovative execution that gives a new spin to known solutions, still in a safe space |
| 5 | **OWNABLE** | Ownable | Stays in the category comfort zone but is clearly attributable to the brand |
| 4 | **DEJA VU** | Vanilla | Stereotype work on safe category codes; any brand in the category could run it |
| 3 | **PUZZLING** | Puzzling | Convoluted; confuses the audience and does not deliver the intended message |
| 2 | **DISCONNECTED** | Lazy | Borrowed interest unrelated to the brand, used to get attention in the absence of a brand narrative |
| 1 | **CALAMITY** | Calamity | A reckless attempt to break codes that damages the brand's reputation |

Reading it:

- **5 is the floor of acceptable, not the goal.** Below 5 the work fails on brand (2, 4), clarity (3) or safety (1). 4 is the most common failure because it feels safe: "uncreative work is like a Trojan horse".
- **Rungs 1–4 are diagnoses, not insults.** Name which failure it is; each has a different fix (§4).
- **Higher rungs cost more time.** The program's rule of thumb: Deja vu work is approved in under 3 months, Game changer work takes over 6. Set the rung knowing the calendar.
- **Rungs 9–10 are outcomes.** Contagious and Cultural movement describe what happened in the world. Before launch, a rung of 9 or 10 is an *ambition* or a *plausible potential*, never an assessment. Do not claim it for unreleased work (`creative-direction-audit.md` §8.2, case-study inflation).

### Relationship to the audit's altitude

`creative-direction-audit.md` §3 keeps its four-level altitude. The two scales answer different questions: the ladder places work on a shared client-agency scale, the altitude judges conceptual ambition inside the department. Approximate correspondence, for translation only:

| Ladder | Audit altitude |
|---|---|
| 1–4 | Below EXPECTED (a failure, not a level) |
| 5 OWNABLE | EXPECTED |
| 6 FRESH | STRONG |
| 7 SHOWTIME, 8 GAME CHANGER | DISTINCTIVE |
| 8 GAME CHANGER with a durable new code; 9–10 as potential | EXCEPTIONAL |

Do not run both on the same object unless both questions are being asked.

### When to use the ladder

1. **Set Creative Ambition** for each brief: one target rung, agreed with the client before the work starts (`brand-strategy.md` §7).
2. **Set benchmarks:** three existing pieces of work at the target rung, ideally from outside the category, agreed by client and agency. They make the ambition concrete.
3. **Assess work** in every phase, against that agreed rung.
4. **Self-assess the partnership** after a campaign: did we reach the rung we set, and if not, where did it slip (§6)?

## 2. Three pitstops, ten questions

The assessment runs in three layers, in order. A failure in an earlier layer is not rescued by a later one: brilliant creative off-brand is still off-brand.

| Pitstop | Tests | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Branding** | Could only this brand have made it? | Generic work any other brand could own |
| **B. Strategic** | Will it change what the audience believes and does? | Vague work that does not solve the business problem |
| **C. Creative** | How high is it on the ladder, and does that match the ambition? | Work that is correct but invisible |

The ten questions (the one-pager numbers the tenth as 0):

| # | Pitstop | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | Is the work an expression of the Brand Promise? |
| 2 | A | Does the work feel like an expression of the Core Creative Idea? |
| 3 | B | Is the consumer insight central to the story? |
| 4 | B | Is the work designed to deliver the Job to Be Done? |
| 5 | C | Does the work make impact? |
| 6 | C | Is the consumer insight brought to life in a creatively brilliant way? |
| 7 | C | Is the work emotionally engaging? |
| 8 | C | Is production craft making the work more creative? |
| 9 | C | Is the work going to trigger conversations? |
| 10 | C | Is the work in line with the desired Creative Ambition? |

Answer each with **YES / PARTLY / NO / NOT ASSESSABLE**, and one line of reason tied to the work itself. NOT ASSESSABLE is honest when the brand foundation, JTBD or ambition was never set, or when the work is too rough for the question (craft on a script, for example). Do not sum the answers into a score. Questions 5–9 are the five drivers (§3); question 10 is the ladder.

The program adds a gut check worth keeping: "Does it make my spine tingle?" (Marc Pritchard, P&G, cited). And its warning: **if it is too easy to approve, it is not creative enough.** No qualms at all is a sign of a comfort zone, not of quality.

## 3. The five drivers of creativity

The more of these present, and the stronger, the higher the work climbs. The program's image is a DJ's mixing console: not every channel at full volume, but the mix decides the level.

### Impact: does it get attention and keep it?

People develop "cognitive immunity" to messages not worth storing; most advertising is neither noticed nor remembered (Trott, *Creative Blindness*, cited). Impact comes from drama: **a character, a want, an obstacle**. Curiosity, entertainment and suspense keep it. Any of the twelve narrative types can carry it (problem/solution, slice of life, testimonial, quest, tragedy, comedy, endorsement, presenter, demonstration, journey and return, rebirth, overcoming the monster). Two questions: does it stop me, and does it hold me to the end? Story structure: `narrative-craft.md`.

### Insight: is a human truth at the center?

**Fact → observation → insight.** People feed pets twice a day (fact); at breakfast and dinner (observation); people feel guilty eating in front of their pets (insight). An insight carries a **tension the brand can resolve**; a fact has nothing to resolve. Consumer-centric work speaks as "you" about how the brand serves a need; brand-centric work speaks as "I" about what the brand does. The work should hold a mirror in which the audience recognizes themselves, and the insight must be executed brilliantly, not portrayed trivially. Product-centric work can still be effective when it announces an innovation that meets an unmet need.

### Emotion: does it make me feel something specific?

Emotion builds memory, and memory builds mental availability. The program cites IPA (Binet and Field) data that emotional campaigns outperform rational ones, and Kantar data that emotional digital ads are more likely to build long-term equity. Name the specific emotion (love, joy, pride, relief, awe, fascination, longing, calm, comfort, anger, sadness, disgust…); "emotional" alone is not an answer. Emotion alone is not enough: the work needs an **emotional peak**, a moment where the feeling lands.

### Craft: does execution raise the idea?

Production craft can turn a stereotyped board into effective work, and poor craft can bury a great idea. For film, the program names eight elements: director, cinematography, casting, production design, visual effects, music, sound design, editing. Two of its rules are worth carrying everywhere: **music is an asset decided upfront**, not picked in the edit, and **the director's vision** is where a script becomes a film. For non-film work, use `visual-craft.md` and `typography.md`. Craft never rescues a missing idea (core principle 6).

### Fame: will people talk about it?

Fame is not awareness. It is word of mouth, advocacy, the sense that the brand is "making the running" in its category (Peter Field, cited). Work earns fame by being bold, entertaining, disruptive, provocative or useful: it gives people a reason to comment, hold a view or even disagree. The image: a firework that sets off many conversations, not a bonfire that warms only the people standing next to it. To design for fame: set it as a goal in the brief, set a high ambition, plan the paid/owned/earned trajectory, and build in things people can extract and pass on.

## 4. Diagnosing low rungs

| Rung | Typical cause | Send it to |
|---|---|---|
| 1 CALAMITY | Code-breaking without a brand reason or a read of the culture | KILL or back to EXPLORE; check the risk with `selling-the-work.md` |
| 2 DISCONNECTED | No Core Creative Idea; borrowed interest standing in for meaning | `brand-strategy.md` §5: find or write the CCI |
| 3 PUZZLING | More than one message; a story with no clear turn | Rule of 1 (`brand-strategy.md` §6); `narrative-craft.md` |
| 4 DEJA VU | Category stereotypes, no insight, safe codes | Category stereotype check (§5); back to the insight |
| 5 OWNABLE (when the ambition is higher) | Right brand and message, low drivers | Raise impact, emotion and fame; `creative-direction-audit.md` PUSH |

## 5. Category stereotypes

Every category has its stereotypes, and some (gender, age, babies, pets, home, holidays, seduction, sport, body shape, driving, eating, drinking, disability…) cut across categories. List the category's stereotypes before EXPLORE and again before JUDGE: "Know them, avoid them." An execution built on them lands on DEJA VU however well it is crafted. Ed Catmull's line, cited in the program, applies: if something works, you shouldn't do it again. This complements the category check in `brief-strategy.md` (step 3), which maps what the category *says*; this list covers what it *shows*.

## 6. Conditions for climbing the ladder

The program puts as much weight on behavior as on the work. CreativeEngine cannot control the client, but CreativeDirector should know the conditions and name them when they are missing.

- **Creative confidence, four behaviors:** WANT (determination to challenge the norms), DEMAND (trust the partner and share an ambition), APPROVE (confidence to make tough calls on the work), SELL (stand up for the work inside the organization). These map onto `selling-the-work.md`.
- **Judge potential, not presentation.** Great work can be hidden behind a poor rough; poor work can hide behind a brilliant presentation. Squint. Intelligence can be a handicap here: "extraordinary work is ordinarily very easy to reject" (Jim Carroll, BBH, cited).
- **Rule of three meetings.** After three meetings on the same creative, produce it or ditch it; do not loop.
- **Tissue meetings.** Share rough work early and safely, so the team can move out of the comfort zone before positions harden.
- **Two-way assessment.** After a campaign, the agency assesses the client (brief, ambition, approvals, timing, behavior) as well as the client assessing the agency.
- **Process is a creative tool.** Diverge, then converge, in repeated cycles (Cannes 2023; the same as EXPLORE → DEVELOP → JUDGE).

## 7. Effectiveness evidence

Brand building and short-term sales are not opposed: the program cites System1 analysis that ads strong at brand building are usually above average for short-term sales too, and that consistent, long-running ideas outperform one-off activations. "Stable on strategy, fresh on execution" (Dove, cited). These are reasons to set ambition and keep a Core Creative Idea; they are not predictions for any piece of work.

Real performance data for a client stays in that client's workspace and follows `creative-direction-audit.md` §10: cite it with source, project, date and status, and never invent or project it. Client KPIs (awareness, brand-health trackers, ad-testing norms, search, sales) belong in the brief's measurement field (`brand-strategy.md` §7), not here.

## Output

For an assessment in DEVELOP or JUDGE:

```
OBJECT / MODE / AGREED AMBITION RUNG (or: not set)
A. BRANDING     Q1 … Q2 …            (YES / PARTLY / NO / NOT ASSESSABLE + reason)
B. STRATEGIC    Q3 … Q4 …
C. CREATIVE     Q5–Q9 drivers …       strongest driver / missing driver
LADDER          current rung, with reason; reachable rung and its condition (if useful)
GAP             current rung vs agreed ambition
NEXT            the one or two changes that would climb a rung, routed to the owning framework
```

Keep it short. A full ten-question pass is for major work in JUDGE. In DEVELOP, ask only the questions that diagnose the current problem.
