# Brand Strategy

How CreativeEngine builds or reviews the strategic assets that sit above any single brief: the brand promise and its brand house, the Core Creative Idea that turns the promise into a lasting narrative, and the Jobs to Be Done that point it at the barriers to growth. It also defines what a creative brief must carry from those assets. BriefMachine owns it.

Source: the Cannes Lions Creative Effectiveness Program (Lions Advisory), client workshop, October 2022, plus the program's brief, brand promise and Jobs to Be Done templates (revised January 2023). This is an interpretive capture. Brand examples are the program's illustrations, used to explain a mechanism, never as models. Quoted figures are the program's citations and have not been verified here. The program was taught to one client; none of that client's facts belong in this file.

## Where it sits

- **Above the brief.** `brief-normalization.md` handles intake; `brief-strategy.md` sharpens the insight and proposition of one brief. This framework works at brand level, across briefs. A brief inherits from it (§7); it does not rebuild it every time.
- **Only on request.** Writing or revising a brand promise, CCI or JTBD set is strategy work, a DERIVED layer, never intake. If the client already has approved brand assets, record them as client knowledge with their source and date, and work from them. Proposing changes is a separate, labeled step.
- **Client truth stays in the client.** A client's brand house lives in `projects/<client>/client/`, with its status (approved, proposed, superseded). Nothing here holds a client's facts.
- **Barry is still the base.** One benefit, credible support, one defined person, the real problem. The tools below scale those rules to brand level.
- **No creative output.** A CCI defines the *kind* of story, not the story. Lines, scripts and visuals belong to the departments.

## 1. Five steps of brand building

The program's model: brand growth is driven mainly by **mental availability**, getting into and becoming preferred in the repertoire of the broadest target. Five must-dos, each with an enabler:

| Must do | Enabler |
|---|---|
| Make the brand the distinctive solution to a need relevant to the broad target | **Brand Promise** (§2) |
| Build distinctive assets that make the brand and its meaning easy to recognize | **Brand Identity** (§4) |
| Develop an underlying narrative that builds and refreshes memory structures | **Core Creative Idea** (§5) |
| Identify and knock down the barriers to recruitment | **Jobs to Be Done** (§6) |
| Maximize the efficiency and effectiveness of spend | **Creative effectiveness** (`creative-effectiveness.md`) |

Use it as a diagnostic: which enabler is missing or weak? Most briefs that fail, fail because an upper enabler was never set.

## 2. The Brand Promise

**A sentence describing the unique and relevant promise the brand makes to a broad audience (inclusive). It drives all the brand's choices and gives it a distinctive reason to exist (exclusive).**

Form: **[Brand] exists to [satisfy a need].** The program's illustrations: Volvo exists to save lives in case of accident; Tesla exists to accelerate the world's transition to sustainable energy; Mini exists to highlight your individuality with excitement.

The program calls this "brand positioning" in its first session and "brand purpose" in its brief template. CreativeEngine calls it the **Brand Promise**.

Test: is the need distinctive, or the benefit every brand in the category delivers? "Brands must find a distinctive reason to be chosen."

### The brand house

The promise sits on these elements. Fill only what the evidence supports; mark the rest open.

| Element | What it is | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **Universal Human Truth** | A human need that is hard to fulfil, so it contains a **tension** | A truth with no tension is a platitude. Maslow's levels (physiological, safety, belonging, esteem, self-actualization) help locate a deep need with broad relevance |
| **Broad target** | The large group the brand could recruit, broader than current category buyers | Narrow targeting is a self-fulfilling prophecy; a brand grows faster by reaching beyond its category (Mars, P&G and Unilever CMOs, cited) |
| **Brand DNA** | The provable assets uniquely tied to the brand: origin, cultural roots, sourcing, manufacturing, ingredients, founders, history | DNA is proof, not adjectives. It is what credential campaigns are built from |
| **Benefits** | Functional (what the brand does), emotional (how it makes me feel), higher order (what it does for society and the planet) | One promise should flex across all three, not three separate promises |
| **Brand Role** | The positive change the brand brings to people's lives ("helps people find their freedom") | A role is a verb about the person, not about the product |
| **Brand Personality** | Tone and behavior at every contact, described with one or two Jungian archetypes | Pair the archetype with "we are / we are never" lists so it can be used |

**One promise, three benefit levels.** The program's Pantene illustration: "exists to infuse strength", read as strength in the hair (functional), strength as aspiration (emotional), strength for women's futures (higher order).

**The higher order benefit** lifts a brand above its category, earns respect, supports premium pricing and recruits, but only if it is honest and consistent with the brand. A higher order benefit the brand cannot prove is a liability. Claims follow the reviewed evidence rules in `brief-normalization.md`.

**The twelve archetypes,** in the program's four families: Stability (Caregiver, Creator, Ruler); Risk (Magician, Hero, Rebel); Belonging (Everyman, Lover, Jester); Freedom (Innocent, Sage, Explorer). Use them to make tone choices concrete, not as a personality test.

### Optional: the north-star house

A simpler brand house the program's materials also use, useful when a client needs one page:

```
NORTH STAR       what the brand stands for, in one or two words (an ideal: Disney–magic, Dove–self-esteem)
WHY WE EXIST     the mission
WHAT WE BELIEVE  the point of view, a conviction that comes from the north star
HOW WE DO IT     the proof: how products and services deliver it
+ personality and behaviors, Core Creative Idea, vision
```

Test of a good north star: it is a shared ideal people could care about without the product, and the brand can show it through passions (music, sport, entertainment), not only stories.

## 3. Brand-centric vs consumer-centric

A recurring diagnostic for every element above and every brief: does it speak about what the brand does ("I"), or about what it means to people ("you")? Most category communication is brand-centric. The promise and the CCI should be written from the person's need outward.

## 4. Brand Identity

**The set of distinctive executional assets that evoke the brand and its meaning:** logo, font, shape, color, tagline, ad style, cast, sound. They build and refresh memory structures.

A visual identity is not a brand. Assets carry meaning only once the promise and the CCI give them some. Ask of any asset: is it distinctive, is it used consistently, and does it extend beyond the logo into every touchpoint? Design detail belongs to DesignChamp (`visual-craft.md`). This framework only records which assets exist and which the brand still lacks.

## 5. The Core Creative Idea (CCI)

**The underlying narrative that brings the Brand Promise to life creatively, inspiring content that is consistently original and proprietary to the brand, over time, across formats and channels.**

Form: **Stories where [Brand] [meta-narrative].**

| Promise vs CCI | |
|---|---|
| Brand Promise | The promise the brand makes |
| Core Creative Idea | How the brand communicates it, as a repeatable kind of story |

The program's illustrations: Dove, "stories where Dove challenges beauty stereotypes"; Volvo, "stories where Volvo spectacularizes its credentials in vehicle safety"; Nike, "stories where people challenge what limits the accomplishment of their dreams", sustained for more than thirty years. And outside advertising, James Bond: a sophisticated British agent wins over crime in a sequence of gadgets and stunts, consistent for fifty years.

**Treat the brand's stories like episodes of a series:** each explores a new edge of the meta-narrative, adds distinctiveness, and connects to what came before.

### Seven tests for a CCI

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| **Insightful** | Is the Universal Human Truth at its core? |
| **Global** | Is it relevant in every market the brand serves? |
| **Creative** | Do its executions climb the Creative Ladder? |
| **Complete** | Can it host everything the brand needs to say to grow, across functional, emotional and higher order benefits? |
| **Fluid** | Does it travel across channels and formats, above and below the line? |
| **Simple** | Is it a simple concept with room to expand? |
| **Long-lived** | Could it run for years? Longevity is the payoff for the investment upfront |

A CCI fails most often by being a tagline (too narrow to generate stories) or a category description (any brand could tell those stories). Run the ownability check: swap in a competitor's name. If the sentence still works, it is not a CCI.

**Why it matters:** the program cites evidence that durable campaigns are the strongest single driver of effectiveness and that short-term ideas are markedly less effective. A CCI also simplifies governance: it defines the creative territory, guides localization, and helps the creative team stay on track instead of drifting.

**If the brand has none,** the next brief can start it. Name that as an explicit objective of the brief, not as a hidden extra.

**CCI vs campaign platform.** A CCI is brand-level and outlives campaigns. A campaign platform (`creative-object-taxonomy.md`) is one campaign's idea and should be an episode of the CCI. When WordSmither or DesignChamp proposes a platform, CreativeDirector checks it against the CCI (`creative-effectiveness.md` Q2).

## 6. Jobs to Be Done (JTBD)

**A JTBD states your understanding of the people you are targeting and the barrier they have against your brand, so the communication can be more persuasive.** Advertising works when it knocks down a barrier. The question to ask: *why don't those who know the brand buy it?*

Most advertising aims at mental availability even when awareness is not the barrier. The JTBD finds the real one.

### Template

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Job** | PERSUADE [whom] TO [do what], as one line |
| **Target audience** | The large group you could recruit, sized where evidence allows |
| **Behavior to change** | What they do today that keeps them from the brand. This is the problem |
| **Insight** | What they believe deep inside, in their words, explaining the behavior |
| **New belief** | What you want them to think about the brand. It must fit the Brand Promise |
| **New behavior** | What you want them to do as a result |

The program's illustration (Smart): city drivers buy SUVs (behavior) because they feel safer in a big car (insight); new belief, Smart is small and tough; new behavior, buy Smart for the city and feel safe.

### Barrier types

The program groups consumer problems into three barrier types, each mostly answered by one benefit level:

| Barrier | Mostly answered by |
|---|---|
| **Credential:** doubt the brand can deliver | Functional benefit and DNA proof |
| **Aspirational:** not "for me", or not desirable | Emotional benefit |
| **Higher order:** values or reputation | Higher order benefit |

To find the barrier, list every reason the target gives for not buying (too expensive, not for people like me, don't understand it, all the same…), then choose the one that, if knocked down, moves the most people.

### Rules

- **About three JTBDs per brand.** They form the backbone of the marketing plan, take most of the investment, align functions on the same goals, carry their own KPIs, and change only when achieved (around three years, per the program).
- **The rule of 1: one JTBD, one creative brief.** "If you need an 'and', you need a different creative brief." Audiences absorb one message at a time ("throw them one tennis ball and they catch it; throw lots and they drop them all", BBH, cited). This agrees with `brief-strategy.md`: separate problem IDs when the jobs differ.
- **No problem, no brief.** "Creativity is about solving a problem in a new way" (Hegarty, cited). If the JTBD cannot be written, the brief is not ready.
- **Insights, not demographics.** Demographic profiles describe; insights explain. Conjure up the person: what they love and hate, how they see themselves, how they want to be seen, what the problem is. Fact vs observation vs insight is defined in `creative-effectiveness.md` §3 (Insight) and `brief-strategy.md` lens 1.

## 6a. The USP

**The product or offer truth only this brand can claim.** The program's material has no USP field; this section adds it. It sits inside each JTBD: the USP is the strongest weapon against that job's barrier. The single-minded proposition ("say this") is usually the USP turned into a benefit for the person.

Rosser Reeves (*Reality in Advertising*, 1961) defined it with three tests. Later practice added four. An excellent USP passes all seven:

| # | Test | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Proposition** | Does it say "buy this and you get this specific benefit"? A slogan or a mood fails |
| 2 | **Unique** | Can competitors not make the claim, or have they not made it? Swap in a competitor's name: if the sentence is still true, it fails |
| 3 | **Strong enough to move people** | Does it pull in large numbers, not a niche? |
| 4 | **True and provable** | Is there evidence today? Unapproved claims carry ⚑ and are a liability until cleared |
| 5 | **A difference that matters** | Does it touch the reason people buy, or the JTBD's barrier? A unique trivial difference fails |
| 6 | **Showable** | Does it give the creatives an image or a demonstration, not just a sentence? |
| 7 | **Defensible** | Can it last? Features get copied; history, place, access and how the brand is built do not |

- **Parity is an answer.** If nothing passes 2 and 5, write "parity". The distinctiveness then has to come from the Core Creative Idea and the brand assets (`creative-effectiveness.md`), not from a weak claim. Never invent a USP to fill the field.
- **The pre-emptive USP.** In parity, the first brand to dramatize a common truth can own it in people's minds (Claude Hopkins, Schlitz's steam-cleaned bottles). Name it as pre-emptive, so nobody mistakes it for a real difference.
- **Who does what.** BriefMachine finds and writes the USP from evidence. `creative-engine-director` tests 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7 at the readiness gate. WordSmither and DesignChamp answer test 6. Only the client or legal clears test 4. The `creative-brief` skill runs this as a loop.

## 7. What a creative brief carries

The program's brief has five parts. CreativeEngine's normalized brief schema (`brief-normalization.md`) stays the canonical structure. These are the brand-level fields a brief should carry or flag as missing:

| Part | Fields |
|---|---|
| **1. Strategic assets** | Brand Promise; Core Creative Idea. The work is an expression of the promise and is inspired by the CCI. Review both at the start of every brief meeting |
| **2. Objectives** | Business challenge (category context); the one JTBD |
| **3. Executional requirements** | Media and channel needs, only if required; timing; production and media budget; mandatories, kept few |
| **4. Creative ambition** | A target rung on the Creative Ladder (`creative-effectiveness.md` §1) plus three benchmark pieces at that rung, agreed with the client |
| **5. Assessment and measurement** | How the work will be judged (the three pitstops) and which metrics define success, with a goal |

### Brief principles

- **A brief is subtraction.** Less mandatory content gives creativity a better chance. Too much information forces creatives to make the client's decisions for them and starts a trial-and-error loop.
- **The usual failures:** a long list of objectives reconciling too many views; no problem to solve; shallow insight with many executional limits; no brand idea, so the horizon is endless; nothing that makes the creatives impatient to start. The program cites a large gap between how satisfied brands and agencies are with briefs.
- **Two sessions.** An *assignment briefing* irons out details with account and planning; an *inspiration briefing* galvanizes the creatives. Do not merge them.
- **Brief as experience.** Immersion beats documents: talk to the people, use the product in its real context, live the problem. A dull, formal briefing produces dull work.
- **Prescribe media only when necessary.** A commercial is not always the answer; let the team solve the problem creatively. The format chosen also sets production cost.
- **Ambition sets the calendar.** Higher rungs need more time (`creative-effectiveness.md` §1). Tight deadlines produce creative compromises; say so in the brief.
- **Fame as a goal.** If the job needs talk value, write it in.

## 7a. The TRAFFIK Brief Standard

A brief goes to the creatives only when it meets all twelve. Each is PASS or FAIL, with no partial credit and no total score. The `creative-brief` loop gates on this list.

| # | Standard | PASS means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Length** | **Three pages at most** once the prompts are deleted. Cut words, never fields. If it won't fit, it's more than one brief |
| 2 | **Complete** | Every template field is filled, or honestly marked MISSING, parity or N/A. Nothing left blank |
| 3 | **A real problem** | The problem names what people do, not a marketing goal ("raise awareness" fails) |
| 4 | **Single-minded** | One proposition per job, with no "and"; three jobs at most |
| 5 | **A true insight** | In the audience's words, with a tension the brand can resolve. A fact or a demographic fails |
| 6 | **A tested USP** | Passes USP tests 1, 2 and 5 (§6a), or is honestly labeled parity or pre-emptive |
| 7 | **A connected chain** | Behavior → insight → new belief → proposition follow from each other, and the new belief fits the brand promise |
| 8 | **Ownable** | Swap in a competitor's name: the proposition no longer works |
| 9 | **Honest evidence** | Every claim is sourced; unapproved claims carry ⚑; assumptions are in italics |
| 10 | **Ambition set** | A ladder rung, three benchmarks, and timing that can support the rung |
| 11 | **Creative space** | Few mandatories, media open unless decided, no executions prescribed |
| 12 | **Showable** | WordSmither and DesignChamp answer SHOWABLE for each job, or the job is honestly parity and flagged |

Owners: BriefMachine writes to the standard. `creative-engine-director` judges 1–11. WordSmither and DesignChamp judge 12. A brief that meets the standard is ready for the creatives, not approved. The client signs it.

## Quality checks

Before handing over brand-level strategy:

1. Is the promise distinctive (no competitor could sign it) and relevant (a real need of a broad target)?
2. Does the Universal Human Truth contain a tension?
3. Is every DNA point provable, and every higher order claim honest?
4. Does the CCI pass the seven tests and the competitor-swap test?
5. Does each JTBD name a real barrier, with an insight that explains the behavior, and a new belief that fits the promise?
6. Is there one JTBD per brief?
7. Are approved assets, proposals and open questions clearly labeled, with sources and dates?

## Output

```
CLIENT / BRAND / SOURCE SCOPE / STATUS (approved, proposed, review)
BRAND PROMISE        [Brand] exists to …
BRAND HOUSE          UHT · broad target · DNA · benefits (F / E / HO) · role · personality
CORE CREATIVE IDEA   Stories where [Brand] …     seven-test notes
IDENTITY             existing assets / gaps
JTBD 1–3             job · target · behavior · insight · new belief · new behavior · barrier type
OPEN                 divergences, missing evidence, questions
```

A brief review uses the quality checks and the §7 fields, and recommends without rewriting the client's approved assets.
