# Creative Direction Audit

> CreativeEngine shared framework. Primary user `creative-engine-director`. Other agents may consult it when ambition is the useful question. Selective, never automatic.

## Terminology

This is an operational CreativeEngine framework. It may reflect the rigor of exceptional Executive Creative Direction, but its levels, lenses, tests and labels are CreativeEngine terminology.

- Do not present any level, lens or test here as an official Cannes Lions, D&AD, One Show, Effie or named-agency criterion. No verified source for such criteria exists in this repository.
- Do not claim an award outcome, jury behavior or industry recognition for any work.

## Purpose

The active frameworks answer other questions.

```
WHAT KIND OF OBJECT IS THIS?        creative-object-taxonomy
HOW CAN THE IDEA GET SHARPER?       hey-whipple-copycraft
IS IT ESCAPING THE GENERIC?         creative-trajectory-evaluation
WHAT DO THE SOURCES ESTABLISH?      brief-normalization
```

This framework answers one question in three parts:

```
HOW FAR DOES THIS IDEA ACTUALLY GO?
WHAT IS ITS CURRENT CREATIVE CEILING?
WHAT IS PREVENTING IT FROM BECOMING SUBSTANTIALLY BETTER?
```

Then CreativeDirector makes the call.

That is a conceptual relationship, not a mandatory sequence. The audit produces evidence and diagnosis. It does not produce a verdict.

## What this framework is not

It is not:

- an awards simulator, Cannes score or award-prediction engine
- a numeric creativity index
- a checklist for trophy work
- a second CreativeDirector or a second verdict system
- a universal interrogation applied to every piece of communication

The question is **how far does this idea go creatively**, not **would this win an award**.

It also carries no industry, category, channel or funnel assumption. A consumer launch, a B2B platform, a public-sector campaign, a retail act, an internal communication and a product experience are all auditable. Nothing here assumes advertising to consumers, a particular medium or a particular business model.

## 1. Eligibility

The audit is selective. Running it on everything is a misuse of it.

**Normally appropriate when:**

- the creative object has meaningful conceptual ambition
- a platform, campaign or major idea is being developed
- a hero execution is expected to carry significant creative weight
- CreativeDirector or the user asks how far the idea can go
- the work is strategically viable enough that ambition is now the useful question

**Normally unnecessary on:**

- an individual headline
- routine CRM or lifecycle messaging
- minor versioning and resizing
- simple functional or transactional copy
- production adaptations
- every social execution
- small tactical objects

`creative-object-taxonomy.md` ("Ambition eligibility") holds the object-level eligibility list. Use it rather than restating it here.

A brilliant headline is allowed to simply be a brilliant headline. A useful tactical execution does not need to change culture.

**When the object is not eligible,** say so briefly and name the lighter evaluation that fits: object-specific criteria from the taxonomy, the relevant copycraft test, or a trajectory check. Declining the audit is a legitimate and useful output.

**Eligibility is about the object's job, not its budget.** A small idea with real conceptual ambition may be eligible. An expensive execution of a settled idea may not be.

## 2. Mode behavior

### EXPLORE

Normally do not run the audit. Do not score, rank, rate altitude or kill rough ideas, and do not impose "exceptional" standards on early thinking.

Ambition provocations may be used to widen possibilities:

- What is the less expected version of this?
- What would make the product or organization necessary to the idea?
- What would make this more ownable?
- What is the braver version?
- What would make the idea *behave* differently rather than merely *say* something differently?

### DEVELOP

This is the framework's primary mode. Use it to identify:

- current creative altitude
- strongest latent creative asset
- what is holding the idea back
- whether the constraint sits in execution, idea or territory
- what kind of escalation could meaningfully improve it

### JUDGE

Apply the audit at full strength only to eligible objects. It contributes evidence to CreativeDirector's decision. It issues no verdict of its own. Verdicts are defined in the root `CLAUDE.md` and belong to CreativeDirector.

## 3. Ambition ladder

A qualitative altitude model. No numeric score, no percentage, no index.

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| **EXPECTED** | Competent and appropriate, but substantially within category or conventional expectations. |
| **STRONG** | There is a real idea, meaningful distinction and good creative craft. |
| **DISTINCTIVE** | Meaningful ownership, surprise, a memorable mechanism and clear identity. |
| **EXCEPTIONAL** | Highly ownable, consequential, difficult to arrive at, and capable of becoming reference-worthy work. |

Altitude is **descriptive, not a verdict**. EXPECTED is not a rejection; for some objects and some jobs, EXPECTED is the correct and sufficient answer. EXCEPTIONAL is not an approval; an exceptional idea can still be wrong for the brief, unaffordable or legally impossible.

Do not equate EXCEPTIONAL with loud, expensive, stunt-shaped, viral, technological or award-baiting. A quiet, reduced, almost invisible piece of work may be exceptional.

### Current altitude and reachable altitude

When useful, distinguish current altitude from a conditional estimate of reachable altitude:

```
CURRENT ALTITUDE:    where the work stands as it actually exists now
REACHABLE ALTITUDE:  a plausible development potential, conditional on stated evidence and changes
```

The distance between them is the subject of sections 4 and 5. Reachable altitude is a creative judgment, not a prediction or a proven maximum. State its conditions; if the supplied work is too limited, leave it undetermined.

When the idea is at its natural ceiling, use PUSH NONE if it is appropriate in its current form. Use PUSH EXECUTION only when a specific execution gap remains. Do not invent more work merely because no conceptual escalation is needed.

Judge current altitude on the work as presented, not on its description, its ambition or what it could become.

## 4. Core ECD lenses

Ten lenses. Use the few that the object and the problem actually need. This is not a checklist, and an audit that mechanically walks all ten is being applied badly.

Each lens below states what it asks and where it defers to another framework. Do not duplicate another framework's mechanics; cite it.

### 4.1 Strategic leap

How far did the creative idea travel from the proposition?

```
STRATEGY  →  IDEA  →  EXECUTION
```

Did something genuinely creative happen in those transitions, and how large was the transformation?

`hey-whipple-copycraft.md` (section 14, strategy is not copy) detects the binary failure: the line merely restates the proposition. This lens evaluates **magnitude and quality** of the transformation when a leap does exist. Small leaps are not automatically failures; a tiny leap in a major campaign platform usually is.

### 4.2 Tension / creative energy

Does the idea contain something alive?

Possible forms: contradiction, unresolved meaning, behavioral truth, emotional friction, surprise, humor, discomfort, desire, recognition, reversal, social tension, product contradiction.

Distinguish:

```
INFORMATION        from  TENSION
OBSERVATION        from  IDEA WITH ENERGY
```

Negativity is not required. Tension may be affectionate, funny or quiet. Mechanics live in `hey-whipple-copycraft.md` (sections 6 and 7) and `creative-trajectory-evaluation.md` (section 2); this lens asks whether the energy is strong enough to carry the ambition being claimed.

### 4.3 Ownability

Could another brand, organization, product or institution plausibly do the same thing?

Look past removing the logo. Consider product truth, brand behavior, business reality, permission, strategic territory, mechanism, tone, proof and execution.

An idea may use a universal human truth and still be uniquely ownable, when the mechanism, proof or behavior belongs to this brand alone. Conversely, a highly specific execution of a territory anyone could claim is not ownable.

### 4.4 Creative inevitability

Strong ideas are often surprising first and obvious afterward.

Once you see it, does it feel like this brand, product or organization should have been the one to do it? Do:

```
BRAND  +  PROBLEM  +  TRUTH  +  IDEA  +  EXECUTION
```

connect in an earned way?

Originality without relevance is randomness. Inevitability without surprise is convention. The lens looks for both.

### 4.5 Memorability / residue

What remains after the communication is gone?

Possible residue: an image, a phrase, a contradiction, a demonstration, a behavior, an emotion, a brand act, a new brand association, an interaction, a piece of product proof.

Do not reduce memorability to catchiness. Ask what specifically survives, and whether what survives is attached to this brand.

### 4.6 Human charge

Does the work produce a meaningful human response — recognition, humor, desire, relief, envy, pride, tension, affection, empathy, delight, discomfort, surprise?

Sentimental advertising is not required, and emotional register is not a quality ranking. A clever idea with little human charge may still be strong; this lens helps locate its ceiling rather than condemn it.

### 4.7 Context / cultural relevance

Use only where relevant.

Does the idea meaningfully connect to real behavior, category rituals, social norms, live conversations, contradictions, cultural conditions, moments or environments?

Distinguish **using culture** from **having cultural relevance**. Borrowed surface references, trend-jacking and memetic decoration are not cultural relevance. Do not require culture on every brief, and do not reward a cultural reference that the idea does not need.

### 4.8 Medium / execution inevitability

Does the medium materially strengthen or complete the idea?

Could the identical thought move between film, print, social, OOH, activation, CRM, product and experience with almost no change?

Portability is not automatically bad — a platform often should travel. But sometimes portability reveals that the medium merely *carries* the idea rather than *completing* it. Use `creative-object-taxonomy.md` for what the object is meant to do in its medium, and the 1 + 1 = 3 test in `creative-trajectory-evaluation.md` (section 10) for visual-verbal work.

### 4.9 Generative power

Apply only when the object is meant to generate a campaign or system.

```
DOES THE IDEA CREATE MORE IDEAS?        not        CAN THE TEMPLATE BE REPEATED?
```

The mechanics are in `hey-whipple-copycraft.md` (section 12, campaign generativity) and the parent/child tests in `creative-object-taxonomy.md`. This lens asks how far the generated family could go, not merely whether one exists.

### 4.10 Creative courage

Does the work make a meaningful choice?

Courage may look like honesty, restraint, specificity, tonal commitment, exposing a real tension, rejecting a category convention, unusual simplicity, unusual media behavior, or allowing the audience to complete the thought.

Do not reward provocation, controversy or risk for their own sake. Quiet can be brave. Refusing to explain can be brave.

## 5. Creative ceiling

This is the framework's central output.

```
WHAT IS PREVENTING THIS IDEA FROM BECOMING SUBSTANTIALLY BETTER?
```

Name the constraint as specifically as the evidence allows. Possible constraints include:

- weak strategic foundation
- generic human truth
- insufficient transformation from strategy to idea
- low ownership
- familiar mechanism
- missing tension
- product or organizational irrelevance
- weak proof
- medium mismatch
- execution failing the idea
- over-explanation
- lack of human charge
- contextual irrelevance where context matters
- a platform that cannot generate
- a craft ceiling
- nothing: the idea is already fully expressed and simply needs execution

Name **one** principal constraint wherever possible. Several named constraints usually mean the real constraint has not been found yet, or that the work has more than one idea in it.

Not every idea can or should become exceptional. This is a legitimate diagnosis:

```
THIS IS A GOOD IDEA AT ITS NATURAL CEILING.
```

So is this one:

```
THE CONSTRAINT IS NOT CREATIVE. IT IS A REAL BUSINESS OR LEGAL CONSTRAINT.   (section 9)
```

## 6. PUSH: execution / idea / territory / none

The ceiling diagnosis resolves into one of four fundamentally different moves. Naming the wrong one wastes a round of work.

| PUSH | Meaning | Typical next step |
|---|---|---|
| **EXECUTION** | The idea is strong enough; the execution is not realizing it yet. | Craft, art direction, copy, medium, reduction. |
| **IDEA** | The territory is promising, but the creative mechanism needs a bigger leap. | Return to DEVELOP, or a focused divergent round inside the territory. |
| **TERRITORY** | The underlying strategic or creative territory has reached its ceiling. More craft will not solve it. | Return to EXPLORE, or to `brief-machine` if the foundation is the problem. |
| **NONE** | The work is appropriate at its current ambition, or the idea has reached a strong natural form. | Make it well. |

Do not automatically escalate. PUSH NONE is a real and frequently correct answer.

PUSH names where the constraint sits, not how much effort to spend. CreativeDirector decides whether the push is worth taking.

## 7. Escalation

Escalation means finding a meaningful way to make the idea:

more consequential · more ownable · more surprising · more emotionally potent · more behaviorally real · more product-proven · more native to its medium · more participatory · more culturally connected where relevant · more elegantly reduced.

Escalation does **not** automatically mean more budget, more channels, celebrity, technology, a stunt, AR, AI, an activation, bigger production or controversy. Reduction is frequently the strongest escalation available.

Useful provocations, not requirements:

- Can the product prove the idea?
- Can the brand make the claim physically true?
- Can the communication become a behavior?
- Can the audience complete or participate in it?
- Can the medium become part of the mechanism?
- Can the context create the meaning?
- Can we remove explanation rather than add scale?
- Can the idea become something people experience instead of something they are told?
- Can one distinctive act do the work of twenty executions?

Escalation must stay inside the brief's real constraints and the claim ledger. An escalation that requires an unsupported claim, an unavailable proof or a prohibited behavior is not an escalation; it is a new open question. See `brief-normalization.md` sections 5 and 10.

## 8. Polish trap and case-study inflation

Two failures that make ordinary work look ambitious. Both are diagnoses about the gap between the object and its presentation.

### 8.1 Polish trap

```
HIGH CRAFT  +  LOW IDEA
```

CreativeDirector must be free to conclude: *beautifully executed ordinary work.*

Do not let production value, visual polish, famous talent, complexity or technology substitute for conceptual strength.

Recognize the inverse as potentially valuable DEVELOP work:

```
STRONG IDEA  +  ROUGH EXECUTION
```

A raw exceptional idea beats a polished mediocre one. The taxonomy's three levels — strategic fitness, conceptual quality, craft quality — keep these separable; name which level is strong and which is weak.

### 8.2 Case-study inflation test

```
IF THE CASE-STUDY NARRATION DISAPPEARED, WOULD THE IDEA STILL BE IMPRESSIVE?
```

Watch for:

- inflated cultural claims
- strategy language making ordinary work sound profound
- impact unsupported by evidence
- complexity mistaken for ambition
- media quantity mistaken for idea strength
- purpose language elevating ordinary communication
- claimed behavior change with no evidence

Judge the creative object, not its retrospective narration. `creative-trajectory-evaluation.md` (section 11) carries the related case-study, vanity-metric and technology warnings as trajectory diagnostics; this test asks specifically whether the ambition being claimed survives without the narration.

## 9. Quality, preference, constraint and restriction

Keep four different things apart. Collapsing them produces false creative diagnoses.

| Category | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Creative quality** | How far the idea goes, judged by this framework. |
| **Client preference** | What this client likes, dislikes or has rejected before. Client and project knowledge. |
| **Business / production constraint** | Budget, timing, feasibility, rights, operations, resourcing. |
| **Legal / claim restriction** | What may be said, shown or promised. The claim ledger and approved legal sources. |

An idea may be creatively strong and still inappropriate because of a real constraint. Say exactly that, rather than lowering the altitude to justify the outcome.

"This client does not like humor" is a client preference recorded in client knowledge. It is not a universal creative principle and it is never evidence about another client.

## 10. Effectiveness

If real effectiveness data exists for this client and project, it is relevant evidence. Cite it with its source, project, date and status.

Do not invent, estimate or imply it. Creative ambition and effectiveness are related but not identical: an idea can be distinctive with no performance data available, and an idea can perform well while being creatively ordinary.

Do not manufacture outcomes, projected reach, engagement or behavior change to support an altitude.

## 11. Evidence and client / project scope

The audit works from:

```
GLOBAL METHOD
+
CURRENT CLIENT KNOWLEDGE
+
CURRENT PROJECT EVIDENCE
+
CURRENT WORK PACKET
```

```
SHARED INTELLIGENCE.  ISOLATED CLIENT TRUTH.
```

- It may not use another client's creative precedent, audience insight, preferences, campaign performance, claims, strategy or proof. Inside one client's work, other clients' workspaces are not eligible evidence.
- Historical work from the **same** client may be used deliberately when relevant, labeled as historical evidence with its project, date and status. It informs; it does not override current evidence.
- Published creative work from the wider culture is creative reference, not client evidence. Name it as reference.
- **Name every external reference used in a judgment.** Name the external campaign, organization, cultural reference or competitive example supporting an ownability judgment, and cite a supporting source when one is available.
- **Label an unverified assertion UNVERIFIED.** A factual assertion with no verified source is a hypothesis to check, not established evidence, and it may not carry an ownability conclusion by itself. State what would verify it. This applies equally to an assertion supplied with the task, such as an unsourced competitive claim in a brief or accompanying note, and to an assertion the agent introduces, including one presented as "general category knowledge". Grouping unsupported assertions under general knowledge does not exempt them.
- **Naming is not substantiation.** Naming an organization or framework does not substantiate a claim about its campaigns, practices, results or competitive position. When an external factual assertion supports a judgment, either cite a verified supporting source, or label it UNVERIFIED, state what needs checking and do not treat it as established evidence.
- **Interpretation is not an external claim.** A creative interpretation ("the mechanism feels familiar", "the line has no tension") is judgment and needs no citation. An assertion about what exists outside the work ("competitors already run this", "the category is saturated with it") is an external factual claim and follows the rules above.
- Permission to use public creative references does not establish factual accuracy. Do not invent references, quotations, results or competitive claims.
- "Client A liked this" is never evidence that "Client B should like this."
- Ownability (4.3) is judged against the category and the competitive field, not against other CreativeEngine clients' work.
- The full rules are in the root `CLAUDE.md` ("Clients, projects and knowledge scope") and `projects/README.md`.

## 12. Relationship to existing frameworks

| Source | Owns |
|---|---|
| `creative-object-taxonomy.md` | What kind of creative object this is and what job it has |
| `hey-whipple-copycraft.md` | How ideas and language may be generated, developed and crafted |
| `creative-trajectory-evaluation.md` | Whether the work is moving beyond literal, generic and expected advertising |
| `brief-normalization.md` | What source evidence establishes, what is derived, and what creative problem exists |
| `creative-direction-audit.md` (this file) | How ambitious, distinctive and consequential the idea is, and what limits its ceiling |
| CreativeDirector | Which lenses matter, and what the final call is |

This framework adds a layer. It does not supersede, replace or absorb any of the others.

**Count each underlying weakness once.** When this audit and another lens identify the same underlying problem — cliché, strategy restatement, redundant visual-verbal relationship, over-explanation — record it once under the most specific useful diagnosis. This framework may add ambition evidence to that entry. It must not create a second weakness, a second penalty or a second verdict. The rule is in `CreativeDirector/AGENT.md` ("Framework orchestration").

Where trajectory evaluation asks whether the work escaped the generic, this framework asks how far it went after escaping. A piece of work can pass trajectory evaluation and still be EXPECTED.

## 13. ECD compression

After an audit, the idea should compress to four sentences:

```
THE IDEA:               [one concise sentence]
WHAT MAKES IT STRONG:   [one concise sentence]
WHAT LIMITS IT:         [one concise sentence]
BIGGEST OPPORTUNITY:    [one concise sentence]
```

If this is impossible, investigate the cause: unclear strategy, a weak thought, several simultaneous ideas, or over-intellectualized explanation. Complexity is not depth.

## 14. Output

When useful, the audit may surface:

```
OBJECT:                         [from creative-object-taxonomy]
CURRENT ALTITUDE:               EXPECTED / STRONG / DISTINCTIVE / EXCEPTIONAL
REACHABLE ALTITUDE:             [same scale, or "at its ceiling"]

THE IDEA:                       [one sentence]
STRONGEST CREATIVE ASSET:       [one sentence]
CREATIVE CEILING:               [the principal constraint]
WHAT LIMITS IT:                 [how that constraint shows up in this work]
BIGGEST ESCALATION OPPORTUNITY: [one sentence]

PUSH:                           EXECUTION / IDEA / TERRITORY / NONE
ECD VIEW:                       [concise, direct]
```

- None of this is mandatory. CreativeDirector may answer simply when the task requires a simple answer.
- No numeric score belongs in this output.
- **An audit request is answered with the audit.** When the user asks only for a creative ceiling audit, an eligibility check or a lighter review, return the assessment and recommended next steps (for an audit, the diagnosis and the PUSH recommendation). Do not append KILL, REWORK, ADVANCE or BET THE PITCH. Critique and revision recommendations remain allowed.
- A formal verdict is a separate CreativeDirector decision. It is appropriate when the user requests a decision, or when the assignment itself includes selection or approval.
- PUSH says where the constraint sits. It is not a verdict, and it does not imply one.
- Do not expose hidden chain-of-thought or lengthy internal reasoning. Surface the decision artifacts only.
- When the object is ineligible, the output is one or two sentences saying so and naming the lighter evaluation that fits.

## 15. Framework principle

Creative direction is not the search for more. It is the search for the strongest form the idea can truthfully become.

Sometimes that means escalation. Sometimes it means reduction. Sometimes it means recognizing that the idea is already what it is, and making it well.
