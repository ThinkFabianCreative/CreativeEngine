# Transform Awards North America 2025: what won, and why

> Shared knowledge, global method. Read by ArtTeacher on 2026-11-18 and written up as teaching material for every role. Outside reference about how award-winning branding works. **It holds no CreativeEngine client facts, and none of the brands below is evidence about any CreativeEngine client.**

**Source:** *Transform Awards North America 2025 Winners Book*, Transform magazine (Cravenhill Publishing), 168 pages, PDF dated 30 Sep 2025, revised 8 Oct 2025. https://www.transformmagazine.net/media/20075/tana-2025_winners-book_online_v2.pdf. Page numbers below are the book's printed page numbers, which match the PDF page numbers.

## How to read this file

- **Three kinds of statement, kept apart.** *Entry and jury* is a paraphrase of what the book says: the publisher's summary of the entry, plus single-judge comments. *ArtTeacher reads* is our own design reading of the image(s) printed in the book. *Lesson* is what a CreativeEngine role should take from it.
- **Results are entrant claims.** Every number in the book (impressions, sales, visitors) comes from the entry. None is verified here. Use them as evidence of what juries reward, never as benchmarks.
- **The book shows little.** Most entries get one image and about 80 words; golds get a larger image and about 200 words. Readings are from that alone, so they are labeled as readings, not audits. Motion, sound and interaction are only described in text; none can be seen or heard in a print book.
- **Jury language is thin.** Comments are mostly adjectives ("fresh", "modern", "bold", "fun"). Treat jury praise as a signal of what impressed, not as craft criticism. The stronger evidence is what the winners have in common.
- **Quotes are kept to a minimum.** The book's text is paraphrased. Short brand lines are quoted only where the line itself is the point.
- **These are candidate lessons, not method yet.** Under `shared/frameworks/critique-learning-loop.md` §4 a lesson enters a framework only after it recurs on independent work or Andres approves it, as a reviewed diff. Section 3 marks which lessons have the most support here.

## 1. The two grand winners

**Grand Prix: Saucy, for Yum! Brands (KFC), by ROWE** (p.166). A new quick-service restaurant brand built around one product truth: tenders served with 11 signature sauces. The sauces drive the name, the color (hot pink and red), the wordmark (puffy, glossy, dripping letterforms), the interior (striped walls, pendant lights shaped like drips) and the tone (cheeky, with pixel "censorship" over the food, described but not shown). It won eight awards, more than any other entry, across visual property, experience, implementation, strategy, portfolio extension, naming, sector and the Grand Prix. **Why it won, in our reading:** every layer says the same single thing, and the name is the idea. Strategy, name, identity, space and operations (drive-thru, kiosks, app, concept to opening in 322 days per the entry) were designed together.

**Best Overall Visual Identity: Bank of America, by Lippincott** (p.164). Instead of inventing, Lippincott took the bank's most recognized asset, the Flagscape symbol, and broke it into parts (stripes, the flag's motion, the red/navy field) that recombine into patterns, frames, embossing and illustration (seen on p.125: a red embossed box, a navy line-field on a tablet, a Boston Marathon pin, floral graphics in the bank's red and blue). The entry's problem statement was a "sea of sameness": competitors had copied the bank's earlier customer-first look. **Why it won, in our reading:** distinctiveness came from depth, not novelty. One owned asset, deconstructed, gives enough range for a marathon, a tech forum and Merrill's wealth materials, while staying recognizably the bank.

## 2. Patterns across the winners

Each pattern lists its strongest examples. Counts are approximate and come from the 112 projects in section 4.

1. **Mine the asset you already own before inventing a new one.** Equity-led evolutions won repeatedly: Bank of America's Flagscape (p.164), Pillsbury's Doughboy and barrelhead plus the Doughboy giggle as sonic logo (pp.66, 101, 128), Lincoln Financial's Lincoln profile (p.126), PLI's red expanded into a subject color system (p.143), Gatorade's bolt on a kids' bottle (p.112), Colgate Total's spectrum (p.129), Maybelline's jingle remade (pp.101, 104), Cuisinart's history (p.66), Orangetheory's orange kept and heated with gradients (p.146). *Lesson:* before proposing a new mark, list what the brand already owns in memory and test whether a deconstruction of it can do the job.

2. **One product or place truth becomes the whole system.** Saucy's sauces; Fruta's oversized orange (p.111); Penta's five-color pentagram for a paint brand (p.110); Superfeet's green insole turned into a neon super-green palette (p.146); Redbreast's robin breast turned into a whisky drop (p.131); Flexcon's layered mark from roll-to-roll manufacturing (p.63); Lobos 1707's wolf and orange label (p.56). *Lesson:* the strongest systems derive from something true and specific about the product, not from a mood.

3. **The name does strategic work.** Naming wins were names that unlocked the system or the story: Saucy; Humane World for Animals, whose rename made the new globe icon possible and fixed a "US-only" misreading (p.118); FFX NOVA, renaming a county to escape old perceptions (p.52); Onvida, "onward" plus Spanish *vida* for a bilingual community (p.82); Cinq, French for five, pointing to the points of Coinstar's star (p.81); Elimini, eliminate plus illuminate (p.72); Polyphonic for a multi-partner health data ecosystem (p.80); Nut Duos (p.76); Candy Con (p.151); Blue Flamingo (p.71); the coined word "Schmoooth" (p.31). *Lesson:* a name earns its keep when design and copy can build on it. Judge names by what they let the system do.

4. **Local, specific detail beats category generics.** Endura's voice is built from how Scots really speak, with a phrasebook to avoid "tartan-ifying" (p.29). Denver's 16th Street uses icons by a local artist, YAMZ (p.40). New York Golf Club combines the state bird (bluebird and "birdie"), a subway-style roundel and classic NYC type (p.44). Britt uses a native flower found only in its region (p.119). North City takes its palette from local landscapes (p.71). Doritos Feira Preta packs were drawn by three local Black Brazilian artists (p.115). NYU's signage borrows Industry City's vernacular signs (p.37). *Lesson:* specificity is visible. Ask "what could only belong here?" before choosing type, color or icons.

5. **Name the category code, then break it on purpose.** Judges rewarded entries that stated the convention they were leaving: Bank of America (sameness), The Baldwin Group (bright gradients against monotone competitors, p.92), Cohesity (optimism instead of cybersecurity fear, p.86), Schneider Electric (cheek instead of engineering jargon, p.31), BrainCheck and Onpoint (warmth instead of sterile clinical looks, pp.132, 135), Amica (empathy instead of insurance jingles, p.100), Candy Con (the "ultimate second controller" instead of elite-gamer tropes, p.151), Ferrous Lighting (craft tension instead of sterile minimal or overdecorated retail, p.145), Adams & Reese (against legal tropes, p.143), BACARDÍ (emerging artists instead of a mnemonic, p.106). *Lesson:* the brief and the presentation should name the code in one line; the work should be visibly not that.

6. **Warmth and wit are allowed in serious categories.** Health, law, insurance, banking and B2B winners leaned warm and witty: BrainCheck's sun yellow and the line "Don't raincheck the BrainCheck" (p.132), Onvida's orange (p.82), Rize's documentary portraits (p.32), Copilot's natural palette and candid photography to ease AI skepticism (p.76), Axinn's "Seriously Friendly" voice tiles (p.31), Schmoooth, Sizzler's "sizzzzle" (p.47), Plano's "Lures are only lucky if you know where they are" (p.57), New York Golf Club's "Always circle the birdies" (p.72). "Cheeky", "playful" and "fun" recur in jury comments. *Lesson:* a human voice is now table stakes in rational categories; what distinguishes is whether the wit is specific to the brand (Schmoooth only works for "everything runs smoother").

7. **A kit of parts, not a logo.** Systems that flex scored well: Bank of America's deconstruct-and-recombine toolkit; Greenlane's few graphic elements used across signage, apps and billboards (p.123); Pearson's Ripple and graphic waves (p.121); ZACH Theater's Light Line and fluid type (pp.90, 120); Orangetheory's modular motion language (p.146); LS Power's mark reused as a photo window on the website (pp.28, 95); Cargill's portfolio of eight brands on one global/local system (p.27). *Lesson:* show the system's grammar (what recombines, what never changes) and prove it on very different touchpoints.

8. **Strategy reframes come first, and judges reward them explicitly.** Gold Creative Strategy entries show a research finding that changed the question: Fairfax found its value was talent, not infrastructure (p.52); Confluent Health moved from physical therapy provider to "partner in national resilience" (p.55); Feast moved beyond food quality to curiosity (p.65); Ara Partners found missing chapters in its story, plant-building experience and analytical rigor (p.89); Lobos built on a founder from a Spanish wine family (p.56). *Lesson:* one sentence of reframe, then one decisive move (often a rename), then the system.

9. **Experience, implementation and operations count as brand.** Saucy's space and back-of-house; Planting Fields' ten color-coded zones that also reshaped programming (p.36); PLI's personalization, "Netflix of continuing education" (p.94); Greenlane's mark as a roadside beacon (p.123); internal rollout (Adams & Reese partner workshops and town halls, p.45; New York Golf Club's high-profile stakeholders, p.44). *Lesson:* the craft of the rollout is judged as craft.

10. **Simplifying architecture is creative work.** Cargill 70 brands to 8 (p.27); Baldwin 56 brands to 1 (p.92); CRISIL six units under one masterbrand (pp.28, 93); Precision AQ nine organizations under one name (p.83); Certerra 16 companies (p.87); Belden to a branded house (p.68); GIP keeping its blue diamond beside BlackRock's black one (p.87). *Lesson:* a clear architecture move, stated as before/after numbers, reads as strategy to a jury.

11. **Sound wins when it does a job.** Uber's three notes mirror ride anticipation with a pause and a release (p.102); Amica's call-and-response motif follows its animated "A" (p.100); Northwell turned its nurse choir's voices into a playable sound library (p.102); TD put its sonic logo in the ATM (p.105); Marantz designed product UX sounds (p.105); Maybelline treated the brand as an artist and launched through creators (p.104); BACARDÍ found artists already making its sound instead of writing a mnemonic (p.106). *Lesson:* a sonic identity needs a functional or emotional moment to live in, not only a logo jingle.

12. **Limited editions win on culture and collectibility.** Pepsi's five illustrated Gladiator II cans (p.114), ANGOSTURA's dark-academia archival volume (p.114), Doritos Feira Preta (p.115), Dove x Crumbl drop culture (p.115), Gatorade's rite-of-passage Rookie bottle and 90,000 AI-generated personal bottles (pp.112–113). *Lesson:* limited packaging is a media and fandom idea first, a pack second.

13. **Every gold arrives with a result.** Nearly all gold write-ups cite an outcome (earned impressions, visitors, sales, sign-ups, engagement). *Lesson for selling the work:* plan the measure with the idea (`selling-the-work.md`, `creative-effectiveness.md`).

### ArtTeacher's counter-reading: what the book also shows

- **The caption test.** The strongest winners can be understood from the picture alone: Saucy's dripping letters, the golf club's bluebird in a subway roundel, Humane World's globe on a jacket next to a horse, Denver's icons standing in for letters. Weaker entries need the caption to explain the mark ("represents connection and strength", "evokes trust", "symbolizes transformation"). A mark whose meaning lives only in the rationale is not yet doing its job (`barry-concept-art-direction.md`: show, don't tell).
- **Generic tells are present among winners too.** Soft gradients, lowercase geometric wordmarks, "warm and human" photography of smiling professionals, and abstract marks with assigned meanings recur across several silver and bronze B2B and health entries. Winning a category does not make these choices distinctive; it often means the category's other entries were weaker. Do not copy the median winner.
- **Typography carried more than color in the best work.** Saucy's custom display type, the Denver poster's heavy condensed caps with icons inline, Fairfax's "FFX MARKS THE SPOT", Candy Con's bubbly wordmark against condensed "CREATE. PLAY. REPEAT.", ZACH's monoline script and outline type, Axinn's serif values list. Where the type was generic, the identity leaned on the mark and palette alone and read as interchangeable.
- **Most imagery is still the mockup.** Many pages show devices, business cards and billboards in perspective. The images that persuade show the identity in the world: a horse and a hand (Humane World), a window poster on a street (Denver), a taxi wrap at a crossing (Cohesity), a reception sculpture (Ara).

## 3. What this means for each role

Strength of support in brackets: **strong** = recurs across many independent winners; **moderate** = several; **single** = one striking case worth remembering.

| Role | Take this from the 2025 winners |
|---|---|
| `brief-machine` | Ask for the brand's existing memory assets and the category code to break, in the brief's own fields [strong]. Carry the strategic reframe as a plain belief, not a line [strong]. Ask what is specific to the place, people or product [strong]. Ask what result would prove it worked [strong]. Do not write the name or the look into the brief (`WORKING-PREFERENCES.md`). |
| `creative-strategist` | Lead with a reframe from evidence (Fairfax, Confluent, Feast, Ara) [strong]. Check whether a rename or architecture simplification is the decisive move [moderate]. Make the distinctive assets an explicit part of the brand house (Flagscape, Doughboy, bolt) [strong]. Set the Bar knowing that juries reward a stated "sea of sameness" escape [strong]. |
| `creative-researcher` | Audit the client's existing memory assets and the category's visual and verbal codes before territories are written [strong]. For place and culture work, find the local vernacular (signs, flora, speech, artists) and document provenance [strong]. Collect outcome evidence alongside references. Note that award books are entrant-written: tag any figure from them as an entrant claim. |
| `word-smither` | Treat names as systems: test what design can build on a name [strong]. Brand-specific wit beats generic warmth (Schmoooth, "Don't raincheck the BrainCheck", "Always circle the birdies") [moderate]. Build voice from real speech and a usable tool (Endura's phrasebook) [single, but a clear model]. Write lines the layout can carry: Fairfax's "FFX MARKS THE SPOT" works because the X is also the image window. |
| `design-champ` | Start from an owned asset or one product truth, then build a kit of parts with a stated grammar [strong]. Prove flex on very different touchpoints, including environment and signage [strong]. Make type do conceptual work, not only color [moderate, ArtTeacher reading]. Pass the caption test [ArtTeacher reading]. Avoid the median-winner look: gradient, lowercase geometric wordmark, assigned-meaning abstract mark. |
| `creative-engine-director` | In JUDGE, ask: does every layer say the same single thing (Saucy)? Is distinctiveness from depth or only novelty (Bank of America)? Does the mark pass the caption test? Is the result planned? Award-level work in this book combines one truth, a name or asset that carries it, and a system proven in the world. Treat jury adjectives as weak evidence. |
| `sprint-facilitator` | Use stakeholder workshops to find the reframe and the local specifics (Cargill's 20 interviews, Cohesity's and North City's workshops, Adams & Reese town halls for buy-in) [moderate]. Internal rollout is itself award-worthy craft. |
| `art-teacher` | Teach with these cases: deconstructing an asset (Bank of America), one truth to a whole world (Saucy), vernacular design (Denver, NYGC, Endura), the caption test, and the median-winner trap. Verify any case against the agency's own case study before citing it in client work. |

`creative-admin` is out of scope.

## 4. Every winning project

Ordered by first appearance in the book. Awards: G = Gold, S = Silver, B = Bronze, HC = Highly commended. Agency names as printed. Where the book names no agency, that is stated.

### Content

**Ladybird, by Nimblebot.** G Visual Property (p.24).
Entry and jury: an independent open-source browser valuing simplicity, independence and honesty; identity enhanced by two logo animations inspired by the insect's movement; judges praised the modernization.
ArtTeacher reads: phone UI on a lavender-to-blue field, a compact line mark, calm sans type, values set as a list (Independence, Simplicity, Freedom, Openness). The animation is described, not shown.
Lesson: for a product with no physical presence, motion of the mark can be the visual property. Brief the motion, not only the static logo.

**Saucy (Yum! Brands / KFC), by ROWE.** G Visual Property (p.24), G Brand Experience (p.33), G Implementation (p.46), S Creative Strategy Business (p.53), G New Brand in Portfolio (p.75), G Naming New (p.79), G Food and Beverage (p.130), Grand Prix (p.166).
Entry and jury: KFC could not attract Gen Z; Saucy puts tenders and 11 sauces at the center; pixels "censor" the food; prototype built in nine months, 322 days concept to opening; 2.4 billion earned impressions claimed; judges called it daring, disruptive and on target for the demographic.
ArtTeacher reads: custom bubbly display wordmark with glossy pink extrusion and an exclamation mark; hot pink exterior, red-striped interior, drip-shaped pendants, orange booths; "by KFC" endorsement kept small. One truth expressed in every medium. See section 1.
Lesson: the name, the type and the space are one idea. The endorsement stays subordinate so the new brand can own its world.

**TOI, by Living Group.** S Visual Property (p.25), G Startup (p.149).
Entry and jury: blockchain startup regaining share; name from the exoplanet TOI-700; identity built on the perfect sphere in the logo.
ArtTeacher reads: black and mint, heavy geometric wordmark where the O is a solid circle, stacked condensed claims ("Scalable. Secured. Reliable. Efficient."), hard silhouette photography.
Lesson: a single geometric primitive can carry a tech identity if it is used with conviction across crop, scale and photography.

**Viiota, by Starfish Co.** Visual Property (p.25: badge Bronze; winners list p.16 says Silver, an open discrepancy in the source), G Healthcare (p.132).
Entry and jury: weight-loss and gut-microbiome program; scientific elegance, hopeful transformation, inclusive, gender-neutral palette; judges liked a logo that suggests microbiology.
ArtTeacher reads: editorial web layouts, muted sage and teal with a lime accent, serif headlines, body photography with a radiant line mark over the stomach.
Lesson: in a stigma-heavy category, editorial calm and real bodies read as credible; the radiant line gives the science a visible form.

**Prime Data Centers, by Starfish Co.** B Visual Property (p.26), B New Brand (p.74), S Technology, Media and Telecoms (p.152).
Entry and jury: custom data-center builder repositioned as a premium boutique partner; bold architectural logo; minimal system; natural tones.
ArtTeacher reads: misty landscape with a rectangular portal cut into it and a lone figure; an eye reflecting a lit city; spaced capitals. Atmospheric photography does the premium work.
Lesson: an infrastructure brand can sell care and scale through framing and restraint instead of server imagery.

**Cargill Animal Nutrition & Health, by Vivaldi.** G Brand Architecture (p.27). (The book prints "Animation"; "Animal" is our inference.)
Entry and jury: three legacy feed-additive businesses unified; 70 brands to 8 after 20 stakeholder interviews; high-equity brands moved from technology-led to species-led; judges called the architecture clear and organized.
ArtTeacher reads: four product brands (Diamond V, Notox, Enzae, TruEquine) on color-coded tiles, each with a single-line icon and the same sans wordmark.
Lesson: an architecture solution is shown as a consistent grammar (icon + wordmark + color) that still lets each brand be found.

**LS Power, by Prophecy.** S Brand Architecture (p.28), B Brand Evolution Corporate (p.70), B Digital Transformation (p.95), B Energy (p.124).
Entry and jury: three arms organized under the original mission of solving complex energy problems; vibrant orange; judges praised the logo and called it a textbook approach.
ArtTeacher reads: an orange mark of two rounded interlocking loops; the same shape reused as a window cropping a transmission-tower photo on the site.
Lesson: a mark that can become a frame multiplies its usefulness across digital and print.

**CRISIL, by Starfish Co.** B Brand Architecture (p.28), S Brand Consolidation (p.93), B Advisors and Consultants (p.141).
Entry and jury: fragmented Indian ratings and analytics firm distilled into a masterbrand with six units; platform "The Coefficient of Confidence".
ArtTeacher reads: bold white wordmark over a macro of a spiraling succulent; a lime field with a blue spiral stair. The spiral recurs as a quiet growth motif.
Lesson: nature macros signal precision and growth, but they are common in finance; the spiral motif is what makes them belong.

**Endura, by Reed Words.** G Copy Style or Tone of Voice (p.29).
Entry and jury: Scottish cycling apparel brand; voice built from how Scots actually speak, not tartan clichés; a phrasebook keeps copy authentic; judges praised the framing of the challenge and the laser focus.
ArtTeacher reads: muddy action photo with a tall condensed yellow headline, "The voice of a joyrider. Fuelled by off-kilter euphoria."
Lesson: verbal identity built from real speech and delivered as a working tool beats adjectives in a guideline.

**Fairfax County Economic Development Authority (FFX NOVA), by Monigle.** S Copy Style (p.30), S Place Brand (p.41), G Creative Strategy Business (p.52).
Entry and jury: losing young professionals to Austin and Silicon Valley; research found talent sustainability, not infrastructure, was the value; renamed the region's identity FFX NOVA; designed like a lifestyle brand; claimed traffic surge crashed the talent site; voice line "Pursue purpose by choosing your own adventure".
ArtTeacher reads: "FFX MARKS THE SPOT" in heavy condensed type beside a giant X used as a window holding a collage (portrait, instruments, kayak, drones); purple and bright accents; environmental cubes reading "PIVOT HERE".
Lesson: line, letterform and image are one device. The X is the name, the headline and the frame.

**Microsoft 50th anniversary "Change needs makers", by Koto.** S Copy Style (p.30), B Brand Experience (p.35).
Entry and jury: verbal identity honoring invention and reinvention; focus on what people made with Microsoft; record engagement claimed.
ArtTeacher reads: a pixelated 3D "50" on pink with icons from the company's history (Paint, Windows, Teams, cursors).
Lesson: an anniversary works when it celebrates users' making, with the brand's own nostalgic assets as the vocabulary.

**Schneider Electric, by Creative Business Company.** S Copy Style (p.31), S Creative Strategy Business (p.53).
Entry and jury: US industrial automation division lacked visibility; challenger strategy; coined "Schmoooth", a cheeky verbal identity unlike jargon-heavy competitors.
ArtTeacher reads: green billboard, "Stuck between a rock and a hard place? Everything runs schmooooother with Schneider."; trade booth headline "Schmooooooth?". The stretched word is a visual and verbal device at once.
Lesson: in a jargon category, one coined word with a benefit inside it can carry a whole campaign system.

**Axinn, by Living Group.** B Copy Style (p.31), B Implementation (p.48), B Creative Strategy Business (p.55), G Legal and Accountancy (p.142).
Entry and jury: specialist law firm; values invested, incisive, inclusive, inquisitive; sophisticated palette, elegant serif, an "a" avatar used as a seal; large digital increases claimed (292% website activity).
ArtTeacher reads: deep teal, coral and brown; lowercase serif wordmark; values set as an italic serif list; patterned eye and arc murals; voice tiles ("Serious. Smart. No-nonsense. Committed." / "Seriously Friendly.").
Lesson: a law firm can be warm without losing authority when serif type and a disciplined palette hold the seriousness.

**Rize Credit Union, by London : Los Angeles.** B Copy Style (p.32), S Financial Services (p.126).
Entry and jury: formerly SCE Credit Union for utility employees; renamed from the idea "Realizing Potential" to reach underserved people; colorful, human, accessible.
ArtTeacher reads: lowercase "rize" with a rising orange accent, "Beyond Banking", documentary portraits at home.
Lesson: a rename from an employer's initials to an aspiration opens the audience; the accent mark carries the verb.

**West Monroe, by Monigle.** B Copy Style (p.32), G Advisors and Consultants (p.139).
Entry and jury: consulting firm; "value accelerator" concept from staff's fast, personal, collaborative ways of working; concept used internally in meetings and recruiting; double-digit web traffic gains claimed.
ArtTeacher reads: navy square with a ring of dots, yellow-highlighted labels ("COLLABORATION"), line illustration with flat color, isometric 3D, bright accent chips.
Lesson: a concept that staff actually repeat is the proof of a professional-services brand; the visual system stays energetic rather than corporate blue alone.

**PUMA, by SGK.** G Brand Experience (p.33).
Entry and jury: a Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles basketball activation at NBA All-Star Weekend in a tight San Francisco space; arcade-style "Krang's Court", turtle pizzas, prizes.
ArtTeacher reads: dark arcade, neon green court lines, purple light, hoops as stage.
Lesson: an experience wins on one playable mechanic plus a licensed world, not on signage.

**Google Android Auto website, by Work & Co.** S Brand Experience (p.34).
Entry and jury: site that lets users picture the system in their own car and find information fast.
ArtTeacher reads: clean product site, dashboard photography, one clear call to action.
Lesson: for a product brand, the experience award goes to clarity of the task.

**Microsoft Pride "Radical Joy", by Sterling Brands.** S Brand Experience (p.34).
Entry and jury: movement by Microsoft's LGBTQIA+ community; shapes and colors from Pride flags; people shared their stories.
ArtTeacher reads: maximal, hand-lettered "RADICAL JOY" inside concentric flag-colored outlines.
Lesson: community-owned movements get permission to be maximal; hand-lettering signals people, not corporation.

**Microsoft voter registration "Be part of something bigger", by O0.** B Brand Experience (p.35).
Entry and jury: Gen Z voter push across Xbox to Windows; vibrant mix of type, color and messages; over 3,000 registrations claimed.
ArtTeacher reads: sticker-collage of mixed lettering ("BE A VOTER", "Register to vote") on navy.
Lesson: a deliberately mixed type system reads as many voices joining in.

**Planting Fields Foundation, by Wiedemann Lampe.** G Wayfinding or Signage (p.36).
Entry and jury: historic arboretum too complex for visitors; ten color-coded zones; "Unfurl" strategy; zoning also shaped programming.
ArtTeacher reads: deep green sign family with white sans, zone color bars, arrow pictograms, a map panel, set into planting.
Lesson: wayfinding that restructures the place (zones) is worth more than signs that label it.

**ING Finance Forward, by Teneo.** B Wayfinding or Signage (p.37).
Entry and jury: event signage in line with ING's brand; films, banners, QR column wraps, a live sketch artist guiding breakouts.
ArtTeacher reads: orange column wraps and event panels in a lounge setting.
Lesson: event wayfinding can carry content (QR, live drawing), not only directions.

**NYU Martin Scorsese Virtual Production Center, by Via Collective.** B Wayfinding or Signage (p.37; Via's own ad on p.38 shows more).
Entry and jury: system inspired by Industry City's vernacular signage; bright floor and wall graphics; wayfinding explores photography fundamentals.
ArtTeacher reads: large illuminated condensed letters and arrows ("619–638", "TV STUDIOS") hung in an industrial corridor; mirrored glass lettering.
Lesson: borrowing a neighborhood's sign vernacular roots a new institution in its place.

**The Shuffle (Sidewalk RED), by Fathom Studio.** B Wayfinding or Signage (p.39).
Entry and jury: former hotel turned apartments; high-contrast digits and three-dimensional letters echo the playful brand.
ArtTeacher reads: a giant white 3D "3" with perforated dots against a dark wall.
Lesson: in residential wayfinding, oversized numerals can be the personality.

**Downtown Denver Partnership (16th Street), by DNCO.** G Place Brand (p.40).
Entry and jury: reclaim 16th Street as the city's stage; principles original, optimistic, open; local artist YAMZ drew the icons; 40,000 opening-weekend visitors claimed; judges praised research and narrative.
ArtTeacher reads: a pale blue window poster, "MILE-HIGH IDEAS LANDING SOON", in heavy condensed black caps with illustrated icons (bowls, snake, cactus, sun) sitting inside the line like letters.
Lesson: local illustration set inline with type makes a place brand feel made by the place.

### Process

**New York Golf Club, by Landor with The Preferred Lie.** G Internal Communications (p.44), S Implementation (p.47), S New Brand (p.72), S Sports and Leisure (p.147).
Entry and jury: team in the new Tomorrow's Golf League, away from country-club stuffiness; eastern bluebird (state bird) plus "birdie"; stark blue; NYC type; circle referencing the subway; many high-profile stakeholders.

Lesson: a double meaning (bird and birdie) plus a city code (subway roundel) is enough for an iconic sports mark. Simple to read, layered to discover.

**Adams & Reese, by Starfish Co.** S Internal Communications (p.45), S Legal and Accountancy (p.143).
Entry and jury: law firm rebrand rolled out through partner workshops and office town halls; ampersand as symbol of partnership and momentum.
ArtTeacher reads: navy field, oversized red ampersand behind white capitals; serif line "Strategic Connections, Relentless Resolve".
Lesson: a glyph already in the name (the ampersand) can become the brand's symbol at no cost to recognition.

**Sizzler, by Tavern Agency.** S Implementation (p.47), S Hospitality (p.137).

ArtTeacher reads: red and cream retro script, cartoon mascots, a doubled "ZZ" device, appetizing close food photography.
