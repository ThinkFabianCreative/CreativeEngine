# Andres Creative OS

Version: 1.0 · Recorded: 2026-09-12

## Purpose and authority

A working model of Andres's creative taste, intended for AI collaborators, designers, writers and creative teams. Use it to develop work, predict his reaction with appropriate uncertainty, and make disagreement useful. It is a personal preference profile, not a universal definition of good work, a client brief, or a scoring framework.

Source: Andres's two taste-calibration messages in the CreativeEngine conversation on 2026-09-12. Those messages include an earlier interpretation of his taste and his requested expansion into an instruction set. The preferences below are adopted as working direction; explanations of why particular references resonate remain hypotheses unless stated directly. No independent campaign or studio research was performed for this file.

Use for concept development, art direction, writing, identity, interaction and creative review for Andres. Retrieve relevant sections rather than making every assignment run a full checklist. BriefMachine may record this as a reviewer preference but must not rewrite client facts or strategy to match it.

Keep three judgments distinct: fitness for the brief, creative quality, and likely fit with Andres's taste. A prediction is not his approval. Client requirements, factual support, accessibility and the job of the deliverable remain binding. Surface tensions instead of quietly removing a mandatory element or making every client look alike.

## The central standard

Find a strong thought. Make it perceptible. Remove what weakens it. Make what remains beautifully.

Concept comes before polish. One strong thought usually beats five competing messages. Simplicity takes work: remove until the next removal would damage the idea, meaning or usability. Do not remove necessary information merely to achieve a sparse surface.

Compression is a diagnostic, not a word-count contest. State the idea in one sentence without selling it. If the sentence only describes the medium, production technique or desired emotion, the concept may still be missing. A short generic statement is not a strong idea.

The desired reaction is often: "Oh, of course" after a surprising connection becomes clear. Look for simplicity in the expression and an interesting leap in the thought.

## Advertising taste

Favor human observation, conceptual leaps, visual arguments, humor, participation and mechanisms that make a brand promise tangible. When claiming a human truth, distinguish observed evidence from a proposed hypothesis.

Three productive forms:

- **Conceptual visual:** an image, action or behavior contains the argument.
- **Compressed proposition:** a large thought expressed with very little language.
- **Generative premise:** one distinctive rule produces multiple genuinely different executions.

Ask what the audience sees, hears or does. Seek the picture before adding a headline that explains everything. Test whether the image can carry the thought, then whether language adds a second meaning rather than a caption. Type, sound and narrative can also be the idea; visual-first is not a prohibition on writing.

For campaigns, test whether the premise can grow without repeating the same trick. For a single ad or functional component, do not impose campaign-scale generativity. Across-the-street comprehension is useful for posters and simple images, not a universal test for film, long copy or an experience that unfolds over time.

Humor should emerge from a human observation, brand role or premise. A detachable gag is weaker than humor that makes the argument. Participation should express the idea through what people do, not merely ask for engagement.

Avoid production virtuosity as the entire reason for an ad to exist. Craft and spectacle may strengthen a concept, but they do not establish conceptual interest on their own.

## Visual philosophy

Favor strong hierarchy, restraint, purposeful negative space, expressive typography, deliberate composition and meticulous craft. Clarity matters more than allegiance to minimalism.

Identify the focal point and intended reading order. Use scale, contrast, density and space to direct attention. Every prominent element should earn its presence through meaning, navigation or expression. Richness is allowed when it carries the idea; emptiness is not inherently sophisticated.

Likely sources of resistance: maximalism for its own sake, ornament standing in for design, gratuitous complexity, visual noise, trend imitation and interfaces that feel assembled from unrelated components. Do not equate "simple" with a predictable centered headline, neutral type and generous margins on every job.

## Typography

Type can be the image, the voice and the conceptual mechanism. Do not treat it as the layer added after selecting photography.

Explore scale, rhythm, tension, juxtaposition, cropping, spacing and hierarchy. Ask what the typography does to the meaning. A dramatic font choice without a conceptual or compositional role is decoration.

Craft includes optical alignment, kerning, line breaks, measure, spacing, contrast and performance at actual viewing sizes. Expressive typography must still do the communication job. Deliberate tension does not excuse accidental illegibility.

Paula Scher and Massimo Vignelli are supplied reference points. Treat expressive type and disciplined systems as possible complementary interests, not proof that Andres wants one fixed typographic style.

## Digital and interaction taste

Do not automatically reproduce the familiar visual vocabulary of Material Design or a generic component library. Reusable components are useful; the objection is to a default appearance without a point of view.

Glassmorphism is welcome when it creates useful depth, hierarchy, tactility or delight. Translucency must earn its place and preserve readability. It is an option, not a house style.

Bring the physicality and discipline associated with print into digital composition, while using what interaction adds: time, behavior, responsiveness, surprise and participation.

Motion should reveal a relationship, explain a transition, establish hierarchy or enact the concept. Ask what the experience loses if the animation is removed. If the answer is only "it feels more premium," the role needs sharpening.

Design coherent states, transitions and feedback. Surprise should not make basic tasks harder. A reduced-motion or static alternative should retain the meaning. Judge interaction through its behavior over time, not only a polished still frame.

## Craft and ambition

The standard is beautifully made. Idea and craft need each other: polishing a weak premise does not repair it, and careless execution can destroy a strong one.

Production should make the thought feel inevitable. Refine the decisions the audience will actually perceive: composition, type, image choice, timing, performance, sound, transitions and finishing. Stop when additional effort only adds ornament or overworking.

Aim for work Andres would willingly put in his portfolio. On suitable assignments, explore ambition that could stand beside exceptional award-recognized work. Do not turn every deliverable into an award project, claim award eligibility without evidence, invent results, or build an elaborate case-story to compensate for an ordinary audience experience.

Client approval is a necessary milestone where applicable, not the sole creative aspiration. Respect scope, budget, format and timing while finding the strongest available expression.

## Creative tensions

| Tension | Working interpretation |
|---|---|
| Minimal, but not sterile | Remove clutter; retain personality, warmth or productive tension. |
| Beautiful, but not decorative | Let visual pleasure strengthen the message or experience. |
| Strategic, but never strategy-looking | Embody the strategy; do not make the audience read the planning deck. |
| Simple, but not obvious | Express the thought simply; avoid the first generic answer. |
| Modern, but not trend-dependent | Use contemporary capabilities without borrowing a fashionable surface by default. |
| Crafted, but never overworked | Make precise decisions; stop before refinement becomes embellishment. |
| Funny, but not gag advertising | Make the humor inseparable from the observation or brand argument. |
| Emotional, but not sentimental | Earn feeling through specificity, behavior and stakes rather than emotional shorthand. |
| Interactive, but never interaction for its own sake | Make participation or response carry meaning. |
| AI-enabled, but never AI-flavored | Use the tool freely; reject generic language, arbitrary detail and undirected polish. |

These are productive tensions, not paired absolute bans. Explain the choice when a particular brief calls for an exception.

## Anti-slop editing rules

- No unnecessary manifestos or "in a world where…" strategy openings.
- No three-adjective positioning statements used as a substitute for a specific thought.
- No generic inspirational copy that could belong to any organization.
- No visual complexity added to simulate sophistication.
- No redundant explanation of an already legible idea.
- No claim that work is bold, disruptive, iconic or ownable without showing what earns the description.
- No automatic wall of concept names, rationales and mood words when a sketch or a sentence would communicate more.

These are editing preferences, not a blind lexical filter. Keep language or detail that is necessary to the actual work.

## Evaluation behavior

Be candid. Do not flatter Andres or reverse a judgment merely to agree with him. Identify what is merely competent, what is strong, and what needs a different underlying idea. Critique the work specifically rather than performing harshness.

Distinguish:

- **Observation:** something noticed; identify its evidence or provisional status.
- **Insight:** an interpretation that reveals a useful human tension or opportunity.
- **Concept:** the organizing creative thought or mechanism that answers the brief.
- **Execution:** the particular expression through copy, image, design, film or behavior.

Do not relabel an observation as an insight or a format as a concept to make the work sound more developed.

Useful questions, selected for the object:

1. What is the idea in one plain sentence?
2. Where is the human observation or conceptual leap?
3. Can we show, enact or typographically express it?
4. Why does this brand have a credible, distinctive role?
5. Does the execution reveal the idea or merely decorate it?
6. Can the premise generate more work, when the assignment needs that?
7. What can be removed without losing meaning?
8. Is what remains interesting, useful and beautifully made?

When something feels derivative, identify the resemblance and the evidence. If no comparison has been checked, say that it feels familiar; do not invent provenance or assert uniqueness. "More ownable" means a stronger brand connection here, not a trademark or exclusivity determination.

A short critique can state: the thought, the strongest feature, the principal weakness, the likely taste reaction with uncertainty, and the next useful move. Do not add numeric scores or formal verdicts automatically. Existing mode and verdict rules still apply.

## Working rhythm

In EXPLORE, allow divergent, strange and unfinished possibilities. Do not eliminate every departure from this profile before seeing its potential.

In DEVELOP, find the strongest mechanisms, make them tangible and test a simpler version. In JUDGE, select decisively when a decision is requested and explain the tradeoff.

Andres's shorthand: "Don't show Andres twenty perfectly reasonable ideas. Find the five with an interesting conceptual mechanism. Kill three. Push two. Make them visual. Remove the unnecessary explanation. Then ask: is there an even simpler idea hiding underneath this one?"

Treat those numbers as a preference for disciplined convergence, not an automatic deliverable count or an instruction to kill rough ideas during exploration. Honor the actual brief's requested counts. Internal exploration may be much broader than the presented shortlist.

## Calibration and maintenance

This is V1, not a validated prediction model. No numerical weights, accuracy claims or fixed approval thresholds are established.

For calibration, review work before learning Andres's preference, state the predicted reaction and reasons, then compare with his actual feedback. Record the mismatch as carefully as the match. A disagreement may reveal context, a mistaken interpretation or a useful exception rather than a new universal rule.

Keep client work, critiques, performance and approval evidence within the appropriate client project. This shared profile must not collect client examples or become a route for cross-client learning. Update it only with Andres's explicitly stated general preferences, without copying client facts. Retain project-specific learning in its original scope.

For a general preference update, record: date, Andres's wording, earlier interpretation, revised interpretation, scope or exception, and remaining uncertainty. Do not promote an agent's prediction to an established preference without his confirmation.
