# Social video scripts

Covers vertical and feed video for social and paid: Reels, Stories and similar short-form placements. Also covers creator-style scripts and cutdowns. Platform-agnostic craft. Durations, ratios, caption support, safe zones and music rules come from the platform's verified record: Meta in `shared/knowledge/meta-paid-ads/video-specs.md` and `production.md`; others UNVERIFIED unless cited.

Story structure (whether there is an obstacle, where the turn falls, what the brand's entrance does, how many beats a duration holds) is judged with `shared/frameworks/narrative-craft.md`. This reference covers how that story is written for the feed.

Camera direction for vertical, POV and phone-style video (framing, lens, light, movement, and the fake-UGC caution) is in `DesignChamp/knowledge/image-direction-reference.md` §9.7–9.9.

## The encounter

A scroll that decides in a moment whether to stay. Often sound off at first, on a phone, with interface over the edges. The viewer did not choose this video; the first frame and first words have to earn the next second.

## Script format

Write social video as a beat table. The layers are separate because they are produced separately and seen differently:

```
TITLE / VERSION / PLACEMENT / TARGET DURATION (cite spec record)
IDEA: one sentence

| # | TIME   | VISUAL (what we see)      | SUPER (on-screen type) | VO / DIALOGUE        | SOUND / MUSIC |
|---|--------|---------------------------|------------------------|----------------------|---------------|
| 1 | 0–1s   | ...                       | ...                    | ...                  | ...           |
```

- **SUPER** is designed type that DesignChamp composes: short and in brand voice.
- **Captions** transcribe speech. They are a separate layer, whether auto or burned in. Note where caption tracks are unsupported for the placement.
- **VISUAL** describes what happens, not camera jargon for its own sake.
- Mark the logo or brand moment and the CTA beat explicitly.

## Craft

**The hook is the idea arriving, not a warm-up.**
- The first beat shows or says the most interesting thing: the tension, the surprise, the product doing something, the human moment.
- No logo sting, slow fade or "Hey guys" before the idea.
- A hook is not clickbait. It must be paid off by the rest of the video.

**Sound off first, sound on as reward.**
- Read only the SUPER and VISUAL columns: the idea must land.
- Then read VO and SOUND: they should add wit, emotion or detail, not repeat the supers word for word.

**Supers are read at the pace they appear.**
- A few words per beat, one thought per super.
- Hold long enough to read on a phone. If a super cannot be read in its beat, cut words, not time.
- Supers and captions must not collide; plan positions with DesignChamp inside safe zones.

**Write for the edit.**
- One beat, one job. Beats should be removable for cutdowns without breaking the idea.
- Put the brand where the idea needs it. Brand early by mechanism, color, product or voice, not only a logo end card.

**End on the action or the payoff, not a restatement.**
- The last beat pays off the hook and makes the next step clear.
- If there is a CTA, use the approved wording exactly.

**Loops.** Short vertical video often replays. An ending that flows back into the opening can reward a second view; do it only when it serves the idea.

## Cutdowns

Plan the longest version with its shorter versions in mind:

- Identify the **spine**: the beats that carry the idea from hook to payoff. Every cutdown keeps the spine.
- A cutdown is re-edited, not truncated. It often needs its own super or VO line to bridge removed beats.
- Durations come from the placement's verified record and the media plan, never a remembered "6/15/30" assumption.
- Each cutdown gets its own beat table.

## Creator-style and talking-head scripts

- **Talking points, not a teleprompter.** When authenticity is the point, write the beats, the must-say lines (claims, mandatories, CTA) and the no-go language. Let the creator phrase the rest in their voice.
- Mark exactly which lines are **verbatim** (legal, claims, offer, CTA) and which are **flexible**.
- Match the creator's real voice. Corporate copy spoken by a creator reads as an ad and loses the point of using one.
- Disclosure of paid relationships follows the platform's current policy (verify). Never write it out.
- **Testimonial truth.** Never script a claim, experience or result the person has not actually had. Testimonials and outcome claims need the claim ledger's support.
- **A testimonial script is a casting spec.** When real people deliver scripted lines drawn from their own experience, write each script with what must be true of its speaker. Cast to that condition, adjust the wording to the person at intake, and have them confirm the lines are true for them. Never move a line from one person to another, and never assemble composites.

## Audio and music

- **VO:** write for the ear. Short sentences, words that are easy to say, rhythm read aloud at the target pace. Time it.
- **Music and sound:** describe the role (tension, lift, comic timing, silence). Licensing and placement exclusions come from the record; for example, some Reels placements exclude licensed music (`production.md`).
- **Trending audio:** only when rights and platform rules allow it, and when it fits the brand. Flag it, never assume it.

## Fidelity check additions

- [ ] The hook delivers the idea in the first beat, with no warm-up.
- [ ] The video works reading only VISUAL and SUPER, sound off.
- [ ] VO adds to supers rather than repeating them.
- [ ] Every super is readable at its beat length on a phone.
- [ ] The spine is marked, and cutdowns are separately scripted.
- [ ] Verbatim lines (claims, mandatories, CTA) are marked; creator lines are flexible where appropriate.
- [ ] Duration, caption support and music rules are cited to the placement record, or UNVERIFIED.
