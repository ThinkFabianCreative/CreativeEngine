# Format copywriting

Copy in social and paid is written for an encounter: a thumb moving fast, sound often off, a surface full of other people's content and interface overlays. The same idea needs different words in a Feed ad's primary text, a Reel's on-screen supers and an organic caption.

This skill is method. WordSmither owns verbal judgment, DesignChamp owns the image, and CreativeDirector owns selection. V1 covers social and paid, in English.

## When to use

- An idea has survived EXPLORE and needs copy for a real format (DEVELOP), or copy is being prepared for review (JUDGE).
- Someone asks for ad copy, primary text, headlines for ads, captions, hooks, a social video script, supers or a cutdown.

Do not use it to write around a missing idea. If the idea cannot be stated in one sentence, stop and return to concept work.

## Process

1. **Anchor the idea.** One sentence. The copy is an execution: it expresses the parent idea, it does not invent a new one.
2. **Name the encounter.** Channel, placement, format, paid or organic, sound on or off, and where the audience is in their relationship with the brand.
3. **Find the format's job for this idea.** Stop the scroll? Deliver a proof? Earn a swipe? Start a conversation? One job per asset.
4. **Map the copy surfaces.** List every place words appear: on-asset type, supers, VO, captions, primary text, headline, description, CTA button, card headlines, form screens, greeting. Decide which surface carries the idea, and which only supports it.
5. **Write the smallest state first.** The first line before truncation, frame one, card one, the hook. If the idea dies there, rewrite before writing the rest.
6. **Write the rest, adding, never repeating.** Each surface adds something the others do not.
7. **Build variants deliberately** (below).
8. **Check** against the copy check below and WordSmither's self-critique.
9. **Hand off** with the note below.

## Craft that holds across social and paid

**The asset carries the idea; fields support it.** On most placements people see the image or first frame before they read. Write with DesignChamp so the visual and the first words make one thought.

**Front-load meaning.** The first words of primary text, the first line of a caption and the first second of video are all that many people see. Put the reason to care there, not a warm-up.

**Every surface must stand alone.** Fields may be truncated, hidden or recombined by the platform. No surface may depend on another to make sense. No "as you can see above", no headline that only completes the primary text.

**One asset, one job, one CTA.** A paid ad that tries to introduce, persuade, prove and convert usually does none.

**Native, not generic.** Write the way people talk on the platform, in the brand's voice. Native does not mean slang, emoji or trend language the brand would never use.

**Specific beats clever beats generic.** A concrete detail stops the scroll more reliably than wordplay. Wordplay is welcome when it carries the idea.

**Proof on the asset is one line.** When a program fact or proof point appears on the asset, use one short line in a fixed slot, drawn from the claim ledger, never a bullet list. Keep a small pool of approved lines that can swap into the slot; everything else goes in primary text or the caption.

**Sound off first.** Video copy must work without audio: on-screen supers or captions carry the idea, and VO rewards those who listen.

**Message match.** The ad's promise, offer and wording must match the destination: the landing page, form, conversation or shop. An ad that promises what the destination cannot deliver is a false promise.

**Never engagement bait.** "Comment YES", "Tag a friend who…" or "Like if…" used only to game reach is out, unless participation is genuinely the idea and platform policy allows it (verify).

## Variants and testing

Paid social invites variants. A senior writer makes them useful.

- **Vary the idea, not the wording.** Each variant tests a distinct hypothesis: a different message job, angle, proof, tension or hook mechanism. Synonym swaps test nothing.
- **Name each variant by its hypothesis**, for example `V2 PROOF-FIRST: leads with the reviewed statistic` rather than `V2`.
- **Recombination.** Where the platform may combine text options with different assets (flexible creative or automated enhancements), every text option must work with every asset it could be paired with. Check what is enabled (`meta-paid-ads/production.md`); approving the copy does not approve a generated alteration.
- **No performance promises.** Do not predict CTR, conversion or "this will perform". State what each variant tests and what result would tell you something.

## Conversion clarity

For copy whose job is a decision: lead and conversion ads, click-to-message, the landing page or destination behind them, nurture email, sales one-liners. A DEVELOP and JUDGE check, never an EXPLORE filter, and never a substitute for the idea. A clear message can still be an idea; clarity is required, cleverness is not banned. Adapted from Donald Miller, *Building a StoryBrand* (SB7); its fill-in copy patterns and 10/10 score are deliberately not adopted.

- **The customer is the hero, the brand is the guide.** Copy about the brand's journey competes with the reader's. Quick tell: the first word is "We".
- **Three levels of problem.** External (the tangible problem), internal (how it makes them feel), philosophical (why it shouldn't be this way). Most copy stops at external; people act on the internal one. Use only what the brief or ledger supports.
- **Empathy and authority together.** Understanding without proof is unearned; proof without understanding is cold. Authority comes from the claim ledger, never invented.
- **Both stakes, lightly.** What doing nothing costs and what success looks like. Name the cost of waiting; no scare tactics, no fake urgency.
- **One direct action, one lighter one.** One obvious primary CTA per asset, and a lower-commitment option for people not ready yet. The button must say what happens on click: nothing paid is called free.
- **A plan of three or four steps** to lower perceived risk, plus any approved risk-removing commitment (guarantee, cancel anytime). Commitments are claims: ledger status applies.
- **One-hearing test.** Can someone repeat the one-liner or line after hearing it once?

## Copy check

- [ ] The parent idea is legible in the smallest state: first line, frame one, card one or hook.
- [ ] Every copy surface is mapped, and each one adds something without repeating another.
- [ ] Meaning survives truncation and hidden fields.
- [ ] Character counts are checked against the cited recommendation for the actual placement, or labeled UNVERIFIED.
- [ ] CTA wording is exactly as approved, and the CTA button choice is not duplicated awkwardly in the copy.
- [ ] Claims are within ledger status; nothing is invented; flags are raised.
- [ ] Voice matches the client's approved voice and English variety.
- [ ] No engagement bait, fake urgency or unverified personal-attribute language.
- [ ] Message matches the destination.
- [ ] Conversion copy: passes the conversion clarity checks above.
- [ ] Video: works sound off; supers are short enough to read at the pace they appear.
- [ ] `humanizer` pass on primary text, captions and long copy (not on hooks or headlines; see `WordSmither/skills/humanizer.md`).

## Handoff note

Use WordSmither's handoff format (`WordSmither/AGENT.md`), and add for each asset:

```
PLACEMENT(S):       as established, or COPY ASSUMPTION
COPY SURFACES:      field-by-field copy, in display order
SMALLEST STATE:     what survives truncation or sound off
SPEC SOURCES:       knowledge record + date, or UNVERIFIED
VARIANT HYPOTHESIS: for each variant
POLICY / CLAIMS:    flags and open questions
```
