# Slideshow and presentation

Covers presented decks (speaker-led), read decks (sent and read alone) and creative presentations of concepts and prototypes. Decide which one it is before designing: they are different formats.

## The encounter

| Kind | Condition | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Presented | A room or a call, a speaker controls pace, a screen at distance | Few words, big images, one point per slide; the speaker carries the detail |
| Read | Alone, at the reader's pace, often on a laptop or phone | Complete sentences, clear structure, each slide self-sufficient |
| Creative presentation | Decision-makers judging an idea | The idea must be stated simply and then experienced in its executions |

## The format's job

Move an audience through a sequence of understanding to a decision, with each slide doing one job.

## Craft

**One idea per slide.** If a slide has two points, it is two slides.

**Headlines state the point.** Write the takeaway as the slide title, not a topic label. Read the titles alone in order: they should tell the whole story.

**Sequence is the design.** Pace reveals so the audience meets the idea at the right moment. A concept shown before its insight lands flat; an insight explained for ten slides kills the idea.

**Show, then explain.** For creative work, let the audience experience the execution (the prototype in context, full-bleed or live) before the rationale.

**A system, not a template.** Set a grid, a type scale and a few layout types that express the campaign, and vary composition within them. Every slide looking the same is monotony; every slide looking different is noise.

**Type at projection distance.** Presented slides are read from the back of a room or a shared small window. Test the deck at thumbnail size.

**Data slides make one claim.** Highlight the number that matters; mute the rest.

**Builds and transitions have a job.** Use them to control reveal or show a relationship, not as decoration.

A creative presentation often moves from the problem or brief truth, to the insight, to the idea in one sentence, to its key visual, to executions in context, to how it extends across formats. That is a common shape, not a formula; CreativeDirector decides the presentation.

## What to prototype

- The full sequence at the real aspect ratio of the venue or tool.
- Presented decks with builds and embedded prototypes or video working.
- Read decks as a PDF or page flow, checked on a laptop and a phone.
- Non-linear decks with clickable navigation when the conversation may jump.

Tools: the `pptx` skill for PowerPoint files, a Claude design artifact, Figma Slides (`figma-use-slides`), or an HTML deck for rich motion and live prototypes.

## Buildability flags

- Embedded live prototypes or video that depend on connectivity in the room.
- Fonts that will not travel with a PowerPoint or Keynote file.
- Builds that do not survive export to PDF for a read deck.

## Fidelity check additions

- [ ] The slide titles alone tell the story.
- [ ] Legible at thumbnail size.
- [ ] Executions are shown in context before they are explained.
