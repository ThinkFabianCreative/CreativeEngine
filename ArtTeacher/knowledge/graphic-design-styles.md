# Graphic design styles and movements

> ArtTeacher global knowledge. A working map of graphic design styles so any role can name, use or avoid one on purpose. No client facts. Written from general design-history knowledge on 2026-11-17; dates and attributions are the commonly cited ones. Verify against a source (`CreativeResearcher/knowledge/design-history-and-deck-sources.md`) before citing in client work.

## How to use this

- **No style is the default.** Pick the one whose signals fit the brief, audience and medium, or the one the user asks for. Andres personally likes Swiss design (see the end of this file); that's a preference, never a rule for a client.
- **Borrow principles, not costumes.** Take the logic (grid, contrast, scale, image treatment), not a pastiche. A style used as decoration over a weak idea fails `barry-concept-art-direction.md`.
- **Movements are not interchangeable.** "Minimal" is not Swiss; Bauhaus is not Constructivism. Name the movement precisely.
- **Check signals.** Every style carries meanings (luxury, rebellion, nostalgia, tech, craft). Make sure they're the ones the brand wants.
- **Type substitutes:** suggestions below are free or widely licensed stand-ins; check the client's brand fonts first, and licensing before production.

## Historical movements

| Style | When / where | Key figures | What it looks like | Typical type | Signals | Use it for | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Arts and Crafts** | 1860s–1910s, UK | William Morris, Kelmscott Press | Dense ornament, woodcut borders, botanical pattern, handmade feel | Blackletter and Venetian revivals (Golden Type) | Craft, heritage, honesty of materials | Artisanal, heritage, slow-made products | Fussy, nostalgic, hard to read at small sizes |
| **Art Nouveau** | 1890s–1910, Europe | Alphonse Mucha, Jules Chéret, Toulouse-Lautrec | Whiplash curves, organic line, framed female figures, flat decorative color | Custom hand-lettering; Arnold Böcklin | Elegance, nature, Belle Époque romance | Beauty, beverages, festivals, botanical | Cliché quickly; ornamental type harms legibility |
| **Vienna Secession / Wiener Werkstätte** | 1897–1930s, Austria | Koloman Moser, Josef Hoffmann, Gustav Klimt | Geometric ornament, squares and grids, black/white/gold, strong framing | Geometric display lettering | Refined modernity, culture | Galleries, luxury, cultural institutions | Can read as costume |
| **Plakatstil (German poster style)** | 1900s–1910s, Germany | Lucian Bernhard, Ludwig Hohlwein | One object, flat color, bold product name, no clutter | Heavy sans or hand-drawn bold lettering | Directness, product confidence | Product-hero posters, OOH, packaging | Needs a strong single object; empty without one |
| **Futurism** | 1909–1930s, Italy | F. T. Marinetti, Fortunato Depero | Words in motion, typographic explosions, onomatopoeia, diagonals | Mixed type set at angles and sizes | Speed, noise, machines | Energy, launches, sound-led work | Political history (ties to Fascism); chaotic legibility |
| **Dada** | 1916–1924, Zurich/Berlin | Hannah Höch, Raoul Hausmann, Kurt Schwitters | Collage, photomontage, random type, absurdity | Found type, mixed fonts | Anti-establishment, irony | Provocative, cultural, satire | Easy to fake; needs a real idea behind the chaos |
| **De Stijl** | 1917–1931, Netherlands | Piet Mondrian, Theo van Doesburg, Bart van der Leck | Primary colors plus black and white, orthogonal grids, rectangles | Blocky geometric letterforms | Purity, order, Dutch modernism | Architecture, art, systematic identities | Mondrian pastiche is a cliché |
| **Russian Constructivism** | 1917–1930s, USSR | El Lissitzky, Alexander Rodchenko, Stenberg brothers, Gustav Klutsis | Red/black, diagonals, photomontage, bold sans, type as structure | Heavy condensed sans; Cyrillic grotesques | Revolution, collective action, urgency | Activism, calls to action, campaigns, music | Propaganda associations; often copied as "retro poster" |
| **Bauhaus** | 1919–1933, Germany | Herbert Bayer, László Moholy-Nagy, Joost Schmidt | Circle/square/triangle, primary colors, asymmetric layout, photography, lowercase | Geometric sans (Futura, Bayer Universal); free: Jost, Josefin Sans | Rational modernity, function, education | Design schools, architecture, functional products | "Bauhaus style" shapes are now a cliché; the school was a method, not a look |
| **New Typography (Die neue Typographie)** | 1920s–1930s, Germany | Jan Tschichold | Asymmetric layout, sans serif, white space as active, functional hierarchy | Akzidenz-Grotesk, Futura | Clarity, function | Editorial, information, documents | Tschichold himself later moved back to classical symmetry |
| **Art Deco** | 1920s–1930s, France/USA | A. M. Cassandre, Erté | Streamlined geometry, sunbursts, metallic palettes, elongated figures, travel posters | Broadway, Peignot, Bifur; free: Poiret One, Limelight | Glamour, luxury, travel, the Jazz Age | Hospitality, spirits, premium events | Gatsby cliché; gold-on-black overused |
| **WPA posters** | 1935–1943, USA | Federal Art Project artists | Screenprint, flat color, simple shapes, public messages | Simple sans and slab lettering | Civic, outdoors, public good | Parks, public health, civic campaigns | Heavily imitated in national-park nostalgia |
| **Swiss / International Typographic Style** | 1950s–1970s, Switzerland | Josef Müller-Brockmann, Armin Hofmann, Max Bill, Emil Ruder, Karl Gerstner | Strict modular grid, flush-left ragged-right sans, objective photography, asymmetry, strong scale contrast, lots of white space | Akzidenz-Grotesk, Helvetica, Univers; free: Inter, Neue Haas substitutes like Inter Tight, Schibsted Grotesk | Clarity, objectivity, trust, modernity | Information, institutions, culture, tech, corporate, decks | Without an idea it turns corporate and cold; not the same as "minimal" |
| **Mid-century American modernism / New York School** | 1940s–1960s, USA | Paul Rand, Saul Bass, Alvin Lustig, Bradbury Thompson | Playful shapes, cut paper, wit, bold color, symbolic simplification | Mixed; hand-cut lettering; Franklin Gothic, Clarendon | Optimism, wit, confidence | Identities, film titles, books, ads with a joke | Mid-century pastiche is common |
| **Big Idea advertising (DDB era)** | 1959–1970s, USA | Helmut Krone, Bill Bernbach, George Lois | One image, honest headline, lots of white space, the idea carries everything | Futura, Franklin Gothic, Clarendon | Candor, intelligence, wit | Advertising generally | Needs a real idea; looks empty when imitated without one |
| **Japanese postwar poster design** | 1950s–1980s, Japan | Yusaku Kamekura, Ikko Tanaka, Tadanori Yokoo, Kazumasa Nagai | Flat geometric color, ma (negative space), strong symbols; Yokoo adds pop collage | Minimal sans plus kanji; geometric display | Precision, poise, craft; Yokoo: wild pop | Culture, events, premium, sport | Exoticizing it; misusing Japanese script |
| **Polish poster school** | 1950s–1980s, Poland | Henryk Tomaszewski, Roman Cieślewicz, Jan Lenica, Franciszek Starowieyski | Painterly, surreal metaphor, handwritten type, dark wit | Hand-lettering | Intelligence, art, metaphor | Film, theatre, culture, idea-led posters | Needs illustration skill and a strong metaphor |
| **Psychedelic** | 1965–1970s, USA/UK | Wes Wilson, Victor Moscoso, Bonnie MacLean | Vibrating complementary colors, melting letterforms filling shapes | Hand-drawn warped lettering | Counterculture, music, altered states | Music, festivals, cannabis, nostalgia | Often illegible on purpose; poor on small screens |
| **Pop Art graphics** | 1960s, USA/UK | Andy Warhol, Roy Lichtenstein | Ben-Day dots, comics, repetition, consumer imagery | Bold comic and display lettering | Mass culture, fun, irony | Retail, food, youth | Lichtenstein pastiche is tired |
| **Supergraphics** | 1960s–1970s, USA | Barbara Stauffacher Solomon | Huge type and stripes wrapping architecture | Helvetica at architectural scale | Bold, environmental, optimistic | Wayfinding, environments, events | Needs space and scale to work |
| **Punk / DIY** | 1976–1980s, UK/USA | Jamie Reid, Raymond Pettibon | Ransom-note type, photocopy texture, xerox grime, collage | Cut-up letters, typewriter | Rebellion, authenticity, anti-polish | Music, youth, protest, streetwear | Corporate punk reads as fake |
| **Postmodern / Memphis / New Wave** | late 1970s–1980s, Switzerland/USA/Italy | Wolfgang Weingart, April Greiman, Dan Friedman; Memphis Group (Ettore Sottsass) | Breaks the Swiss grid: layered type, texture, squiggles, pastel plus black, confetti | Mixed weights, letterspaced type, Emigre fonts | Play, irony, '80s energy | Youth, fashion, entertainment | Memphis squiggles = instant cliché |
| **Grunge / Emigre / Ray Gun** | late 1980s–1990s, USA | David Carson, Rudy VanderLans, Zuzana Ličko, Neville Brody (The Face) | Distressed, overlapping, deconstructed type; legibility challenged | Emigre faces, distressed fonts | Rebellion, editorial edge, '90s | Music, skate, surf, fashion editorial | Hard to read; quickly dated |
| **Dutch design / conceptual graphic design** | 1990s–now, Netherlands | Experimental Jetset, Studio Dumbar, Irma Boom | Systematic but witty; typographic ideas; unexpected materials | Neutral sans used conceptually | Intelligence, culture, self-aware wit | Culture, institutions, books | Can be too insider |

## Contemporary styles and aesthetics

| Style | What it looks like | Signals | Use it for | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Flat design** | Flat color, simple shapes, no texture or shadow | Clarity, digital-native | UI, icons, explainers | Generic; illustration "corporate Memphis" cliché |
| **Corporate Memphis / Alegria** | Flat cartoon people with long limbs, bright colors | Friendly tech | Almost nothing now | The most recognizable tech cliché; avoid |
| **Neo-brutalism / web brutalism** | Raw HTML feel, heavy borders, hard shadows, clashing color, system fonts | Honest, anti-slick, indie | Startups, culture, youth | Can look broken; legibility and accessibility |
| **Neo-grotesk minimalism** | Big grotesk type, near-monochrome, lots of white space | Premium, calm, confident | Tech, fashion, studios | Easily generic; needs one strong idea |
| **Editorial / magazine** | Serif-sans mix, big pull quotes, image-led spreads, columns | Authority, storytelling, culture | Long-form, case studies, decks | Over-styling thin content |
| **Y2K / Frutiger Aero** | Chrome, bubbles, glossy gradients, aqua, lens flares | Early-2000s nostalgia, optimism | Youth, fashion, music | Trend-bound; dates fast |
| **Acid / rave graphics** | Chrome type, liquid shapes, neon, sci-fi | Club culture, experimental | Music, nightlife, fashion | Trend-bound |
| **Anti-design / maximalism** | Clashing type, collage, loud color, rule-breaking | Energy, Gen Z, irreverence | Youth, streetwear, entertainment | Chaos without hierarchy |
| **Swiss revival / new Swiss** | Swiss grid with oversize type, tight tracking, color fields, motion | Smart, contemporary, design-literate | Studios, culture, tech, decks | Overused in portfolio sites; keep an idea |
| **Retro-futurism / 70s revival** | Warm earth tones, rounded display type, rainbow stripes | Nostalgia, warmth | Food, drink, lifestyle | Heavy use since 2019 |
| **Bento grid** | Modular rounded tiles showing features | Product overview, Apple-style | Product and feature pages | Card-grid cliché; flagged in `visual-craft.md` |
| **Glassmorphism / gradient mesh** | Frosted glass, blurred blobs, soft gradients | Tech, AI | Rarely | A generic AI-aesthetic tell; avoid without a reason |

## Matching a style to a brief (questions to ask)

1. What should the audience feel and believe? List the signals, then find styles that carry them.
2. Does the category already use this style? If every competitor is minimal, minimal won't be distinctive (`creative-effectiveness.md` category stereotypes).
3. Does the medium suit it? Psychedelic and grunge die on a phone; Plakatstil and Swiss survive at thumbnail size.
4. Does the brand allow it? Client brand standards override style choices.
5. Pastiche or principle? Say which principle you're taking.

## Andres's personal preference

Andres likes **Swiss / International Typographic Style** and its contemporary revival: grid discipline, typography as the hero, flush-left sans, strong scale contrast, white space, objective imagery. Use it as the starting point for **Andres's own and internal TRAFFIK work** (pitch decks, internal presentations, his own projects) unless he asks otherwise. For client work, the client's brand and the brief decide; propose Swiss only when it fits that job.
