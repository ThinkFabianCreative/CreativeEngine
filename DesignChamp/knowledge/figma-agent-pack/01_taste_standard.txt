01 · THE TASTE STANDARD

THE CENTRAL RULE
Find the strong thought. Make it perceptible. Remove what weakens it. Make what remains
beautifully.

Every ad has ONE idea. Before designing, state it to yourself in one plain sentence.
Every design decision should make that sentence easier to see. If a decision does not
serve the idea, remove it.

WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE
- Strong hierarchy: the eye knows where to go first, second, third.
- Restraint: few elements, each one earning its place through meaning or navigation.
- Purposeful negative space: space that points at something, not space left over.
- Expressive typography: type can BE the image, not a caption laid on top.
- Meticulous craft: alignment, spacing, line breaks and size checked at real viewing size.

PRODUCTIVE TENSIONS (aim for the left side without falling into the right)
  Minimal, but not sterile.
  Beautiful, but not decorative.
  Simple, but not obvious.
  Modern, but not trend-dependent.
  Crafted, but never overworked.
  AI-enabled, but never AI-flavored.

"Simple" does NOT mean the predictable default: centered headline, neutral type, big
margins on every job. That is the generic answer. Find the composition this specific
idea needs.

WHAT GETS REJECTED
- Ornament standing in for design.
- Visual complexity added to look sophisticated.
- Maximalism or noise for its own sake.
- Trend imitation (fashionable gradients, glass, bento grids) without a reason.
- Layouts that look assembled from unrelated template components.
- Polish on a weak idea. Craft cannot rescue a missing concept; say so instead.
- Anything that looks like a generic AI-generated ad.

THE REACTION WE WANT
"Oh, of course." A small surprise that becomes clear. If the viewer has to be told what
the design means, the design is not doing its job yet.
