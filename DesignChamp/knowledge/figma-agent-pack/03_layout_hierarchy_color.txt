03 · LAYOUT, HIERARCHY AND COLOR

READING ORDER
Before placing anything, decide: what is seen first, second, third, last.
Typical ad order: the idea (headline or image) > supporting line > brand > program label.
The brand logo is never the first thing seen unless the request says so.

COMPOSITION
- Give the main element real scale. Timid headlines waste the frame.
- Use space to direct attention toward the idea, not as evenly distributed padding.
- Align elements to a clear grid or edge. Random offsets look accidental.
- Group related items tightly; separate unrelated items clearly.
- One focal point per frame.

COLOR
- Use the client palette only, in flat fills.
- Color carries meaning. An accent color should mark the one thing that matters
  (a key word, a kept promise, a line of progress). If the accent is sprinkled on
  several unrelated things, it stops meaning anything.
- Dominant color large, accent color small. Accent covering big areas loses its power.
- No gradients unless the client supplies an official gradient and the request asks for it.

WHAT TO REMOVE
- Duplicate branding: if the logo already says the name, do not also type the name.
- Drawn buttons (fake "Apply Now" buttons) in ad images. Platforms add real buttons.
- Rules, dots, arrows, frames and dividers that do not separate or point to anything.
- Arrows in general: they imply direction or travel and often contradict the message.
- Footer text stacks that nobody can read at phone size.

ILLUSTRATION AND ICONS
- An illustration must be recognizable in under a second at phone size.
  If it could be mistaken for something else (a plant that looks like a sun or a hat),
  it has failed. Add the identifying details or stop and report.
- Do not add icons to explain the idea. If the idea needs an icon, the idea is unclear.
